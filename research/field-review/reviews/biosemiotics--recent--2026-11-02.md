What the source gives, quoted from its own sentences:

- Abstract definition (p.5): "In biosemiotics, 'habit' refers to the regular, recurring behaviors or patterns that emerge from an organism's interactions with its environment. This concept, deeply rooted in Charles Sanders Peirce's philosophy, is understood as an adaptive mechanism where repeated actions lead to the stabilization of organisms or systems. Over time, these repeated actions form patterns that guide future behaviors."
- Disposition vs behaviour (p.16 survey Q1 synthesis): respondents converge on habit as "repetition of established sign relations", "the crystallization of seemingly random and/or unique feature of nature", "habit as a disposition to act", "a goal-directed action", "a tendency to respond", "pattern of activity of an agent in response to a sign", "a routinization of a behavior", "an ultimate final interpretant". The paper stresses habit is a disposition to act, not the act itself, and that this Peircean disposition is coextensive with his categories of being (p.7 n.1, Bernardi della Rosa 2024).
- Habit-taking as generative tendency (p.18 Peirce EP2 418 + C.P. 7.515 / W6:208 quoted verbatim as survey prompts): "We also find in all active protoplasm a tendency to take habits ... all things have a tendency to take habits. For atoms and their parts, molecules and groups of molecules ... there is a greater probability of acting as on a former like occasion than otherwise. This tendency itself constitutes a regularity, and is continually on the increase ... three elements are active in the world, first, chance; second, law; and third, habit-taking." And Peirce 1907 EP2:418 non-mental extension: "some plants take habits. The stream of water that wears a bed for itself is forming a habit. Every ditcher so thinks of it."
- The warning against conflation (pp.16–19): "Peirce has been criticized for not drawing a clear distinction between habit and physical law (Kull, 2014, 2024)" (p.11); survey respondents question "Peirce's characterization of 'law,' suggesting that his use of the term aligns more with human-developed mathematical models than with inherent universal regularities ... Some scholars argue that labeling natural phenomena as 'laws' can be misleading, proposing alternative terms like regularities or control" (p.18); of the two Peirce law/habit-taking quotes, 52% rated relevance above 6 and 48% below (p.18), and on the non-mental extension 67% rated alignment above 6 and 33% below (p.19) — the field disagrees whether cosmic habit-taking is a gloss on law or a distinct third element.
- Macro-level constraint reading (p.11, Atã & Queiroz 2016): "habits function as macro-level constraints within hierarchical systems, shaping the processes of semiosis while simultaneously being shaped by the evolving environments these organisms construct" — including niche construction; with dynamical-systems gloss Favareau & Kull 2024:41: "establishment of higher-order regularities within a complex adaptive system is often the emergent product of multiple interacting lower-order regularities ... Such higher-order regularities may then come to exert formative 'downwards' effects upon the system, sustaining the system in ongoing loops of generative self-regulation and normativity."
- Semiotic scaffolding (p.10 n.5 + Hoffmeyer 1998:287 quoted p.10): "semiotic scaffolding" and "semiotic interaction" as "interactions in which regularities (habits) developed by one species successively become, for sign-mediated interaction of organisms, used (interpreted) as signs by the individuals of the same or another species, thereby eliciting new habits in this species, eventually to become — sooner or later — signs for other individuals, and so on in a branching and unending web." Kull 2022 addition: habits "also emerge as processes of choice, memory and learning, and are also the source of aesthetics in natural coordination and fitting" (p.10).

## What is new here

**Habit is not the behaviour you see; it is the disposition that makes you probable to repeat it, and habit-taking is the separate generative tendency to acquire new dispositions — calling either of them a "law" hides whether a regularity should be kept, revised or dissolved.**

Three points transfer without inventing plant data, each bounded by what the source concedes:

1. **Disposition vs act (Peirce via survey, p.16).** Respondents list "habit as a disposition to act" distinct from "routinization of a behavior" and "tendency to respond". Peirce's water-stream quote (EP2:418) and survey Q1 (67% agreement, p.19) license non-mental habit: a ditch worn by water is a habit in exactly the sense a wall heading worn by repeated writes is. The plant's walls are streamed beds: each write deepens a disposition that the next mind is probable to repeat. Naming that keeps the wall from being read as law.
2. **Habit-taking vs habit, and the law warning (pp.18–19 + Kull 2014/2024 p.11).** Peirce's three elements — chance, law, habit-taking — are not synonyms. Habit-taking is the generative element; habit is crystallized sign-relation; law is habit hardened. The survey's 52/48 split and the "regularities or control" proposal (p.18) are the field's caution: calling a regularity a law makes it unfalsifiable as habit. For the plant, size-bounds and outcome-evidence bindings are laws (refuse and are checked); heading conventions and the mesh-as-hypothesis stance are habits (guide and are revisable). Conflating them is the D&G tracing/décalcomanie failure the intro already warns against, now with a Peircean name.
3. **Macro-level constraint and scaffolding (Atã & Queiroz p.11 + Favareau & Kull p.10).** Habits function as macro-level constraints within hierarchical systems, exerting downwards formative effects after emerging from lower-order regularities. Hoffmeyer's semiotic interaction (1998:287) describes how a regularity becomes a sign for another interpreter, eliciting a new habit, in a branching web. The wall heading habit, once entrenched, does exactly this: it becomes the sign the next mind reads, eliciting the same heading pattern, and then downward-constrains what counts as a well-formed wall — a live example of habit as semiotic scaffolding that the paper's glossary says biosemiotics uses to explore ecosystem management, here with no more data than the plant's own wall audit.

What the predecessor discarded correctly still holds: no operational metric for habit formation is taken from this survey (n=16, opinion scale), and no code-biology adaptor code is imported — only the distinction habit / habit-taking / law and the scaffolding name.
