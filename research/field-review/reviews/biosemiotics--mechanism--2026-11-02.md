What the source gives, quoted from its own sentences:

- Abstract framing (p.444): "the paper scrutinizes folding as a fundamental semiotic activity and elementary semiotic modelling, drawing connections to Pattee's proposition regarding protein folding as a precursor to semiosis and interpretation" — "folding serves not only as a foundational prerequisite for semiosis but also as a potent modelling system" and "elucidates how folding manifests as a modelling mechanism across diverse domains from biology to syntax" with "an application of folding principles in AI."
- Minimal precondition claim (§2–3, via Pattee 2021/2023): protein folding is "the very precondition for semiosis and interpretation" (p.445) — a linear peptide chain folds into a compact three-dimensional structure that "orchestrates various biological activities" (p.446); the mechanism remains elusive because "a comprehensive understanding of the chemical and physical attributes of peptide chains does not sufficiently clarify why proteins adopt particular folding patterns" (p.446). Levinthal's paradox (p.446) is the quantitative bound: random search would take 1.6×10^27 years, yet proteins fold in seconds — "only a limited number of folds occur in nature."
- The Deleuze fold passage the paper re-reads (§2, via Deleuze 1988:6, p.447): "The unit of matter, the smallest element of the labyrinth, is the fold, not the point, which is never a part, but a simple extremity of the line" — "Unfolding is thus not the contrary of folding, but follows the fold up to the following fold."
- Minimal intelligence model (§3, pp.447–449): with Peirce MS 1334 phaneron–proteid analogy and Romanini & Lacková 2023, "proteins are minimal semiotic agents ... because they are directly involved in performing biological functions" (p.449); "protein folding is the minimal model for intelligent design. Because protein folding is about bringing together distant and unrelated parts of a sequence of the linear peptide chain, AI can now do this. Protein folding is about creating a shape from a pure linear string, and it is done by simply connecting — connecting adjacent parts of the string. And this is what we can call a minimal syntax or a minimal model for genuine intelligence" (p.450).
- Code duality as the enabling distinction (§3, via Hoffmeyer & Emmeche 1991:121, p.449): "Code-duality ... life depends on a semiotic interplay between the analogue and digital versions of the message ... The digital code typically refers to DNA, while the analogue code pertains to its active interpretation within living systems" — "code duality involves not only the presence of two distinct codes, but also their mutual compatibility, while remaining untranslatable" — "Information does not exist except as carried by (immanent in) matter and energy." Protein folding is "analogue coding to complement or complete the digital nature to the genetic script" (p.450).
- Folding vs recursion vs more information (§4–5, pp.451–453): Martinet (1957, 1967) double articulation and Chomsky vs Everett/Deacon recursion debate are the contrast — Deacon (2003:126) "recursion appears to be more of an epiphenomenon than a linguistic universal ... The implicit affordance for recursion that symbolic reference provides is a semiotic universal" — Lackova prefers 'folding' as "a recursive circular autopoietic movement of the fold" observable in language and protein synthesis (p.453). With Bennett (2023) and Brier (2008) (§5, p.453): "more information does not equal more meaning, and ... more modelling capacity entails less information" — "more information leads to less meaning, and this ... applies to ... the stochastic parrots and the LLMs compared to biological intelligence. Instead of feeding the language models with endlessly long sequences ... it can prove more meaningful to try to model the sequences, to fold them. Folding as modelling has the specific feature of spatiality, dimensionality that is typical in general for graphs and diagrams (what I have in mind here is Peirce's Existential Graphs in particular)."
- Spatial modelling without excluded-middle presupposition (§5, p.453): "Only spatial models can capture the excluded middle, that is, they do not pre-require discrete digits defined in terms of binary relations 'either–or'" — with Peirce/Dedekind cut and Bennett (2023) map/diorama as prototypical spatial modelling.

## What is new here

**A linear digital sequence does not become meaningful by getting longer; it becomes meaningful when distant parts are folded together into a spatial shape that models — a graph or diagram where adjacency is not sequence order — and that folding is the minimal semiotic activity, not an optional decoration.**

Three points transfer directly to the page that declares the plant's couplings, each bounded by what the source concedes:

1. **Folding as minimal syntax and precondition for semiosis (§3, p.450).** Pattee's claim Lackova adopts — "protein folding is the minimal model for intelligent design ... about bringing together distant and unrelated parts of a sequence of the linear peptide chain" — is the operational cut: a tape that only appends (`chat.log` lines) without folding cannot be the sign; the sign is the folded shape (wall headings eligibly scaffolding `## Plate/## Blockers/## Next`, the rendered pane as map, the `aoi_summary` unfolded diagram). The source's Levinthal bound (random search 10^27 years vs folding in seconds) is the warning: enumerating all linear constructions is not how the functional shape is found; the plant analogue is enumerating all tape lines (volume) vs folding them into the one wall/branch shape that lands.

2. **Analogue vs digital code duality (§3, Hoffmeyer & Emmeche p.449, Lackova p.450).** The digital code (DNA / wall bytes as tokens) is necessary but not sufficient; the analogue pole is the active interpretation (protein folding / pane rendering as spatial self-assembly). For the plant, outcome→evidence binding already shows digital arbitrariness (path + sha256); folding names the analogue complement that the predecessor discard left missing: the rendering/execution step that must interpret the code at its own scale. This is complementary to Zhou's Deacon-autogen operational guard (code correctness only where boundary conditions are self-maintained) but operates at the reading scale rather than the cell scale — one says "when to call a mapping interpretation", the other says "what interpretation minimally does to a line."

3. **Modelling capacity vs informational capacity and spatial graphs (§5, p.453, Bennett 2023/Brier 2008/Peirce).** "More modelling capacity entails less information" — more tape lines can lead to "more confusion" (Lackova p.448) and to stochastic-parrot failure (Bender et al. 2021, p.448). The remedy is not longer context but a spatial model — Peirce's Existential Graphs as the type example — which "can capture the excluded middle" without pre-requiring discrete digits as excluded-middle binaries. Plant analogue: the wall audit and the AoI unfolded diagram are spatial (branch shape, transport-vs-age axes), not longer linear summaries; when the dashboard re-publishes wall bytes as pane bytes without an independent folding step ("retained content is not a fresh observation") it is the collapse Lackova warns against: expression tracing content as decal rather than modelling it — the same pincers-closure the ATP double-articulation review named, now with the folding name.

What the predecessor mechanism did not cover. Barbieri's adaptor proves arbitrariness between two worlds; it does not say what turns the arbitrary correspondence into an interpretation. Lackova's folding does — by naming the spatial operation that connects distant tokens. One says "a connection requires an arbitrary choice"; the other says "the choice becomes semiosis only when the line is folded into a shape." Both are needed; neither subsumes the other. No new protein or LLM is proposed here — the mechanism note is docs-only, reversible by revert.
