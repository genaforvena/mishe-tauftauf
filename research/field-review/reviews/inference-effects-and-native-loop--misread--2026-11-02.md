## What this plant already does

The plant already implements the end-to-end placement the paper demands, and its tests pin it:

- Allocation before action: `EffectBoundary` allocates a stable `operation_id` and persists an `EffectIntent` (with `request_digest` over content+authority+call id) before calling the provider, then persists the observed outcome; `dispatch` re-reads that store and never invents a completed outcome. A crash between intent and outcome is read back as `unknown` and `drive_native` calls `stop('unknown')` rather than retrying. Tests: `tests/test_inference_effects.py:430` interruption between start and outcome leaves unknown and no rerun, `tests/test_inference_loop.py:1049` reconcile never dispatches for unknown, `tests/test_inference_loop.py:362` unresolved effect stops without retry.
- Correctness at the ends, not in the middle: `request_digest` binds the exact operation the caller authorized (including `native_call` call id) so a later retry can join to the same operation rather than create a new one; the store lock is held across read/check/start/execute/outcome and `os.fsync` is an explicit durability site. The loop's authority check is re-applied inside `dispatch` after persistence, not delegated to the transport. `complete` is documented as an oracle result, not proof of close.
- Low-level incompleteness acknowledged: the paper's careful-file-transfer list (disk read fault, buffering/copy mistake, transient memory error, packet loss/corruption, host crash after unknown amount) maps onto this plant as provider transport errors, process crashes, and corrupted journal lines. The plant treats low-level transport retries or packet checksums as insufficient — an `unknown` outcome still requires a fresh-process reconciliation, and `completed` still requires re-reading the evidence artifact with digest verification (`_evidence_target`/`_re_read_evidence`).

## What is new here

The misread this cell looks for is exactly the one Saltzer et al. name: reading "reliable transport" or "durable store" as sufficient for correct effects, and therefore placing retry, duplicate suppression, or commit logic in the low-level communication/provider layer. The paper shows that misread fails even when the low level is made very reliable, because other end-to-end failure modes remain (the gateway byte-pair interchange p.4 that a per-hop checksum did not catch is the canonical example; every packet was checksummed and the file still corrupted).

Applied to this plant, three corollary gaps are visible even though the behaviour is already correct:

1. The `request_digest`/`operation_id` binding is an end-to-end file-checksum, not a transport checksum. A reader could misread it as a performance optimisation for deduplication and propose replacing it with provider-level idempotency keys or transport retries. The paper says that replacement cannot be correct without the application endpoints — only the operation's content, arguments and authority define sameness, and `dispatch` must recheck authority after its own persistence.
2. `os.fsync` and the append-only JSONL journals are correctness, not performance. A reader could misread `fsync` as a tunable and argue for flush-only journaling because the cloud disk is durable. The paper's distinction is that correctness lives end-to-end; low-level durability claims (mount, disk, gateway buffer) do not remove the need for the endpoints to make the outcome durable and re-readable as `unknown` vs `completed`.
3. "No automatic resubmission after unknown" is an end-to-end placement decision, not a missing feature. A reader could misread `stop('unknown')` as pessimistic and propose a low-level retry inside `inference_worker` or the provider client. The paper's criterion says that retry must be an end-to-end, application-supplied checksum-and-retry/commit plan (here: `recover_native` with explicit continuation journal and `reconcile_caller_journal`), with low-level retry at most a performance enhancement that must never synthesize `completed`.

None of this contradicts the 2026-11-02 recent revisit for this field (Anthropic "Building effective agents", applied to `inference_loop.py` as docs-only agent/workflow naming plus ground-truth journaling). That review strengthened never-retry-unknown as ground truth; this review explains *why* never-retry-unknown must be end-to-end and not a transport property. One names the loop form, the other names the placement principle.
