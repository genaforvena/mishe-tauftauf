# The plant corrects errors inside its governing variables — it never questions the variables (a single-loop misread)

## What we read

**Chris Argyris, "Single-Loop and Double-Loop Models in Research on Decision Making," *Administrative Science Quarterly* 21, no. 3 (Sep. 1976): 363–375** — read full text at:

- <https://www.jstor.org/stable/2391848> (JSTOR stable URL, accessed page header 363/ASQ)
- <https://ia801400.us.archive.org/13/items/argyris_chris_org_learning/argyris1976_3.pdf> (archive.org reproduction of the same ASQ printing, 1.7 MB, fetched 200 2026-10-29 at /tmp/argyris3.pdf; `pdftotext` verified header `Single-Loop and Double-Loop Models in Research on Decision Making` / `September 1976, volume 21 363/Administrative Science Quarterly`)

Four passages carry the misread this plant reproduces. Page numbers are the printed ASQ pagination carried in the PDF header (363–375).

1. **Single-loop keeps the design, double-loop questions it (p. 366).** "One might say that participants in organizations are encouraged to learn to perform as long as the learning does not question the fundamental design, goals, and activities of their organizations. This learning may be called single-loop learning. In double-loop learning, a participant would be able to ask questions about changing fundamental aspects of the organization (Allison, Neustadt, Halperin, and others)." The next paragraph adds that most groups in their usual settings "permit only single-loop learning" and that humans are acculturated to be "primarily single-loop learners."

2. **Every action is judged against governing variables; Model I's variables are unilateral control (p. 367–368).** "It was hypothesized that human behavior, in any situation, represents the most satisfactory solution people can find consistent with their governing values or variables, such as achieving a purpose as others define it, winning, suppressing negative feelings, and emphasizing rationality." "The primary strategies are to control the relevant environment and tasks unilaterally and to protect themselves and their group unilaterally." "Control as a behavioral strategy … tends to produce defensiveness and closedness, because unilateral control does not tend to produce valid feedback." Leaders who stay inside these variables then "receive little genuine feedback and others would tend not to violate their governing values and so disturb the accepted fundamental framework" — the circular stability Argyris calls "ultra stable" (citing Schön 1972).

3. **Model II replaces the variables, not the tactics — valid information, free and informed choice, internal commitment (p. 368–369).** "The governing variables or values of Model II are not the opposite of Model I. The governing variables are valid information, free and informed choice, and internal commitment." Behaviour that satisfies them "is not the opposite of Model I": advocacy is kept, but "coupled with an invitation to confront one another's views and to alter them, in order to produce the position that is based on the most complete valid information possible." Strategies "involve sharing power with anyone who has competence … Face saving is resisted because it is seen as a defensive nonlearning activity." Under those conditions "defensiveness … would tend to decrease and free choice would tend to increase, as would feelings of commitment" and "the probabilities that errors and failures would be communicated openly … would" increase.

4. **The transition is not more of the same — knowing the new model at espoused level does not change theory-in-use, and no structural fix substitutes for it (p. 369–371).** "Learning to become aware of one's present theory-in-use and then altering it is a very difficult process, because it requires that individuals question the theories of action that have formed the framework for their actions. Learning about double-loop learning through lectures, reading, and case discussions will lead to learning at the espoused level rather than at the level of theory-in-use." "This also means that changes in organizational structure, management information systems, and organizational norms will not lead directly to changes in behavior of the people within the new system." The paper demonstrates the point with T-groups and alternative schools that swung to "the opposite of Model I" — an "oscillating Model I" — without reaching Model II.

## What this plant already does

*The loop observes itself, but the thing it observes is bounded by fixed governing variables that no code path ever questions.*

- **Initiative as a bounded single-loop reader.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative.py:34-43` fixes the clause and the window — `EVIDENCE_BINDING_CLAUSE_TIME = 2026-10-06T16:22:11+00:00`, `WINDOW_SECONDS = 86400`, `MAX_PATCHES = 512`, `SNAPSHOT_MAX_AGE = 900` — and `collect():185` scans at most `MAX_TAPE_BYTES`/`MAX_ENTRIES` and classifies every outcome as `OBSERVED`/`PARTIAL`/`UNKNOWN` inside those variables. The recent `INITIATIVE_ARCHIVE_VERSION / STAGED_SUBSETS / STAGED_TRIGGER / stepping_stone_score / is_stepping_stone` addition (`:45-151`, landed 2026-10-29) extends empirical lineage measurement but keeps the same governing variable: the score is patch-delivery/benchmark within the clause, never a question about whether the clause, the window, or the state vocabulary is the right frame.

- **Initiative-watch as the plant's single-loop detector.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative_watch.py:37-44` fixes the finding thresholds — `AGED_SECONDS = 3600`, `CONTINUATION_SECONDS = 1800`, `PRODUCER_MAX_AGE = 180`, `ESCALATE_SECONDS = 600`, `REMINDER_SECONDS = 1800` — and `evaluate():317` is a pure function that emits `findings` with `id/kind/role/ref/summary/primary/escalation/action` and maps any incomplete coverage to `unknown` (`:325-332`, `:334-378`). A repeated `aged-wake` or `continuation-review` finding is simply emitted again next cycle and routed on the `initial → changed → escalation → reminder` schedule (`:266-274, :470, :676`); no path asks whether `AGED_SECONDS` itself, or what counts as `OBSERVED`, is the variable that needs changing. That is exactly Argyris's definition of single-loop: detect and correct the error (an old wake, a missing receipt) without questioning the governing variable that labelled it an error.

- **Substrate and follow-through peers behave the same way.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/substrate_watch.py` hashes HEAD/index/tracked files and reports drift against the pin; `src/mishe_tauftauf/follow_through.py` projects refusal counts. Both are measurement inside fixed budgets and vocabularies — they make valid information *within* the variable, they do not make the variable itself confrontable in Argyris's sense (p. 369: "present the evaluations and attributions so as to encourage open and constructive confrontations").

- **The plant's doctrine already knows the trap it then re-enacts.** `docs/wall-coordination.md:219` and `docs/mesh.md:53` describe follow-through and initiative as "the plant observing and changing itself." Yet the observation the plant trusts is the one the existing vocabulary can name. When `evaluate()` cannot establish absence of review signals — `history/outcomes state != OBSERVED` or `lifecycle_gaps` — it correctly emits `unknown` (`:325-332`), i.e., "do not conclude from absence." But when the same `aged-wake` finding recurs for days, the watcher treats each emission as a fresh instance to route and escalate, never as evidence that the governing variable (the threshold, the definition of settlement, the `OBSERVED` predicate) may be the mismatch Argyris calls "error as any feature of knowledge or of knowing that makes action ineffective" (p. 365).

## What is new here

- **The misread is structural, not a missing constant.** The plant reads "the plant observing and changing itself" as "run the observation loop faster or on more items." Argyris's loop is about *what* is observed: single-loop observes actions against governing variables; double-loop observes the governing variables themselves. Adding another scan, another archive, or a lower `AGED_SECONDS` is still single-loop — it corrects within the variable. The paper's point about "opposite of Model I is still Model I" (p. 370–371, alternative schools and T-groups as oscillating Model I) is the exact shape of the plant's risk: a watcher that watches the watcher, but with the same unilateral-control values, is still the same loop.

- **Why the variables matter.** Argyris's Model I variables — winning, suppressing negative feeling, unilateral control, emphasizing rationality — are not abstract vices; they are observable in how the plant handles valid information. The plant's loop is unilateral (producer writes `checks/initiative.json`, watcher reads it, roles receive addressed notices), it suppresses threat by mapping it to `UNKNOWN` and routing it away rather than inviting confrontation of the variable that produced it, and it emphasizes rationality by insisting the existing `OBSERVED` predicate and `AGED_SECONDS` are the rational frame. Model II's variables — valid information, free and informed choice, internal commitment — would require that when the same finding recurs, the system make the variable that produced it *visible and confrontable* so participants can freely choose to alter or retain it, rather than merely correcting the instance.

- **The transition caveat transfers directly.** Argyris (p. 369–370) warns that lecturing about double-loop produces espoused-theory change without theory-in-use change, and that structural/IT/norm changes alone do not produce Model II behaviour. For this plant that means: publishing a document that says "we do double-loop" or lowering a threshold does not make the loop double-loop. What makes it double-loop is that the governing variables are first made explicit as data, then accompanied by a detector that says "this error has recurred under these variables" and invites — not compels — their revision, with the same sandbox, digest-bound, human-reviewable discipline the clause already enforces.

## Application

**Make the governing variables explicit and add a pure double-loop signal beside `evaluate()` — additive, no threshold change, no new verdict word, same `UNKNOWN`/`ATTENTION`/`OBSERVED` vocabulary.**

1. **Expose the governing variables as data beside the existing constants (`:37-44`).** Add `GOVERNING_VARIABLES: Final[dict[str, object]]` (name illustrative) that maps each variable that `evaluate()` and `check()` treat as given to `{value, unit, provenance, role}` — e.g. `AGED_SECONDS` ("open wake is aged", `initiative_watch.py:361`, threshold 3600 s), `CONTINUATION_SECONDS` (continuation-review window, `:357`), `PRODUCER_MAX_AGE` (stale producer, `:528`), `ESCALATE_SECONDS`/`REMINDER_SECONDS` (routing cadence, `:470`/`274`). Docstring cites Argyris 1976 p. 367–368 (governing variables) and notes that Model II variables (p. 368: valid information, free and informed choice, internal commitment) are the invitation the watcher cannot enforce — the code makes the variable visible, participants choose to alter it. No I/O, no import change, bounded literal dict.

2. **Add two pure, deterministic helpers that implement the single-loop vs double-loop distinction — no tape scan, no file read.** `governing_variables() -> dict` — returns a JSON-safe copy of `GOVERNING_VARIABLES` (so a review, a wall, or a test can cite the exact threshold that produced a finding, as Argyris p. 369 requires: "the meaning given to them by the formulator … would be open to scrutiny"). `is_single_loop_trap(findings: list[dict], history: list[dict]) -> dict | None` — true when the same `kind+role` (e.g. `aged-wake:field-review`) appears in at least three successive `evaluate()` outputs despite prior remediation attempts recorded in `history` — the plant's observable analogue of Argyris's "error persists because the governing variable is unchanged" (p. 365, 367). The helper returns a structured hint `{trap: True, kind, role, repetitions, governing_variable, suggestion: "question AGED_SECONDS / OBSERVED predicate, not just the instance"}` or `None`; it never emits a new finding kind and never changes `evaluate()`'s own output. This is the detector the paper says single-loop lacks: single-loop would simply correct again, double-loop names the variable as candidate.

3. **Keep the plant's existing vocabulary and keep the contract intensive.** No new `state` word is published, no `checks/initiative-watch.json` shape changes, no `check()` routing change. Missing or stale governing variables are surfaced as the same `unknown` the watcher already uses for incomplete coverage; a `None` from `is_single_loop_trap` is not a finding — it is a hint a consumer (wall, docs, a future sandboxed trial) can invite confrontation with. Every helper is pure and bounded; `python tools/check_parsing.py` stays `PASS` because `initiative_watch.py` is not a `parsing-policy.json` boundary file and the new symbols add no literal-boundary bytes.

**One thing this review does not ask for.** It does not lower a threshold, add a watcher, auto-question a variable, or wire the trap detector into `evaluate()`/`check()` routing. Argyris's warning (p. 369–370) that structural fixes alone do not produce Model II behaviour applies: the plant cannot automate free and informed choice — it can only make the governing variable visible and make repetition observable so participants can choose, under valid information, to alter or retain it. Auto-triggered double-loop would be another Model I control strategy.

**Limits the review keeps from the source.** Argyris's evidence base is public-sector and laboratory groups, not software plants; the "Model I is ultra stable" claim (p. 368, citing Schön 1972) is offered with that scope. The transfer is structural — an error that persists under unchanged governing variables is a candidate for double-loop inquiry — not a claim that Argyris discussed tape, patches, or `OBSERVED` predicates. The plant's existing `UNKNOWN` vocabulary stays the detector for incomplete information; the new helpers add only the second clause the paper distinguishes: incomplete information about the *variable* that frames the observation.
