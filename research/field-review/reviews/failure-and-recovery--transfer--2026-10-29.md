## What we read

**Circuit Breaker** — Martin Fowler, *bliki* 6 March 2014, popularising Michael Nygard, *Release It! Design and Deploy Production-Ready Software* (Pragmatic Bookshelf, 1st ed. 2007, 2nd ed. 2018, Ch. 5 "Stability Antipatterns / Circuit Breaker") — read full text at:

- <https://martinfowler.com/bliki/CircuitBreaker.html> (HTML, Fowler bliki, last modified 2014-03-06, 3 states + Ruby sketch, ~1 600 words)
- <https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0978739213/> — Nygard book page (origin the bliki cites: "In his excellent book Release It!, Michael Nygard popularized the Circuit Breaker pattern")
- Context implementation surveyed: Netflix Hystrix / Resilience4j documentation linked from the bliki's Further Reading (Hystrix dependency command, thread-pool isolation, metrics)

Four passages carry the transferable mechanism, each with the plant-level analogue it replaces.

1. **The breaker is a wrapper that counts failures and fails fast without calling the supplier (bliki §1–2, sketch.png).** "You wrap a protected function call in a circuit breaker object, which monitors for failures. Once the failures reach a certain threshold, the circuit breaker trips, and all further calls to the circuit breaker return with an error, without the protected call being made at all. Usually you'll also want some kind of monitor alert if the circuit breaker trips." The point is not recovery reasoning but *load removal*: an unresponsive supplier is not hammered while it is known to be down, and the caller does not tie up a thread waiting for a timeout that is known to fail.

2. **A useful breaker self-resets through half-open — one trial call after a timeout (bliki "self-resetting", state.png).** The simple breaker would "need an external intervention to reset it when things are well again" — acceptable for a building's electrical breaker, not for software. The fix adds a third state: `closed → open → half_open → closed/open`. In Ruby: `state` is `:open` when `failure_count >= threshold`; `:half_open` when that plus `Time.now - last_failure_time > reset_timeout`; `:open` otherwise `:closed`. On `half_open`, `call` tries the protected block once; success calls `reset`, failure calls `record_failure` and restarts the timeout. No new thread is started to probe — the *next* caller's call is the probe.

3. **Not every error should trip the breaker, and rate matters more than count (bliki "more sophisticated", § thread pool / queue).** "Often they will protect against a range of errors that protected call could raise, such as network connection failures. Not all errors should trip the circuit, some should reflect normal failures and be dealt with as part of regular logic." And: "A more sophisticated approach might look at frequency of errors, tripping once you get, say, a 50% failure rate. You might also have different thresholds for different errors, such as a threshold of 10 for timeouts but 3 for connection failures." Two companion mechanisms: isolate the supplier on its own thread pool so exhaustion itself trips ("By drawing these threads from a thread pool, you can arrange for the circuit to break when the thread pool is exhausted"), or put requests on a queue "which the supplier consumes at its speed … the circuit breaks when the queue fills up." The breaker therefore needs a *window* (volume + error fraction), not just a monotonic counter.

4. **The breaker is a monitoring and operator-control point, and the caller must have a fallback (bliki "Circuit breakers are a valuable place for monitoring" + "Breakers on their own … need to react").** "Any change in breaker state should be logged and breakers should reveal details of their state for deeper monitoring. Breaker behavior is often a good source of warnings about deeper troubles in the environment. Operations staff should be able to trip or reset breakers." And: "As with any remote invocation you need to consider what to do in case of failure. Does it fail the operation … or are there workarounds? A credit card authorization could be put on a queue to deal with later, failure to get some data may be mitigated by showing stale data." The breaker without a fallback is just a fast error — the pattern's value is the *policy* the open circuit invokes (queue, stale read, degraded response), logged as a state transition rather than as one more exception.

**Limits the source states, which this review keeps.** The bliki's Ruby counts `failure_count` that "resets on a successful call" — no window, no decay, so a single success after 4 failures erases history. Frequency-based tripping and per-error thresholds are described as aspirations, not as the sketch's code. Synchronous breaker on a thread pool does not map mechanically to the async/queue breaker. Hystrix/Resilience4j are offered as production implementations precisely because a hand-rolled breaker tends to get threshold, window, thread-pool and fallback wrong. The bliki does not prescribe where the breaker lives (client vs supplier) or how long `reset_timeout` should be — that is deployment-specific.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** The field `failure-and-recovery` has no transfer reading yet (recent=pilot rehearsal, misread=crash-only external switch, critique=handler hygiene). The plant's bounded recovery is the exact place the breaker was invented: a caller that retries an unresponsive supplier and can cascade by holding resources. The electrical→software transfer is explicit in the source ("Release It! popularized…", sketch.png), so the *angle* transfer is not an analogy the reviewer invents — it is the mechanism's own history applied once more, onto `recovery_policy`.

## What this plant already does

*Bounded count-based retry, not a breaker — hammering is limited by attempts, not by circuit state.*

`src/mishe_tauftauf/native_recovery.py:92-100` defines `SETTLED/RETRYABLE/RECONCILE/UNKNOWN`, `TRANSIENT_STATUS = {408, 429} ∪ [500,599]`, `DEFAULT_RETRY_LIMIT = 3`. `classify_attempt()` (:374-448) decides `RETRYABLE` only when no effect was dispatched (`_journal_effect_rows` :355), the terminal failure is transient (`_transient` :267), and `failure.txt` is a complete traceback (`_failure_record` :200, `TRACEBACK_HEADER` :112). `fresh_attempt()` (:489-502) returns `Path(directory)/f'{wake}-retry-{number}'` for `number in 1..limit` or `None`. `attempt_disposition()` (:568-622) chooses `live`/`settled`/`reconcile`/`fresh-attempt` from the classified reports, exhausting the 3-attempt budget then reconciling.

`src/mishe_tauftauf/recovery_policy.py:8-49` `transition(previous, *, result, now, owner, backup, deadline, stable_seconds)` is the other half: it tracks one detection's `first_failed`/`passing_since`/`restored_at` and flips `state` among `HEALTHY/FAILED/MITIGATED/RECOVERING/RESTORED/UNKNOWN`, requesting a backup after `deadline`. `capacity()` (:58-77) tracks host pressure (`memory_full >= 10 / io_full >= 40`) into `NORMAL/DEGRADED`. Neither module has a breaker vocabulary: no `OPEN/HALF_OPEN/CLOSED`, no `failure_count`/`last_failure_time`/`reset_timeout`, no rolling window, no per-error threshold, no "do not call supplier at all" path.

*What that means operationally.* A wake whose 3 attempts each saw `500` on the upstream tool (`transient_status` true) and wrote no effect row will be re-attempted 3 times with no circuit between them — `attempt_disposition` counts attempts, but the *next* retry may start immediately after the previous failure, holding `STORE_LOCK` (:303-332 ` _store_guard`) and re-reading `journal.jsonl` + 3 ledger journals each time. There is no `open` that says "this wake's supplier has failed 5 times in 60 s, do not re-probe for 120 s" and no `half_open` that says "let one probe through and gate the rest." The only backoff is implicit in the mind's scheduling, not in the recovery policy.

*Neighbouring isolation already exists, but without the breaker state.* `minimal_supervisor.py` (`MinimalSupervisor` :31, `dispatch` :80, `reconcile` :120) is durable dispatch with a bounded worker — a bulkhead in the bliki's terms — but it has one slot (`state_path` :50) not a pool whose exhaustion trips a breaker. `recovery_io.py:196` `controller_lock` and `native_recovery.py:109` `STORE_LOCK` already exist as the locks a speculative wrapper must not block (§4.2.2 of the pilot review), and `host_containment.py` latches the whole plant on cgroup pressure (`pressure` :182, `reason` :382), but none of them publishes a per-wake `state` that `observe()` or `check` can render. The delivery outbox (`recovery_io.deliver` :339) retains the exact event for reconciliation but does not refuse to enqueue while the downstream is known to be down — exactly the queue-fill breaker case the bliki names.

*Monitoring that would carry the breaker transition already has a shape.* `native_recovery.write_blocked_state()` (:659) writes `verdict/reason/evidence/attempt` under `BLOCKED_STATE = '{role}-native.blocked.json'` (:641) read by the readiness gate; `recovery_policy.transition` already logs `episode/checked_at/state`. A breaker state transition is one more `reason` string in that vocabulary (`reason: breaker_open threshold=5 window=60s failures=5`), not a new monitoring plane.

## What is new here

**The plant bounds attempts but does not bound *attempt rate* or refuse to call a known-bad supplier — the exact load-removal the breaker provides.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number in this repository where it would land and each bounded by what the source concedes.

1. **Closed / open / half_open is not another verdict — it is a gate in front of the verdict.** Today `classify_attempt`/`attempt_disposition` answer "what *was* this attempt?" after it ran; the breaker answers "should the *next* attempt run at all?" before it does. The Ruby sketch's `state` (`failure_count >= threshold ? :open : :closed` plus timeout → `:half_open`) maps to a pure function `breaker(previous, *, result, now, threshold, window, reset_timeout)` beside `recovery_policy.transition` (:8). `previous` carries `failure_count`, `window_start`, `state`, `last_failure_time`, `opened_at`. `result is None` (UNKNOWN) and non-transient failures do not increment `failure_count` — "Not all errors should trip the circuit" (bliki). Only `transient_status(status)` true or an `OSError/Timeout` on the attempt's terminal failure increments it, so a deterministic rejection (`422`, `ValueError` on bad input) is "normal failure" handled by `RECONCILE`/`UNKNOWN`, not by opening the circuit.

2. **Count → rate, with a window and a trial, not a monotonic counter that a single success erases.** The bliki's simple `reset` on success is the defect a production breaker fixes: "a 50% failure rate" over a window, not `5` total. The transfer therefore uses a *sliding window* (e.g. `window=60 s`, `min_calls=5` inside the window before the breaker may open; `failure_rate_threshold=0.5`) rather than the sketch's bare `failure_threshold`. When `state == OPEN`, calls do not read `journal.jsonl`/ledgers/attempt directories at all — they return `reason: breaker_open until={opened_at+reset_timeout} state=OPEN failures=5/6 window=60s` and the caller queues or serves stale without touching `STORE_LOCK`. When `now - opened_at > reset_timeout`, state becomes `HALF_OPEN` and exactly one caller is admitted as the trial; that trial's outcome decides `CLOSED` (reset) vs `OPEN` (restart timeout). This keeps the Ruby shape (one variable for last failure, one for timeout) but replaces the count that a single success erases with a window the caller can inspect.

3. **Pool/queue exhaustion as a breaker, and fallback as the open-circuit policy.** The bliki's two exhaustion breakers — "circuit to break when the thread pool is exhausted" and "circuit breaks when the queue fills up" — map to the plant's two queues: `attempts` directories on disk and the `recovery_io` delivery outbox. A trip condition on `len(wake_attempts) >= limit` already exists as `fresh_attempt is None → reconcile` (:605-617); the new condition trips *before* the limit when `wake_attempts` in the last `window` are all `RETRYABLE` with transient terminal failures — systemic supplier failure, not one wake's bad input. The open-circuit fallback in the plant is not an exception but a disposition: `Disposition(recoverable=False, verdict=RECONCILE, evidence=breaker_evidence)` or `UNKNOWN` with `reason: breaker_open`, so the wall/operator sees a *named* fast-fail rather than a silently queued retry. Reconciliation still owns the attempt's bytes — the breaker adds the "refuse to call" path, not a new terminal word.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** The bliki's `Timeout::timeout(0.01)` wrapper and thread-pool breaker are deployment-specific; this review does not propose forking the plant or changing `host_containment` cgroups — only the wake-level policy function in `recovery_policy.py` gains the circuit state. `reset_timeout` is not derived here; it must be at least the supplier's P95 plus `observations._RENDERER_TIMEOUTS`/`RETRY_BACKOFF` so a half-open trial is not a timeout false positive. Per-error thresholds ("10 for timeouts but 3 for connection failures") are noted but not asked for in the first cut — one `transient` threshold suffices to prove the gate. As with pilot and crash-only, the first implementation is behind a flag (`MISHE_RECOVERY_BREAKER=0/1`) and additive: `classify_attempt`/`fresh_attempt`/`attempt_disposition` keep their signatures, and `MISHE_RECOVERY_BREAKER=0` reproduces the current 3-attempt sequence byte-for-byte.

## Application

target: src/mishe_tauftauf/recovery_policy.py

**Add a breaker gate beside `transition`/`capacity` — closed/open/half_open over a rolling window, refusing to call the supplier while open and admitting one half-open trial, additive behind a flag and expressed in the plant's existing `reason` vocabulary.**

1. **Add `CircuitBreaker` state and `breaker_transition()` beside `transition()` (:8) and `capacity()` (:58) in `src/mishe_tauftauf/recovery_policy.py`.** Pure functions, no I/O, no new dependency:

   - State carried in a plain `dict` (mirroring `transition`'s `row`) with keys `state ∈ {CLOSED, OPEN, HALF_OPEN}`, `failure_count`, `window_start`, `opened_at`, `last_failure_time`, `checked_at`. Initial row: `CLOSED, failure_count=0`.
   - `breaker_transition(previous, *, result, now, threshold=5, window=60, min_calls=5, failure_rate_threshold=0.5, reset_timeout=120, trip_on=("transient", "timeout", "OSError")) -> row` — `result` is the same `True/False/None` the existing policy uses (`True` = probe passed / attempt settled, `False` = failed with a classified reason, `None` = UNKNOWN / unreadable). Only `False` where `transient_status` or the named probe exception caused it increments `failure_count` within `window` (`now - window_start <= window` else rotate window). When `calls_in_window >= min_calls` and `failure_count/calls_in_window >= failure_rate_threshold` and `failure_count >= threshold`, row flips to `OPEN` with `opened_at=now`. While `OPEN` and `now - opened_at <= reset_timeout`, the function returns `OPEN` without touching the supplier. When `now - opened_at > reset_timeout`, it moves to `HALF_OPEN` and admits exactly one `result` through (the next caller); that caller's `result is True` flips to `CLOSED`/`reset`, `False`/`None` flips back to `OPEN` with `opened_at=now`.

2. **Wire as a gate before `fresh_attempt`/`attempt_disposition` without changing those call sites.** The caller that today does `fresh = fresh_attempt(dir, wake)` first does `circuit = breaker_transition(load_circuit(wake), result=classify_transient(last_report), now=time.time())`. If `circuit["state"] == "OPEN"`, the caller does **not** create `attempt-N/` or acquire `STORE_LOCK` — it writes `BLOCKED_STATE` with `verdict=UNKNOWN` or `RECONCILE`, `reason=breaker_open until={circuit["opened_at"]+reset_timeout} failures={circuit["failure_count"]}/{calls} window={window}s`, and `evidence=(breaker_row_json, last_attempt_evidence)` so the wall/gate renders the breaker state. If `HALF_OPEN`, the caller admits one `fresh_attempt`+run as the trial; the trial's outcome feeds the next `breaker_transition`. With `MISHE_RECOVERY_BREAKER=0` (default) `breaker_transition` is the identity on `CLOSED` and the wire is a no-op — same `Attempt`/`Disposition` bytes as before.

3. **Reuse the plant's existing evidence vocabulary for the open-circuit fallback.** The `reason` string is the only new user-visible surface; it follows `observations.py:61` / `initiative_watch.py:565`'s `f'reason: {exc}'` idiom and `native_recovery.write_blocked_state`'s `reason` field so broken monitoring stays UNKNOWN with a named cause rather than silently defaulting. Metrics are the row itself — `state`, `failure_count`, `opened_at`, `checked_at` — persisted beside `transition`'s row (e.g. `SITE/recovery/breaker-{wake}.json` or in-memory for the trial) so `evaluate`/`check`/`guard` can log transitions and operators can trip/reset by writing `OPEN`/`CLOSED` (the "Operations staff should be able to trip or reset breakers" bliki clause).

**Ownership.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/recovery_policy.py` is the pure-function recovery discipline (`transition`/`capacity`) consumed by `recovery_io` and by `native_recovery`'s disposition loop; `health` is the resolver for `failure-and-recovery` / `native recovery, health, supervisors, watchdogs` and for substrate `UNKNOWN` delivery gaps that hammering a transient supplier creates. The breaker gate lives beside the host-pressure `capacity` breaker (`DEGRADED/NORMAL`) so the two breakers share one monitoring point (`evaluate`/`guard`).

**One thing this review does not ask for.** It does not propose a thread pool, bulkhead isolation, queue-depth limiter, or Hystrix-style metrics stream — those are the bliki's aspiration paragraphs and Netflix-scale concerns, not the minimal breaker. It does not change `DEFAULT_RETRY_LIMIT`, `TRANSIENT_STATUS`, or the journal shape. The queue-fill breaker for `recovery_io.deliver`'s outbox is noted as the next step once the wake-level breaker proves that `HALF_OPEN` trials are safe.
