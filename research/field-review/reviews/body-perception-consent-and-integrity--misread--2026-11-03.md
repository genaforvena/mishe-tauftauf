## What is new here

The plant's CI layer implements Barth as a single kind of rule — a positive allow-list — and treats deny as closed-world absence. Barth's text supports a different reading in three distinct places, none of which the 2026-11-01 misread addressed.

1. **Positive and negative norms are not the same operation, and Formula (1) is not "allow if in table."** The paper explicitly types two duals that generalize allow/deny (Abstract, §1, §3). Satisfaction is a conjunction of two different quantifications: a disjunction over positives ("allowed by at least one") and a conjunction over negatives ("must respect all"). A negative norm's force is that it must hold regardless of which positive authorized the communication — the doctor/researcher example makes this concrete: with a positive confidentiality rule, another positive could authorize the flow and Bob would not be obliged to keep confidence; with the same confidentiality as a negative, Bob must keep confidence "regardless of how he obtained them." The plant has no negative norms at all. Any flow not in `_CI_FLOWS` is denied by absence (closed-world deny, correctly hardened per Diamantis BEQ §§3.2–3.6), but no flow can be constrained to impose a future obligation that survives alternative authorization. Confidentiality — Barth's own running example ("if Alice tells Bob her age under confidentiality, then in the future Bob must not disclose," §3.3) — cannot be typed except as "not in the flow table."

2. **Future obligations are typed separately from past preconditions, and the plant has only the latter.** Past operators capture opt-in ("has previously given permission"); future operators capture confidentiality/notification ("must not disclose," "notification must be sent"). The 2026-11-01 misread correctly caught the past half (static grant vs temporal opt-in). The complementary half is that `TRANSMISSION_PRINCIPLES` includes `confidential-to-operator` as a label but the table `_CI_FLOWS[("personal-carrying","confidential-to-operator")] = {senses, operator}` is read as a positive permission (may flow to operator if principle is confidential), not as Barth's negative-cum-future obligation "if you receive it this way, you must not redisclose." No temporal ψ is evaluated and no trace is checked; the predicates are pointwise booleans over one proposed flow.

3. **The five-parameter shape is present but two parameters and two structural features are vacuous.** The `contextual_integrity_allows` signature carries `sender` and `subject` (and the module docstring at lines 3–8 names context/subject/sender/recipient/type/principle), yet no table discriminates on them — the psychiatrist-vs-HR distinction Barth uses to motivate roles ("communication perfectly acceptable between psychiatrist and patient is completely unacceptable between HR specialist and job applicant," Abstract/§1) has no analogue; all `personal-carrying` flows from `redmi10` about `human` are judged identically. Similarly, the role generalization closure and data-hierarchy closure (postal address → postal code) are not modeled: a message that bundles `light` + `acceleration` + `ble_scan` does not entail that the recipient now also holds a derived type, because no derived type is named and no I rule is enumerated. For a phone that yields rare attributes per row (Narayanan & Shmatikov 2007 §§1,3, cited in the `DENSE_*` comments), this matters: the Barthi sense of "type" is meant to be up to computable containment, and the plant's flat five types understate what travels.

Limits. Barth's logic is a decidability/expressiveness result for policy compliance (entailment as implication, policy combination as conjunction/disjunction, pp.1,6), not an enforcement engine for a Redmi polling loop; a sensor plant does not need LTL model checking to decide whether to read sysfs. The plant's closed-world, positive-only tables are a deliberate, hardened choice and the module says so (Diamantis BEQ p.9 value-indeterminacy typed as deny, and the docstring "any … not listed is denied"). The finding is that the module's words "either norm violated is a breach" and "transmission principle" understate that Barth has two breaches with different quantifiers, and that confidentiality and hierarchy are not among the listed principles/rows.
