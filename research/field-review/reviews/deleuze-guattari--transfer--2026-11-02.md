## What we read

- **Alvin Hoi-Chun Hung — "DAO as Rhizome: Reimagining Rules, Governance and Organisations", *Law and Critique*, Vol. 37, pp. 203–224, published online 26 September 2025, DOI 10.1007/s10978-025-09432-w (Open Access CC BY 4.0)** — read full primary source at:

Pagination below is the journal pagination 203–224; § headings as printed. Quotations via `pdftotext` of the fetched PDF. D&G 1980/1989 via Hung's citations (Deleuze & Guattari 1989) unless noted.

1. **The transfer question and its four rhizome tests (§ Introduction pp.203–205, § Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari's Rhizome pp.205–207).** Hung stages the gap explicitly: "there remains a conspicuous gap in interrogating [technological] transformative implications … through the ontopolitical prism of Deleuze and Guattari" (p.204); DAOs are then read as "a dynamic network marked by perpetual growth and metamorphosis, facilitated by … new connections that challenge traditional confines and hierarchical frameworks (Deleuze & Guattari 1989)" (Abstract p.203). The four tests he carries: Transcending Boundaries (pp.209–213), Embodying Multiplicity (pp.214–216), Asignifying Rupture (pp.216–217), Mapping and Tracing (pp.217–218) — each a §. The paper is a cross-domain transfer, not a new D&G exegesis: the rhizome is the diagnostic, the DAO is the organizational substrate.

2. **Boundaries, multiplicity and the infrastructure that enforces them (§ DAO as Rhizome pp.209–211 + p.208 DAO definition).** DAO defined as "a blockchain-based system that enables self-coordination and decentralised governance among individuals to collaborate and attain a shared goal through a set of self-enforcing codes (Hassan & De Filippi 2021)" (p.208); first DAO Slock.it 2016 (Jentzsch et al. 2016) and its 2016 exploit (USD 55m, Meier & Schuppli 2021). Common characteristics (Kondova & Barba 2019 p.209): tokens as shares/voting rights (Nielsen 2019), open-source self-enforcing smart contracts (Rikken et al. 2019), proposal→majority-vote, curators/contractors not CEOs. Transfer § "Transcending Boundaries": "Any individual from any country and time zone with a mobile device, internet connectivity, and a bank account may acquire tokens, becoming a member" (p.210); "absence of rigid demarcations creates the potential for diversity and heterogeneity" (p.210) via Toender & Thomassen 2014 haecceities and Dahlberg & Siapera 2007 radical democracy; internally "rather 'chaotically' distributed network without a strict hierarchical structure … any point to link with any other point" (p.211). The paper pairs each rhizome claim with an infrastructure claim (token membership, blockchain transparency, smart-contract enforcement).

3. **The central caution: protocol retains hierarchy inside rhizomatic rhetoric, empirically shown by MakerDAO concentration (pp.212–214).** Via Galloway (2004 p.212): "decentralisation does not inherently dismantle control or hierarchical oversight. Protocols, as mechanisms through which power operates in decentralised systems, embed hierarchical control by enabling entities to surveil and regulate information flows. DAOs, enmeshed in the logics of protocols, replicate these dynamics, often embedding authority within those who write the code or wield influence through significant token holdings." MakerDAO (p.213): "Governance, structured as one vote per MKR token, reveals a profound asymmetry … voting power concentrates disproportionately in the hands of a few large token holders who dominate decision-making processes (Sun et al. 2024) … Despite a large body of token holders, governance participation is quite low … Proxy voting … further exacerbate[s] this dynamic (Fritsch et al. 2024)." The paper distinguishes "two trajectories of decentralisation: one as a socially transformative movement … and the other as an extension of neoliberal paradigms that devolve power only to reinscribe it in the hands of local elites or market oligarchs (Pieterse 2001; Conway & Singh 2011)" (p.212). Corporate-DAO capture then named (Wyoming legal personhood, Cointelegraph Research 2022 p.213) vs alternative experiments Democracy Earth, VitaDAO (Golato & Kohlhaas 2021 p.213).

4. **Rupture, multiplicity-as-founder-exit, and map vs tracing (§§ Embodying Multiplicity pp.214–216, Asignifying Rupture pp.216–217, Mapping and Tracing pp.217–218).** Multiplicity staged as founder phase-out: "founders commit to decentralise governance and transfer power … stepping away, founders relinquish control … A DAO … remains but a conceptual blueprint until it embodies decentralisation in practice" (p.214) with Compound DAO as positive case (Axelsen et al. 2022) — multisig, delegation thresholds, autonomous execution (p.215) — and Lawley 2005 "constant process of flow, or change, rather than being a thing unto which change is done" (p.215). Asignifying rupture: "'A rhizome may be broken, shattered at a given spot, but it will start up again on one of its old lines, or on new lines' (Deleuze & Guattari 1989, p.9)" (p.216); DAO hard fork as rupture that is not failure but "an intrinsic characteristic … that encourages improved governance practices (Sims 2019)" yet via Laclau & Mouffe/Schmitt framed as "fragmented pluralism" that "disperse[s] [antagonism] across parallel spaces" (p.217), unlike radical democracy's shared-space antagonism. Mapping vs tracing: "The map is open and connectable in all of its dimensions; it is detachable, reversible, susceptible to constant modification (Deleuze & Guattari 1989, p.2)" (p.206); for DAOs "Many DAOs originated as experimental undertakings, and their persistence … do[es] not negate their experimental essence … A map always has multiple entryways" (p.217); yet "any assemblage will inevitably evidence tendencies toward both change (mapping) and stability (tracing) … we should avoid the simple dualism … as good and bad sides (Deleuze & Guattari 1989)" (p.217); MolochDAO→MetaCartel fork replicates grant-funding pattern as decalcomania inside mapping (pet3rpan 2019 p.218). Closing Kromatika manifesto (pp.218–219) stages multiplicity without board/CEO.

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** The DAO evidence is secondary synthesis (Sun et al. 2024, Fritsch et al. 2024, Axelsen et al. 2022, Rodrigues 2019, Nabben & Zargham 2022) — no new DAO measurement was made. The four § are ideal-typical readings, not scored indices; no concentration cutoff or fork-rate threshold is derived. Galloway/Chandler/Land/radical democracy (Habermas, Laclau & Mouffe) framings are interventions. Blockchain/DeFi specifics (tokens, Bitcoin, Web3 Zheng & Lee 2023) are substrate, not claims about this plant's filesystem. The 203–224 pagination is the final CC BY version; no supplement consulted.

## What is new here

**Hung's DAO-as-rhizome transfer: a rhizomatic organization claim must be checked against three material, code-level facts — protocol-encoded hierarchy, concentration measured as actual voting/landing power (not member count), and rupture cost as hard-fork parallel dispersal — while keeping map and tracing as coexistent tendencies rather than a good/bad dualism. The transfer to land is that check, not another definition of rhizome.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with where it lands in this repository and each bounded by what the paper concedes.

1. **Protocol as retained hierarchy (Galloway 2004 via p.212) — the code-writer's knot inside the mesh.** Galloway's point as Hung uses it is not anti-protocol but diagnostic: in decentralised systems, protocols "embed hierarchical control by enabling entities to surveil and regulate information flows" (p.212) — for DAOs, "those who write the code [and] wield influence through significant token holdings" (p.212). Applied without importing blockchain: the plant's couplings are rhizomatic ("any point to link with any other" p.211, multiple entryways p.217) *and* they are protocol-governed — the Supervisor wake issuer, `seed yield` lock implementation, pin-promote scripts, CI gate, and `wall-write-audit.jsonl` digest are the plant's protocols. Naming them as a distinct knot from "walls/tape/branch" prevents the map from hardening into a tracing that replicates without adapting. No new gate or key is added; the declaration is made legible as protocol-dependent.

2. **Concentration as material voting/landing power, not member count (MakerDAO p.213 vs Compound DAO p.214–215).** Hung's empirical pair is the mechanism: MakerDAO shows "governance, structured as one vote per MKR token … voting power concentrates … (Sun et al. 2024) … governance participation is quite low" (p.213) and proxy delegation "further exacerbate[s]" (p.213); Compound DAO shows the counter-process — deliberate founder phase-out via delegated voting thresholds and multisig collective oversight reaching "sufficient decentralisation … long-term equilibrium" (p.214–215, Axelsen et al. 2022). The transfer is Ovezik-compatible: mesh.md already warns "a count of participants is not a measure of decentralisation: a lane can grow while concentrating" (mesh.md:41–42) — Hung supplies the *why* for this plant: code authorship and landing/concentration on `main` (genome push, wall-write shares, lock holds) are the analogue to token-weighted voting, not DAOs themselves. The plant-level audit is the same windowed share the introduction already publishes, read against concentration rather than rhetoric.

3. **Asignifying rupture as hard fork with cost, and map/tracing as concurrent (pp.216–218).** "'A rhizome may be broken … but it will start up again on one of its old lines, or on new lines' (Deleuze & Guattari 1989, p.9)" (p.216); for DAOs the rupture is executable: "hard fork … breaking off ongoing development and initiating new development in another direction (Kurniawan 2022)" with "no quorum requirement" (p.216) — "ruptures are neither construed as anomalous disruptions … it is perceived not as a defect but as an intrinsic characteristic" (p.216). Hung keeps the cost: Laclau & Mouffe demand antagonism be managed "within a shared political space" (p.217) — DAO exit "disperse[s] them across parallel spaces for divergent communities" (p.217), producing "fragmented pluralism" (p.217). Map/tracing then is not a choice: "we should avoid the simple dualism of 'contrasting maps to tracings, as good and bad sides' (Deleuze & Guattari 1989)" (p.217) — MolochDAO's new DAO still replicated grant-funding patterns as decalcomania (p.218). For this plant that is wall rupture (supervisor-pane loss, audit divergence) resuming on "old lines, or on new lines" and the branch that forks: the fork is not proof of failure, but it is a governance dispersal with shared state cost, and a forked wall/branch still carries tracings ("knots of arborescence in rhizomes" p.218).

Limits this review keeps from Hung. Only the governance-diagnostic transfer moves; no token, Bitcoin, Web3, Wyoming corporate, or DeFi financial fact is imported as a plant property. MakerDAO/Compound/MolochDAO/Kromatika are cited as the paper's *evidence for the transfer test*, not as properties of this plant's filesystem, pane, or sensor. No concentration threshold, fork-rate metric, or gate into `evaluate()`/`check()` is added — the paper offers no such threshold (see Limits above), and this plant already stages concentration as windowed shares without a single published number (Ovezik et al. 2025). The radical democracy and Galloway/Chandler/Land framings are cited as Hung's diagnostic lens, not as adopted doctrine.
