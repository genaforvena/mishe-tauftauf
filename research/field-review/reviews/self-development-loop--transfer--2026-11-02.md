Four passages carry the transfer this plant has not made. Page numbers are the journal pagination (467–482); PDF pagination of the iText reprint is offset by +2.

1. **Hierarchy as partitioning into subsystems with stronger intra- than inter-interaction (pp. 468–469, § Hierarchy).** "By a hierarchic system, or hierarchy, I mean a system that is composed of interrelated subsystems, each of the latter being, in turn, hierarchic in structure until we reach some lowest level of elementary subsystem" (p. 468). A crystal or gas volume is a "very 'flat' hierarchy" — many first-order subsystems but no deeper nesting (p. 469) — whereas a book is a hierarchy of chapters → sections → paragraphs → sentences. The hierarchy is defined by interaction intensity, not formal org chart: "there will generally be more interaction … between two employees who are members of the same department than between two employees from different departments" (p. 473).

2. **The watchmaker parable — stable intermediate forms decide the time to assemble complexity (pp. 469–471, Fig. p. 470).** Hora and Tempus each make 1,000-part watches but Hora builds ten-element subassemblies, ten of those into larger subassemblies, ten of those into the whole watch, so an interruption (phone call, probability p≈0.01 per part) loses only ~5 parts for Hora versus ~100 for Tempus; Tempus fails .99^1000≈44×10^-6 per attempt while Hora succeeds .99^10≈0.9 per subassembly, so Tempus takes ~4,000× as long. Lessons glossed pp. 470–471: (a) no teleology required — direction is provided by the stability of the complex form once it exists ("survival of the fittest — i.e., of the stable," p. 471); (b) polymers/nylon are the span-one limiting case; (c) evolution absorbs free energy from the sun, so entropy bookkeeping does not explain the rate — stable intermediates do. For our plant: an interruption is a seed-lock wait, a stale producer, a wedged pane; the question is whether the plant loses 5 parts or 100 when it answers the phone.

3. **Nearly decomposable systems — short-run independence, long-run aggregate dependence (pp. 473–474, § Nearly Decomposable Systems; Simon & Ando 1961, Econometrica 29:111-138).** Two formal propositions: "(a) in a nearly decomposable system, the short-run behavior of each of the component subsystems is approximately independent of the short-run behavior of the other components; (b) in the long run, the behavior of any one of the components depends in only an aggregate way on the behavior of the other components" (p. 474). The running example is heat flow through cubicles in rooms in a building: within-room cubicle-to-cubicle conductance r_ij is large, room-to-room conductance is small but non-zero (ε), so the coefficient matrix is nearly block-diagonal and the dynamics separate by time scale — hours for within-room equilibration, days for building-wide. The matrix picture (Fig. 1, p. 474) makes decomposability a matter of degree (ε bounding off-diagonal elements), not a binary.

4. **Time scales as logs n when stable subassemblies share span s (pp. 470–472, 477–478).** "If there exists a hierarchy of potential stable 'subassemblies,' with about the same span, s, at each level of the hierarchy, then the time required for a subassembly can be expected to be about the same at each level — that is proportional to 1/(1-p)^s" so "the time required for the assembly of a system of n elements will be proportional to log_s n, that is, to the number of levels in the system" (p. 471). Applied to problem-solving (pp. 476–478): selectivity equals feedback of information from the environment — trial paths remembered as stable configurations, and previous experience (reproduction) reusing them — which cuts the maze to reasonable size without postulating "processes more sophisticated than those involved in organic evolution" (p. 478).

**Why this literature, for this cell.** The field already has three recent/misread/critique readings (DGM/SRLM exploration–exploitation; Argyris 1976 governing variables; Levinthal & March 1993 myopia) plus three applied transfers/mechanisms/metrics (Meadows 1999 12→1 leverage in `initiative_watch.py`; Ashby 1960 ultrastability in `substrate_watch.py`; DORA metrics in `initiative.py`) and Bateson 1972 learning levels in `follow_through.py`. None types hierarchy or near-decomposability. The `transfer` angle for self-development-loop asks for a cross-domain mechanism onto one of this plant's parts — substrate observer / follow-through / initiative watch. Simon is that mechanism's canonical statement: a definition of hierarchy, a parable of stable subassemblies, and a formal ε-bound on decomposability that turns "the plant observing and changing itself" from flat list composition into leveled choice with time-scale separation.

## What this plant already does

*The loop observes itself, but it composes a flat list where Simon would build a hierarchy of stable subassemblies.*

- **Follow-through is a flat composition, not a hierarchy.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/follow_through.py:110-118` `lines()` returns `[*_outcome_refusal_lines() , *substrate_lines() , *initiative_lines() , *watch_lines()]` — four projections concatenated. `follow_through.py:192-274` `health()` routes one alert per freshness-failure episode. Both treat substrate / initiative / watch as peers in a list, not as levels with spans. The module already carries Lawrence 2005 power vocabulary (`LEARNING_PROCESSES`/`POWER_FORMS`/`PROCESS_POWER_MAP`/`ROUTING_POWER` at :30-69 and helpers `follow_through_power_form:72` / `follow_through_mode_for_form:97`) and Bateson 1972 learning-level vocabulary (Learning 0/I/II/III context marker, wake 199725), but neither types hierarchical level nor interaction intensity. A crystal of 1,000 parts and a Hora watch of 1,000 parts both appear as 1,000-part lists.

- **Stable subassemblies exist but are unnamed as such.** The plant does have subassemblies that survive interruption: a passing `substrate_watch._runtime()` verdict (`OBSERVED` on HEAD==pin + clean working tree) is a stable ten-element block; a committed `wall outcome` evidence file (SHA256 binding, `EVIDENCE_BINDING_CLAUSE_TIME`) is a stable hundred-element block; a `checks/initiative.json` window snapshot with `covers` is a stable block at the next level. The watchmaker lesson is that Hora "designed them so that he could put together subassemblies of about ten elements each" (p. 469) — the stability is designed, not incidental — while Tempus's failure is that "if he had one partly assembled and had to put it down … it immediately fell to pieces" (p. 469). Today's plant behaves like Tempus on interruption categories it has not designed for: a stale `producer` (phone call) can force re-assembly of the whole follow-through line as `UNKNOWN` rather than loss of one subassembly.

- **Near-decomposability is not typed, so short-run independence is not observable.** `substrate_watch.py` hashing HEAD/index is independent of `initiative.py:185` `collect()` tape scan in the short run — they read different sources — but in the long run `initiative.py` depends on substrate only in aggregate (whether HEAD moved). The building heat-flow analogue (p. 474) would say rooms are subsystems and cubicles are sub-subsystems; ε is the wall conductance. The plant's analogue is `MAX_BYTES` / `SNAPSHOT_MAX_AGE` / `WINDOW_SECONDS` as wall conductance between subsystems. No symbol in `follow_through.py` names ε or states the two propositions (a) and (b), so a dashboard line cannot say "substrate and initiative behave independently in the short run; their coupling is aggregate in the long run via HEAD drift" — it can only list them.

- **Time-to-evolve is rendered as linear, not logarithmic.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative.py:34-43` fixes `WINDOW_SECONDS=86400`, `MAX_PATCHES=512`, `MAX_ENTRIES` as constants; `initiative_watch.py:52-170` types governing variables and single-loop trap; `Levinthal & March` adds myopia kinds but still at one level. None states the p/span/log_s n result (p. 471) that predicts why one-per-turn discipline (Hora's ten-at-a-time) lands in 231 reviews while the retired mesh's flat 376-at-once (Tempus) left 245 unlanded. Without the hierarchy vocabulary the advantage remains anecdotal rather than checkable.

## What is new here

**Type every follow-through projection as a level in a Simon hierarchy with span s and ε-bound — so an interruption loses one subassembly, not the whole watch, and near-decomposability is observable rather than assumed.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number in this repository where it would land, each bounded by what the source concedes.

1. **Hierarchy is a levelled partition, not a score (p. 468).** Making the hierarchy explicit as `SIMON_HIERARCHY_LEVELS: Final[tuple[str, ...]]` (e.g. `("cubicle", "room", "building")` analogized to `("projection", "subsystem", "plant")`) plus `HIERARCHY_SPAN: Final[int]` (ten in the parable, quoted p. 469) and `HIERARCHY_LEVEL_FOR_SOURCE: Final[dict[str,int]]` mapping `_outcome_refusal_lines→1 / substrate_lines→1 / initiative_lines→2 / watch_lines→2` lets `follow_through.lines()` be typed as a Hora watch: ten-element stable blocks composed into larger assemblies. The typing is detector, not causal claim; a flat hierarchy (crystal/gas, p. 469) is the legitimate limiting case and is represented, not forbidden.

2. **Near-decomposability is an ε-bound on interaction, testable without clock/I/O (pp. 473-474).** Adding `NEAR_DECOMPOSABLE_EPSILON: Final[float]` (the off-diagonal upper bound ε from Fig. 1) and a pure predicate `is_nearly_decomposable(interaction_matrix: dict, epsilon: float | None = None) -> bool | None` plus `hierarchy_level(source: str | None) -> int | None` makes Simon's two propositions checkable data: short-run independence is "within-level coupling dominates," long-run dependence is "cross-level coupling is only aggregate." The ε stays a declared bound, not a live measurement, matching Simon's "weak, but not negligible" phrasing (p. 474).

3. **Stable subassemblies explain the one-cell-per-turn discipline (pp. 469-471).** The transfer preserves both prior vocabularies and orders them: Lawrence's power forms (typed in this file) sit at different hierarchy levels; Meadows's leverage ladder (typed in `initiative_watch.py`) is orthogonal to Simon's level; Ashby's veto (typed in `substrate_watch.py`) is the stability condition for a subassembly ("S in neutral equilibrium iff essential variables all within limits," 1960 p. 84). Simon adds what none provides: the quantitative link between span, interruption probability p, and expected time to assemble — which is why Hora's discipline (111 ten-part subassemblies) beats Tempus despite making 111× as many assemblies.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** Simon presents no data beyond the parable and the heat-flow illustration; the transfer is therefore a typed vocabulary/detector, not a claim that labelling a line as level 2 fixes any particular `aged-wake` this week. The parable's numerical estimate (4,000×) assumes constant p and ignores orientation/entropy of partial assemblies — Simon himself calls it "with more illustrative than literal intent" (p. 471) and lists three caveats (no teleology; span-one polymers; free-energy/initial-entropy irrelevance). The plant's existing `UNKNOWN`/`OBSERVED`/`ATTENTION` vocabulary stays the detector for incomplete information; the new helpers add only the second clause Simon distinguishes: incomplete information about the *level* and *span* of the hierarchy and the *interaction intensity* (ε) that makes short-run independence observable.
