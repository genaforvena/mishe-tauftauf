- Claims relied on (verbatim or close paraphrase with page refs from the NBER PDF pagination):
  - Abstract (p.1): "randomization does not equalize everything but the treatment across treatments and controls, it does not automatically deliver a precise estimate of the average treatment effect (ATE), and it does not relieve us of the need to think about (observed or unobserved) confounders. Estimates apply to the trial sample only, sometimes a convenience sample, and usually selected; justification is required to extend them to other groups" // "Demanding 'external validity' is unhelpful because it expects too much of an RCT while undervaluing its contribution" // "RCTs can play a role ... but they can only do so as part of a cumulative program, combining with other methods, including conceptual and theoretical development, to discover not 'what works,' but 'why things work'."
  - Introduction diagnoses "gold standard" thinking (p.2): US DOE "strong evidence" requires an RCT, J-PAL's 843 RCTs with no non-RCT mention, Imbens (2010) "very top" hierarchy — as examples of lexicographic preference for RCT evidence. "Which method is most likely to yield a good causal inference depends on what we are trying to discover as well as on what knowledge is already available" (p.3).
  - Three routine misunderstandings (p.3): (a) randomization ensures treatment/control differ only in treatment; (b) RCTs give unbiased *and* precise ATEs; (c) inference is straightforward by simple mean comparison.
  - "Internal validity is not only necessary but almost sufficient" belief traced to Campbell & Stanley's "primacy of internal validity" (p.3); authors argue the opposite: "credibility in estimation can lead to incredibility in use" (p.3).
  - Section 1 opening (p.5): the trial "sample" is "usually selected ... or is simply a convenience sample ... Given random allocation ... the data allow identification of F0, F1 ... The estimated ATE is the difference in means" — but policymakers may care about other distributional features, and the trial estimates are for that sample only.
  - Section 2.2 "Using results, transportability, and external validity" (pp.26–28): the binary concept of external validity ("holds universally or at least widely") is "often unhelpful because it asks the wrong question — away from more complex but more useful applications" and "The failure of external validity interpreted as simple generalization ... says little about the value of the experiment." "Establishing causality does nothing in and of itself to guarantee generalizability" (p.27). Transport requires a theoretical account: "If we misunderstand the setting, if we do not understand why the treatment in our experiment worked ... we have no principled way to predict whether it will work elsewhere" (p.29). Cartwright's support-factor / capacity language and illustrations (pp.29–33) — a policy "what works somewhere" needs a "why things work — with what support" account to warrant use elsewhere.
  - What the critique concedes (pp.3,5,33): "Nothing ... should be taken as a general argument against RCTs; we are simply trying to challenge unjustifiable claims" — the minimal-assumption nature of RCTs is "an advantage when persuading distrustful audiences, but it is a disadvantage for cumulative scientific progress, where prior knowledge should be built upon and not discarded" (abstract, p.5). RCTs remain "enormously useful ... when they are designed to enhance" cumulative knowledge (p.3).

## What is new here

- Deaton & Cartwright's transferable lesson for this plant is that an RCT-style O1/O2 delta — even with a clean decoder and matched cost — estimates an effect *for that sample of windows*. Treating it as a general "what works" claim reproduces the exact failure mode they diagnose: magical thinking that credibility in estimation guarantees credibility in use. Their abstract's three negations map directly onto how `trial_metrics report()` could be misread: (1) placing a trial at `trial_start` does not equalize history/maturation across placebo and trial windows; (2) a single-window ATE estimate is not precise because `mean|Δ|` is noisy when entries are autocorrelated shared-regime windows; (3) the `q` ratio does not relieve us of thinking about confounders (Huang's oracle/cost, Campbell's history, and Deaton's selection all remain).
- The binary "external validity" question ("does the trial generalize? yes/no") is the wrong question for this plant. The useful question is "under what support does this effect travel?" The paper replaces "external validity" with transportability: you can use a trial result elsewhere only with a causal account of *why* it worked and what support factors it needed in that context — load regime, feed regime, role mix, elapsed-time regime, host-capacity regime. The trial-window delta alone is not that account; `placebo_scale` shows ordinary variation but not the support.
- The convenience-sample point is literal here: the trial windows are not a random sample from "all future windows" but the windows that happened to fall in the 10 h trial interval — selected by calendar, host regime (`LIMITED` vs normal), and coordinator liveness. Selection justification is required to extend any window estimate to another group (next week, normal capacity, a different role set). The precursor Goodhart discard noted that no threshold defines a critical proxy point; Deaton gives the complementary guard: no statistic defines transport — only a why-story plus corroboration does.
- Why this matters beside Campbell and Huang: Campbell asks whether the design can speak causally at all (internal threats); Huang asks whether the delta survives equal information/cost/prompt; Deaton asks whether even a causally clean delta can be *used* elsewhere. The three audits are orthogonal. The chapter the paper cites as the source of the "primacy of internal validity" myth is the very Campbell & Stanley (1963) the misread REVISIT reads — Deaton reframes that primacy as necessary but not sufficient for usefulness, which is exactly the label this plant's report needs.

  - Also return `placebo_regime` derived from `placebo["kept"]` / `placebo["excluded"]`: when kept < 6 or placebo windows span a known `LIMITED` episode (via `history_label`), transport is flagged as "support unknown — regime differs."

- Limits, in the paper's own terms and ours: (a) Transfer from economics RCTs to feed-count windows is analogy; Deaton & Cartwright's "gold standard" is about policy economics, ours about autonomic trial windows — the mechanism (convenience sample, transport needs why-account) transfers, the substance and the support factors do not. (b) The paper concedes RCTs with correct expectations are valuable and "can be enormously useful ... when designed to enhance" cumulative knowledge; this guard does not forbid using deltas, it forbids presenting them as self-warranting "what works" claims. Both blocks remain.
