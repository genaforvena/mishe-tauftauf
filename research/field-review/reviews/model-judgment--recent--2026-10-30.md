# Verbalized probabilities are already softmax — don't re-softmax them; invert first, then calibrate

## What we read

**Cheng Wang, Gyuri Szarvas, Georges Balazs, Pavel Danchenko, Patrick Ernst — "Calibrating Verbalized Probabilities for Large Language Models", arXiv:2410.06707v1 [cs.CL] 9 Oct 2024** — read the full primary source at:

- <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06707> (arXiv abstract, fetched 200 2026-10-30; PDF 1_533_844 bytes fetched 200 at /tmp/2410_06707.pdf, 8 pp., sha not pinned in doc but bytes match header)
- <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06707.pdf> (PDF, same bytes; rendered via pdftotext, 8 pp., Fig.1 invert-softmax diagram p.1)

The paper builds on Tian et al. (2023) "Just Ask for Calibration" and Xiong et al. (2024) verbalized confidence, and on Guo et al. (2017) temperature scaling (TS). The authors extend confidence elicitation to **full verbalized probability distributions** for discriminative classification and diagnose why naive TS on those distributions fails.

Four passages carry what this plant has not made (quotations via pdftotext, pagination approximate):

1. **What the paper asks to be new — from confidence to distribution (Introduction, §2.1).** "Can we generalize confidence elicitation to probability elicitation for discriminative tasks that require a full probability distribution over all categorical classes? ... instead of generating prediction and associated confidence score, can LLMs generate probability distribution?" The authors craft prompts that ask the LLM to emit a dictionary over labels — e.g. IMDB `{'positive':0.99,'negative':0.01}`, Emotion `{'sadness':0.8,…}`, Massive intent `{'audio_volume_down':0.99,…}` (Table 1 p.2) — and verify LLMs *can* do this robustly on IMDB / Emotion / Massive.

2. **The re-softmax trap — theory (§2.2, Propositions 1–2).** Temperature scaling is defined `p_i = exp(z_i/τ)/Σ_j exp(z_j/τ)` on *logits* `z`. The authors show applying `SOFTMAX` directly to already-softmax verbalized probabilities `p` produces `q = SOFTMAX(p)` whose entries are bounded away from 0 and 1: Prop.1 `0 < 1/(K−1+e) ≤ q_i ≤ e/(K−1+e) < 1` for K>1, so `q` is strictly more uniform than `p`. Prop.2 then shows the optimal temperature for `q` is *smaller* than for `p` (`τ_q < τ_p`), i.e. calibrating `q` inverts the expected direction (`τ>1` for overconfident `p` becomes `τ_q<1` for `q`). Fig.2 p.3 makes the empirical point: LLM verbalized `p` is overconfident (avg confidence > accuracy), so correct calibration needs `τ>1` to flatten it; but after re-softmax, the estimated optimum is `τ_q≈0.31 <1`, which would sharpen an already overconfident mean — unreliable calibration.

3. **The repair — invert-softmax trick (§2.3, Fig.1).** Rather than softmax `p` again, estimate logits by inverting: `ẑ_i ≈ log p_i` (up to additive constant; §2.3 eq.5 discussion), then apply `SOFTMAX(ẑ/τ)`. Fig.1 pipeline: prompt → black-box LLM (Claude, Mistral) → verbalized `p` → invert to `ẑ` → temperature scale → calibrated `p̂`. Eq.5 notes the sum-to-one repair: when LLMs emit non-normalized `p` (empirically observed, §3.2), the inversion renormalizes before scaling, which naive re-softmax cannot recover from. The method is post-hoc and weight-free: `τ` is tuned once on a held-out validation split by minimizing NLL or ECE.

4. **How they know — ECE/MCE/reliability on three datasets (§3, Tables 2–4).** Calibration is measured by Expected Calibration Error `ECE = (1/N) Σ_m |b_m| |Acc(b_m)−Conf(b_m)|` (binned accuracy vs confidence), Maximum Calibration Error (MCE) and reliability diagrams (Naeini et al. 2015; Guo et al. 2017). Table 2 validates the split (Claude-v2/Mistral validation/test sizes), Tables 3–4 show invert+TS consistently lowers ECE vs uncalibrated `p` and vs re-softmax+TS on the intersection of LLM outputs; ablations (§3.3, Table 8) confirm the sign flip `τ_q<1` without the inversion. The paper does not claim logit access or fine-tuning — only verbalized text plus a held-out split for `τ`.

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** The authors evaluate three text classification sets (IMDB sentiment, Emotion 6-way, Massive 60-way intent) on Claude-v2 / Mistral-family black-box APIs; results are for prompted distribution elicitation, not for arbitrary agent traces. Some LLMs fail to emit valid distributions summing to 1 (discussed eq.5). `τ` must be tuned on a validation set — no tuning means no calibration. Invert `log p` is an *estimate* of logit, not the model's true logit; the trick works because TS only needs the logit up to affine equivalence. Reported gains are ECE/MCE reductions, not accuracy lifts.

**Why this source, for this cell.** The plant's model-judgment field (docs/decision-models.md, docs/completions-judge.md, src/mishe_tauftauf/completions_judge.py, src/mishe_tauftauf/jev_judge.py) currently treats judge output as categorical. The recent result the field needs is a 2023–2026 *operational* way to make a hosted judge's numbers mean what they say — exactly what Wang et al. publish as a concrete, black-box-compatible calibration procedure with a testable trap to avoid.

## What is new here

**Ask the judge for a distribution, not a confidence on one label — and if you calibrate that distribution, invert it first; re-softmaxing is the bug, `log p` → `softmax(z/τ)` is the repair. Report calibration (ECE) separately from decision (yes/no), and tune τ once on held-out pairs rather than baking a prompt bias.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each bounded by what the paper concedes:

1. **Verbalized distribution elicitation as a calibration probe (§2.1).** For any classification the plant already asks categorically (e.g. "does the trace support causal claim X? — yes / no / unknown"), an additive prompt variant asks for `{"yes":p1,"no":p2,"unknown":p3}` summing to 1, with "Give ONLY the probability distribution ... Provide ONLY the probability in a format of Python dict" (Box p.2). This makes calibration *testable* without needing logit access: the same judge, same evidence, but now the numbers have a sum-to-one invariant to check and bins to measure. The paper's Table 1 shows even 6-way/60-way distributions are elicitable from black-box APIs; the plant's failure mode — LLM fails to output a valid distribution — is already anticipated (eq.5).

2. **The re-softmax propositions as a concrete trap to encode (Props.1–2).** Applying `SOFTMAX` to the already-softmax `p` is not neutral — Prop.1 proves the result lives in `[1/(K−1+e), e/(K−1+e)]`, erasing extremes, and Prop.2 proves the temperature tuned on `q` will point the wrong way. Encoding this as a named check (e.g. `is_resoftmax = abs(τ_q − 1) < ... and τ_q < 1 while empirical_p_overconfident`) turns a philosophy ("be careful with probabilities") into a testable inequality. The repair is one line: `ẑ_i = log(p_i + ε)` then `p̂ = softmax(ẑ/τ)` with ε for the `p_i=0` edge the paper handles.

3. **Post-hoc, weight-free, split-tuned calibration as the plant's cheapest comparator upgrade (Fig.1 + §3).** Tune a single scalar `τ>0` on a small held-out validation set of `(evidence, expected ∈ {yes,no,unknown})` pairs by minimizing NLL or ECE (the paper uses both), then apply `τ` to future verbalized `p` via the invert path — no model retrain, no prompt overfit, no new `checks/*.json` gate. Reporting `ECE` / `MCE` / reliability bins beside the existing categorical smoke `accuracy` keeps the two concerns distinct, as `completions-judge.md` already insists: smoke ≠ calibration. `τ` is diagnostic, not a threshold change — a high `τ` means the judge was overconfident, not that the verdict word changes.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the paper.** Only the invert+TS *method* and the *metric family* transfer; no paper threshold (`τ≈...`) is imported, because `τ` is always fit to the plant's own validation split. The `p` sum-to-one failure is not hidden — it is counted as a distribution-validity rate alongside ECE. Calibration is reported, not gated — a miscalibrated judge does not become a different `state` word. The three-set evaluation (IMDB/Emotion/Massive) is not reclaimed as a plant benchmark; it is the paper's evidence that the elicitation and the trick generalize beyond one prompt.
