# Calibration preserves the verdict — temperature scaling does not fix what you said, only how confidently you said it

## What we read

**Chuan Guo, Geoff Pleiss, Yu Sun, Kilian Q. Weinberger — "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", Proceedings of the 34th ICML, Sydney, PMLR 70 (2017)** — read full primary source at:

- <https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599> (arXiv v2 3 Aug 2017 abstract)
- <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.04599.pdf> (PDF 1_318_000 bytes fetched 200 2026-10-30 at /tmp/guo17.pdf, 8 pp., sha not pinned but bytes match header `On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks`)
- <https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html> (PMLR landing, same paper)

Guo et al. is the foundational calibration paper cited by Wang et al. (2024, field-review model-judgment--recent), Tian et al. (2023) and the plant's own docs. Four passages carry what this plant has misread — page numbers are ICML/PMLR pagination, equations as numbered in the paper, quotations via `pdftotext` (approximate line breaks):

1. **Perfect calibration is not accuracy — definitions (§2, p.2, Eq.1–5).** "We would like the confidence estimate P̂ to be calibrated, which intuitively means that P̂ represents a true probability. For example, given 100 predictions, each with confidence of 0.8, we expect that 80 should be correctly classified. More formally, we define perfect calibration as P(Ŷ=Y | P̂=p) = p, ∀p ∈ [0,1] (1)." ECE then approximates E|P(Ŷ=Y|P̂=p)−p| by binning: `acc(B_m) = (1/|B_m|) Σ 1(ŷ_i=y_i)` and `conf(B_m) = (1/|B_m|) Σ p̂_i` over `M` equally-spaced bins, `ECE = Σ_m |B_m|/n · |acc(B_m)−conf(B_m)|` (3, M=15 in Table 1); `MCE = max_m |acc−conf|` (5); NLL `L = −Σ log π̂(y_i|x_i)` (6). Reliability diagrams (Fig.1 bottom) plot `acc(B_m)` vs `conf(B_m)`; the diagonal is perfect calibration.

2. **Modern nets are accurate but overconfident, and capacity makes it worse — observation (§3, p.2–4, Figs.1–3).** Fig.1 (p.1) LeNet (1998) on CIFAR-100: avg confidence ≈ accuracy, bars on diagonal; ResNet-110 (2016): same dataset, higher accuracy but avg confidence >> accuracy, large red gaps. Fig.2 (p.2) varies depth, width, Batch Normalization, weight decay on CIFAR-100 — ECE grows with depth/width/BN and with too little weight decay, even as error falls. Fig.3 (p.3) shows ResNet-110 training: after 0/1 loss (error) saturates, NLL continues to be minimizable by increasing confidence — "neural networks can overfit to NLL without overfitting to the 0/1 loss." Caption: NLL overfitting is visible as test NLL rising after epoch ~250 while error stays flat.

3. **Temperature scaling preserves the verdict — method (§4.2, p.4, Eq.9 and text).** "Temperature scaling, the simplest extension of Platt scaling, uses a single scalar parameter T>0 for all classes. Given the logit vector z_i, the new confidence prediction is q̂_i = max_k σ_SM(z_i/T)^{(k)} (9)." Immediately after Eq.9: "Because the parameter T does not change the maximum of the softmax function, the class prediction ŷ_i' remains unchanged. In other words, temperature scaling does not affect the model's accuracy." T is called temperature, "softens" with T>1 (raises entropy, T→∞ → 1/K), T=1 recovers p̂, T→0 collapses to point mass. T is optimized w.r.t. NLL on a held-out validation set (p.4, §5: Birds/Cars/ImageNet/CIFAR-10/100/SVHN setups with explicit train/validation/test splits). Table 1 (p.5) shows T-scaling ECE 12.67→0.96 on CIFAR-100 ResNet-110(SD) and beats vector/matrix scaling.

4. **Miscalibration is low-dimensional; simple beats general — results (§5, p.5–6, Table 1, Fig.4).** "Vector scaling recovers essentially the same solution as temperature scaling — the learned vector has nearly constant entries, and therefore is no different than a scalar transformation. In other words, network miscalibration is intrinsically low dimensional." Matrix scaling overfits on many-class sets (quadratic params). Binning methods (histogram, isotonic, BBQ) improve but hurt accuracy because they can change ŷ, unlike T-scaling. Fig.4 reliability: uncalibrated ResNet overconfident, all post-hoc methods bring bars toward diagonal.

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** Guo evaluates vision (ResNet, DenseNet, WideResNet) and NLP (DAN, TreeLSTM) on image/document classification with softmax logits and a held-out validation set for T; results are for multi-class softmax confidence on those splits, not for arbitrary hosted LLM `P(yes)` without logits. ECE depends on M (they use 15); reported gains are calibration (ECE/MCE/NLL) not accuracy — accuracy is held fixed by construction for T-scaling. No claim that T transfers across datasets or prompts.

**Why this source, for this cell.** `model-judgment--misread` asks for a foundational idea we may have misread. The plant's `model-judgment` field names calibration as vocabulary (`fields.json:81-83`) and already cites Guo indirectly via Wang et al. (2024), but its own text treats calibration as a vendor trait and hosted probability as a threshold — exactly the accuracy-confidence conflation Guo's §2–3 + Fig.1–3 were written to separate.

The plant's current posture — "we don't claim calibration, so we need an no measurement" — keeps the `unknown` discipline (good) but leaves the hosted judge's P(yes) in the exact regime Guo predicts is overconfident by default, with no bin to see it.

## What is new here

**Guo's three invariants that the plant has not made, and that change what a "calibration result" means here: measure calibration separately from accuracy with bins; expect modern judges to be overconfident; and when you soften that overconfidence, preserve the verdict.**

1. **Calibration is a binned gap, not a threshold — ECE/MCE/reliability vs accuracy (Guo §2, Eq.3/5).** `ECE = Σ |B_m|/n |acc(B_m)−conf(B_m)|` (weighted bin gap, M=15 in Table 1) and a reliability diagram are *different numbers than accuracy* because `acc(B_m)` and `conf(B_m)` are grouped by `P̂` not by count correct. The plant's smoke accuracy (12/12 yes/no) never bins by `P̂` — so it cannot see that a judge can be 90% confident on average while 70% accurate. Making `ECE`/`MCE` a second number beside `accuracy`, with `M` stated, turns "calibrated" from a vendor adjective into a checkable inequality.

2. **Expect overconfidence; do not infer calibration from accuracy (Guo §3, Figs.1–3).** Fig.1 LeNet vs ResNet is the exact shape of hosted-comparator risk: the newer, more accurate model has larger ECE (ResNet gaps >> LeNet). Fig.2 shows ECE grows with depth/width/BN/less weight decay even as error falls; Fig.3 shows NLL can climb while error stays flat — minimizing NLL can make confidence more extreme after accuracy has saturated, which is indistinguishable from "the judge is being helpful" if you only watch accuracy. For this plant: Jev 1.13 / Nimble 9B / Tev1 / Clef are all modern, capacity-trained models — the prior should be overconfident P(yes) near 0 or 1, not well-calibrated 0.6–0.8.

3. **Temperature scaling's defining invariant: T does not change argmax, so calibration is orthogonal to the yes/no verdict (Guo §4.2 Eq.9 text).** `q̂_i = max σ_SM(z_i/T)` with `T>0` preserves `argmax_k σ_SM(z_i/T)^{(k)} = argmax_k z_i^{(k)}`; "the class prediction remains unchanged … does not affect the model's accuracy." Tuning `T>1` softens (entropy ↑), `T<1` sharpens. Vector scaling's collapse to near-constant entries (p.6: miscalibration is low-dimensional) means even the most general linear fix often reduces to this one scalar. Consequence for the plant: if the plant ever softens a hosted `P(yes)` (e.g. via Wang et al.'s `log p → softmax(z/T)` invert trick from `model-judgment--recent`), the softened score must be reported beside the categorical `yes/no` without moving the threshold that decides `yes/no` — otherwise the plant repeats the binning-method error (p.6) that trades calibration for accuracy. The held-out validation NLL objective for T (§5) also clarifies what counts as evidence: not a prompt tweak, a split with `n` pairs.

**Limits the review keeps from Guo.** Only the *definitions* (Eq.1/3), the *overconfidence expectation* (Figs.1–2), the *NLL-vs-0/1 divergence* (Fig.3), and the *argmax-preserving T-scaling* invariant (Eq.9 text) transfer; no dataset ECE, no fitted T, no Table 1 number is imported, because T and ECE are always fit/measured on the plant's own held-out `(evidence, expected)` split. ECE's bin count M is a reporting choice (Guo uses 15); the plant's helper defaults below may use 15 to match Guo or 10 to match Wang — the invariant is that M is stated. Larger-model → larger-ECE is an empirical association (Fig.2), not a law.
