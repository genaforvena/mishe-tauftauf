The source's own claims for this transfer, in its words:

- Abstract: "We provide the first mechanism — the Wasserstein Mechanism — which applies to any general Pufferfish framework. Since this mechanism may be computationally inefficient, we provide an additional mechanism that applies to some practical cases such as physical activity measurements across time, and is computationally efficient" (Abstract p.1).
- §1 p.2 running example: "measurement of physical activity of a single subject across time. The goal here is to release aggregate statistics on the subject's activities over a long period (say a week) while hiding the evidence of an activity at any specific instant (say, 10:30am on Jan 4). If the measurements are made at small intervals, then the records form a highly correlated time-series as human activities change slowly over time." Entry-privacy adds ≈1 noise, group DP adds ≈O(T) noise "thus destroying all utility" — Pufferfish is adopted to allow utility when "the 'average amount' of correlation is low."
- §2 Definitions: Pufferfish triple (S set of secrets, Q⊆S×S secret pairs, Θ class of data-generating distributions) — "Privacy is provided by ensuring that the secret pairs in Q are indistinguishable when data is generated from any θ∈Θ" (§1 p.2, Def 2.1 p.5). For the time series, "S is the set of activities at each time t, and secret pairs are all pairs of the form (Activity a at time t, Activity b at time t). Assuming that activities transition in a Markovian fashion, Θ is a set of Markov Chains over activities" (§1 p.2).
- §3: ∞-Wasserstein distance (Def 3.1 p.6) "can be interpreted as the minimum distance that mass has to be moved to transform one distribution into the other" (p.6) — the relevant metric for Pufferfish noise calibration. Algorithm 1: add Laplace noise with scale `W/ε` where `W = sup_{(s_i,s_j)∈Q, θ∈Θ} W_∞(μ_{i,θ}, μ_{j,θ})` over output distributions of Lipschitz query F. Theorem 3.2: Wasserstein Mechanism provides ε-Pufferfish privacy; Theorem 3.3: for L-Lipschitz F, `W ≤ L·max sensitivity`, and when Pufferfish reduces to DP the mechanism reduces to the Laplace mechanism.
- §4: max-influence (Def 4.1 p.8) measures worst-case change in distribution on XA when Xi changes value under Θ; Markov Quilt (Def 4.2 p.9): X_Q is a quilt for Xi if it partitions X into (X_N, X_Q, X_R) with X_R ⊥ Xi | X_Q for every θ∈Θ. Unlike Markov blankets, "a node can have many Markov Quilts" (p.9). Algorithm 2: for each Xi search SQ,i, pick quilt minimizing `|X_Q| / (ε - e_Θ(X_Q|Xi))` where `e_Θ` is max-influence; add Laplace noise scaled by that minimum. Theorem 4.3: provides ε-Pufferfish privacy when each SQ,i contains the trivial quilt `X_Q=∅`. Theorem 4.4: sequential composition holds with linearly decaying privacy parameter when the active quilt (Def 4.5) is unchanged across releases — "Pufferfish privacy does not always compose [20]" but quilts do under this condition (§4.3 p.10).
- Limits stated in source: Wasserstein is general but "may be computationally inefficient" (§3 p.7); MQM is efficient only when correlation is a Bayesian network / Markov Chain with `|Q|` small; calculating `e_Θ` and enumerating quilts is expensive — Lemma 4.6 shows searching O(T²) quilts suffices for chains, and §4.4.2 gives O(T·k³+`²) bound; Lemma 4.9 shows length `=4a*` suffices for long chains; Theorem 2.4 bounds leakage when adversary's true θ̃∉Θ by distance from Θ.

What this transfer does NOT borrow: the full Bayesian-network learning of Θ, the wavelet or Gaussian-MRF instantiations discussed in Related Work (§1.1 p.3), or the experimental utility numbers (§5) as thresholds — only the mechanism shape (W∞ / quilt / max-influence) and the time-series correlated-data framing as vocabulary for the plant.

## What is new here

Song et al. give the first implementable answer to the gap the plant's own Pufferfish note names: declaring S/Q/Θ is not enough without a mechanism that turns the declaration into calibrated noise.

Mechanism mapping onto the plant's dense queue:

- **Physical-activity time series is the plant's setting.** The paper's §1 example is verbatim the Redmi dense queue: one subject, correlated measurements across time, goal "release aggregate statistics … while hiding the evidence of an activity at any specific instant." Group DP's O(T) noise destroys utility; entry-privacy's ≈1 noise is insufficient for correlated activities (§1 p.2). The Wasserstein Mechanism interpolates between them via W∞.
- **Wasserstein (general).** For an L-Lipschitz query F (e.g., histogram of `light_class` windows, count of `dark` runs) the required Laplace scale is `W/ε` where `W` is the ∞-Wasserstein distance between output distributions under the two secrets of a pair, maximized over Q and Θ (Algorithm 1, Theorems 3.2–3.3). This generalizes Laplace: when Θ is product (independent records) and Q is neighboring databases, `W` equals global sensitivity. For the dense queue, `W` would be the earth-mover distance between histograms under "activity a at time t" vs "activity b at time t" under the Markov Chain Θ — strictly more than k's counting.
- **Markov Quilt (efficient for chains).** When Θ is a set of Markov Chains (the paper's assumption for activity data: "activities transition in a Markovian fashion" §1), correlation is local. A quilt X_Q separates remote variables X_R from Xi, so only X_N ∪ X_Q matters; its max-influence `e_Θ(X_Q|Xi)` measures residual leakage through the quilt. Noise scale is `|X_Q|/(ε - e_Θ)` minimized over SQ,i (Algorithm 2). Figure 3 pick: `X_Q={Xi-2,Xi+2}` leaves correlated window `X_N` small. Composition (Theorem 4.4) is not automatic for Pufferfish but holds for MQM when the active quilt per Xi is stable — relevant if the plant releases successive windows. Approximation Algorithm 4 with Lemma 4.9 (`=4a*`) makes search O(T·k³) not exponential.
- **Why this is distinct from t-closeness (2026-11-01 discard):** t-closeness compares per-class vs whole-table distributions via EMD with an uncalibrated t and needs a declared sensitive attribute; Wasserstein/MQM compare output distributions under two secrets via W∞/max-influence and calibrate Laplace noise to keep those secrets indistinguishable under Θ, needing declared S/Q/Θ and a Lipschitz query instead. The prior discard's limit (t's meaning unclear) is replaced here by Theorem 2.4's Θ-misspecification bound and Theorem 4.10's utility guarantee, both stating conditions explicitly.

Limits the source states and kept here: declaring the triple is a policy/content choice reviewed under `docs/protocol-boundaries.md`, not inferred; no Θ, ε, or query is specified here and no θ̃ distance, k, or Lipschitz constant is derived. Wasserstein is computationally heavy without a Bayesian-network factorization; MQM needs a Markov Chain / quilt enumeration that the plant does not have. As with t-closeness, adding the mechanism now without declaring S/Q/Θ and a query would be a gate with no input. No noise, sampling, or Bloom is added.
