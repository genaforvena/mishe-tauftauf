Four passages carry the misread this plant makes about consent, practice and a reading.

1. **Boundaries between practice and research, and when the blur triggers review (Part A).** "Practice refers to interventions that are designed solely to enhance the well-being of an individual patient or client and that have a reasonable expectation of success" vs "research designates an activity designed to test an hypothesis, permit conclusions to be drawn, and thereby to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge." Research is "usually described in a formal protocol." Crucially: "When a clinician departs in a significant way from standard or accepted practice, the innovation does not, in and of itself, constitute research… Radically new procedures … should, however, be made the object of formal research at an early stage" and "if there is any element of research in an activity, that activity should undergo review for the protection of human subjects."

2. **Respect for persons as two convictions — autonomy plus protection (Part B.1).** "Individuals should be treated as autonomous agents, and … persons with diminished autonomy are entitled to protection." An autonomous person is "capable of deliberation about personal goals and of acting under the direction of such deliberation." Lacking respect is "to repudiate that person's considered judgments, to deny an individual the freedom to act on those considered judgments, or to withhold information necessary to make a considered judgment." Capacity "matures … and some individuals lose this capacity wholly or in part because of illness, mental disability, or circumstances that severely restrict liberty" and "The judgment that any individual lacks autonomy should be periodically reevaluated."

3. **Beneficence as obligation, not charity — do not harm + maximize benefit/minimize harm (Part B.2).** "Two general rules have been formulated as complementary expressions of beneficent actions in this sense: (1) do not harm and (2) maximize possible benefits and minimize possible harms." The Hippocratic "do no harm" extended by Claude Bernard, and the problem "to decide when it is justifiable to seek certain benefits despite the risks involved, and when the benefits should be foregone because of the risks." Obligations "affect both individual investigators and society at large," including "the longer term benefits and risks that may result from the improvement of knowledge."

4. **Justice and the three applications — informed consent, risk/benefit assessment, selection (Part B.3 + C).** Justice is "fairness in distribution" and "equals ought to be treated equally" with explication (equal share / need / effort / contribution / merit). Historically "burdens … fell largely upon poor ward patients, while benefits … flowed primarily to private patients" and Tuskegee. Application C.1: informed consent requires three elements — **information, comprehension and voluntariness**; information must meet "the reasonable volunteer" standard (more than clinical care), comprehension is not satisfied by disclosure alone, voluntariness requires freedom from coercion/undue influence and "the opportunity to ask questions and to withdraw at any time"; incomplete disclosure only if (1) truly necessary, (2) no undisclosed risk > minimal, (3) debrief plan. C.2 systematic risk/benefit assessment of probability and magnitude. C.3 selection must not "systematically select … simply because of their easy availability, their compromised position, or their manipulability."

Limits the source states, kept here. Belmont is a framework, not a regulation; it does not set risk thresholds, consent-form wording, or IRB procedure — those followed as the Common Rule. Scope is "research involving human subjects" — not every operational reading, but any activity with an element of research that draws generalizable knowledge from a person.

## What is new here

**We misread Belmont as "sign a broad consent," and the report's own Part A says the harder, prior question is whether the work has crossed from practice into research — and then that respect, beneficence and justice each add an independent check that a consent string alone does not satisfy.**

- **Practice vs research is not a label, it is a trigger for review.** A daily `sense.body.redmi10` that only tells this host whether its phone link is fresh is practice (well-being of this deployment). The same stream retained in git history, counted in `research/field-review/reviews/`, plotted for validity-window tuning, or used to justify a general claim about "carried light context" is research (testing a hypothesis, contributing to generalizable knowledge, described in a formal protocol/review). Belmont's rule — any element of research in an activity means the activity should undergo review — does not ask whether the datum is sensitive; it asks whether the *use* is generalizable. The plant's blanket "including new not-yet-developed ones" treats a future microphone/geolocation/csi_presence channel as practice; Belmont treats each new channel that departs from standard practice as candidate research that should be "made the object of formal research at an early stage."

- **Informed consent is information + comprehension + voluntariness, not disclosure alone.** The mandate file discloses purpose, but Belmont requires that a reasonable volunteer would have the information needed to decide, would *comprehend* it, and would remain free to withhold or withdraw without undue influence. The module's `personal_consent_allows` is a disclosure check (does a receipt exist for this `info_type` × `recipient`?); it does not test comprehension (was the per-type scope_note understood?) and does not test voluntariness (is continued collection contingent on continued willingness, with a withdrawal path that actually stops the flow?). The Belmont passage on "periodically reevaluated" for diminished autonomy maps directly: a stale or coerced consent that is never re-asked still passes the predicate.

- **Beneficence requires a systematic risk/benefit assessment, not just "do not harm."** The current CI tables encode "is light appropriate for personal-carrying under human-consented-personal-analysis?" — a normative appropriateness judgment. Belmont adds: for each collection purpose, what is the probability and magnitude of harm (re-identification from light+accel sequences, linkage across repos, retention in git) versus the benefit (panel freshness, recovery diagnostics), and is there a less-burdensome alternative? No check in `src/` enumerates that tradeoff per information type.

- **Justice constrains selection, not just distribution.** The CI flow check gates *recipient*; Belmont's justice gates *subject selection*: are we systematically selecting this one human's device because it is available/manipulable, while benefits flow elsewhere (e.g. model judgment research)? Belmont's examples (poor ward patients, Tuskegee) are about selection bias in who bears the burden. A plant with one consenting human and one Redmi10 that feeds every research lane reproduces the exact pattern the report names unless the per-type assessment says why this subject for this question.

- **Not a new sensor or a new consent form.** Belmont (1979) predates the phone, Wi-Fi sensing and 802.11bf/bh, and it recommends no specific form. The new work is vocabulary that makes the practice/research boundary and the three Belmont applications reviewable *before* the next body channel is added. Removing a stale Note3 row or adding a constant does not satisfy it; nor does renaming the receipts.
