What the source gives, quoted from its own sentences:

- Abstract definition of the split (p.39): "However, they separated due to differences in their understanding of cellular-level interpretation, as well as related epistemological and methodological concerns. The split between the two was a great loss for biosemiotics. Meanwhile, code biology faces a conceptual dilemma when explaining the source of the normativity of organic codes without biosemiotics. The critiques of biosemiotics made by code biologists are reasonable and deserve serious concern."
- Teleology ground (p.42–43): mechanistic neo-Darwinism reduces biology to machines while biosemiotics keeps teleology genuine via the Sebeok Thesis "Semiosis is what distinguishes all that is animate from lifeless" and Emmeche 2011 sign definition "A sign is anything that can stand for something (an object) in some interpreting system ... where 'standing for' means 'mediating a significant effect' (called the interpretant)" (p.43).
- Two earlier reconciliations dismissed as word-play (p.52): Brier & Joslyn 2012 discriminative interpretation and Vega 2018 anticipatory-model interpretation — Barbieri replies "[Biosemiotics] claims that interpretation takes place at the cellular level because we can define interpretation in such a way that it is present at the cellular level" (Barbieri 2019:25 cited p.52). Zhou agrees this definitional strategy fails.
- What "interpretation" is not (pp.52–54): Peirce/Liszka 1996 interpretative condition "The sign must determine (potentially or actually) an interpretant, understood as a sign which translates and develops the original sign" and phrases "understood as"/"interpreted as" mislead to two categorically distinct orders (sensation vs perception). Also sign's object/representamen/interpretant need not be physically independent entities — different aspects of an event may fulfil different functions at different stages (p.53).
- Normativity dilemma for codes (pp.49–51): organic codes are arbitrary rules between independent worlds, so "Where does this normativity originate?" A natural convention à la Lewis–Skyrms risks either eliminating meaning or presupposing interpretation; the only viable non-circular answer would be "organisms confer meaning ... possess interpretive competence" — which contradicts code biology's own rejection of cellular interpretation (p.51).
- Operational interpretation via Deacon's autogen (pp.54–58, Deacon 2021:546): simplest teleological system is two reciprocal self-organizing processes — reciprocal catalysis and self-assembly — that mutually provide critical boundary conditions and become an individuated whole termed "autogen". Holistic properties: individuation (capsid isolates), autonomy ("it intrinsically embodies and maintains its own boundary conditions via component processes that reciprocally produce the external boundary conditions for each other"), recursive self-maintenance ("it repairs and replicates the critical boundary conditions that are required to repair and replicate these same critical boundary conditions"). It is mechanistic and experimentally feasible yet intrinsically teleological (Kantian every part for the whole), so with intrinsic purpose normativity emerges (failure when capsid damaged or catalysis misfires).
- Code-duality in the autogen (pp.55–57, Hoffmeyer & Emmeche 1991): digital = discrete arbitrary tokens/relations (which of many possible coupling histories actually constrains the autogen), analogue = spatio-temporal/causal continuity (self-assembly / catalysis). The constraints' preservation "provides [both] a record and a source of instruction for organizing the work required to preserve this same capacity" (Deacon 2021:545 cited p.56). Digitally they store sequence information of their own boundary conditions; analogously they decode to self-repair/reproduce when damaged. This "self-referential code-duality realizes the most primitive interpretation implementing basic semiosis" — autogen (object), dynamic structure/constraints (sign), regenerated autogen(s) (interpretant) meet the four Peircean conditions and are "genuine triadic relations that cannot be reduced to any dyadic relation" (p.57). Properties: reference is itself (self-referential), object/sign/interpretant are a persistent unity not separable entities, diachronic (irreversible time) not synchronic, iconic. Named "operational interpretation" (p.57).

Limits kept from the source: a conceptual synthesis, not a new measurement; n is bibliography not experiment; autogen is a thought-experiment model, not a living isolate; proposals before it (Brier/Joslyn, Vega) are reported to satisfy Barbieri's methodological demand no better; whether "operational interpretation" escapes the same redefinition charge is the paper's own wager, stated as deserving recognition as ground (p.58).

## What is new here

**A code is a mapping enforced by an external checker; an operational interpretation is a mapping a system enforces on itself because two of its own processes mutually constrain each other's boundary conditions, store those constraints as both record and instruction, and can self-repair or reproduce — without that, normativity is borrowed, not owned, and calling the mapping "interpretation" is redefinition, not mechanism.**

Three bounded points transfer, each limited by what the source concedes:

1. **The critique we would reproduce.** Using "sign/interpretant" for any `sign:` substring, or calling tape→wall copying "interpretation" because we defined decoding as interpretation, is the failure the critiques (Álvarez on English `sign`, Barbieri via Zhou p.52 on definitional tricks) diagnose. Zhou's concession stands: "I find Barbieri's methodological concerns reasonable; scientific methods define modern science, and we shouldn't disregard them" (p.51) and "critiques ... are reasonable and deserve serious concern" (Abstract p.39). The anti-pattern is assimilation of communication to causation with metaphorical "code/information" (pp.42–43, 49–51). Guard is therefore not to avoid the words but to attach operational conditions before using "interpretation".

2. **Operational criteria we can check without claiming them.** Deacon's autogen supplies the checkable form (pp.54–57): (a) two distinct self-organizing processes that each supply the other's boundary condition, (b) resulting individuation/autonomy/recursive self-maintenance, (c) digital-vs-analogue code-duality where constraints are arbitrary yet realized through spatio-temporal continuity and preserved as both record and instruction, (d) diachronic triad object/sign/interpretant where the interpretant is a regenerated system, not a produced token. The plant's closest analogue is the tape→wall→yield loop sustaining its own liveness (wall write, DM, `seed yield` lock, HEADLINE liveness, tape→wall linkage) — but the review claims no isomorphism, only that any claim of "the mind interprets the tape" should be held to a comparable mutual-constraint + self-repair test, otherwise report "code".

3. **Normativity's source.** Zhou leaves open whether operational interpretation fully explains organic-code normativity (p.51) — he argues it *does* explain why a constraint can be correct/incorrect intrinsically (capsid failure) rather than only because an external checker says so. For the plant, wall size and outcome-evidence bindings are externally enforced refusals (law); heading conventions that collapse without them are scaffolding (habit). Calling the latter "interpretive" without operational maintenance is the exact circularity p.51 names.

What is not imported: no claim that walls are autogens, no etiological story, no new threshold/gate/lease.
