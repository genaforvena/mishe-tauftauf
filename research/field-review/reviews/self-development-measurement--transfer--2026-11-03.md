- Claims relied on (verbatim or close paraphrase with page/line refs from arXiv pagination):
  - Abstract (p.1): considers "identification, estimation, and inference procedures for treatment effect parameters using Difference-in-Differences (DiD) with (i) multiple time periods, (ii) variation in treatment timing, and (iii) when the 'parallel trends assumption' holds potentially only after conditioning on observed covariates"; shows "a family of causal effect parameters are identified in staggered DiD setups, even if differences in observed characteristics create non-parallel outcome dynamics"; identification allows "outcome regression, inverse probability weighting, or doubly-robust estimands"; proposes "different aggregation schemes" and a "bootstrap procedure to conduct asymptotically valid simultaneous (instead of pointwise) inference."
  - §1 (pp.3–5): the canonical DiD has two periods and two groups; without parallel paths the ATT by "comparing the average change in outcomes experienced by the treated group to the average change ... of the comparison group" is biased. With heterogeneity, "standard TWFE linear regression specifications" mis-aggregate; the paper separates identification, aggregation and estimation/inference. Doubly-robust estimators "rely on less stringent modeling conditions than the outcome regression and the IPW procedures" (p.4). Simultaneous bands "asymptotically cover the entire path of the group-time ATTs with fixed probability and take into account the dependency across different group-time ATT estimators" and are "arguably more suitable for visualizing the overall estimation uncertainty than pointwise CIs" (pp.4–5).
  - §2.1 setup: notation with T periods, Di,t, Assumption 1 Irreversibility/Staggered Adoption (D1=0, Dt-1=1 => Dt=1) (p.8).
  - §2.2 identification core: defines the group-time average treatment effect ATT(g,t) — "the average treatment effect for group g at time t" for units first treated at g — as "the main building block" that "does not directly restrict heterogeneity" (pp.5–6; definition p.10). Identified under Assumption 3 (no anticipation, with δ anticipation window) plus one of two conditional parallel trends assumptions: Assumption 4 "Conditional Parallel Trends based on a Never-Treated Group" (p.11–12) and Assumption 5 "Conditional Parallel Trends based on Not-Yet-Treated Groups" (p.12) — both condition on covariates X. "Assumption 4 states that, conditional on covariates, the average outcomes for the treated group and the never-treated group would have followed parallel paths in the absence of treatment. Assumption 5 imposes conditional parallel trends between a treated group and not-yet-treated groups" (p.11–12). Unconditional versions are "weaker than" de Chaisemartin & D'Haultfoeuille but unconditional DiD "generally not appropriate when differences in observed characteristics create non-parallel dynamics" (pp.6,12).
  - Choice between assumptions: practitioners may favour Assumption 4 when a never-treated group exists and is "large"; otherwise favour 5 which "allows one to use more groups" but "Assumption 5 restricts observed pre-treatment trends across groups, whereas Assumption 4 does not" (pp.12–13). Increasing δ (anticipation) "become[s] stronger" (p.13) and pre-test selection between versions is discouraged.
  - §2.3/§3 aggregation (p.5): the many ATT(g,t) must be aggregated into "intuitive summary parameters" — overall ATT (similarity to canonical ATT), "how ATEs vary with length of exposure" (event-study), "how ATEs vary across groups," and "how cumulative ATEs evolve over calendar time" — with explicit trade-off of balancing in event time. This makes clear "'best' aggregation is application-specific" (p.5).
  - §4 estimation (p.4–6): outcome regression, IPW and doubly-robust estimands are "equivalent from the identification point of view" but suggest different estimators; inference via "multiplier-type bootstrap" for simultaneous bands.

## What is new here

- Callaway & Sant'Anna's transferable lesson is a checklist and an aggregation discipline for any windowed before/after count. The delta `trial - baseline` identifies an effect on the treated windows only under a parallel-trends assumption, and that assumption is more credible *conditional on* the covariates that differ between windows than unconditionally. With autocorrelated feed windows sharing a host regime, unconditional parallel trends will often fail even when conditional does not — which the report must make visible rather than assert.
- Staggered timing matters even in one plant: the trial is not two periods but T periods (the K placebo windows plus trial), with one treated window and many not-yet-treated windows. The natural unit is ATT(g,t) where g is the trial window and t runs over later sub-intervals within the trial; averaging them without weighting is the TWFE error the paper shows is not ATT. The file should therefore name an explicit aggregation (overall ATT as a weighted mean of ATT(g,t), plus an event-study view by exposure length) and state the weighting, rather than printing a single delta.
- Doubly-robust equivalence matters for fragility: outcome-regression and IPW give the same estimand but fail on different misspecifications; reporting both (or their agreement) is a robustness signal that a single unconditional mean cannot provide. Simultaneous coverage matters for the lane's current practice of printing five counts side by side: five pointwise "inside placebo range" notes without multiplicity adjustment understate joint uncertainty. The paper's multiplier bootstrap is the discipline: cover the *path* with fixed probability, not each point.
- Why this is distinct from Abadie: Abadie asks whether a gap is rare among in-space placebos and discards poorly-fit placebos; Callaway asks whether the gap is *identified* given observables and how to *aggregate* many ATT(g,t) without TWFE bias while covering them *simultaneously*. The two guards can coexist — Abadie rank for rarity, Callaway for conditional identification and joint coverage.
- Limits in the paper's own terms and ours: (a) Transfer from economics panels to feed-count windows is analogy; irreversibility (Assumption 1) does not hold for feed counts (treatment is calendar window, not adoption), so the "never-treated" group must be stipulated as placebo windows that never receive the trial; the mechanism (conditional vs unconditional, aggregation, simultaneous bands) transfers, the substance does not. (b) Conditional parallel trends is an assumption, never proved by the placebo pre-trend; the report must label it as assumed, not verified, and favouring Assumption 5 (not-yet-treated) imposes pre-treatment restrictions that Assumption 4 avoids — the choice must be stated. (c) Doubly-robust refers to model-robustness (outcome model vs propensity model), not to robustness to regime shift; a host-pressure episode still violates support. (d) No new data collection is proposed — the gap is about assumption and aggregation, not about gathering a donor pool this plant does not have.
