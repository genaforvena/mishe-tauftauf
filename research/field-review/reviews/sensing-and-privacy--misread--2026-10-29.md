## What we read

Helen Nissenbaum — **"Privacy as Contextual Integrity"**, *Washington Law Review* Vol. 79 No. 1, Symposium: Technology, Values, and the Justice System, 2-1-2004, pp. 119–159 — read full 41 pp. PDF at:

- <https://nissenbaum.tech.cornell.edu/papers/H.%20Nissenbaum,%20_Privacy%20as%20Contextual%20Integrity.pdf> (Cornell Tech copy, fetched 200 2026-10-29, pdfHarmony 2.0, 41 pp.)
- <https://digitalcommons.law.uw.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=4450&context=wlr> (UW Law Digital Commons, WLR 79:119 canonical)

Barth/Datta/Mitchell/Nissenbaum — **"Privacy and Contextual Integrity: Framework and Applications"**, Stanford/NYU 2006 preprint formalizing the 2004 construct into five parameters — read for the transmission-principle vocabulary at:

- <https://nissenbaum.tech.cornell.edu/papers/Privacy%20and%20Contextual%20Integrity%20-%20Frameworks%20and%20Applications.pdf>

Four passages carry the misread this plant makes.

1. **Contextual integrity as the benchmark — two informational norms (Abstract, p. 101; §III, p. 119ff).** "Contextual integrity ties adequate protection for privacy to norms of specific contexts, demanding that information gathering and dissemination be appropriate to that context and obey the governing norms of distribution within it." Privacy is violated when *either* norm is violated, even when the information is not secret, sensitive, or intimate. Contexts are social spheres (healthcare, friendship, workplace, marketplace) on the Walzer/Bourdieu model, each partly constituted by its norms that govern roles, expectations, and limits.

2. **Norms of appropriateness — what information is fitting to reveal in a context ( §III.A).** "Norms of appropriateness dictate what information about persons is appropriate, or fitting, to reveal in a particular context." Examples: in medical contexts the patient shares physical condition with the physician but not vice versa; among friends romantic entanglements are fitting, with an employer religious affiliation is not. "There is no place not governed by at least some informational norms" — even the most public square has an appropriate "none of your business." Appropriateness is context-relative, variable in explicitness and restrictiveness, and is not the same fact as sensitivity.

3. **Norms of flow/distribution — who may move what to whom ( §III.B, via Walzer *Spheres of Justice* 1983).** "In addition to appropriateness, another set of norms govern what I will call flow or distribution of information — movement, or transfer of information from one party to another." Building on Walzer's distinct distributive spheres each with its own justice criteria, distribution is not a free copy: friendship defaults to discretion/confidentiality and subject choice; healthcare defaults to physician-need with unidirectional flow and HIPAA-constrained redistribution; marketplace defaults to payment/address need. A privacy violation may occur without any inappropriate information type, solely because the *distribution* crossed a sphere boundary.

4. **Transmission principles and the multi-parameter judgment (§III, §III.D; Barth et al. §2 formalization).** Nissenbaum 2004 argues a complaint is sound only by "painstaking analysis of details (or *facts*)" of the governing norms; Barth et al. make the parameters explicit: *context, actors (subject/sender/recipient), information type (attribute), and transmission principle* (consent, confidentiality, need, entitlement, obligation, etc.). Altering even one — e.g., moving public records from courthouse-drawer access to whole-web broadcast (Case 1), or aggregating credit-card trails into Axciom profiles (Case 2), or attaching an RFID-readable chip to a sweater (Case 3) — changes the distribution pattern and is the locus of the privacy question, not whether the bits were formally public. "Whether the action breaches contextual integrity" is the question, not whether the data was marked private beforehand.

The paper's stated limits: it offers a justificatory framework, not specific policies; scope is "information about people" for individual, identifiable persons (not group/institutional privacy or constitutional abortion privacy); and even a breach may be justified when a more serious value is at stake. It predates smartphone body sensors, Wi-Fi sensing, and 802.11bf.

## What this plant already does

*The plant knows a reading is not a person, treats blanket human consent as sufficient, and guards publication — but it has no per-context appropriateness or flow vocabulary, so every sensor flow looks alike.*

- **Host-state decoding with an honest non-inference line.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/host_radio.py:1` decodes "this host's radio, wired-network and tailnet adapter state" from sysfs/`bluetoothctl` and "never mutates a radio"; `src/mishe_tauftauf/body_perception.py:134` `read_redmi10()` returns "one bounded, receipt-fresh reading; never infer human movement" and `docs/how-it-works.md:713` "Acceleration magnitude includes gravity; it is device evidence, not a human movement, presence or continuous-carrying claim. The human's carrying context remains contextual, not sensor-certified." The consumer stores phone-sample vs receipt time separately and keeps `UNKNOWN` for missing/stale/future without reusing last-known values (`body_perception.py:71` `_row_status`, `docs/how-it-works.md:702`).

- **A blanket personal-analysis consent without transmission principles.** `.mishe-tauftauf/research/shared/personal-multibody-analysis-20261015.md:5` records the human's 2026-10-15 consent for analysis "about themselves from authorized sensed evidence" across "all authorized bodies and senses, including new not-yet-developed ones", with the charter note "This consent concerns the human, not bystanders." The implementation, however, is a single mandate: Redmi10 `sense.body.redmi10` (battery/light/accel + Wi-Fi/BLE auxiliary), Note3 light, host radio, and gap logs each publish to any reader that can open `SITE/body/perception/redmi10.json` or `SITE/body/space.json`. No per-flow `transmission principle` (consented personal analysis vs confidential-to-operator vs need-for-health vs public-by-owner) is carried beside the reading, and no appropriateness table says which information type fits which context.

- **Publication and wall retention as context-collapsing channels.** `docs/protocol-boundaries.md:224` `outcome_publication.py` treats a wall outcome as a reserved-header + prose + single immutable record reference; `docs/how-it-works.md:239` chat tape shows every wall, DM, and discovery event to every attached mind. In CI terms this is a single broadcast distribution: a Redmi `L=105lux |a|=9.84m/s² incl-g` that was appropriate in the *carrying-device* context (subject=human, sender=phone, recipient=senses, principle=human-consented-personal-analysis) becomes identically available to `discover`, `health`, `genome`, `witness`, and to any `wall outcome` that quotes it, without a distributional check. The recent WLAN-sensing review (`sensing-and-privacy--recent`, `host_radio.py:25` `WLAN_SENSING_SUPPORTED=False`) correctly refused to *collect* person-through-radio, but left the *redistribution* of already-collected personal-body readings undecorated.

- **The "privacy" vocabulary is about posting, not about spheres.** `rg -n "privacy"` shows `docs/how-it-works.md:493` "privacy limits" in the Pub charter and `fields.json:62` the field name itself; there is no occurrence of "appropriateness", "transmission principle", "contextual integrity", or "sender/recipient/subject" in `src/mishe_tauftauf/body_perception.py` or `host_radio.py`. The plant therefore applies a sensitivity model (is the datum sensitive/public?) where Nissenbaum requires a CI model (is this flow appropriate for this context under this principle?).

- **The same 30 s datum has different readings per context already, without naming the contexts.** `body_perception.py:14` `FRESH_SECONDS=30.0` and `docs/how-it-works.md:702` make `sense.body.redmi10` current only when receipts are within 30 s; `host_radio.py` uses `COMMAND_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=10.0` with different UNKNOWN words (`rfkill_unreadable`, `bluetoothctl-timeout`). These are liveness gates, not appropriateness gates: a fresh, correctly timed battery `1.0+` that is appropriate for `health` diagnosis may be inappropriate for `publication-and-attribution` DEV posting, but both gates would call it fresh.

## What is new here

**The plant misreads Nissenbaum as "privacy = keep sensitive bits secret / do not sense a person." The paper's claim is "privacy = appropriate information stays in its context and flows only on its principle" — and that reframes every Redmi/host reading the plant already collects.**

- **Sensitivity vs appropriateness.** Under the misread, `lux=105` and `|a|=9.84` are "not sensitive" and therefore safe to move anywhere once consented. Under CI, they are device evidence about a person collected in the *personal-carrying* context whose appropriateness is already narrowed by the plant's own line ("not a human movement claim") — yet the reading remains about the human's carried device in a time/place. Whether sharing the pair `(lux, accel, 2026-10-29T18:02Z)` with `inference-research` for longitudinal analysis is appropriate is a different question from whether sharing it with `pub` for a DEV article quote is appropriate. One information type, two contexts, two answers.

- **Collection vs distribution.** The `WLAN_SENSING_SUPPORTED=False` boundary guards *collection* (do not solicit CSI through walls). Nissenbaum's Cases 1–3 are distribution violations *without new collection*: public court records become a privacy problem when moved from courthouse-drawer access to whole-web broadcast; credit-card trails become one when shipped to Axciom for profiling; an already-attached RFID becomes one when it broadcasts to any hallway scanner. Analogously, a Redmi light transition `measured-light-transition=2->4 validity=300s` that was appropriately produced for *personal analysis* becomes a CI breach when it is quoted verbatim in a wall outcome that is git-committed, pushed, and readable as `HEAD` — the distribution, not the sensing, moved it across spheres.

- **Blanket consent vs transmission principle.** The 2026-10-15 mandate's "including new not-yet-developed ones" reads as consent for any future sensor under the same blanket, whereas Nissenbaum requires a principle per flow: "human-consented-personal-analysis confined to operator context and not republished", "confidential to health for diagnosis", "public by explicit per-item disclosure", "need for health watchdog", etc. Without the principle carried beside the reading, a later mind cannot tell whether quoting the reading in `research/field-review/reviews/` or in a DEV comment respects the original grant.

- **The plant has the five-parameter shape but does not carry it.** The needed tuple already has sources: *context* can be named per sense (personal-carrying vs host-radio vs publication), *subject* is the consenting human vs the host, *sender* is the phone/host, *recipient* is the role that consumed the reading, *information type* is `battery`/`light`/`acceleration`/`wifi_link_rssi_dbm`/`ble_scan`, *transmission principle* is the mandate-derived label. Today only `(SENSE_ID, source, site, channel)` are published.

- **Not a second sensing stack.** The 2024 CSI-fuzzing paper read for the recent cell assumes a CSI source to perturb; there is none, so the defence is moot. Here the new work is not a sensor at all — it is vocabulary that makes an existing personal-body flow reviewable before a future sensor is added. Removing a warning or renaming stale Note3 data does not satisfy it.

## Application

target: src/mishe_tauftauf/body_perception.py

**Make contextual integrity a typed, testable fact beside the existing Redmi decoders — pure, additive vocabulary that names appropriateness and flow without changing any `sense.*` verdict, without new I/O, and without publishing a person claim.**

1. **Add module-level contextual-integrity vocabulary beside `SENSE_ID`/`FRESH_SECONDS` in `src/mishe_tauftauf/body_perception.py`.** New symbols (names illustrative, additive only): `PERSONAL_CONTEXTS: Final[frozenset[str]]` (e.g. `"personal-carrying"`, `"host-radio"`, `"host-network"`, `"publication"`, `"health-diagnosis"`), `BODY_INFORMATION_TYPES: Final[frozenset[str]]` (e.g. `"battery"`, `"light"`, `"acceleration"`, `"wifi_link_rssi_dbm"`, `"ble_scan"`), `TRANSMISSION_PRINCIPLES: Final[frozenset[str]]` (e.g. `"human-consented-personal-analysis"`, `"confidential-to-operator"`, `"need-for-health-diagnosis"`, `"public-by-explicit-disclosure"`). Docstrings cite Nissenbaum 2004 §§III–III.B and Barth et al. §2 and enumerate the five CI parameters (context, sender/recipient/subject, information type, transmission principle), stating that a flow is appropriate only when *both* the context's appropriateness norm and its distribution norm are upheld.

2. **Add two pure predicates beside `read_redmi10`/`read_redmi10_light` with no sysfs/daemon I/O.** `is_body_info_appropriate_for_context(context: str, info_type: str) -> bool` (appropriateness check per the table in the module docstring) and `contextual_integrity_allows(*, context: str, sender: str, recipient: str, subject: str, info_type: str, transmission_principle: str) -> bool` (conjunction of appropriateness and flow). Both are pure views over the typed vocab; they publish no `GREEN`/`RED`/`UNKNOWN`, add no `SENSE_ID` row, and are callable without a `home` or `now`. An additional helper `personal_body_flow_allows(info_type, recipient, transmission_principle)` may specialise the general predicate for the Redmi personal-carrying context, decoding the 2026-10-15 human consent as the principle `"human-consented-personal-analysis"` confined to non-public recipients.

3. **Document the negative/flowing boundary in the module docstring and per-symbol docstrings.** The header that already says "never infer human movement" gains one sentence naming Nissenbaum WLR 79:119 contextual integrity (appropriateness + flow/distribution, Walzer *Spheres of Justice*) as the privacy lens and stating that any redistribution of `sense.body.redmi10` beyond its originating context's transmission principle is a privacy-boundary change that must be reviewed under `docs/protocol-boundaries.md` and `.mishe-tauftauf/research/shared/personal-multibody-analysis-20261015.md`. Each new symbol's docstring reproduces the paper's line that "either norm violated is a breach," so a reviewer can see the closed world being refused.
