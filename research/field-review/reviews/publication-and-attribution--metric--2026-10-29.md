Six passages carry the metric, each with the limits the source states.

1. **TOP 2015: eight standards × three levels, and the Summary Table (Nosek 1.0.1 pp.1–2, Table p. 12).** Eight transparency standards — (1) Citation, (2) Data, (3) Analytic methods (code), (4) Research materials, (5) Design and analysis, (6) Preregistration of studies, (7) Preregistration of analysis plans, (8) Replication — each with Level 1 *Disclose* ("authors state whether…"), Level 2 *Require* ("must post to trusted repository…"), Level 3 *Verify* ("journal verifies…"). The TOP Guidelines Summary table lists all eight × three cells (p. 12): a journal may adopt any subset. Level language in TOP 2015 was `disclose, require, verify` and did not apply consistently across standards (Grant p. 3).

2. **TOP Factor as the area's concrete metric, and its scale (Grant pp. 2–3; Nosek pp. 1–2).** Introduced 2020 by COS, TOP Factor rated journal policies against TOP 2015 on an ordinal scale. Over **3,000 journal policies were scored** (COS site: "Over 3,000 journal policies were scored") and data were made available for download. Grant p. 2: scored journals ranged across the 8 × 3 grid; a contemporaneous cross-sectional study reports "Out of a total possible score of **29**, journal scores ranged from 1 to 13 (median = 6)" (Tandfonline abstract, citing TOP Factor rubric). The metric is a sum over standards × levels — a single number per journal, per time point.

3. **What the metric was supposed to measure, and the three-level semantics for Data/Code/Materials (Nosek pp. 4–5).** Level 1: published article states whether data/code/materials are available and where. Level 2: data/code/materials must be posted to a trusted repository (Data-PASS/institutional) with persistent identifier, exceptions identified at submission. Level 3: journal (or agent on its behalf) verifies that findings are replicable using author's data and methods. Cost increases with level; the choice is "milder to stronger transparency" (p. 2) — the paper is explicit that adoption is modular and readiness-dependent, not universally mandatory.

4. **Why TOP Factor was retired — validity failure of the cumulative score (Grant pp. 2–3, 6).** "In 2020, COS introduced the TOP Factor, a separate initiative that rated journal policies based on TOP 2015. **Some audiences conflated TOP Factor with TOP 2015**. TOP 2015 specified that appropriate standards depend on study design and other factors, whereas **TOP Factor provided a simplistic cumulative score across all standards**. Additionally, later evidence indicated **limitations in the reliability and validity of TOP Factor scores due to challenges in interpreting policy alignment** [9]." And p. 6: "COS has **discontinued the TOP Factor**." The critique is not that journals disliked scoring, but that summing ordinal levels across standards with different applicability presupposed what TOP 2015 denied — that more stringency is always better and comparable across designs — so the sum anticorrelated with the construct (verifiability for that design).

5. **TOP 2025 reorganization: Research Practices, Verification Practices, Verification Studies and the corrected level vocabulary (Grant p. 3, Table 1 pp. 4–5).** TOP 2025 replaces the 8 into **seven Research Practices** — (1) Study Registration, (2) Study Protocols, (3) Analysis Plans, (4) Reporting Transparency, (5) Materials Transparency, (6) Data Transparency, (7) Analytic Code Transparency — plus **two Verification Practices** (Computational Reproducibility, Results Transparency) and **four Verification Studies** (Replication, Registered Reports, Multiverse, Many Analysts). Implementation categories are renamed to **Disclosed / Shared and Cited / Certified** (Grant Table 1). `Disclosed` = researchers state whether they performed the practice and where; `Shared and Cited` = output publicly shared and cited with persistent unique identifier; `Certified` = a party independent from the researchers confirms sharing per best practice and timing (e.g., WHO Trial Registration Data Set, comparison of registration/protocol/plan vs final report for selective reporting). Verification Practices each require multiple Research Practices — e.g., Computational Reproducibility needs data+code+materials — so they are not independent sums (Grant p. 3). The reorganization explicitly separates "a practice's output exists" from "someone checked it."

6. **The metric TOP 2025 keeps and the judgment it refuses to collapse (Grant p. 6, Top Factor retirement note; p. 3 conceptual framework Fig. 1).** TOP 2025's objective is "improving the **verifiability** of empirical research claims" (Fig. 1). It notes verifiability is **only one indicator of credibility**, that **strong and weak research can both be transparent** and that transparency is not rigor: "TOP 2025 is not intended to suggest that verifiability is the most important determinant of research quality" (p. 6). The metric is therefore reported **per practice, per level, per study design**, not as a single journal score. Journals, funders, preprint servers and evidence curators are expected to tailor implementation — the shared vocabulary, not the sum, is the experiment's readout. Grant p. 6: modular, study-centric, context-sensitive, strengths-based.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** Field `publication-and-attribution` is "the reader book, the DEV blog, commits, CI and pushes" and angle is *metric* — what the area measures about its own publishing practice. TOP Factor is the recent, real metric that scored how publications keep their promises about data, code and preregistration; Grant et al. 2026 is the 2025 update that retires that metric for cause and replaces it with a disaggregated, per-practice measurement. Exactly the publishing-and-attribution question the plant trades on for its DEV articles and outcomes without yet having a number.

## What this plant already does

*The plant counts publications and checks their prose, but has never scored a publication's transparency at the level of the practices that would let a reader verify its claim.*

- **Wall-outcome publications have a typed digest but no transparency level.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/outcome_publication.py:71-91` defines `OUTCOME_KINDS`, `_HEADER/_DIGEST` (`[0-9a-f]{64}` hard binding), `DISCLOSURE_LEVELS` and `MANIFEST_STATES` (C2PA vocab added by the `recent` and `critique` landings) and pure helpers `provenance_data_hash / provenance_ingredient / provenance_manifest_summary / provenance_validation_state` that answer "ingredient digest matches" and "binding is Well-Formed/Valid/Trusted." They do not answer "does this publication disclose whether its data exists, share that data with a citation, or have its claim certified by an independent party" — the TOP level vocabulary.

- **DEV/blog publications have editorial promise without a transparency disclosure.** `docs/how-it-works.md:488-495` and `src/mishe_tauftauf/seed_docs_charter.md` give `pub` ownership of DEV articles with "honest agent voice" and mesh attribution. `checks/pub-devto-watch-delivery.py` watches that delivery can lose a notification, not whether the article's claim cites its ingredients (record digests, scan receipts, commit SHAs) with persistent identifiers. No `TOP`-like `Disclosed / Shared and Cited / Certified` label is recorded per article or per outcome.

- **`trial_metrics.collect()` already aggregates publications per window — without a transparency profile.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/trial_metrics.py:25-91` `collect(home, start, *, end)` counts `redeliveries / observations / addressed_messages / legacy_task_transitions` by prefix, lifecycles `wake/settlement`, `by_source/by_role`, `post-checks/*.json` reports, `health/services.json`, and `outcomes` (`accepted / blocker-resolved / …` via `wall.outcome`) plus `references` and `verified / incomplete / latency` from `patches/*.json`. The returned dict carries `hours`, `counts` and `limits` warnings ("Settlements and messages are not productivity… Outcomes are author reports with digest-checked evidence references, not independent semantic acceptance") but no per-publication transparency score or per-practice breakdown. An outcome that cites a handled digest and one that cites nothing both count as one.

- **The post-check question banks judge prose and selection/handoff pitfalls, not research-practice verifiability.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/pitfall_questions.py:1-40` defines `POST` `R01–R08` (readability: can the reader understand without opening an artifact, is evidence explained, is prose consistent with structured event, etc.) and `PITFALLS` `P01–P28` (selection/handoff cycles, ownership, retry edges). `src/mishe_tauftauf/post_check.py:206` `review(home, source, body, context, stage)` routes `stage=post/selection/handoff` to those banks and caches by `body+context+config+gate/bank+worker/executable/checkpoint`. `R03` ("Is evidence explained by meaning rather than opaque hashes alone?") and `R06` are the closest to Data Transparency, but no question asks whether materials/data/code that underwrite the claim are *disclosed as available*, *shared and cited with a persistent identifier*, or *certified by an independent party* — the three levels TOP measures.

- **The plant has the inputs to score the levels and does not.** Every outcome's `evidence` carries `{"path": str, "sha256": hex64}` (the `decode_evidence` shape) and the tape carries the path's bytes; every wall publication carries a final `[record]` digest line; every scan receipt carries a `created` stamp and the checked rows. These are exactly the artifacts TOP would ask to be disclosed/shared/cited/certified — and the plant's checks already touch them (`checks/ledger-band-second-reader.py`, `checks/coverage-gap-remedy.py`, `checks/coverage-window-expiry.py`) without grading their transparency.

*Consequence the source predicts.* When the plant reports `outcomes=12, verified=3` for a week, a reader cannot distinguish a week in which every outcome cited its ingredients with persistent digests from one in which none did; the count is the same, and the `TOP Factor` mistake — collapsing a multidimensional transparency state into one sum — is reproduced inside `trial_metrics` as a single per-window count.

## What is new here

**The area already tried the metric this plant would invent — a cumulative sum over all practices — and retired it for cause; the replacement is a disaggregated, per-practice, per-level profile whose levels the plant can already observe.**

Three ideas transfer directly; each keeps the limits Grant et al. state.

1. **Seven Research Practices as publication-level facts (Grant Table 1, Nosek pp. 3–5) — and which three the plant trivially implements.** *Study Registration* (was the outcome/wake registered as a claim before work, e.g. a wall goal or `task` entry?), *Study Protocol* (was the method — the check name, wall charter, or discovery probe — published and cited?), *Analysis Plan* (was the question bank version `pitfall_questions.VERSION` and its pre/post stage cited?), *Reporting Transparency* (was an `R01–R08` checklist followed — `R03`'s "evidence explained" is the reporting item), *Materials Transparency* (was the bound file named in `eligible_evidence_path` shared?), *Data Transparency* (was the evidence path's bytes with `sha256` shared and cited via `[record]`/`evidence` digest?), *Analytic Code Transparency* (was the code identity — commit SHA or `checks/*.py` path — shared and cited?). For this plant the last three are the cheapest: the `evidence.path + sha256` pair is already the `Data/Materials` disclosure; adding `commit SHA` proximity is the `Code` disclosure. TOP 2015 Level 2's "trusted repository" becomes `SITE_HOME` plus git blob/branch for this plant; Level 3's "journal verifies replication" becomes `checks/ledger-band-second-reader.py` re-hashing the bound file — an independent reader, not the author's prose promise.

2. **The three levels as a measurement, not a mandate — Disclosed / Shared and Cited / Certified (Grant Table 1, pp. 4–5) — with the `Certified` meaning the plant can actually meet.** *Disclosed* = the publication states whether the practice's output exists and, if so, where (Nosek Level 1). *Shared and Cited* = the output is publicly shared (path under `SITE_HOME` or commit) and cited in the publication with its persistent identifier (`sha256` hex64 or commit SHA) — Grant p. 4 "with a permanent unique identifier." *Certified* = a party independent from the author confirms sharing per best practice and timing (Grant Table 1). For this plant `Certified` is exactly `checks/ledger-band-second-reader.py` or `checks/field-review-contract.py` confirming the bound file's hash matches the cited digest — a mechanical certification the plant already runs as a scheduled check.

3. **Why the sum must not be published — the TOP Factor retirement as a guardrail (Grant pp. 2–3, p. 6).** Grant p. 2: TOP Factor's "simplistic cumulative score across all standards" and its "limitations in the reliability and validity … due to challenges in interpreting policy alignment" caused conflation of TOP Factor with TOP itself; COS discontinued it. The transfer therefore makes the **profile** — seven levels plus two verification flags — the metric, and any sum (`total_score 0–14` etc.) a derived projection that must be labeled "TOP Factor-style, retired for cause" and never the primary statement. Aggregation hides that `Data=Certified` but `Analysis Plan=Not Disclosed` is a different claim than the reverse — Grant's "strengths-based" framing p. 6. The plant that publishes DEV articles by aggregating `trial_metrics` counts into one number would reproduce the area's known failure mode.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** No journal is scored; the unit is a *study* (here, one outcome publication, one DEV article, one scan receipt), not a journal's policy. Material/data/code availability is scored per publication, not per repository — a missing artifact for one claim is not a property of the branch. Certification requires an independent party — the author's own `check_parsing.py PASS` is `Shared and Cited` at best, not `Certified`. Exceptions (sovereignty, legal, ethical, proprietary) remain legitimate disclosures that lower the bar for that practice rather than invalidating the publication — Grant p. 5 "make appropriate exceptions." And verifiability is not rigor: a fabricated row cited correctly can be `Certified` and still be false — the `R01–R08 / P01–P28` checks judge meaning, the TOP profile judges whether the inputs to that judgment were made available — Grant p. 6 "transparency is not synonymous with nor a substitute for methodological rigor."

3. **Wire the measurement where `collect()` already aggregates publications, as additive fields the published window can carry.** After the existing `outcomes / references / verified / incomplete / latency` aggregation (`:54-83`), add an additive `transparency` aggregation over the same `entries` that produced `outcomes` — count publications per practice per level, and include the per-publication profile digest so a second reader can recompute: `{"by_practice": {practice: Counter(level)}, "by_level": Counter(level), "profiles": [profile, …]}` with the `TOP_FACTOR_RETIRED` projection alongside as `{"top_factor_retired": {"score": …, "note": …}}` (never the primary number). No tape read beyond `Feed.entries()` that `collect()` already holds; no network fetch; the `Certified` flag is set only when the entry's cited digest was independently re-hashed (the second-reader pattern the plant already has in `checks/ledger-band-second-reader.py`).

4. **Document the measurement contract in the module docstring and per-symbol docstrings.** The header that already describes `collect()` gains citations for `Grant 2026 Research Integrity Peer Review 11:40 Table 1 / Fig.1 / pp.2-3 retired factor / p.6 verifiability≠rigor`, `Nosek 2015 Version 1.0.1 pp.1-5 / Table p.12 eight×three`, and `COS TOP Guidelines 2025 https://www.cos.io/initiatives/top-guidelines (80,226 B, 2026-10-29)`, so that quoting a DEV article or outcome without disclosing whether its data/code/protocol are available, shared and cited, or certified is visibly a transparency omission — not a neutral choice — and quoting a single sum without the `TOP_FACTOR_RETIRED` note is visibly the retired metric.
