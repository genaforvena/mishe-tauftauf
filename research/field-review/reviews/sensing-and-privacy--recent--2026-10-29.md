Supplemental 2024–2025 privacy-defence source read for the critique boundary (not the primary sensing definition):

- Zhong et al. — **"Privacy Protection in WiFi Sensing via CSI Fuzzing"**, *Proc. 2024 IEEE/ACM Symposium on Edge Computing (SEC '24)* — <https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/SEC62691.2024.00045> — CSI obfuscation as the deployed countermeasure to the sensing procedure.

Four passages carry the privacy boundary this plant lacks.

1. **WLAN sensing as a first-class MAC procedure with named roles and CSI as the only report (§I, §III.A–C).** The amendment adds a *WLAN sensing procedure* in license-exempt bands below 7 GHz (plus DMG 60 GHz variant) with two role pairs — sensing *initiator/responder* (who starts vs who replies) and sensing *transmitter/receiver* (who sends the sounding PPDU vs who measures) — and a staged procedure: Sensing Session Setup (capability exchange), Sensing Measurement Setup (MSID + Operational Parameters), Sensing Measurement Instance(s) (the packet exchanges), Setup Termination, Session Termination (Fig. 3). Every measurement instance is either Trigger-Based (AP-initiated, multistatic-capable, polling + NDPA + TF sounding + reporting) or Non-Trigger-Based (client-initiated, single-AP responder). Reuse of Null Data PPDUs (NDPs) "allows to avoid any modifications to the PHY" (§III.A fn.3) while CSI — "the channel frequency response between each transmit and each receive antenna" quantized to 8/10 bits with 1/4/8/16 subcarrier grouping (§III.C) — is "the only sensing measurement report available. [10] specifies…" and "the only measurement considered. Thus, it is also the only report" (fn.4).

2. **Range resolution, dense deployment, and the bistatic/multistatic requirement (§II.B.1–2).** WLAN sensing range resolution is 0.5–10 m (`r_res = c/(2B)`, Fig. 1), coarser than DMG/UWB/FMCW, and is recovered only "by exploiting Wi-Fi dense deployment" and "intelligently combining low-resolution measurements obtained with multiple spatially diverse devices" — 20% motion-classification gain with two devices [13]. Hence "A Wi-Fi sensing protocol must thus support bistatic and multistatic architecture[s]" (p. 2) — the amendment's reason for TB multistatic. Sensing accuracy is simultaneously tied to SINR and to joint sensing-and-communication scheduling ("sensing measurements can be seen solely as overhead", §II.B.3) and to interference that "cannot be avoided" (§II.B.2).

3. **Client-sensing asymmetry and sensing-by-proxy / proxy AP enhancements (§V).** A client in bistatic mode sees less than an AP that can poll many clients (multistatic). The D0.5 draft therefore defines three enhancements: *sensing by proxy* (an AP senses on behalf of a non-AP STA), *passive client sensing* (AP shares measurements it obtained), and *proxy STA sensing* (§V.B–D) — all ways to give a client the AP's multistatic view without the client itself driving the sounding. The sensing initiator may be either AP or client; the measurement reporter is only "a sensing responder acting as a sensing receiver or both sensing receiver and sensing transmitter" (§III.A).

4. **What the paper says about privacy, and what it leaves for the boundary.** The introduction frames Wi-Fi sensing as privacy-preserving *relative to video*: "As opposed to video-based sensing, wireless sensing … preserves user's privacy" and works "in non-line-of-sight conditions" with "wider coverage areas" (§I). The same property is the new privacy risk: a standardized, interoperable, device-free presence/motion sensor that works through walls and in the dark, with CSI exchange that is now a defined MAC capability negotiated in Session Setup and reported in quantised CSI reports. The amendment itself is not a privacy control; IEEE's parallel privacy amendments are P802.11bh (randomized/changing MAC, 802.11bh-2024) and P802.11bi (Enhanced Service with Privacy Protection) — both listed on <https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.11bf/11574/> — while practical defences such as CSI fuzzing/obfuscation (SEC '24, *Computers & Security* 157 Oct 2025) perturb the very CSI reports the procedure was designed to make interoperable.

**Limits the source states, kept here.** The paper describes TGbf D0.5 (April 2022 draft, Letter Ballots Jan/Sep 2023, ratification expected Sep 2024 per fn.1 p.1 — later ratified as 802.11bf-2025, 2025-09 publication). CSI is the only defined report; RSSI is prior work, not the standard. The sensing-vs-communication tradeoff, interference, and single-device vs multi-device accuracy limits are stated as out-of-scope signal-processing, not solved by the MAC. No consent, retention, or disclosure rule is defined in the procedure; capability exchange is the only negotiation point.

## What is new here

**Name the WLAN sensing procedure as the concrete recent result that the host-radio boundary must be measured against, and make the plant's refusal to perform it a typed, testable fact in `host_radio.py`.**

802.11bf D0.5 turns an ordinary Wi-Fi chipset into an interoperable, device-free sensor network: any STA that negotiates a sensing Session Setup can solicit NDPA/TF sounding and receive quantised CSI reports — through walls, in the dark, without the sensed person carrying a device or associating to the observer's network (monostatic = self-sounding, bistatic = one peer, multistatic = many peers; §II.A–B). Until D0.5 this required proprietary stacks; after D0.5/802.11bf-2025 it is a standard MAC capability with an explicit capability exchange the peer can see. The paper's "preserves privacy vs video" line does not survive this step: a bistatic AP-client exchange is precisely one that the sensed person's device need not participate in, which is why the amendment needed companion privacy work (802.11bh randomized MAC, 802.11bi ESP) and why 2024–2025 defences resort to perturbing CSI at the source (SEC '24 fuzzing; *Comput. & Security* 157).

For this plant the distinction is: *reading the host's own radio state* (powered, up, carrier, rfkill, admin-up — `host_radio.py`'s current row) versus *reading a person through a radio* (presence, motion, respiration, count). The plant intentionally does only the first; 802.11bf standardises the second. The new guarantee to make explicit is therefore negative: `host_radio` does not implement Sensing Session Setup, does not send or solicit NDP/NDPA/TF, does not exchange or decode CSI, does not store an MSID, does not support bistatic/multistatic roles, and does not treat a CSI report as evidence. Framing it as a bearing from the paper's Figs. 2–3 and §III.B–C makes future drift checkable: any import of a CSI decoder, any NDP-sounding call, or any MSID-handling path is a privacy-boundary change that must be reviewed as such, not a routine host-state extension.

The metric the review applies is binary and local: the module's WLAN-sensing capability is `False`, and the repository's CSI vocabulary footprint is zero — the same style as the `AoI` warmup-None and the `congruence` denominator-None signals. A second consumer (a boundary check or a future `checks/host-radio-privacy.py` reporter) can re-plug the same constant without the producer learning to sense.
