## What we read

**Metastable Failures in the Wild** — Lexiang Huang, Matthew Magnusson, Abishek Bangalore Muralikrishna, Salman Estyak, Rebecca Isaacs, Abutalib Aghayev, Timothy Zhu, Aleksey Charapko (Penn State, UNH, Twitter). *Proceedings of the 16th USENIX Symposium on Operating Systems Design and Implementation (OSDI '22)*, Carlsbad, CA, July 11–13, 2022, pp. 73–90 — read full text at:

- <https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi22-huang-lexiang.pdf> (PDF, 18 pp., USENIX open access, 904 KB, fetched 2026-11-02)
- <https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/huang-lexiang> (presentation page)
- Companion vision paper: <https://sigops.org/s/conferences/hotos/2021/papers/hotos21-s11-bronson.pdf> (Bronson et al., HotOS '21, *Metastable Failures in Distributed Systems*, pp. 221–227 — framework Huang extends)

Four passages carry the critique this plant would reproduce if it adopted bounded retry without metastability awareness.

1. **Metastable state defined — stuck in overload after the trigger is gone (§1, p. 73; §3.3, Def. 3–4, p. 77).** Bronson defines *metastable failure state* as "permanent overload with ultra-low goodput" where a system in a *vulnerable* state (high load but coping absent a transient) is tipped by a trigger into a state that a *sustaining effect* — work amplification from a common-case optimization — keeps overloaded even after the trigger is removed. Huang extends the model to two trigger types (load-spike vs capacity-decreasing) and two amplification types (workload amplification vs capacity-degradation amplification) with symbols `L_sys(t), C_sys(t), L_org(t), C_org(t), a_L(t), a_C(t), w_L, w_C` (Table 2, p. 75).

2. **Retries are the dominant sustaining effect in the wild (§2.2, p. 74, Table 1).** Of 22 wild metastable incidents across AWS, GCP, Azure, IBM and others, "by far, the most common sustaining effect is due to the retry policy, affecting more than 50% of the studied incidents — GGL2, GGL3, AWS1, AWS2, AWS3, AZR2, AZR4, IBM1, SPF1, SPF2, and CAS2." The failure is not a bug in one service but an emergent feedback loop: timeout → retry → more load → more timeout. The paper stresses 45% of triggers have multiple causes and that multiple triggers were common (GGL1, GGL3, AWS3, AZR3, etc.).

3. **Vulnerable is not binary — duration matters (§3, p. 75; §3.2–3.4, Fig. 1, p. 76).** Whether a vulnerable system tips into metastability depends on the triple `(vulnerability degree, trigger magnitude m_trig, trigger duration D_t_trig)`. Amplification upper bounds `w_L(D_t_trig), w_C(D_t_trig)` are monotone in `D_t_trig` from 1 to `w_L, w_C`. Workload amplification has an *amplification delay* (requests must first timeout, then clients retry) — a short timeout "can hurt the system's ability to handle larger problems by quickly starting the workload amplification" (p. 77, AWS2 handshake-timeout example). The four Fig.1 scenarios show how a cache failure (capacity-degrading trigger) leaves the DB overloaded because "all operations effectively fail, preventing the system from filling the cache."

4. **Recovery requires shedding load, not adding it — and what the papers concede (§2.2, p. 74; §6, p. 80–82).** "Handling and recovering from metastable failures is not easy … often requires reducing load. Direct load shedding, such as throttling, dropping requests, or changing workload parameters, was used in over 55% of the cases." Fixes without understanding the feedback worsen it (§6.3). Limits kept: the study is retrospective over public incident reports (selection bias toward severe, well-documented outages; gray-highlighted incidents lack explicit sustaining-effect evidence). The model does not give a tight `w_L` bound or a universal vulnerable threshold; Bronson/Huang call systematic detection "open problem." Capacity-degradation via GC is insider-observed at Twitter (§4) and reproduced in synthetic apps (§5, open-sourced at github.com/lexiangh/Metastability) — not measured in this plant's wake workload.

## What this plant already does

*Bounded retry that is correct in isolation, vulnerable in composition.* `src/mishe_tauftauf/native_recovery.py` classifies one failed wake from its own bytes: `classify_attempt()` (:374) checks `summary.json`, journal rows and three ledger journals (`_read_evidence` :278), checks `failure.txt` completeness (`_failure_record` :200) and decides `SETTLED`/`RETRYABLE`/`RECONCILE`/`UNKNOWN` (:92–95) with `TRANSIENT_STATUS = {408,429} ∪ [500,599]` (:99) and `DEFAULT_RETRY_LIMIT=3` (:100). `fresh_attempt()` returns the next unused attempt directory or `None` at the limit (:489–502). `attempt_disposition()` chooses the bounded next action (:568–622). The decision is per-wake, per-attempt, stateless across roles — it asks "was this failure transient and was nothing dispatched?" not "is the plant in a vulnerable state where another retry will amplify load?"

*Capacity observation exists next door but is not a retry gate.* `src/mishe_tauftauf/recovery_policy.py:capacity()` (:58–90) already tracks `memory_full, io_full, available_ratio` into `healthy/failed/mitigated` and `src/mishe_tauftauf/recovery_io.py:pressure()` (:167) and `status()` (:367) expose `host-pressure LIMITED/CRITICAL` events (dashboard episodes 37/40 on 2026-11-02, `recovery/events/*.json` `available_ratio ~0.51`). `host_capacity.py` and `recovery_io.host_cgroup()` (:391) are the organic-capacity signal `C_org(t)` in Huang's terms. But `native_recovery.attempt_disposition` never reads them — a wake that failed with `500` during `CRITICAL` is still `RETRYABLE` and `fresh_attempt` creates `attempt-N/` immediately.

*The retry is immediate, the timeout is implicit.* `fresh_attempt` is called as soon as the runner decides to retry; there is no `b(k)` backoff, no jitter, no amplification-delay accounting. The paper's warning that a short timeout accelerates workload amplification maps directly: the plant's effective timeout is the wake's own execution time (seconds to minutes), but retries are still synchronous at the scheduler's cadence, so three roles retrying three times each can produce the `RETRYABLE^d` load spike the paper's sustaining loop describes, even though each `RETRYABLE` was locally justified.

*Prior lands on this file addressed orthogonal halves.* The 2026-10-29 PILOT land (rehearsal) asks "would this retry corrupt state?" The 2026-11-02 RAF land (Mehan arXiv:2608.25403) adds a per-tier retry budget `B0=0.2` and jittered backoff `b(k)` for rate. The 2026-11-02 Bohrbug land (Gray 1985 TR-85.7) adds repeatability detection. None gates on vulnerable-state duration `D_t_trig` or distinguishes workload- vs capacity-degradation amplification — the two axes Huang adds to Bronson.

## What is new here

**The critique is that retry is not recovery — it is the most common way a vulnerable system makes itself metastable.**

Huang's field data says retries sustained >50% of the wild metastable outages, and Bronson's framework predicts why: a system run in a vulnerable state for efficiency (the plant *is* run vulnerably — cache-like ledger lock `STORE_LOCK = '.ledger.lock'` :109 and single-host `available_ratio` 0.51 leave little headroom) will, on a trigger that overloads it (`L_sys > C_sys`), enter a feedback loop where the *remedy* (retry) becomes the load. The plant's current retry is exactly the sustaining effect the paper names: `transient_status() == True` → `RETRYABLE` → `fresh_attempt` → more attempts holding locks and reading the same degraded store, keeping `a_L(t) > 1` even after the organic trigger (e.g., `host-pressure CRITICAL` or a transient 500) has cleared.

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number in this repository where it would land, and each bounded by what the papers concede.

1. **Vulnerable is a precondition to check, not a state to infer after.** Bronson/Huang's `C_stable` (Table 2) — the stable capacity below which the system recovers — maps to `recovery_policy.capacity`'s `healthy` vs `vulnerable` band. Before `attempt_disposition` returns `RETRYABLE`, read the plant's current `status()`/`host-pressure` as `C_sys(t)` proxy: if `available_ratio < available_low` or `state in (FAILED, MITIGATED)` and the trigger duration `D_t_trig` (time since `first_failed` in `previous` row :31) exceeds a small threshold, the plant is in the vulnerable→metastable window — do not create `attempt-N/` now, even though the failure was transient.

2. **Workload vs capacity-degradation amplification are different gates.** Workload amplification (`a_L`) is retry-driven: more attempts → more `L_sys`. Capacity degradation (`a_C`) is GC/queue/lock-driven: the retry holds `STORE_LOCK` or fills the journal, lowering `C_sys` (the paper's GC example, §4). The plant has both: `fresh_attempt` increases `a_L`, while a held lock (`_writer_active` :335, `LiveWriter` :115) or unflushed `starts.jsonl` decreases `a_C`. The gate should distinguish them — workload → jittered backoff + budget; capacity → fail-fast and load shedding (drop the retry, reconcile).

3. **Recovery from metastability is load shedding, not another retry.** >55% of Huang's cases recovered by throttling/dropping. For this plant that means `attempt_disposition` returning `RECONCILE` or `UNKNOWN` with `reason="metastable guard: vulnerable state — shedding retry"` and *not* creating `attempt-N/`, rather than returning `RETRYABLE` and letting the caller immediately retry. The retry budget (RAF land) says *how many* retries the system can afford; the metastable guard says *whether* any retry now will sustain overload.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the sources.** The 22 incidents are selected on severity and report detail — not a prevalence census, and gray rows are plausible-not-proven metastability. No tight `w_L` or universal `C_stable` is given; the threshold must be learned from this plant's own `available_ratio`/`capacity` history, not imported. The synthetic reproductions (§5) are simplified; wake-recovery durations are seconds–minutes, not RPS, so millisecond `RAF` constants do not transfer. The papers do not claim retries are always wrong — they are the fix in stable state and the poison in vulnerable state, which is why the gate is conditional.

## Application

target: src/mishe_tauftauf/native_recovery.py

**Add a metastable guard beside `attempt_disposition`/`fresh_attempt` — conditional on vulnerable state and amplification type, additive, behind a flag.**

1. **Add a `metastable_guard()` helper beside `transient_status` (:143) and `_transient` (:267) in `src/mishe_tauftauf/native_recovery.py`.** Signature `def metastable_guard(home: Path | None, now: float | None = None) -> tuple[bool, str]` — reads `recovery_io.status(home)` / `recovery_io.pressure()` (or `host-pressure` event files) as `C_sys(t)` proxy and the `previous` row's `first_failed` as `D_t_trig` proxy. Returns `(True, reason)` when vulnerable: `available_ratio is not None and available_ratio < 0.12` (≈ `available_low` + headroom) or `status["state"] in ("FAILED","MITIGATED","UNKNOWN")` with `now - first_failed > 30` s, or `LiveWriter`/`STORE_LOCK` held indicating `a_C` degradation. Otherwise `(False, "")`. When `home is None` or pressure is unreadable, return `(False, "metastable guard: pressure unreadable — UNKNOWN")` — missing evidence stays `UNKNOWN`-biased, matching `recovery_policy.transition` (:34).

2. **Gate `attempt_disposition` (:568) on the guard after the existing `RETRYABLE` check.** When `MISHE_NATIVE_METASTABLE_GUARD=1` (default `0` — backward compatible) and `metastable_guard(home)` is `True`, return `Disposition(RECONCILE, evidence=..., reason="metastable guard: shedding retry — vulnerable state D_t_trig=…s, C_sys below C_stable")` instead of `RETRYABLE` — no `attempt-N/` is created. Distinguish `a_L` vs `a_C` in `reason`: `a_L` → `"workload amplification — retry budget deferred"` (composes with RAF budget `B`), `a_C` → `"capacity degradation — lock/GC/queue"` (requires reconciliation, not backoff). This is the paper's "load shedding, not retry" recovery.

3. **Jittered deferral, not immediate re-queue, for the workload case.** When the guard tripped on `a_L` (not `a_C`), the caller (`runner.py`/`mind_loop.py`) should not immediately re-evaluate `attempt_disposition` — wrap the `fresh_attempt` call site with `delay = min(30.0, 0.1 * 2**attempt_index + random.uniform(-0.02, 0.02))` (same shape as the RAF jitter, intentional composition) or schedule the wake after `T=1.0 s` (Huang's amplification delay), so the retry wave spreads rather than synchronizes. With the flag off, the delay is `0` and bytes are identical to today.

4. **Emit amplification as evidence, not a new verdict word.** Extend `Attempt`/`Disposition` evidence tuple (`_evidence` :361, `unseen_attempts` :847) with `metastable_guard={"vulnerable": bool, "a_L": float|None, "a_C": float|None, "D_t_trig": float|None}` computed from `status` and `previous`. `BLOCKED_STATE` verdict stays one of `SETTLED`/`RETRYABLE`/`RECONCILE`/`UNKNOWN` (:92–95); the guard decision is `evidence` + `reason`, preserving the ledger's vocabulary. The `RAF` budget from the 2026-11-02 recent land and the Bohrbug signature from the 2026-11-02 misread land compose: `RAF` says *rate*, Bohrbug says *repeatability*, metastable guard says *vulnerability*.

**Acceptance (deterministic, runnable from this repository).**

- With `MISHE_NATIVE_METASTABLE_GUARD=0` the existing `classify_attempt`/`attempt_disposition`/`fresh_attempt` sequence for a fixture wake produces the same `Attempt`/`Disposition` bytes as before (replay the last 3 `failure.txt` + journal fixtures through `classify_attempt`; `python tools/check_parsing.py` stays green — `native_recovery.py` is not a boundary file in `parsing-policy.json`, and `metastable_guard` is additive).
- With `MISHE_NATIVE_METASTABLE_GUARD=1`, synthetic `host-pressure CRITICAL` (`available_ratio=0.05` or `status FAILED` with `first_failed=now-60`) and a `500`-transient `RETRYABLE` wake, `attempt_disposition` returns `RECONCILE` with `reason` containing `metastable guard` and `evidence` containing the guard dict — no `attempt-N/` is created. Today it returns `RETRYABLE`.
- With the same flag but `status HEALTHY` and `available_ratio=0.6`, the same wake returns `RETRYABLE` and `fresh_attempt` after `b(k)+jitter` creates `attempt-N/` — the guard does not suppress retries in stable state.
- With a held `STORE_LOCK` (`LiveWriter` active) and truncated `failure.txt`, the guard trips on `a_C` and returns `RECONCILE` (capacity degradation), not workload-deferred `RETRYABLE`.

**Ownership.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/native_recovery.py` is the bounded fresh-wake recovery discipline consumed by the `health`/`genome` life-support slice; `health` is the resolver for `native recovery, health, supervisors, watchdogs` (field `failure-and-recovery`'s `plant_part`) and for substrate `UNKNOWN` delivery gaps, and owns `recovery_io`'s probe/pressure sampling that the guard's `C_sys(t)` signal reports to. The flag `MISHE_NATIVE_METASTABLE_GUARD` lives beside `MISHE_NATIVE_RECOVERY_WAKE` checked in `checks/native-recovery-optin.py` and beside `MISHE_NATIVE_RETRY_BUDGET` / `MISHE_NATIVE_BOHRBUG_DETECT` from the two 2026-11-02 revisits.

**One thing this review does not ask for.** It does not propose throttling the whole plant, rewriting journals as Postgres transactions, or fixing administration-caused outages — those are the paper's §6 concerns and plant-wide changes. It only asks that the wake's bounded retry observe Huang's one distinction the plant currently ignores: whether the plant is in a vulnerable state where another retry will sustain overload after the trigger is gone.
