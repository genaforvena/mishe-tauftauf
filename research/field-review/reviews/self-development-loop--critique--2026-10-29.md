# The plant learns without a theory of power — and will keep institutionalising the wrong learning (a 4I critique)

## What we read

**Thomas B. Lawrence, Michael K. Mauws, Bruno Dyck, Robert F. Kleysen — "The Politics of Organizational Learning: Integrating Power into the 4I Framework," *Academy of Management Review* 30, no. 1 (Jan. 2005): 180–191** — read full text at:

- <https://presencial.moodle.ufsc.br/pluginfile.php/1698118/mod_resource/content/3/2005%20-%20The%20Politics%20of%20Organizational%20Learning.pdf> (publisher PDF, 13 pp., 98 KB, fetched 200 2026-10-29 at /tmp/politics_org_learning.pdf; `pdfinfo` 13 pages / `pdftotext` verified header `THE POLITICS OF ORGANIZATIONAL LEARNING: INTEGRATING POWER INTO THE 4I FRAMEWORK`)
- <https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2005.15281431> (DOI, same AMR printing)
- Framework under critique: Mary M. Crossan, Henry W. Lane, Roderick E. White, "An Organizational Learning Framework: From Intuition to Institution," *Academy of Management Review* 24, no. 3 (1999): 522–537 — cited by Lawrence p. 180.

Four passages carry the critique this plant would reproduce if it adopted the self-development loop blindly. Page numbers are the printed AMR pagination.

1. **Learning is political and the canonical model ignores it (p. 180, opening note).** "We argue that power and politics provide the social energy that transforms the insights of individuals and groups into the institutions of an organization. Moreover, we propose that different forms of power in organizations are connected to specific learning processes … and that an examination of these different forms of power provides a basis for understanding why some insights become institutionalized while others do not." The note is offered as a repair to Crossan et al. (1999): three reasons to connect power — cumulative theory demands it, any power-free theory "will always be incomplete" because organizations are "inherently political," and it explains why only some available useful innovations are embraced.

2. **The 4I gap by process — which ideas survive each feed-forward step is not explained (pp. 181–182, § THE 4I MODEL).** Crossan's four processes are `intuiting` (preconscious pattern, individual → metaphor), `interpreting` (explaining idea to self and others → language/cognitive map), `integrating` (shared understanding + coordinated action, group level), `institutionalizing` (embedding in systems/structures/procedures/strategy, org level). Lawrence's diagnosis: which half-thoughts become metaphors, which metaphors become maps, which maps become shared action, which actions become institutions is left unexamined. "While we might imagine that it will be 'the best' or 'most valuable' new ideas that are successfully intuited and interpreted, this would ignore the fact that organizations are often political arenas populated by self-interested actors." The transformation into "coherent, collective action" is "precarious" (Brown & Duguid; Benford & Snow cited p. 182); institutionalisation "depends on the actions of interested actors who work to embed them" (DiMaggio; Lawrence 1999; Fligstein cited).

3. **Two modes of power and four forms mapped onto the 4I — discipline/influence/force/domination (pp. 182–184, § POWER, POLITICS, AND ORGANIZATIONAL LEARNING + Figure 1/Table 1).** The paper imports a typology of power: *episodic* (discrete strategic acts by self-interested actors; Pfeffer 1981) vs *systemic* (routine ongoing practices diffused through social systems; Clegg 1989; Foucault 1977; Giddens 1984; Hardy & Clegg 1996). It then narrows to four forms that are "relatively exhaustive" (Hardy 1996): *influence* (affect costs/benefits: moral suasion, persuasion, ingratiation, exchange; Kipnis et al. 1980; Yukl & Falbe 1990), *force* (affect the counterpart's ability to act: restricting agenda, formal authority), *domination* (affect what is seen as legitimate/possible through systems), *discipline* (affect what is imaginable through socialisation/accreditation/technology; Covaleski et al. 1998; Noble 1984). Mapping proposed (p. 183, Fig. 1): `interpretation ↔ influence`, `integration ↔ force`, `institutionalization ↔ domination`, `intuition ↔ discipline`. Influence/force are episodic; domination/discipline are systemic. Propositions follow (pp. 184–188): episodic power sponsors whose ideas become legitimate interpretations and shared action; systemic power (and its absence for challengers) decides which become institutions and then frame the next generation's intuitions.

4. **What the critique concedes — so the transfer is bounded (§ Discussion, pp. 188–190; limits stated p. 184).** The authors concede the 4I's three strengths they keep (multilevel, dynamic, four-process feed-forward/feed-back loop) and that "any form of power might be evident in any of the four processes" — the one-form-per-process mapping is "most effective," not exclusive, and "fluid" in practice. Power is not the only determinant (skill, resources, timing matter), and episodic/systemic are ideal types. The note is explicitly theory-building, not an empirical test (no new data; propositions offered for future work). Coopey & Burgoyne (2000, cited p. 180) — the Lancaster review of politics and organizational learning — is invoked for the same gap: recognition that organizational learning is "fundamentally political" without systematic theory connecting politics and learning; Burgoyne & Jackson (1997), Blackler (2000), Fox (2000) similarly. This review keeps that modesty: the repair is a vocabulary and detector, not a claim that power explains all variance.

## What this plant already does

*The loop observes itself through bounded readers whose politics are typed as "findings," "verdicts," and "coverage" — which is exactly the move Lawrence warns will always be incomplete.*

- **Initiative as empirical benchmark inside a fixed institution.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative.py:34-43` fixes `EVIDENCE_BINDING_CLAUSE_TIME = 2026-10-06T16:22:11+00:00`, `WINDOW_SECONDS = 86400`, `MAX_PATCHES = 512`, `SNAPSHOT_MAX_AGE = 900`; `collect():185` classifies every outcome as `OBSERVED`/`PARTIAL`/`UNKNOWN` inside those variables. The DGM-derived archive addition (`INITIATIVE_ARCHIVE_VERSION / STAGED_SUBSETS / STAGED_TRIGGER / stepping_stone_score`, landed 2026-10-29) extends lineage measurement but keeps the same institutionalisation decision: who counts as a resolver, what counts as evidence, and what window counts as "recent" are already-systemic facts (domination/discipline in Lawrence's terms) that then frame the next intuition about what is "useful work."

- **Initiative-watch as episodic router that reads as neutral detector.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative_watch.py:37-44` fixes `AGED_SECONDS = 3600`, `CONTINUATION_SECONDS = 1800`, `PRODUCER_MAX_AGE = 180`, `ESCALATE_SECONDS = 600`, `REMINDER_SECONDS = 1800`; `evaluate():317` emits `findings` with `id/kind/role/ref/summary/primary/escalation/action` and routes `initial → changed → escalation → reminder` (`:266-274, :470, :676`). In 4I language this is *influence* (which finding becomes a legitimate interpretation on the dashboard) and *force* (escalation/reminder determines who can act and whose agenda is constrained) exercised episodically — but the row renders as `"unknown" / "attention" / "observed"` state words, with no typed distinction between "unknown because the reader was incomplete" and "unknown because the publisher lacked influence to get the condition interpreted." Two identical `aged-wake` findings for different roles receive different escalation paths by `primary/escalation` lists, which is a power allocation rendered as routing.

- **Follow-through as the shared institution that hides its own domination.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/follow_through.py:20-28` composes the dashboard line `MIND WALL — shared follow-through evidence` from `initiative_lines + substrate_lines + watch_lines` plus refusal counts — the plant's "institutionalization" step in Crossan's sense: systems that make learning available "at least partially independent of their individual or group origins" (Lawrence p. 181, quoting Crossan p. 525). The projection is deliberately non-recursive ("changing diagnostic counts must not recursively create another wake" `:4-5`). But the guard `health():102` and `_outcome_refusal_lines():35` count denials per `role/kind` while the authority that produced the denial — outcome writer `COVERAGE` binding, `REFUSAL_AUDIT` writer, pin `CHECKS` verifier — is systemic and unnamed in the count. Lawrence's point applies verbatim: the survival of an insight from `integrated` (group coordinated action) to `institutionalized` (check/snapshot the plant trusts) depended on episodic advocacy and then on domination (the check that enforces it), but the wall line reports only "6 refusals" with a role tag, not whose systemic power made the institution stick and whose did not.

- **Substrate as discipline.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/substrate_watch.py` hashes HEAD/index/tracked files and reports drift against the pin — the "routine, ongoing practices" (socialisation, accreditation, technology) that Lawrence/Clegg call *discipline*: they shape "not only the behavior of individuals but also their perception and imagination" (p. 184, citing Covaleski; Townley). The wall shows `SUBSTRATE: UNKNOWN` when the pin drifts, which reads as a freshness fact while functioning as a constraint on what a mind is allowed to imagine as done.

- **Doctrine knows the power is there and then archives it.** Wall coordination (`docs/wall-coordination.md`) documents "addressed messages" and `genome` as pusher/activator — explicit episodic power — but no check measures whether an insight's institutionalisation correlated with its sponsor's influence versus its measured quality. The field sweep for `follow-through/initiative/substrate` already surfaced this: every literal occurrence of those keywords in `checks/` is treated as a presence count, never as evidence that the same passage exercises force (who may claim settlement) or domination (which verdict word is available to which role). The plant therefore already reproduces the exact failure Lawrence diagnoses: a learning theory without a power term that cannot explain why the same benchmarked patch would be institutionalised when sponsored by one role and refused when sponsored by another, and that will then frame the next generation's intuitions accordingly.

## What is new here

**The critique turns the self-development loop from "an observer that improves measurement" into "a political cycle whose four steps each have a distinct power form — and whose past institutionalisation decisions now silently frame what the plant can intuit as useful work."**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number in this repository where it would land, and each bounded by what the source concedes.

1. **Why some patches become institutions has less to do with `stepping_stone_score` than with who could sponsor their interpretation.** Lawrence's gap (p. 181) — which half-thoughts become metaphors/maps/shared-action/institutions — is this plant's gap: which wall edits/diff hunks become `commit → push → pin → check PASS` and which remain refused/aged/continued. The benchmark that DGM uses (`is_stepping_stone` in `initiative.py:127-151`) is an episodic sponsor's proposal; whether it survives is partly the sponsor's *influence* (can they make the score legible to peers) and *force* (can they hold the agenda long enough for the `watch_lines` to render it), then the system's *domination* (does a `checks/*.py` exist that makes the old bytes UNKNOWN and the new bytes OBSERVED). Treating the score as a neutral "learning metric" without typing the power that carried it reproduces the politics-free 4I.

2. **Episodic vs systemic is the cut the plant needs, not a moral judgment.** The paper's distinction (p. 182–183) gives the plant a pure, deterministic split: *episodic* = a discrete routing/append/address act (`Feed.append`, `initiative_watch._send`, `wall.write`, `seed yield`); *systemic* = a persisted regime that outlives the actor (`checks/initiative.json` snapshot shape, `parsing-policy.json` legacy budget, `checks/initiative-watch.json` state, wall template `MIND WALL — …`). The same finding (`aged-wake:field-review`) can be routed episodically today and rejected systemically tomorrow (expired `SNAPSHOT_MAX_AGE`, `PRODUCER_MAX_AGE` stale, `REFUSAL_AUDIT` cap). Without the cut the dashboard conflates the two and invites the inference "the plant did not learn because the value was not useful" when the truer description is "the value did not acquire a systemic form because its sponsor lacked episodic power at that cycle."

3. **Intuition is disciplined before it is measured — which is where double-loop and power meet.** The recent `self-development-loop--misread` review (Argyris 1976) made governing variables explicit as `GOVERNING_VARIABLES`. Lawrence adds that `intuition` itself is linked to *discipline* (p. 183, Fig. 1): the structures/procedures/systems that organise working lives provide the context for experience and "affect not only the behavior of individuals but also their perception and imagination" (Covaleski et al.). The plant's discipline is literal: the window (`WINDOW_SECONDS`), the clause time, the patch cap (`MAX_PATCHES`), the refused-kinds vocabulary. Those do not just measure intuition — they produce which intuitions are imaginable. A politics-free double-loop detector that asks "which governing variable recurred?" without asking "whose discipline made that variable sticky and whose influence could have changed it?" answers the second-order question with a first-order vocabulary.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** The mapping `influence → interpreting / force → integrating / domination → institutionalizing / discipline → intuiting` is presented as "most effective," not exclusive (p. 184); this review does not claim a finding's `stage` uniquely identifies a power form, only that the form can be typed as the detector. The paper offers propositions, not empirical tests; the transfer is a vocabulary/detector, not a causal claim that influence explains this week's refusals. Four vocabularies already exist in the tree (CUDOS in `post_check.py`, C2PA provenance in `outcome_publication.py`, AoI in `scan_freshness.py`, governing variables in the misread review's `initiative_watch.py` proposal); none is replaced — power words are additive and sit beside them, and the same `UNKNOWN`/`OBSERVED`/`ATTENTION` state words remain the only published verdicts.

**Make the 4I power critique measurable beside the follow-through projection — additive, no new I/O, no `Feed.append`/`seed yield`/wall mutation, no new `checks/*.json` shape, no change to the literal-boundary set, same `UNKNOWN`/`OBSERVED` vocabulary.**

1. **Add module-level political vocabulary beside the existing `REFUSAL_AUDIT_MAX_BYTES` and `lines/health` in `src/mishe_tauftauf/follow_through.py`.** New symbols (names illustrative, additive only): `LEARNING_PROCESSES: Final[tuple[str, ...]] = ("intuiting", "interpreting", "integrating", "institutionalizing")` (Crossan et al. 1999 via Lawrence p. 181), `POWER_MODES: Final[tuple[str, ...]] = ("episodic", "systemic")` and `POWER_FORMS: Final[tuple[str, ...]] = ("influence", "force", "domination", "discipline")` (Lawrence p. 182–183, Hardy/Clegg), `PROCESS_POWER_MAP: Final[dict[str, str]] = {"interpreting": "influence", "integrating": "force", "institutionalizing": "domination", "intuiting": "discipline"}` (Fig. 1/Table 1 mapping), `ROUTING_POWER: Final[dict[str, str]] = {"initial": "influence", "changed": "influence", "escalation": "force", "reminder": "domination"}` (initiative-watch routing stage → dominant power form: initial/changed attempt to make an interpretation legitimate, escalation restricts who can avoid acting, reminder relies on the institution that keeps the notice available). Docstrings cite Lawrence 2005 pp. 180–183 and note that any form may appear in any process in practice (p. 184 caveat) — the map names the *most effective* form, not an exclusive one.

2. **Add two pure, deterministic helpers that type a row's political provenance — no `Path`/clock/net I/O, no tape scan, only caller-held strings.** `follow_through_power_form(*, stage: str | None = None, kind: str | None = None) -> str | None` — returns the power form for a routing stage (`initial/changed → influence`, `escalation → force`, `reminder → domination`) or for a learning process (`institutionalizing → domination`), else `None` for incomplete/unknown inputs; `follow_through_mode_for_form(form: str) -> str | None` — `episodic` for `influence/force`, `systemic` for `domination/discipline`, else `None`. Both return `None` (the plant's existing `unknown` idiom for incomplete evidence) rather than a new state word, and both are pure/bounded so `follow_through.health`/`lines` and existing refusal counting are unchanged. A third optional helper `follow_through_process_for_kind(kind: str) -> str | None` maps watch kinds onto 4I where applicable — e.g. `aged-wake/continuation-review → interpreting` (making an insight a legitimate interpretation), `missing-receipt → integrating` (coordinated action stalled), `undecidable/unknown → institutionalizing` — but is documented as heuristic and itself returns `None` when the kind is outside the 4I scope.

3. **Document the measurement contract in the module docstring and per-symbol docstrings.** The header that already describes "rendering never scans Git or chat" gains a `Lawrence 2005 politics-of-4I` block mapping the four processes, two modes, and four forms plus the routing-stage → power-form table, with AMR DOI/URL, so that a dashboard line that today reads `6 refusals (0 coverage denials)` can in future be accompanied by "of those, N were `influence` failures (interpretation not legitimised) vs `domination` failures (institution not acquired)" without changing the line the wall publishes today. The helper docstrings state the caveat that influence may also appear during institutionalisation, etc. — the vocabulary is the detector, not a causal assignment.

**One thing this review does not ask for.** It does not add a power-weighted score, does not change `primary/escalation` routing, does not add a new `checks/*.json` state word, does not infer intent from prose, does not assign blame to a role, and does not automate institutionalisation — it only makes the 4I and power-form mapping explicit as data so participants can see, under valid information, whether the same patch would have been `OBSERVED` had its sponsor's episodic power at that cycle been different. Automating that choice would be another domination move (Lawrence p. 184).
