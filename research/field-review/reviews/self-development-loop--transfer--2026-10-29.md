# The plant tunes parameters when the leverage is in the paradigm — Meadows's 12 places to intervene as a transfer onto the initiative watch

## What we read

**Donella Meadows, "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System," The Sustainability Institute, Hartland VT, 1999 (21 pp., 95 KB, fetched 200 2026-10-29 at /tmp/meadows_leverage.pdf; `pdfinfo` Title "Leverage cover" / Creator "Adobe PageMaker 6.0" / 21 pages verified, `pdftotext` header "Leverage Points / Places to Intervene in a System / by Donella Meadows" verified)** — read full text at:

- <https://donellameadows.org/wp-content/userfiles/Leverage_Points.pdf> (canonical Sustainability Institute PDF, 1999; shorter version in *Whole Earth*, Winter 1997, cited p. 1)
- Archive mirror listing the same title: <https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/> (index page referencing the 1999 report)

Four passages carry the transfer this plant has not made. Page numbers are the PDF pagination (1–19 of content plus cover).

1. **The ordered list, least to most effective (p. 3, revised list after "a cooler dawn").** In increasing order of effectiveness: `12. Constants, parameters, numbers (such as subsidies, taxes, standards)` → `11. The sizes of buffers and other stabilizing stocks, relative to their flows` → `10. The structure of material stocks and flows (such as transport networks, population age structures)` → `9. The lengths of delays, relative to the rate of system change` → `8. The strength of negative feedback loops, relative to the impacts they are trying to correct against` → `7. The gain around driving positive feedback loops` → `6. The structure of information flows (who does and does not have access to what kinds of information)` → `5. The rules of the system (such as incentives, punishments, constraints)` → `4. The power to add, change, evolve, or self-organize system structure` → `3. The goals of the system` → `2. The mindset or paradigm out of which the system — its goals, structure, rules, delays, parameters — arises` → `1. The power to transcend paradigms`. The essay repeats the header on each spread; the list itself is on p. 3.

2. **Parameters are the weakest leverage and where 99% of attention goes (pp. 5–7, §12).** Parameters are "the numbers that determine how much of a discrepancy turns which faucet how fast" (p. 5): faucet hardness, drain blockage, fire-hose force. Examples: debt/deficit tax and spending dials, air-quality standards, allowed annual cuts, wage and price rates. The verdict: "Parameters are the points of least leverage … Probably 90—no 95—no 99 percent of our attention goes to parameters, but there's not a lot of leverage in them" (p. 5). The caveat immediately follows: parameters become leverage only when they push the system into a range that kicks off a higher item — a chaotic edge, a goal change, a feedback-gain shift (p. 6: "Interest rates … control the gains around positive feedback loops. System goals are parameters that can make big differences").

3. **Information flows, rules, and self-organization outrank parameters because they change who can correct the system (pp. 11–15, §§6–4).** Level 6 is illustrated by the electric-meter story (p. 11): identical houses, identical prices, 30% lower consumption where the meter was in the front hall and visible — same parameters, different information access. Level 5 (rules) includes incentives, punishments, constraints such as the U.S. Constitution's amendments; level 4 (self-organization) is "the power to add, change, evolve, or self-organize system structure" (p. 15: the raw material of innovation — genetic variation, cultural diversity — and the capacity to experiment). The essay pairs these with Forrester's dictum that "People know intuitively where leverage points are … Everyone is trying very hard to push it in the wrong direction!" (p. 2) — i.e., the system already attends to the wrong level.

4. **Goals, paradigms, and transcendence are the top three and the least discussed (pp. 15–19, §§3–1).** Level 3: "The goal of a system is a leverage point superior to the self-organizing ability" (p. 15) — the example is Reagan re-articulating the goal from "government can help people" to "government off our backs" and thereby swinging the same stocks, flows, and rules toward a different attractor. Level 2 quotes Emerson on paradigms clothing themselves in "societies, houses, cities, language, ceremonies, newspapers" (p. 17) and invokes Kuhn's *Structure* (1962, p. 18 n.7): paradigms are "the sources of systems. From them … come system goals and information flows, feedbacks, stocks, flows and everything else" (p. 17). Level 1 — "The power to transcend paradigms" — is the ability to stay unattached in the realm of paradigms, knowing none is true in an absolute sense, and therefore to choose among them. The final paragraph warns that paradigm change is not produced by parameter argument (p. 2, Forrester's world model) nor by re-labeling — it is made scrutable through information and choice, not enforcement.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** The field already has three landed readings (recent DGM in `initiative.py`, misread Argyris in `initiative_watch.py`, critique Lawrence 4I+power in `follow_through.py`). The transfer angle for self-development-loop asks for a cross-domain mechanism landed on one of this plant's parts — the substrate observer / follow-through / initiative watch. Meadows is that mechanism's canonical statement: a ranked intervention vocabulary that turns "the plant observing and changing itself" from parameter tuning into leveled choice. It connects directly to both predecessors — Argyris's governing variables (Meadows level 12) and Lawrence's power-as-social-energy (Meadows levels 6/5/4/3) — while adding what neither provides: an explicit effectiveness ordering and the 12→1 ladder as checkable data.

## What this plant already does

*The loop observes itself, but it observes every governing variable as if it were the same kind of lever.*

- **Every threshold is a level-12 parameter in Meadows's terms, with no level attached.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative_watch.py:37-43` fixes `AGED_SECONDS=3600`, `CONTINUATION_SECONDS=1800`, `PRODUCER_MAX_AGE=180`, `ESCALATE_SECONDS=600`, `REMINDER_SECONDS=1800` (and `MAX_REMINDER_SECONDS=14400`, `MAX_STATE_BYTES`, `MAX_ENTRIES`). The Argyris-derived addition `GOVERNING_VARIABLES:52-93` makes those variables scrutable as `{value, unit, provenance, role}` and `governing_variables():103-109` returns a copy, while `is_single_loop_trap():112-167` names repetition under the same variable — but it does not type the variable's leverage depth. A repeated `aged-wake:field-review` can therefore be diagnosed as "same variable, third time" without being diagnosable as "same *level-12* variable, third time, while level 6/5/3/2 interventions remain unexamined."

- **Window, patch-cap, and tape budgets are buffers and stock structures (levels 11/10) but are rendered as neutral bounds.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative.py:35-43` fixes `WINDOW_SECONDS=86400`, `MAX_PATCHES=512`, `MAX_TAPE_BYTES=128 MiB`, `MAX_ENTRIES=250000`, `SNAPSHOT_MAX_AGE=900`; `src/mishe_tauftauf/follow_through.py:20-28` composes `lines()` from `initiative_lines + substrate_lines + watch_lines` plus refusal counts — the plant's institutionalization step (Crossan via Lawrence). All are level-11/10/9 material (buffer size, plumbing, delay relative to change rate) but the dashboard treats them as infrastructure, not as leverage choices with a rank.

- **Information flow, rules, self-organization, goals, and paradigm are present in the plant but not typed as leverage.** Level 6 (who sees what): `initiative_watch._route():377-386` and `initiative_watch.evaluate():440` decide `primary/escalation/action` recipients, and `follow_through._outcome_refusal_lines():35` counts denials per `role/kind`. Level 5 (rules): `parsing-policy.json` legacy budgets, `EVIDENCE_BINDING_CLAUSE_TIME=2026-10-06T16:22:11+00:00`, `wall outcome` binding. Level 4 (self-organization): the ability to add a new mind, wall, or check via `research/field-review/fields.json` grid. Level 3 (goal): `docs/wall-coordination.md:219` / `docs/mesh.md:53` state the plant's goal as bounded follow-through observation, not productivity score — but no detector says "this retry aims at the wrong goal." Level 2 (paradigm): the doctrine's unstated assumption that valid information + free choice + internal commitment (Argyris Model II) is the frame — exactly what Meadows calls a paradigm source. None of these is returned as data by a pure helper; the plant can observe a finding but not the leverage level of the intervention that would address it.

- **The failure mode Meadows predicts is already visible.** The field-review lane's own 12-week history (376 reviews, 245 "uncommitted in the tree" in the retired mesh; `research/field-review/README.md:2-10`) and this plant's current 39/72 coverage after one-per-turn discipline show the pattern: attention concentrates at level 12 (tune `AGED_SECONDS`, lower a cap, retry a denied `wall outcome`) while the deeper question — whether the information flow (level 6) makes the finding legible, whether the rule (level 5) makes the evidence eligible, whether the goal (level 3) is the right one to institutionalize — remains untyped and therefore unchosen. Meadows's line "Everyone is trying very hard to push it in the wrong direction!" (p. 2) is the plant's current `is_single_loop_trap` truth, extended one step: not just the same variable, the same *weak* level.

## What is new here

**Type every governing variable and every finding kind by Meadows's 12→1 effectiveness ladder — so a repeated failure can be diagnosed as "still pushing a level-12 lever" and the next proposal can name which deeper level it instead engages.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number in this repository where it would land, each bounded by what the source concedes.

1. **The 12→1 ladder is checkable data, not a score or a verdict word.** Meadows stresses that paradigms are not changed by parameter argument and that influence/force/domination/discipline (Lawrence, landed in `follow_through.py:29-68`) are social energy at different levels — information-flow leverage (6) is episodic influence, rule leverage (5) is domination, self-organization (4) is discipline at the intuiting edge. Making the ladder explicit as `MEADOWS_LEVERAGE_POINTS / LEVERAGE_RANK` lets `is_single_loop_trap`'s output be augmented by a rank without changing its `None`-on-unknown contract: the trap is not just "variable X repeated" but "variable X at rank 12 repeated while rank 6/5/3 alternatives are available and unexamined."

2. **Levels 12–8 are physical/material; levels 6–1 are informational/normative — the cut the plant needs to stop conflating them.** The plant's substrate observer (`src/mishe_tauftauf/substrate_watch.py` hashing HEAD/index/tracked files) reports drift as a freshness fact while functioning as a level-10/8 constraint; the initiative producer reports `checks/initiative.json` staleness (`PRODUCER_MAX_AGE`) as a level-9 delay fact while the routing that makes that staleness actionable is level 6. Without the ladder, the dashboard merges "the faucet turns hard" (parameter) with "the meter is in the basement" (information flow) into the same `UNKNOWN` line.

3. **Levels 3–1 connect goals and paradigms to the misread and the critique.** The Argyris review made governing variables scrutable; Meadows ranks *changing the goal* (3) above *changing the ability to self-organize* (4) and *changing the paradigm* (2) above the goal — which is why the Lawrence power vocabulary (influence→interpreting, force→integrating, domination→institutionalizing, discipline→intuiting) sits inside Meadows rather than beside it: influence at level 6 and domination at level 5 are different leverage depths of the same 4I cycle. The additive typing therefore preserves both prior vocabularies and orders them.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** Meadows presents no data or experiment — the essay is a ranked synthesis of systems lore, Forrester anecdotes (Club of Rome world model p. 2, Urban Dynamics 1969 p. 2), and illustrative stories (national debt, air-quality standards, landlord rent p. 6, lake vs river buffers p. 7, Hungarian road network p. 7, London hotel shower delay p. 8, thermostat/anti-trust/democracy negative loops pp. 9–11, interest/birth-rate positive loops pp. 11–12, meter-in-hall information flow p. 11, Reagan goal shift p. 16, Forrester tax-fairness paradigm p. 16, Emerson/Kuhn paradigm sources p. 17). The transfer is therefore a typed vocabulary/detector, not a causal claim that a higher number fixes any particular `aged-wake` this week, and the fluidity caveat applies: any variable can operate at multiple levels in practice — the mapping is "most effective," not exclusive, exactly as Lawrence's process→power map was. Paradigm transcendence (level 1) is not automated — the code makes the paradigm nameable, participants choose under valid information.

Both are pure/bounded and return `None` rather than a new state word; `python tools/check_parsing.py` stays `PASS` because `initiative_watch.py` is not a `parsing-policy.json` boundary file.

3. **Document the measurement contract in the module docstring and per-symbol docstrings.** The header that already describes "deterministic review signals and durable addressed notices" gains a `Meadows 1999 leverage 12→1` block mapping each plant threshold to its rank plus the DOI/URL, so that a dashboard line that today reads `aged-wake:field-review` can in future be accompanied by "leverage 12 (parameter) — deeper options at 6 (information flow) / 5 (rules) / 3 (goal) remain unexamined" without changing the line the wall publishes today. Auto-escalating to level 2 would be another Model I control strategy (Argyris p. 369–370).
