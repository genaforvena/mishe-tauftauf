# No single centralization number survives its own axioms: Saberi & Aref's 2025 battery of 11 measures vs six postulates, and why the plant's couplings publish no metric

## What we read

What the source gives, quoted from its own sentences and tables:

- The problem it sets: "Network centralization, driven by hub nodes, impacts communication efficiency ... Although numerous centralization measures exist, a major challenge lies in determining measures that are both theoretically sound and empirically reliable" (Abstract p.1). 11 normalized measures are compared (p.2): degree centralization NDC, betweenness NBC, closeness NCC, degree variance NDV, assortativity-based hubness ABH, eigenvector centrality dispersion ECD, Gini NGC, hub dominance NHD, hub formation tendency NHT, natural connectivity NNC, degree entropy NDE — each normalized to [0,1].
- The axiomatic ruler it adopts (Palak and Nguyen 2021 [16], p.4): six postulates — P1a-c calibration at 0 for single node / complete / empty graphs; P2 calibration at 1 for a star graph ("A star graph has been consistently identified as the graph with the highest centralization" p.2–3); P3 invariance to isomorphism; P4 "If G has no node of degree |V|−1, then C(G) < 1" (max requires a saturated node); P5 centralization decrease principle when saturating a second node in a graph that already has a saturated node (C(G) ≥ C(Y)); P6 centralization increase principle when saturating the first node in a graph with none (C(G) ≤ C(Y)). Proofs and counterexamples are given pp.5–7.
- Table 1 (p.5) compliance, corrected against Palak & Nguyen's claims: no measure satisfies all six. The three best at five axioms each are NBC (✓✓✓✓✓✗), NCC (✓✓✓✓✓✗) and NDC (✓✓✓✓✓✗) — each violates only P6 (single counterexample graph `[(0,1),(0,3),(0,4),(0,5),(1,2)]` where saturating reduces NBC 0.82→0.36 and NDC 0.7→0.6). Next are NHD (5) and NNC (4); the rest score 2–3. Two specific failures the paper stresses that matter for a rhizome claim: NGC (Gini) satisfies P1 but violates P2 (takes 0.5 for star graphs) and violates P4–P6 (reaches 1 for one-edge graphs with isolated nodes, p.6); NDE/NDV violate P2 "returning particularly low values for large star graphs" and trend toward zero as n grows (Fig.1 top left + § Numerical p.7).
- The numerical complement (Fig.1 + Table 2, pp.7–8): on synthetic graphs where expected behaviour is known — star (expect 1), ring (expect 0, all nodes identical), complete (expect 0), plus perturbed variants (one edge rewired/removed). Six measures (ABH, NBC, NCC, NDC, NHD, NHT) correctly return 1 for stars; NDE "substantially below 1 for small" and trending to 0 for large stars, NGC/NDV "distinctly below 1", ECD/NNC "do not reach 1". On rings, eight return 0; NHD/NHT decay only slowly; NNC "failing to return zero and instead increasing to large values as n grows" (p.7). The paper's combined recommendation (Abstract + § Conclusion): when used jointly, NDC + NBC + NCC "demonstrate the required sensitivity" and are complementary — NDC captures degree-based hub concentration, NBC path-based dominance, NCC accessibility/efficiency of reach (Abstract p.1) — where no single measure suffices.

Why this source for this cell. `deleuze-guattari--metric` asks for a concrete metric or experiment this area uses to measure itself. Deleuze & Guattari offer none; the literature that claims to be rhizomatic borrows one, and the choice of borrowing decides the answer. The predecessor metric in this lens (`deleuze-guattari--metric--2026-10-30`, Ovezik et al. SoK 2025) supplied a histogram toolkit (entropy, Gini, HHI, Nakamoto) for share distributions `s_i` and its window/clustering warning, now landed in `docs/mesh.md:34-44`. That toolkit measures concentration of fungible shares while stating its own preprocessing sensitivity. This revisit supplies the orthogonal, graph-topological counterpart: global centralization via Freeman-style normalizations against the star, with an explicit axiomatic test of whether a claimed "centralization = 0.73" even meets the minimal postulates of centralization. It is the same question ("how centralized is this web?") asked at a different scale, and it shows why two plants can report opposite numbers for the same graph and both call it "centralization" — a direct audit of the plant's decision to publish checks, not a number.

Limits kept from the source. All graphs are simple undirected (p.4 definition); the axioms are for that case — directed, weighted, temporal or bipartite couplings are out of scope. Axioms are minimal requirements, not sufficiency: passing five of six does not certify a measure as right, and the P6 counterexample holds for the three recommended measures. Star = 1 is stipulated (P2), not derived — other traditions could choose a different maximal form. Numerical expectations (star→1, ring→0) are stipulated ideals for the test harness; real collaboration/communication graphs are perturbed and mixed. No claim is made that the plant's couplings form a single graph whose centralization should be optimized; the transfer is diagnostic about measurement, not prescriptive about topology.

## What is new here

**The area's own measure of "how rhizomatic is this web?" does not survive its own axioms unless you name which centralization you mean — and even then the three least-bad measures jointly describe complementary aspects that no single one captures.**

Three points transfer directly to the page that declares the plant's couplings, each bounded by what the source concedes:

1. **Centralization is not one number; it is a family that must be calibrated at 0 and at the star at 1, with saturation behaviour.** The six postulates make that calibration explicit (P1a-c, P2, P4). For the plant, any future graph claim (mind→wall writes, mind→tape messages, mind→`main` landings) would need to state its graph construction (nodes = roles, edges = ?direction/weight/window) and its postulate compliance. Otherwise `centralization = 0.5` is uninterpretable — Saberi shows NGC returns 0.5 for the very star that by stipulation should be 1, while NHD returns 1 even for complete graphs (violates P1b, Table 1).

2. **No measure passes all six — including the three the paper recommends.** NBC/NCC/NDC each violate P6 (first saturation can decrease the value on the published counterexample). The paper keeps that counterexample rather than hiding it, and still recommends the three jointly because they pass the numerical test where NDE/NDV/NGC fail: NDE "trending toward zero as network order increases" for stars, NGC "distinctly below 1" for stars and spuriously 1 for one-edge graphs, NNC "increasing to large values" for rings that should be 0 (pp.7–8). Plant analogue: a D&G audit that cited "Gini = 0.42 so the mesh is decentralized" would be citing a measure the axiomatic battery flags as violating maximal centralization itself.

3. **Joint use, not single score.** The paper's positive recommendation is the triad (Abstract + Numerical): NDC (degree-hub concentration), NBC (path dominance), NCC (reach efficiency). They are complementary, not interchangeable — Saberi's real-world networks (neuroscience, biology, social, ecology) vary differently across the three, and no single one captures hubness. This matches the plant's four questions (break/resume, join without hub, mixed origins, detachable declaration) as a deliberate refusal to score one axis. The metric note should therefore name that centralization, if ever reported, is reported as a quoted triple under a stated window and construction, not as a plant number — the same posture the Ovezik paragraph already takes for share summaries, now extended to the graph family.

What the predecessor metric did not cover. Ovezik's toolkit applies to a share histogram `s_i` (who produced how many blocks/writes) and warns about window/clustering/threshold. Saberi's toolkit applies to a graph `G=(V,E)` and warns about axiom compliance and star-normalization. One can be correct while the other is misapplied; neither entails the other. Publishing `Gini = 0.42` without saying whether it is SoK's distribution Gini or Saberi's network NGC is the confusion the two reviews together close.

One thing this review does not ask for. No new ledger, gate, `checks/*.py` threshold or `field_review.py` contract change; no computed NDC/NBC/NCC for any current window; no claim that this plant is or should be star-like or ring-like; no import of the paper's neuroscience/biology/ecology findings as plant properties; no replacement of existing wall/DM/lease/Git mechanics — only that the concentration row the introduction already declares window-bound is also, in its graph-topological reading, family-bound and postulate-bound, with three named complementary aspects if it is ever measured.
