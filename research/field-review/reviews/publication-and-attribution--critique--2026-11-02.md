"Why Johnny Adopts Identity-Based Software Signing: A Usability Case Study of Sigstore", arXiv:2503.00271v6 (read PDF head 2026-11-02, /tmp/johnny_sigstore.pdf) — interview study of 17 Sigstore practitioners on identity-based signing usability — used only to name the Sigstore-side usability gap the SLSA critique predicts, not as the review's source.

Six passages carry the critique this plant would reproduce if it adopted its own SLSA/attestation vocabulary (landed 2026-10-29 onto `ci_watch.py` and 2026-11-02 onto `ci_watch.py` via Rosen+GH Docs+Sigstore) blindly — each with its stated limits kept.

1. **Four challenges from 1,523 SLSA GitHub issues (Abstract + §III, Table I).** Thematic LDA over 1,523 issues from 233 repos yields four challenges: Complex Implementation (CI), Unclear Communication (UC), Limited Feasibility (LF), and Updates/Maintenance fragility. Two main challenges reported are `complex implementation` and `unclear communication`. Subthemes: CI.1 Complicated Provenance Generation (blocking pre-submit verifier jobs, "non-build" configurations unsupported by slsa-github-generator), CI.2 Intricate Maintenance (incompatibility, silent messages, hard-coding, workflow-blocking steps, data staleness) (§III.A). Limit kept: qualitative GitHub-issue corpus, not a controlled experiment; LDA topics are interpretive, not ground truth.

2. **UC.1 Unclear Definitions — "provenance" vs "attestation", "hosted" vs "hermetic", "non-falsifiable" (UC.1, p. 5).** Practitioners confused terminology: "words 'resource' and 'artifact' are only briefly explained", "I was confused on the wording of 'hosted' and asked in Slack", "provenance and attestation … absence of defined definitions". The paper notes inconsistency when terms are used interchangeably and "standardization between SLSA and industry" is missing. Limit kept: definition confusion is practitioner-reported, not a spec defect per se; framework authors face "conventional terms is not easy … vary based on context".

3. **UC.2 Unclear Documentation and where attestations live (UC.2 + LF.1, pp. 5-6).** "Following the documentation to migrate … I was not able to understand that SLSA does not support 'non-build' configurations", "attack graphs are confusing", discrepancies between docs and actual code. Crucially for this plant: "One aspect … is where generated attestations should be stored. Basically, there isn't really a standardized way of doing this yet? And it sounds like you're implying that Sigstore is one candidate and storing attestations within an app's own repo is an option … whether or not to do that is still an 'open question'." Limit kept: docs migrated since 2024; Sigstore vs in-repo is explicitly unsettled, not a recommendation to pick one.

4. **LF.1 Limited Attestation Verification — attestation distinct from provenance, verification complexity and redundancy (§III.A LF.1, p. 6).** "Practitioners are confused regarding attestation, such as its distinction from provenance and the necessity of automation … Tools such as the slsa-github-verifier have been highlighted for complexity and redundancy, making the verification process less efficient. Moreover, a lack of clear guidance on how to communicate attestation data hinders downstream systems' ability to verify". Diversity of generating/storing attestation data introduces complexities, plus "not all signatures meet the integrity and authenticity requirements". Limit kept: verification complexity is tool-specific (slsa-github-verifier), not inherent to SLSA levels; paper does not measure verification latency.

5. **LF.2 Two-party review burden and policy inconsistency (§III.A LF.2 + §III.B, p. 6).** Two-party (two qualified reviewers) required at higher SLSA levels: "Many open-source projects have only one maintainer … finding a second reviewer challenging. … ongoing burden in terms of time and resources". Plus policy variation: "discrepancy exists between npm registry and package manager … installing with npm install P names package A while npm download … names it B. This inconsistency affects attestation, metadata, and provenance". Limit kept: two-party applies to SLSA Source/Build tracks, not directly to Build L2 via `actions/attest` alone (Docs: attestations by themselves provide L2); the plant's single-author wall commits cannot meet it.

6. **Five strategies that name the fix vocabulary (§III.B, p. 7).** Strategies suggested by practitioners: streamlining provenance generation processes, improving SLSA verification process, providing specific/detailed documentation, standardizing terminology, and clarifying feasibility/policy guidance. The paper notes "some strategies can help mitigate multiple challenges, and some challenges need future research and tool enhancement." Limit kept: strategies are practitioner suggestions, not validated interventions; the paper reports them, not tests them.

## What this plant already does

*The plant has a typed SLSA ladder and a typed attestation bundle word, but no typed word for whether that bundle is verifiable, where it lives, or whether its words are understood — the three gaps Tamanna proves are the common case.*

- **SLSA ladder exists without the UC critique.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/ci_watch.py:35-48` (post 39939773) carries `SLSA_BUILD_LEVELS=("L0","L1","L2","L3")`, `SLSA_PROVENANCE_STATES`, `SLSA_ISOLATION_LEVELS`, `SLSA_PREDICATE_TYPE="https://slsa.dev/provenance/v1"` plus 2026-11-02 attestation vocabulary `GITHUB_ATTESTATION_PREDICATES / ATTESTATION_SUBJECT_KINDS / SIGSTORE_INSTANCES / ATTESTATION_LOG_STATES / GH_ATTESTATION_VERIFY_SUBCOMMAND / ATTESTATION_PERMISSIONS` with pure helpers `slsa_build_level_is_valid / slsa_level_for / attestation_predicate_is_valid / subject_address_is_valid / predicate_flag_for / gh_attestation_verify_args_is_valid / slsa_level_for_github_attestation`. `delivery.py:30-41` carries Benjamin's `REPRODUCTION_KINDS / CULT_EXHIBITION_VALUES / BENJAMIN_AURA_WITHERS` for the aura-withers distinction but no SLSA-challenge vocabulary. The plant can say `L3 = attested+hosted+reusable` but cannot say `L3 declared but verification is complex/redundant` or `"provenance" used where "attestation" meant` — the UC.1 confusion reproduced as two callers both computing `L2` and believing they checked the same thing.

- **CI is poll-read with retry, not provenance-generation-shaped — the CI.1/CI.2 gap by construction.** `ci_watch.read()` (`:369-410`) shells `gh repo view` + `gh run list --commit SHA` with `_COMMAND_ATTEMPTS=5, _COMMAND_RETRY_SECONDS=2.0` for transport resilience, mapping `state in {pass,fail,running,unknown}`. There is no `slsa-github-generator` invocation, no blocking pre-submit verifier job, no "non-build" configuration flag, and no maintenance/silent-failure signal — the paper's CI.1 ("blocking nature … delay submission") and CI.2 ("silent messages, hard-coding, workflow-blocking steps, data staleness") are untypeable here. `rg -n "SLSA_CHALLENGE|SLSA_VERIFICATION|attestation.*store|non-build|provenance.*vs.*attestation" src/` is empty outside this review's proposal, so a reviewer cannot tell a poll `CI:PASS` from a generated provenance `exists/authentic` — the paper's central Category error.

- **Attestation storage and verification remain "open question" — the LF.1 gap kept verbatim.** `ci_watch.py` now distinguishes `logged (Rekor)` vs `not-logged (GitHub instance)` and `public-good` vs `github-instance`, but does not carry the paper's storage-location question: "Sigstore is one candidate and storing attestations within an app's own repo is an option … still an 'open question'." `slsa_level_for_github_attestation` maps `is_attested+hosted+is_reusable → L3` but does not distinguish "attestation generated" from "attestation verified by downstream consumer" or "verifier reported complexity/redundancy". The paper's LF.1 ("lack of clear guidance on how to communicate attestation data hinders downstream ability to verify") is reproduced as a bundle existing without a typed consumer-visibility fact.

- **Two-party review and policy inconsistency are untyped — LF.2 by design.** `delivery.py` admits candidates via `_push` (`git push --force-with-lease`) and `submit/check` family; `ci_watch` has `slsa_provenance_is_valid / slsa_subject_is_valid` shape checks but no `two-party-reviewed` predicate or `npm-registry-vs-package-manager` inconsistency flag. The plant's single-author wall commits (205 standing-audit rows, single `genaforvena` committer) cannot meet two-party at SLSA Source levels, and the review explicitly kept this limit ("no trust-anchor list or offline bundle fetch"), so claiming `L2` via `actions/attest` alone while LF.2 applies to higher levels is invisible without a typed `two-party-not-applicable-for-Build-L2` word.

*Consequence the source predicts.* Abstract + §IV: SLSA adoption stalls on complex implementation and unclear communication despite growing interest, and "some challenges need future research and tool enhancement." A reader holding only `CI:PASS + L2 bundle exists` without `verification-verified / location-typed / definition-agreed` will infer the bundle is consumer-verifiable and its level is understood — when the paper shows practitioners file issues precisely because the verifier blocks or silently fails, the terms are confused, and no standard storage exists. The critique's Warning: adding the ladder without these words makes `Valid/L2` look stronger than the ecosystem's actual ability to generate and verify it.

## What is new here

**Tamanna's issue-corpus reframes the plant's SLSA+attestation vocabulary from "a ladder and a bundle dialect" to "a ladder whose provenance generation, terminology, storage location, and verification are empirically the four places practitioners file issues — so declaring L2/L3 without typing generation-vs-verification, location, and definition agreement reproduces the stall the paper measures."**

Five ideas transfer directly, each bounded by what the paper concedes.

1. **Four challenge categories as a closed vocabulary, not just `L0-L3` (Abstract + Table I).** Today the plant names `L0-L3 / exists-authentic-unforgeable / none-hosted-isolated`; the transfer adds `SLSA_CHALLENGE_CATEGORIES: Final = ("complex-implementation","unclear-communication","limited-feasibility","intricate-maintenance")` as the typed reason a `declared L2/L3` may not be `verified L2/L3`. No `gh` work is added; the vocabulary is the detector that a retry `Py_COMPILE OK` is not a generation proof. Limit kept: categories are thematic labels over issues, not measured causality — the tuple keeps them as words, not as severity scores.

2. **UC.1 terminology confusion as a typed predicate — `provenance` vs `attestation` vs `non-falsifiable` vs `hosted` (UC.1, p. 5).** The paper quotes confusion over "provenance/attestation", "resource/artifact", "hosted/hermetic/non-falsifiable". The transfer makes `SLSA_CONFUSED_TERMS: Final = ("provenance","attestation","resource","artifact","hosted","hermetic","non-falsifiable")` and a pure predicate `slsa_terms_are_consistent(terms_used: list[str]) -> bool` that flags mixing `provenance` where `attestation` meant (or vice versa) as a reviewable disagreement, not a silent pass. Limit kept: the predicate checks word presence, not semantics — it detects the paper's "used interchangeably" symptom, not the intended meaning.

3. **UC.2/LF.1 attestation location as an open, typed fact — `Sigstore (Rekor)` vs `in-repo` vs `undecided` (UC.2/LF.1 quotes, p. 6).** The paper's exact quote keeps the location question verbatim: "Sigstore is one candidate and storing attestations within an app's own repo is an option … still an 'open question'." The transfer adds `SLSA_ATTESTATION_LOCATIONS: Final[tuple[str,...]] = ("sigstore-rekor","in-repo","undecided")` so that a claim of `attested` without a location is visibly `undecided` rather than implicitly `rekor`. Complemented by `SIGSTORE_INSTANCES`/`ATTESTATION_LOG_STATES` already in `ci_watch.py`, this makes storage the third word beside `instance` and `log state`. Limit kept: no recommendation — `undecided` is the honest default, per paper.

4. **LF.1 provenance-generated vs attestation-verified as distinct states, with verifier complexity as a typed gap (LF.1, p. 6).** The paper distinguishes provenance generation from attestation verification and flags "complexity and redundancy" of `slsa-github-verifier` plus "lack of clear guidance on how to communicate attestation data". The transfer adds `SLSA_ATTESTATION_STATES: Final = ("none","generated","verified")` and a pure helper `slsa_attestation_state_is_valid(state)->bool` plus `attestation_is_consumer_verifiable(is_generated:bool, is_verified:bool, communication_clear:bool)->bool` that is True only when generated and verified and communication clear — so `generated-but-unverified` or `generated-but-not-communicated` cannot be shown as `verified L2`. Limit kept: tool complexity cited is verifier-specific, not universal; the helper types the gap without measuring latency.

5. **Strategies as checkable remediation words, especially `non-build` and blocking-job awareness (§III.B + CI.1).** The five practitioner strategies — streamline provenance generation, improve verification, provide specific detailed documentation, standardize terminology, clarify feasibility/policy — already imply the fixes the plant needs: a `non-build-supported: bool` flag (paper: "SLSA does not support 'non-build' configurations" for templates like python-package-template) and a `provenance_generation_is_blocking: bool` / `verifier_is_silent: bool` typed check that a future `read / tick` can measure instead of hiding behind transport retry. The transfer documents that `read()`'s `_COMMAND_ATTEMPTS=5` with `EOF/TLS` retry is transport resilience, not provenance-generation blocking — they are distinct failures. Limit kept: strategies are suggestions, not validated; the vocabulary carries them as words pending measurement.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** No `slsa-github-generator` or `slsa-verifier` is invoked, no provenance is generated or fetched, no Sigstore/Fulcio/Rekor or `GITHUB_TOKEN`/`id-token` is requested, no workflow YAML is changed, no two-party reviewer is required or simulated, no post-check bypass, no `Feed.append / seed yield / wall / delivery._push` mutation, no trust-anchor list or offline verifier, no build artifact produced, no claim that `L2` via attestations alone implies `L3` or source provenance, and no translation of SLSA pages — exactly the paper's "needs future research and tool enhancement" boundary: the vocabulary is pure predicate over caller-held strings/dicts, import-safe without site home, clock, or network, and publication remains the DEV/blog + commit path the `pub` role already owns.
