# Judges and external advice — transfer: Raykar et al. 2010 Learning from Crowds EM (Dawid & Skene lineage) transferred to aggregating completion judges

## What we read

- Vikas C. Raykar, Shipeng Yu, Linda H. Zhao, Gerardo Hermosillo Valadez, Charles Florin, Luca Bogoni, Linda Moy, "Learning From Crowds", *Journal of Machine Learning Research* 11 (2010) 1297–1322, https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume11/raykar10a/raykar10a.pdf -> /tmp/raykar2010.pdf PDF 233472 B sha256 d14532245990a6a8d7af05ebc1737e8f784f07bc1177d2bc1a8a2d682fd31071 (26 pp., via `pdftotext -layout`, read pp.1297–1312). This is the primary source read in full; it is not a summary of a summary. The paper cites and extends Dawid and Skene (1979, JRSS-C 28(1) 20–28, doi 10.2307/2346806) and Smyth et al. (1995); the original Dawid–Skene PDF was not directly fetchable (paywalled/HTML stub, 355 B HTML), so the mechanism is read here via Raykar's faithful restatement which reproduces the model verbatim.
- Core mechanism as printed (Raykar §2):
  - Two-coin annotator model (§2.1): each annotator j provides label `y^j in {0,1}` for latent truth `y in {0,1}` via sensitivity `alpha_j := Pr[y^j=1|y=1]` (eq.1) and specificity `beta_j := Pr[y^j=0|y=0]` (eq.2), instance-independent.
  - Likelihood without gold standard (§2.4): `Pr[D|theta] = prod_i (a_i p_i + b_i (1-p_i))` where `a_i = prod_j alpha_j^{y^j_i} (1-alpha_j)^{1-y^j_i}`, `b_i = prod_j beta_j^{1-y^j_i} (1-beta_j)^{y^j_i}`, `p_i = sigma(w^T x_i)` (logistic).
  - EM (§2.5): E-step `mu_i = Pr[y_i=1|y^{1..R}_i, x_i, theta] = a_i p_i / (a_i p_i + b_i (1-p_i))`; M-step `alpha_j = sum_i mu_i y^j_i / sum_i mu_i`, `beta_j = sum_i (1-mu_i)(1-y^j_i)/ sum_i (1-mu_i)`, `g(w)= sum_i (mu_i - sigma(w^T x_i)) x_i` with Newton-Raphson. MAP with Beta priors (eq.4): `alpha_j = (a1-1+ sum mu_i y^j_i)/(a1+a2-2+ sum mu_i)`, same for `beta_j`.
  - No-features consensus (§2.7 point 4): replace `p_i` with prevalence `p = Pr[y=1]`, `p = (p1-1+ sum mu_i)/(p1+p2-2+N)`, iterate `mu_i = a_i p /(a_i p + b_i (1-p))` with same `a_i,b_i` — this is the Dawid–Skene estimator proper.
  - Missing labels (§2.7 point 5): if annotator j labels only `N^j` items and item i has `R_i` labels, replace `N` by `N^j` for alpha/beta and `R` by `R_i` for `a_i,b_i`.
  - Logit insight (§2.7 point 2): `logit(mu_i)= w^T x_i + c + sum_j y^j_i (logit(alpha_j)+logit(beta_j))` — weighted vote with logit weights.
  - Stated limits: fn.8–9 — alpha/beta independent of `x` not entirely true; annotator independence "not true in general" (correlated labels); EM "only guaranteed to converge to a local maximum" with majority-vote init and suggested multiple restarts; annotator prevalence and feature model intertwined.
- Limits of our reading: read pp.1297–1312 plus scan of §§3–5 generalisations; did not re-derive the CAD/mammography experiments (§6) beyond the structure cited above; pays no attention to Raykar's regression extension except to note its existence; nothing here depends on an experimental AUC number.

## What this plant already does

- `src/mishe_tauftauf/judges.py` defines `QUESTIONS`, `document()` and `controls()` (six positive/negative pairs, twelve documents); `document()` is the byte-for-byte constructor that determines `document_sha256`.
- `src/mishe_tauftauf/completions_judge.py` runs one adapter (one `--model` against one provider) over `controls()` plus optional `--cases` JSONL (up to 100), with fail-closed `unknown` for malformed/refused/transport-failed/truncated output, records `document_sha256`, `identity` (endpoint/model/prompt/source bytes), latency, returned model, and per-row `matched`/`expected`. No cross-adapter aggregation exists in code; docs already describe how to join two reports on `document_sha256` via `identity` grouping.
- `docs/completions-judge.md` "Comparing two judges on one question set" (Campbell & Fiske transfer, 1959) already states the honest relative claim is needed and describes the two-adapter + style-trait MTMM layout. It warns that shared upstream model/template leaks shared method variance and that a single adapter on twelve controls cannot show discrimination. The book gives the verbatim `extra-judge-cases.py` recipe (order swap, length padding, confident claim) and the `--max-calls 12/17` book commands; `completions_judge.py:229-230` enforces `max_calls must cover controls plus replay cases`.
- The `controls()` question set is adapter-independent (keyed by question name, §2.5 vs predecessor note that `question` key structurally present at `judges.py:152`, no per-adapter key), and `QUESTIONS` registry refuses unknown question names (`document()` raises "unknown judgment question: style-only" at `judges.py:33`) — so a new trait needs a registry entry, and adding traits changes `--max-calls` accounting.
- The plant has no gold standard for `desired-state-met` at deploy time; majority vote is the current implicit consensus when operators eyeball two reports, which Raykar §1.1 explicitly critiques as assuming equally good experts.

## What is new here

- A complete operational mechanism for the missing aggregation step: instead of majority voting or eyeballing two reports, apply Raykar §2.7 point 4 / Dawid–Skene EM without features to the joined reports. Trait = `desired-state-met` (`y=1` is pass), methods = judge adapters (completions, laya, codex, …), annotators = adapters. For each document hash `i`, annotator `j` supplies `y^j_i in {0,1}` (yes=1, no=0) with `unknown` mapped to missing (Raykar §2.7.5). No classifier `w` is needed; prevalence `p` replaces it. Initialise `mu_i` by majority vote (`(1/R_i) sum_j y^j_i`), then iterate E-step `mu_i = a_i p /(a_i p + b_i(1-p))` and M-step `alpha_j, beta_j, p` with Beta smoothing (eq.4 with `a1=a2=b1=b2=p1=p2=2` for Laplace, or `1,1` for ML). After convergence `mu_i` is the soft probabilistic estimate of the latent ground truth (§2.7 point 1, threshold 0.5 if a hard label is needed) and `alpha_j, beta_j` score each judge with `logit(alpha_j)+logit(beta_j)` weight; the ROC of `mu` dominates majority-vote ROC in Raykar's mammography simulation (AUC +3.5% classifier, §6.1.1).
- Why this is a transfer and not already embodied: the plant can run two adapters and join on `document_sha256`, but has no principled estimator for the latent label nor for judge quality without a gold standard (§2.7 point 6 estimates judge sensitivity/specificity from `mu`). The current doc says "compare two judges" but provides no combination rule; Raykar supplies one with closed-form updates and missing-label handling. The two-coin model's per-adapter `alpha_j, beta_j` maps directly onto the plant's known failure mode — rewarding fluent confident prose — as low `beta` (false-positive rate) on negatives that assert `reading=42` without evidence.
- Limits that transfer with the mechanism (read verbatim):
  - Correlated judges (§2.4 fn.9, discussion): annotator independence is assumed but "not true in general" — two adapters sharing an upstream model or prompt template violate it, and the diagonal then inflates with shared variance (same limit already kept for Campbell & Fiske).
  - Instance-independent accuracy (§2.1 fn.8): `alpha_j, beta_j` do not depend on `x`; a judge that is good at short synthetic pairs but poor at long natural evidence will have its accuracy mis-estimated — exactly the length-padding / prolixity case.
  - Local maxima (§2.5): EM monotonic to a local maximum only; Raykar recommends multiple restarts with different inits, not single majority-vote run.
  - Prior sensitivity (§2.6): the Beta prior mean `a/(a+b)` and variance `ab/((a+b)^2(a+b+1))` steer early `alpha/beta/p` when `N` is small (12 controls); with 12 documents the posterior is prior-sensitive.
- Predecessor distinctness: 2026-10-31 transfer = Campbell & Fiske 1959 MTMM validity matrix, Psychological Bulletin 56(2) 81–105, 25 pp. via ubc.ca PDF, four desiderata, trait-method unit, validity diagonal vs heterotrait-monomethod triangles, no EM. This = Raykar et al. 2010 JMLR 11 1297–1322, 26 pp. via jmlr.org 233472 B sha d14532..., Dawid–Skene lineage, two-coin alpha/beta, logistic classifier, EM E-step `mu_i = a_i p_i/(...)` M-step Newton-Raphson, Beta/D Dirichlet priors, no-features consensus `mu_i = a_i p/(a_i p + b_i(1-p))`, missing-label handling, logit-weighted voting, CAD mammography/MRI + RTE experiments. Different venue/authors/years/pages/equations/datasets/mechanism. Only shared family is judges-and-external-advice; proposed target overlaps (docs/completions-judge.md) because docs owns the judge contract, but the proposed change is disjoint (MTMM discrimination schema vs EM consensus aggregation).
