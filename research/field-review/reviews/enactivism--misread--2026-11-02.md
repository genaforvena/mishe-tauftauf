## What is new here

This 2009 source is the foundational paper the misread angle requires, read here in full for the first time on this lane (prior enactivism--misread had only an abstract). It does three things the plant has not embodied:

1. **It makes "autonomy = closing the loop" a named misreading.** §2.4 (p.373–374) is explicit: individuality, asymmetry and normativity are each necessary and no one or two suffice; organisational closure / being a networked autonomous system (1.1) satisfies individuality and generates normativity (self-maintenance under precariousness pp.376–377) but does not by itself give asymmetry — a closed loop that drifts passively (passive osmosis row) or trembles (Parkinson row) is not an agent. The paper's formal test is Eq.3 `Δp = H_T(S)`: does the *system* modulate coupling constraints for an interval T, and does that modulation contribute to self-maintenance (2.2)? "Fog/landing/gate drift means minds cannot close" conflates inability to close (a closure failure) with absence of asymmetric modulation and with absence of viability-relative regulation — the paper insists these are distinct failure modes (Table 1).

2. **It operationalises each condition so the misreading can be caught in review without new runtime.** Individuality: is the boundary system-generated or observer-accorded (workplace/clock/robot examples p.369–370, Jonas 1968 regress)? Asymmetry: Eq.3 interval-T modulation of `p ⊂ Q`, not continuous energy dominance, with surfing as the positive example (pp.371–372) and diver-at-cliff as contextuality of Q — same muscle sequence does different work depending on coupling constraints. Normativity: internally generated viability conditions under precariousness (Jonas "to be is its intrinsic goal" p.376; Di Paolo 2009), not "fog is bad because the operator says so". The paper's own boundary note (p.378) explicitly permits non-metabolic autonomy (sensorimotor loops, habits), which licenses applying the vocabulary to this plant's couplings without claiming biological aliveness.

3. **It supplies a discardable check that is stricter than the recent paper's.** Tomczyk 2025 (recent, pp.58–62) uses this same paper to note that autopoiesis ≠ sensorimotor autonomy and adds the observer-cut warning; this source adds the generative definition (p.377) and the Table 1 counterexample set that can be applied as a literal checklist. Where Tomczyk flagged the cut, Barandiaran et al. give the test for which side of the cut a drift belongs to.

Limits retained: the paper defers complexity to spatio-temporality (§5) and to layered autonomy hierarchies (p.378); none of that is imported. The enactive–ecological and functionalism tensions noted by Tomczyk are not re-litigated here; this review imports only the three-condition machinery and the generative definition the footnotes already invoke.
