## What we read

**The Interdisciplinary Study of Coordination** — Thomas W. Malone and Kevin Crowston, MIT Center for Coordination Science Technical Report CCS WP #157, November 1993; published as *ACM Computing Surveys* 26(1), March 1994, pp. 87–119 — read full text at:

- <https://ccs.mit.edu/papers/CCSWP157.html> (HTML, MIT CCS, CC BY-equivalent public TR, ~38 pp., TOC §§1–5 + Appendices A/B + REFERENCES)
- <https://doi.org/10.1145/182139.182140> (ACM DL DOI 10.1145/182139.182140, same paper)

Four passages carry the transferable mechanism, each with the plant-level analogue it replaces.

1. **Coordination = managing dependencies between activities (Abstract; §2.1 "What is coordination?", p. ~2–4).** "Coordination can be seen as the process of managing dependencies between activities. Further progress, therefore, should be possible by characterizing different kinds of dependencies and identifying the coordination processes that can be used to manage them." The paper makes the definition operational: without a dependency there is nothing to coordinate; with one, coordination is the extra activity that manages it. It rejects the intuition "well-coordinated = smooth" — good coordination is nearly invisible; poor coordination is visible (airport gate, double-booked hotel, OS-incompatible word processor). The move is to name the dependency first, then choose a process for it, not to treat "coordination" as a single scalar.

2. **A shared-resource-to-process taxonomy with three relations per dependency (Table 1, §2.2; §2.2.1–2.2.4, Table 2 p. ~5–12).** Table 1 lists dependencies and, for each, alternative coordination processes — the paper's core inventory:
   - **Shared resources** (§2.2.1) — multiple activities use one limited resource (money, storage, actor time). Processes: resource allocation / task assignment; mechanisms surveyed include markets (decentralized pricing/bidding, Simon 1981; Smith 1776), managerial authority/hierarchy, and first-come-first-served/locking. Key observation: markets can be decentralized yet coherent without a central controller.
   - **Producer / consumer** (§2.2.2) — one activity produces what another consumes (assembly line, one program's output is another's input, one person's information used by another). Three specializations: *prerequisite constraints* (consumer cannot start before producer finishes), *transfer* (move the thing, with inventory/Just-in-Time/EOQ management), *usability* (consumer must be able to use what was produced — standardization, ask users, participatory design, concurrent engineering / design for manufacturability).
   - **Simultaneity constraints** (§2.2.3) — activities must (or must not) occur at the same time (meeting scheduling, concurrent instruction streams on one or many processors). Processes: scheduling and synchronization (mutual exclusion, rendezvous).
   - **Task / subtask** (§2.2.4) — a set of activities are subtasks of a larger goal. Processes: *goal selection* (choose the goal) and *goal decomposition* (choose the subtasks that achieve it — strategic planning, top-down design). §2.2.5 *Managing other dependencies* adds shared-reputation and shared-characteristics as reminders the list is suggestive, not exhaustive; Table 2 then cross-lists how the same processes appear in CS, economics, organization theory, and biology.

3. **The task-assignment worked example and "other processes needed for coordination" (§2.3, §2.4, pp. ~13–18).** §2.3.1 defines a generic task assignment as a shared-resource dependency (one task, many potential actors) and §2.3.2 enumerates *alternative* mechanisms for the same dependency: market-like bidding, managerial assignment, self-selection, rotation/negotiation — the same dependency admits multiple processes with different cost/fairness/knowledge tradeoffs. §2.4 makes two processes first-class rather than implicit: *group decision-making* (proposing, evaluating, choosing — §2.4.1) and *communication* (§2.4.2, with Malone's own §3.3 discussion of incentive/cooperative-work tool adoption). The paper's §3 then applies the lens in three domains including cooperative-work tools and distributed/parallel systems (§3.3, §3.4) — computer coordination is one instance, not a separate theory.

4. **Two analytic stances that bound the transfer ( §3.1, §3.1.1–3.1.3).** §3.1.1 distinguishes *parametric analysis* (compare mechanisms on cost/delay/robustness) from *baseline analysis* (ask what would happen without the coordination process at all). The paper warns the lists are not exhaustive and that characterizing a dependency is prior to picking a mechanism; the wrong mechanism for the right dependency still fails. Limits stated: taxonomy is suggestive (§2.2.5); quantitative results about coordination come from other fields instantiated in Appendix B, not from this framework alone; physical vs human vs computational instantiations share structure but differ in cost.

**Limits the source states, which this review keeps.** The paper is a framework/synthesis (CCS WP → survey), not an experiment; it does not prescribe thresholds, does not rank mechanisms universally, and does not claim exhaustiveness — Table 1 is "by no means intended to be exhaustive" and §2.2.5 invites new dependencies. Winner among mechanisms is situational. Byzantine/permissioned distinctions are out of scope.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** The field `coordination-and-tasks` names "addressed messages, walls and settlement" — the plant's coordination system. The *angle* is *transfer*: a cross-domain transfer of this area onto one of this plant's parts — the paper's explicit project is inter-disciplinary transfer (CS ↔ organization theory ↔ economics ↔ biology) via the dependency vocabulary, so the transfer is the mechanism's own history applied once more, onto `task_state.py`.

## What this plant already does

*Tasks are homogeneous lifecycle tokens; dependencies are implicit in lock order and eligibility, not named — exactly the pre-taxonomy state §2.1 is written to replace.*

`src/mishe_tauftauf/task_state.py:22` `TaskState` carries `identity`, `owner`, `status ∈ {ready,waiting,done,dropped}`, `activity`, `sequence`, `next_step`, `reason`, `helpers`, `retry_event/retry_at/retry_task`, `evidence`/`evidence_sha256`, `delivery`, `attempt_wake/attempt_observation`, `parent`, `managed`, `offer_evidence`, `delivery_token`. `registry()` (:140) projects one lifecycle per task identity by scanning `FeedEntry` bodies for `TASK_RE`/`STATE_RE`/`CLAIM_RE` and typed `[task-state]/[task-add]/[task-close]/[task-reopen]/[task-claim]` payloads validated via `records.payload`. `states()` (:241) filters to non-terminal, `eligible()` (:255) checks `status==ready` plus optional `retry_at`, `candidates()` (:294) and `select_task()` (:503) offer advisory scheduling sorted by `sequence`, and `claim()` (:429) / `set_step()` (:552) / `wait_for()` (:570) / `offer()` (:623) / `add_task()` (:693) publish transitions under the supervisor's seed lock.

`src/mishe_tauftauf/feed.py:42` `publication_lock` (`threading.RLock` + `fcntl.flock` on `.publication.lock`) serialises appends to `v1 {sequence:020d} {timestamp} {source}` entries; `src/mishe_tauftauf/wall.py:312` `write` enforces `WALL_MAX_BYTES/LINES` and peer-copy guards; `src/mishe_tauftauf/wall.py:433` `message` appends `[dm] to=ROLE`; `src/mishe_tauftauf/wall.py:648` `settle` / `wall.py:909` `deliver` publish settlement. `task_state.py:549` `_append` is the only writer of task transitions.

*What that means in Malone's terms.* Every dependency the plant manages is rendered as a generic eligibility or lock effect, without naming which kind it is:

- **Shared resource** — the single `publication_lock` and the single advisory queue (`candidates` sorted by `sequence`) are the resource allocator for *every* task, regardless of whether the task contends for tape, wall, human attention, or a site replant. There is no distinction between first-come-first-served, managerial assignment, or market-like bidding — `select_task` is arrival-order advisory with no per-resource threshold. This is §2.2.1 before Table 1 is applied.
- **Producer / consumer** — a wall edit that must precede a settlement (prerequisite), a `[dm]` that must be transferred, and a wall body that must be *usable* by its reader (standardization/ask-users) are all the same `TaskState.next_step` string — the prerequisite/transfer/usability trisection of §2.2.2 is not represented. `coordination/site_sync.py:326` `_close_stale_sync_tasks` and `chat_protocol.py:28` `LifecycleEvent` already close stale `kernel-sync-*` obligations on replant, i.e. a task/subtask decomposition (§2.2.4) where a parent replant supersedes children, but that decomposition is ad-hoc string handling ("kernel-sync-"), not a typed `parent`/`managed` relation carried beyond `TaskState.parent: str|None` (:31) which is only checked on `task-add` (:216) and `task-reopen` (:225) for existence.
- **Simultaneity** — `wall.py:668` `RETRY_HOLD_SECONDS=1800` decides liveness by timeout, and `task_state.py:51` `_deadline` parses `retry_at`, but there is no scheduling/synchronization vocabulary distinguishing "must occur together" (rendezvous) from "must not overlap" (mutual exclusion) — §2.2.3 is collapsed to one `retry_at`.
- **Group decision-making / communication** — `task_state.py:637` `signal()` and `wall.py:433` `message()` exist as communication primitives (§2.4.2), but the task does not record which dependency's decision they serve.

*Consequence the mechanism predicts.* Without named dependencies, any tuning of "coordination" (e.g. changing `candidates` sort, publication_lock fairness, or retry delay) moves all dependencies at once. The paper's §2.3.2 prediction is that the same dependency should be managed by *comparing* alternative processes — without typing, comparison is impossible. And §3.1.1's parametric vs baseline question ("what would happen without this coordination?") cannot be asked per dependency — only globally.

*The plant's best nearby practice.* `task_state.py:216` `parent`/`managed` is already a task/subtask decomposition hook, and `task_state.py:298` `reserved = set(pending_tasks(...).values())` already prevents double-assignment of a shared resource (an actor's attention). `feed.py:42` lock and `task_state.py:549` single-writer `_append` are the honest shared-resource allocator the paper surveys — they just do not carry the dependency name.

## What is new here

**Name the dependency before choosing the process — stop treating all tasks as homogeneous tokens ordered by arrival.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number in this repository where it lands and each bounded by what the source concedes.

1. **Typed dependencies on TaskState, not a new scheduler — shared-resource / prerequisite / transfer / usability / simultaneity / task-subtask as an additive field (§2.1, Table 1).** Today `TaskState` has no `dependency` field; every task's coordination is eligibility + lock order. The transfer adds `dependency: str` (values from Table 1: `shared-resource`, `prerequisite`, `transfer`, `usability`, `simultaneity`, `task-subtask`, `other`) and optional `depends_on: str|None` (the producer identity for prerequisite/transfer) beside `parent`/:31, carried in the `[task-state]` payload exactly as `helpers`/`evidence` are (validated in `registry()` :183–236, filtered through `fields(TaskState)` :191). This does not change `sequence` ordering or `publication_lock`; it labels what is being managed so §3.1.1 parametric analysis ("without this coordination vs with process X") can be asked per dependency rather than per plant.

2. **One process per dependency, compared before picking — prerequisite uses notification/sequencing/tracking, shared-resource uses allocation, simultaneity uses scheduling/synchronization (Table 1, §2.3.2).** Instead of one advisory `candidates()` sorted by `sequence` for every task, `candidates()` gains an optional `dependency=` filter (pure view, no new lock) so callers that manage prerequisite tasks can select notification/sequencing (`eligible` + `depends_on` done-check + `signal` tracking) while callers that manage shared-resource tasks select allocation (`reserved` + `claim`). The paper's §2.3.2 market vs hierarchy vs self-selection comparison is realized as: for `shared-resource`, compare `select_task` (first-come) vs `add_task` with explicit `owner` (managerial assignment) vs `offer/claim` helpers (self-selection/bidding) — the three already exist in `task_state.py:623` `offer` / `:693` `add_task` / `:429` `claim`, but only with typed dependencies can their tradeoff be measured. For `prerequisite`, `eligible()` is extended to check `depends_on` terminal status (`registry[depends_on].status in {done,dropped}`) before returning true — the prerequisite constraint (§2.2.2) that is currently implicit in human next_step prose.

3. **Task/subtask decomposition and group decision-making as recorded coordination, not string convention (§2.2.4, §2.4.1).** `TaskState.parent` already links a child to a parent for goal decomposition, but `site_sync.py:326` task identity `"kernel-sync-{sha[:12]}-{target}"` and `_close_stale_sync_tasks` manage subtask supersession by string prefix, not by the typed relation. The transfer makes `parent` decomposition typed (`dependency=task-subtask`) so `registry()` can validate that closing a parent with `finish(..., status=done)` via `task-close` requires children in `{done,dropped}` — the goal-decomposition invariant the paper names. Group decision signals (`signal()` :637, `[dm]` delivery) then carry `dependency` so `lines()`/:667 and `board()`/:393 can render "decision pending for shared-usability" vs "prerequisite waiting" — the §2.4.1 proposing/evaluating/choosing vocabulary that is currently one `reason` string.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** Table 1 is suggestive, not exhaustive — the `other` value preserves that boundary (§2.2.5). No mechanism is ranked universally; the proposal only makes comparison *possible* by typing, it does not mandate market vs hierarchy for any dependency. The paper's quantitative Appendices are not imported — analysis remains local to the plant's own `checks/` and `TaskState` payload. No new lock, max, or wire format is implied beyond the additive payload field.
