# Coordination, delivery and release — transfer: DvP settlement models as the linkage between code delivery and consumer activation

## What we read

- Bank for International Settlements, Committee on Payment and Settlement Systems, *Delivery Versus Payment in Securities Settlement Systems*, Basle, September 1992. The landing HTML at https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d06.pdf (334 B HTML redirect) was not used.
- Passages read directly (page numbers from extracted text, section numbers original):
  - §1.7 p.2: "In some markets no mechanism exists to ensure that delivery occurs if and only if payment occurs. Without such a mechanism (delivery versus payment) counterparties are exposed to principal risk, that is, the risk that the seller of a security could deliver but not receive payment or that the buyer of a security could make payment but not receive delivery."
  - §1.8 p.3: "Because principal risk involves the full value of the securities transferred, a default … may well entail credit losses so sizable as to create systemic problems. For this reason, it is critical … to create the strongest possible linkage between delivery and payment. Even if principal risk is eliminated … participants are still exposed to replacement cost risk and liquidity risk."
  - §1.8 p.3 analogy the report keeps: "Principal risk in securities settlements is readily seen to be analogous to what is termed cross-currency settlement risk (Herstatt risk) in foreign exchange settlements."
  - §1.9 p.3–4 the three models: Model 1 "settle transfer instructions for both securities and funds on a trade-by-trade (gross) basis, with final (unconditional) transfer of securities … occurring at the same time as final transfer of funds"; Model 2 "settle securities … on a gross basis … throughout the processing cycle, but settle funds … on a net basis … at the end of the processing cycle"; Model 3 "settle transfer instructions for both securities and funds on a net basis, with final transfers of both … at the end of the processing cycle."
  - §1.10 p.4 Model 1 caveat: even Model 1 "can eliminate principal risk … However, the completion of settlements … may require participants to maintain substantial money balances … high 'fail' rates may result, implying substantial liquidity risk and replacement cost risk … most model 1 systems extend credit … it creates another source of risks — the failure of a participant to repay."
  - §1.11–1.12 Model 2/3 principal risk recreates via collateral: Model 2 "clearly has the potential to expose sellers … to substantial principal risk … creation of an assured payment system … guarantor typically seeks a lien … Nonetheless, the guarantor may be exposed to credit risk of the same magnitude as principal risk"; Model 3 "can eliminate principal risk … However, some model 3 systems inadvertently allow credit risk of the same magnitude as principal risk to arise."
  - §1.13 p.5 extension: "nearly all … systems … extend credit to their participants, either explicitly … or tacitly by allowing funds transfer instructions to be settled on a net basis … The primary question … is how well the system could cope with the failure of one or more participants (or guarantor banks) to repay such credit extensions."
  - Scope note §1.20 cross-border not read for this review; §1.8 footnote "This report focuses on transfers … between direct participants … Even if a DVP mechanism is available for such transfers, transfers between direct participants and third parties (their customers) may not receive the protection" — kept as explicit limit.

## What is new here

The report's transfer is narrow enough to apply without importing finance policy. Its claim is structural: principal risk is the *full-value* loss when delivery happens without payment or vice versa (§1.7, §1.8); DVP eliminates it only by the iff linkage "delivery occurs if and only if payment occurs"; even then replacement and liquidity risk remain; and each model's risk profile depends on safeguards more than on the model label (§1.10). Fail rates, collateral quality, lien enforceability and unwind pressure (§1.14–1.15) are the measurement points, not the model diagram.

For this plant the mapping is: securities delivery = main contains exact head (the code); funds payment = consumers act on it (loaded bytes equal head). The current pipeline is Model 2 — securities gross throughout the cycle, payment net at the end via a single rollout file — and it mimics the report's warning that Model 2 "clearly has the potential to expose sellers … to substantial principal risk" unless an assured payment (here, an observer-evidenced consumer set) backs it, and that even Model 1 recreates equivalent credit risk if balances are insufficient and intraday credit is extended (§1.10 third paragraph). The dashboard's LIMITED shedding is that credit extension in process terms: observer capacity lent to complete settlements.

What the plant does not yet surface is therefore: which DVP model a given `finish` claims, whether principal risk is actually eliminated (every direct participant paid simultaneously), and which residual risks dominate after the linkage is claimed.

## Application

target: src/mishe_tauftauf/delivery.py

Proposal for genome (owner; genome pushes `main`, checks exact-SHA CI and live consumers, so the change is theirs to author or decline). The predecessor transfer's observer-acknowledgement proposal (Saltzer) and this DVP linkage are complementary; this proposal adds the settlement-model layer without changing the CI gate or the observer itself.

1. Add pure, deterministic vocabulary to `delivery.py` (no I/O, no HOLD change): constants `DVP_MODELS = ("model-1-gross-simultaneous","model-2-gross-then-net","model-3-net-both")` naming the report's §1.9 three models, plus helpers `delivery_leg_is_complete(record)` (head == integrated), `payment_leg_is_complete(consumer_shas, head)` (every direct consumer SHA == head in the same observation window), and `dvp_model_for(record, consumer_presence)` returning the model label and whether the iff linkage holds. Name the residual in the return: `{"model":..., "principal_risk": bool, "residual": "liquidity|replacement|none"}` where `principal_risk` is true iff `delivery_leg != payment_leg` (§1.7 definition), matching the report's "full value" language.

2. In `finish`, keep the existing exact-SHA CI check and phase check unchanged. After the `rollout.json` provenance read, also accept an optional live observation snapshot (the same bytes `wall_patch --observe` reads, or per-consumer `runtime-release.json` loaded SHA set); when present, compute `dvp_model_for` and append one informational line to `record["reason"]` via the existing `line()` suffix pattern used by the 2026-11-02 Helland critique (`scope=...`): e.g. `dvp=model-1 principal=eliminated residual=liquidity` or `dvp=model-2 principal=exposed residual=principal`. When the observation is absent, record `dvp=unknown principal=unknown`. No new HOLD or fail: the field is informational in this step, exactly as the report distinguishes model labelling from the safeguards that actually contain risk (§1.10 "depends more on the specific risk management safeguards … than on which model is employed").

3. Render `line(home)` to include the DVP suffix so `delivery show`/`delivery line` surfaces whether the last `finish` was Model-1-simultaneous or Model-2-deferred, and whether principal risk was eliminated. The report's scope footnote (§1.8 fn2) — protection between direct participants only — maps to direct consumers listed in the deployment manifest; third-party/customer consumers remain out of the principal-risk claim and should be labelled `indirect` when present.

Tests (deterministic, no network, live verification separate):
  - `dvp_model_for` with head integrated and all consumer SHAs == head in one window → `model-1` , `principal_risk == False`.
  - `dvp_model_for` with head integrated but one consumer still on previous SHA → `model-2` shape, `principal_risk == True` (delivery without payment).
  - `dvp_model_for` with no observation → `model unknown`, `principal_risk` unknown, no exception.
  - `finish` with missing observation still completes on current criteria and records `dvp=unknown`.

Live verification after activation (running-code change): exercise one real delivery twice — once with a consumer held on the previous SHA (controlled failure: `delivery line` must show `principal=exposed` and `finish` must still refuse on the existing CI/consumers check, the DVP field merely names why) and once after consumers roll (expect `principal=eliminated`). The report's §1.10 fail-rate and §1.15 unwind pressure map to the `residual` label and to the already-shedding controller status; preserving that evidence as UNKNOWN/RED when observation lags is correct per §1.8 footnote.

Limits. The report is 1992 G-10 securities settlement, not software delivery; the mapping to code-vs-activation is an analogy, and the report's netting/settlement-bank/custodian safeguards (§1.14–1.17) and Lamfalussy standards (Annex 4) have no direct plant counterpart. Model labels are structural, not safety proofs: the report's Caveat §1.10–1.13 that safeguards matter more than the model applies here — labelling `model-1` does not itself eliminate principal risk without the observer that makes payment leg complete. Cross-border transfers (§1.20) and customer protection beyond direct participants are explicitly out of scope here, as they are in the report.
