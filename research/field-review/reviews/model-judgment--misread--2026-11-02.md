# Post-hoc calibration does not survive shift — measure trust where the evidence shifts

## What we read

**Yaniv Ovadia, Emily Fertig, Jie Ren, Zachary Nado, D. Sculley, Sebastian Nowozin, Joshua V. Dillon, Balaji Lakshminarayanan & Jasper Snoek — "Can You Trust Your Model's Uncertainty? Evaluating Predictive Uncertainty Under Dataset Shift", NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1906.02530v2 [stat.ML] 17 Dec 2019 (33rd NeurIPS, Vancouver)** — read the full primary source at:

- <https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530> (arXiv abstract, v2 17 Dec 2019)
- <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02530.pdf> (PDF 5_887_184 bytes fetched 200 2026-11-02 at /tmp/ovadia19.pdf, 25 pp., Creator pdfTeX-1.40.17, header `Can You Trust Your Model's Uncertainty? Evaluating Predictive Uncertainty Under Dataset Shift`)
- <https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/8558cb408c1d76621371888657d2eb1d-Abstract.html> (NeurIPS proceedings landing, same paper)
- Code and predictions: <https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/uq> (benchmark 2019, cited p.2 fn.4)

The paper is the large-scale benchmark that directly tests whether the calibration fix the previous `model-judgment--misread` (Guo et al. ICML 2017, temperature scaling) actually holds when the input leaves the validation distribution — the foundational idea this plant has misread. Four passages carry what this plant has misread — page numbers are the arXiv v2 PDF, equations/figures as numbered there, quotations via `pdftotext`:

1. **The misread stated — i.i.d. calibration is not shift calibration (§1 p.2, §2 p.2–3).** "Another popular non-Bayesian approach involves re-calibration of probabilities on a held-out validation set through temperature scaling (Platt, 1999), which was shown by Guo et al. (2017) to lead to well-calibrated predictions on the i.i.d. test set." Immediately reframed: "One reason for this is that post-hoc calibration gives good results in independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) regimes, but can fail under even a mild shift in the input data." (§1 p.2, col.2). Background (§2 p.3) defines two shifts evaluated throughout: covariate-shifted versions where `q(x,y) ≠ p*(x,y)` but label stays in `{1..k}` (Hendrycks & Dietterich 2019 corruptions) and fully OOD datasets where label is outside `k` — the two diagnostics the plant conflates into one "accuracy".

2. **How they measure — same four numbers every shift intensity must carry (§3 p.3–4).** Seven methods (§3 p.3): Vanilla (max softmax, Hendrycks & Gimpel 2017), Temp Scaling (Guo 2017), MC-Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani 2016), Ensembles `M=10` (Lakshminarayanan et al. 2017), SVI, LL-SVI, LL-Dropout (Riquelme et al. 2018). Four metrics (§3 p.4): NLL `↓` (proper scoring, over-weights tails), Brier Score `↓` Eq.(1) `BS = |Y|⁻¹ Σ_y (p(y|x)−δ(y−y_n))² = |Y|⁻¹(1−2p(y_n|x)+Σ_y p(y|x)²)` with DeGroot & Fienberg decomposition `BS = uncertainty − resolution + reliability` (Bröcker 2009), ECE `↓` `ECE = Σ_s |B_s|/N |acc(B_s)−conf(B_s)|` with buckets `B_s = {n: p(y_n|x)∈(ρ_s,ρ_{s+1}]}` and `ŷ_n = argmax_y p(y|x)` (Naeini et al. 2015), and entropy / confidence histograms on OOD where no ground truth exists. Crucial caveat kept: "Neither [ECE nor entropy] is a proper scoring rule, and thus there exist trivial solutions which yield optimal scores; for example, returning the marginal probability p(y) for every instance" — ECE alone can be gamed, and Bröcker notes each proper scoring rule induces its own calibration measure but ECE is not that decomposition.

3. **The finding that breaks the i.i.d. reading — temp scaling fails under shift, ensembles do not (§4.1 p.5 Fig.1, §4.2 pp.6–7 Figs.2–3).** MNIST (§4.1 Fig.1a–b): rotated/translated LeNet — accuracy degrades similarly for all methods, but Brier diverges; SVI worst on i.i.d. test yet best under strong shift; "while calibrating on the validation set leads to well-calibrated predictions on the test set, it does not guarantee calibration on shifted data. In fact, nearly all other methods (except vanilla) perform better than the state-of-the-art post-hoc calibration (Temperature scaling) in terms of Brier score under shift." (p.5). CIFAR-10 / ImageNet (§4.2 Fig.2 p.6, Fig.3 p.7): 16 corruption types ×5 intensities =80 shifted sets (Hendrycks & Dietterich 2019). Box plots per intensity: ensembles achieve highest accuracy under shift; "while temperature scaling achieves low ECE for low values of shift, the ECE increases significantly as the shift increases, which indicates that calibration on the i.i.d. validation dataset does not guarantee calibration under distributional shift." (p.7, col.1). Capacity control: doubling filters for Vanilla/Dropout does not recover ensemble's lead — not just larger aggregate capacity (Appx.C). OOD diagnostics (Fig.3c/f, Fig.1e–f): ensembles and dropout have higher entropy / lower overconfidence on SVHN / NotMNIST; temp scaling gives highest entropy on OOD but not best Brier — the method that hedges most is not the most accurate. Text (20newsgroups LSTM, §4.3) and categorical (Criteo, §4.4) show same ordering with smaller gaps — modality matters.

4. **What to report instead — accuracy–confidence and entropy together, stratified by intensity (§3 p.4, §4.1–4.2, Appx.G).** "There is no ground truth label for fully OOD inputs. Thus we report histograms of confidence and predictive entropy on known and OOD inputs and accuracy versus confidence plots" (§3 p.4): `confidence = p(y=ŷ|x)=max_k p(y=k|x)`, threshold `τ∈[0,1]` filtering. Fig.1c–d / Fig.3a–b show accuracy vs confidence and count-vs-confidence at a fixed corruption level (Gaussian blur intensity 3). Fig.2 box plots show per-method ECE vs intensity with quartiles/min/max across 16 types. The actionable limit: shift is evaluated at 6 points (valid, test, intensities 1–5), not as one pooled number — pooling hides the slope.

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** Results are classification with softmax logits and held-out validation tuning, on LeNet/MNIST, ResNet-20/50 CIFAR-10/ImageNet, LSTM/20newsgroups, and Criteo — not verbalized LLM `P(yes)` without logits, though the benchmark's conclusion about post-hoc scaling is method-level. Shifts are synthetic corruptions + one OOD dataset per modality; real evidence shift (new phrasing, new claim shape, new reasoning depth) is not imaged rotation/blur — the transfer is the measurement shape, not the pixel kernel. No dataset-specific ECE/Brier/entropy number is imported. ECE binning (`S` buckets, quantile vs even) affects values (§3 caveat); the paper uses standard `S` but notes estimation error varies when `|B_s|≠|B_k|`. `M=10` ensembles and dropout `p` are defaults, not fitted plant constants. Synthetic shift intensity 1–5 is a reporting convention, not a threshold.

**Why this source, for this cell.** `model-judgment--misread` asks for a foundational idea we may have misread. The plant's `model-judgment` field already embodies Guo's definitions (ECE/MCE, temperature scaling Eq.9 that preserves argmax) via the proposed `model-judgment--misread 2026-10-30` helpers in `jev_judge.py`, and Nixon's binning critique and Lakshminarayanan's ensemble averaging via the next two reviews — all measured on one held-out `(evidence, expected)` split. Ovadia is the paper that proves that reading misleads: the same `T` and same ECE that look "calibrated" on `valid→test` fail at intensity 1, and the slope across intensities is the trustworthiness test the plant never runs.

## What is new here

**Calibration is a curve over shift intensity, not a scalar on the validation set. Report ECE/Brier/accuracy together per intensity, plus the two Ovadia diagnostics that catch a silently overconfident judge on OOD: accuracy-vs-confidence and entropy/confidence histograms. Post-hoc temperature scaling is the enumerated misread; model-marginalization (ensembles/dropout) is the family that survives the slope.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each bounded by what the paper concedes:

1. **From "ECE = 0.013" to "ECE(intensity)" — the i.i.d. scalar misleads (Ovadia §1 p.2, §4.1 p.5, §4.2 p.7, Fig.2).** The paper's explicit sentence is the transfer: "calibration on the i.i.d. validation dataset does not guarantee calibration under distributional shift." Operationally this means the plant must never publish a single pooled calibration number as "the judge is calibrated." Instead publish the vector `ECE(0)=valid, ECE(test), ECE(1)…ECE(5)` and `Brier(0)…Brier(5)` on the same held-out `(evidence, expected∈{yes,no,unknown})` pairs partitioned by shift — for this plant, shift is not pixel blur but evidence shape: the held-out set sliced by a measurable shift proxy (e.g. markdown fence density, evidence token count quartile, or a synthetic perturbation suite analogous to Hendrycks' 16×5). The Brier decomposition `BS = uncertainty − resolution + reliability` (§3 Eq.1 note with Bröcker/DeGroot & Fienberg) is the reliability term's proper-score anchor; ECE remains reported because it is intuitive (§3) but never alone. No intensity threshold is imported — `1…5` is a reporting convention, not a plant gate.

2. **Two diagnostics that a single ECE hides — accuracy@confidence and confidence/entropy histograms (Ovadia §3 p.4, Fig.1c–f, Fig.3).** `confidence = max_k p(y=k|x)`, `accuracy@τ` filters to `{n: confidence ≥ τ}` (Lakshminarayanan et al. 2017), and the histogram pair `Count vs Confidence` at a fixed shift level plus `Entropy on OOD` (Fig.1d–e, Fig.3b–c/e–f) separate "confident and right" from "confident and wrong." A judge that holds `accuracy@0.9 ≈ 0.95` on valid but `accuracy@0.9 ≈ 0.62` at intensity 3 is miscalibrated under shift even if pooled ECE moves little — exactly the gap Fig.1c vs 1d and Fig.3a vs 3b expose. For OOD evidence (evidence whose label is not in `{yes,no,unknown}` — e.g. a malformed window), no ground truth exists, so the plant reports only `confidence` and `predictive_entropy = −Σ_k p_k log p_k` histograms (Fig.1e–f). This keeps Ovadia's §3 warning that ECE/entropy are not proper scoring rules: returning `p(y)` for every instance is perfectly calibrated but useless — the histograms catch that trivial solution.

3. **Method ordering under shift — ensembles marginalize, temp scaling does not (Ovadia §4.2 p.7, §3 p.3, Appx.C).** Across MNIST/CIFAR-10/ImageNet, ensembles (`M=10`, with `M=5` already capturing most gains per Appx.C) are top on accuracy and Brier/ECE under shift; MC-dropout second; temp scaling below both despite winning i.i.d. The capacity control (wider Vanilla/Dropout with double filters shows no recovery, Appx.C) means the gap is not just parameters. The plant's implication is not "train 10 Jev's" — hosted judges are black-box — but that a caller-held ensemble formed from `M` independent calls (different model alias / temperature / prompt paraphrase, as already proposed in `model-judgment--mechanism` via Lakshminarayanan) is the shift-robust family to prefer when reporting the curve, while a single `τ`-scaled `p(y|x)` is the family expected to degrade. SVI's MNIST win vs ImageNet loss (Fig.1 vs Fig.2) is kept as the modality caveat: ordering is consistent image→image but not image→text, so the plant measures ordering on its own text shift, not imports it.

**Limits the review keeps from the paper.** Only the *measurement shape* (per-intensity ECE/Brier/accuracy + histograms + thresholds) and the *method-family expectation* (post-hoc scaling brittle, marginalization robust) transfer; no MNIST/CIFAR/ImageNet ECE value, no `M=10` as fitted plant constant, no Hendrycks pixel kernel is imported as literal evidence perturbation. Quantile-vs-even binning caveat (§3 `|B_s|≈|B_k|`) and bin count sensitivity are kept. The OOD histogram has no accuracy counterpart by construction. The plant's existing `unknown` (None) for incomplete evidence stays the detector for missing pairs; the new helpers only add the second clause Ovadia distinguishes: *calibrated on what distribution*.
