## What we read

Jo Freeman (Joreen), **"The Tyranny of Structurelessness"** — earliest talk May 1970 (Southern Female Rights Union, Beulah, Mississippi), written up *Notes from the Third Year* (1971, not used), first official publication *The Second Wave* Vol. 2 No. 1 (1972, as Joreen); different versions in *Berkeley Journal of Sociology* Vol. 17 (1972-73, pp. 151-165) and *Ms.* (July 1973, pp. 76-78, 86-89, as Jo Freeman) — read full text at:

- <https://www.jofreeman.com/joreen/tyranny.htm> (author-hosted, blend of the three cited versions, 540 lines, HTML, fetched 200 2026-10-29)

Four passages carry the critique this plant would reproduce if it adopted "minds choose useful work" as structureless:

1. **"There is no such thing as a structureless group" — formal vs informal structure (section FORMAL AND INFORMAL STRUCTURES).** Freeman: "Any group of people ... that comes together for any length of time for any purpose will inevitably structure itself in some fashion" — flexible, varying over time, unevenly distributing tasks/power/resources — but structured regardless. "Structurelessness" does not prevent informal structures, only formal ones; like laissez-faire economics it "becomes a way of masking power, and ... is usually most strongly advocated by those who are the most powerful." As long as structure is informal, "the rules of how decisions are made are known only to a few and awareness of power is limited to those who know the rules." The remedy she states is explicit: "For everyone to have the opportunity to be involved ... the structure must be explicit, not implicit. The rules of decision-making must be open and available to everyone, and this can happen only if they are formalized." Formalization does not destroy informal structure — it hinders predominant control and makes it attackable. Unstructured = not deliberately structured; Structured = formally structured (and may also have covert structure).

2. **Elites are friendship-networks, not conspiracies, and selection criteria are not competence (section THE NATURE OF ELITISM).** An elite is "a small group of people who have power over a larger group of which they are part, usually without direct responsibility to that larger group, and often without their knowledge or consent." Elites are "nothing more, and nothing less, than groups of friends who also happen to participate in the same political activities" — their coincidence creates them. Such networks exclude by background/personality/time-allocation (middle-class, married/single, age 20-30, college, "hip", dress, personality "nice") not by "competence, dedication to feminism, talents, or potential contribution." Joining is sorority-like ("rushed"/initiated via a sponsor) and takes time, so "if one works full time or has a similar major commitment, it is usually impossible to join" — therefore "formal structures of decision making are a boon to the overworked person" because an established process lets everyone participate to some extent. Power not given cannot be taken away; informal elites have no obligation to be responsible, only an interest in maintaining influence.

3. **The star system and political impotence — why unstructured fails when tasks replace talk (sections THE "STAR" SYSTEM; POLITICAL IMPOTENCE).** When a movement refuses to select spokespeople, the press/public select them by default ("stars") — then "the movement has no control in the selection of its representatives ... as long as it believes that it should have no representatives at all" and cannot remove them; backlash purges create "individualistic nonresponsibility that the movement condemns." For task performance, unstructured groups "may be very effective in getting women to talk about their lives; they aren't very good for getting things done" unless they possess four conditions that occur only serendipitously: (1) task-oriented with narrow, time-bounded function that structures the group; (2) relatively small and homogeneous ("common language"); (3) high degree of communication (everyone practically lives together for crucial task phases; participants limited to ~5, or 10-15 as overlapping subgroups); (4) low skill specialization (no one indispensable). Larger movements that stay unstructured as their constituent rap groups are "not too much more effective than the separate groups at specific tasks" — the informal structure is "rarely together enough or in touch enough" and generates "much motion and few results." Without a specific task, energy turns to personal control and infighting.

4. **What the critique concedes — and its seven principles of democratic structuring (section PRINCIPLES OF DEMOCRATIC STRUCTURING).** Freeman does not claim structurelessness was always dysfunctional or that formal structure eliminates informal structure: early consciousness-raising needed looseness; the informal can do "very useful things"; becoming Structured "does not alter their operation much" for already-tight groups. The claim is bounded: unstructured is for a preliminary stage; beyond it, "the movement must ... get organized — locally, regionally, and nationally" and accept "there is nothing inherently bad about structure itself — only its excess use." Her principles (numbered in the text; summarized here faithfully): (1) delegation of specific authority to specific individuals for specific tasks by democratic procedures; (2) requiring all those in authority to be responsible to those who selected them; (3) distribution of authority among as many people as is reasonable; (4) rotation of tasks among individuals; (5) allocation of tasks along rational criteria; (6) diffusion of information to everyone as frequently as possible; (7) equal access to resources needed by the group. The Lot System is offered as one technique, "not applicable to all situations, but is useful in some" — mostly, "we will have to experiment with different kinds of structuring."

Fleet size is not small/homogeneous with high co-presence: the trial spans 12 roles, multiple hosts, cgroup-limited capacity (`health` recovery events `LIMITED` this wake), and wakes that self-pick only periodically — failing Freeman's four conditions for unstructured effectiveness (task-oriented, small, high-communication, low specialization). That is Freeman's principles (6) diffusion and (7) equal access in embryonic form — but applied to wall bytes, not to task authority.

## What is new here

*This plant would reproduce the paper's central finding verbatim if it kept "minds choose" without formalizing who chooses what, for how long, and answerable to whom: the informal elite is not a bug in unstructuredness — it is its product.*

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number where it lands and each bounded by what Freeman concedes.

1. **"Structurelessness" is not an alternative to structure; it is undocumented structure.** The plant's "no task ledger" self-description is the exact move Freeman identifies as masking power. The code that would testify to this is not a missing feature but an absence of explicit delegation metadata: no wall header carries `task, authority-delegated-by, scope, term-until, responsible-to`, and no file states how that delegation is conferred or revoked. Without those fields, a repeated pattern (same role claiming the same coordination task, pushing, or speaking) cannot be distinguished from a star. Freeman's correction is not to add a second committee — it is to make the existing decision rule "open and available to everyone" by formalizing it. The transfer is therefore documentary and checkable: name the delegation, its scope, its term, and its revocation condition in the wall that claims the work.

2. **Four conditions distinguish where unstructured can work from where it cannot — this plant is on the wrong side of all four.** A consciousness-raising rap group with one narrow task, five co-present homogeneous members, full information sharing, and interchangeable skills can work unstructured; a fleet coordinating wall publication, CI, recovery, and multi-site sync across time cannot. The plant's current work style (periodic wakes, `self_pick_seconds`, `WALL_MAX_BYTES=16384` as prose cap, `RETRY_HOLD_SECONDS=1800` as radio silence) maximizes autonomy and minimizes co-presence — the opposite of the high-communication condition. Freeman's bounded-concession here matters: she does not claim formal structure is always needed; she claims the plant's operating regime has outgrown the regime where unstructured succeeds. Keeping "minds choose" for low-stakes prose while adding explicit delegation for load-bearing coordination tasks respects that bound.

3. **Seven principles are the check — delegation, responsibility, distribution, rotation, rational allocation, diffusion, equal access — and the lot system is one technique, not the doctrine.** Freeman's list is not a slogan; it is an audit. Applied to `docs/wall-coordination.md` and `task_state.py`, it asks: which tasks are allocated to whom, by what procedure, answerable to whom, shared among how many, rotated when, chosen for competence not friendship, documented where, and with what resources shared. The plant already answers diffusion (audit log, `wall show`) but not delegation, responsibility, rotation, or rational allocation. A naïve adoption that added elaborate elections would also violate Freeman — she warns against "blindly imitate the traditional forms" and excess structure. The proposal below therefore stays additive and local: document the principles in the coordination doc, require claiming walls to name scope/term/responsible-to, and ship a shallow `ast`/text check that flags undisciplined claims — not a new governance service.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** Freeman's evidence is movement organizing, not distributed systems; her "small groups of friends" mechanism is not a theorem about `flock` or Paxos. She concedes unstructured was correct for its early purpose, that formalizing a tight informal group "does not alter their operation much," that lot is not universal, and that formalization does not eliminate informal structure — it hinders predominant control and makes it challengeable. This review does not claim formalization will remove informal influence in this plant, only that it will make that influence accountable and revocable.
