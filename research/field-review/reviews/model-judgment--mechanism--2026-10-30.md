# Ensembles are the mechanism: a proper scoring rule plus random-init averaging turns one overconfident judge into a calibrated distribution

## What we read

**Balaji Lakshminarayanan, Alexander Pritzel, Charles Blundell — "Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles", arXiv:1612.01474v3 [stat.ML] 4 Nov 2017 (NIPS 2017, Long Beach)** — read full primary source at:

- <https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474> (arXiv abstract, v3 4 Nov 2017)
- <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.01474.pdf> (PDF 1_552_740 bytes fetched 200 2026-10-30 at /tmp/deepensembles.pdf, 15 pp., sha256 185b16b3530d9c886b3c3811581a815b4dccee3b31b8705177ce8a3a31c259e5, Creator pdfTeX-1.40.17, 15 pages, LaTeX with hyperref)

The paper is the non-Bayesian baseline that reframes predictive uncertainty as an engineering recipe rather than an inference approximation, with toy regression, UCI, MNIST/SVHN/ImageNet, and OOD entropy experiments. Four passages carry the mechanism this plant can borrow without lifting its theorems — pagination is v3 page numbers, quotations via `pdftotext`:

1. **The three-line recipe — proper scoring rule, adversarial smoothing, ensemble averaging (§2.1 p.2, Algorithm 1 p.4).** Problem setup (§2.1): model predictive distribution `pθ(y|x)` over `K` classes or `N(µ,σ²)` for regression, dataset `D={xn,yn}ⁿ_{n=1}` i.i.d. Recipe (bullets p.2): "(1) use a proper scoring rule as the training criterion, (2) use adversarial training [18] to smooth the predictive distributions, and (3) train an ensemble." For classification the ensemble is uniformly-weighted mixture `p(y|x)=M⁻¹ Σₘ pθₘ(y|x,θₘ)` — averaging predicted probabilities (§2.4 p.4). For regression the ensemble is mixture of Gaussians approximated as single Gaussian with `µ*(x)=M⁻¹ Σₘ µθₘ(x)` and `σ*²(x)=M⁻¹ Σₘ(σ²θₘ+µ²θₘ)−µ*²`. Default `M=5`, epsilon `ε=1%` of input range (§2.4/§3.1).

2. **Why a scoring rule, and which ones — NLL and Brier are proper (§2.2 pp.2–3).** Definition: `S(pθ,q)=∫ q(y,x)S(pθ,(y,x))dydx`, proper iff `S(pθ,q) ≤ S(q,q)` with equality iff `pθ=q` (p.2). Then the two losses the plant already names are proper: `S=log pθ(y|x)` (softmax cross-entropy, Gibbs inequality) and Brier `S=−Σₖ(δ_{k=y}−pθ(k|x))²` (p.3 eq. cited as `L=−S= Σₖ(δ−p)²`). For regression the Gaussian NLL `−log pθ(yn|xn)= (y−µθ)²/(2σθ²)+log σθ²/2+const` (Eq.1 p.3, with `σ²` via softplus+1e−6). Footnote 1: asymptotically maximizing any proper scoring rule recovers true parameters. Training criterion is `L(θ)=−S`.

3. **Diversity without bootstrap — random init is enough for NNs, bagging hurts (§2.4 pp.3–4).** Background on ensembles: randomization vs boosting, Breiman bound in strength/correlation. Key choice (§2.4 p.4): "We used the entire training dataset to train each network since deep NNs typically perform better with more data … We found that random initialization of the NN parameters, along with random shuffling of the data points, was sufficient to obtain good performance in practice. We observed that bagging deteriorated performance." Rationale: bootstrap sees only 63% unique points (`1−(1−1/N)^N →1−e⁻¹`, fn.4). Lee et al. independently found same for accuracy; here it is for uncertainty. Random init + data shuffling decorrelates NNs because loss has many local optima — no convex-solver trick needed.

4. **Adversarial smoothing as directional Laplace — FGSM inside the loop (§2.3 p.3, Algorithm 1 line 5–6).** Adversarial example `x' = x + ε sign ∇ₓ ℓ(θ,x,y)` with `ℓ=−log pθ(y|x)` (Goodfellow 2014) used as augmentation `(x',y)` alongside `(x,y)` minimizing `ℓ(θ,x,y)+ℓ(θ,x',y)`. Interpretation: smooths predictive distribution in `ε`-neighbourhood along the direction where loss rises fastest — more efficient than `2ᴰ` random directions (§2.3 p.3 middle). VAT alternative noted for semi-supervised case without `y`. Effect measured on toy 1-D cubic (Fig.1 p.5: NLL alone learns variance, +adversarial smooths, +ensemble tightens OOD) and MNIST/SVHN (Fig.2 p.6: adversarial helps MNIST for all `M`, SVHN only for `M=1`; effect drops as `M` grows).

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** Requires training `M` models (cost `M×`); ensemble is mixture average, not Bayesian posterior — §1 notes BMA vs model combination distinction and that even exact BMA is not guaranteed OOD-robust. Proper scoring rule alone does not guarantee diversity — that comes from random optima. Adversarial `ε` is input-range-relative (0.01×range per dimension, §3.1) and may not change boundary when classes are well-separated (SVHN discussion p.6). Evaluation is on vision/UCI with held-out NLL/Brier/accuracy and NotMNIST OOD entropy (Fig.3 p.7) — no language-model verbalized distribution is measured. ImageNet result is ensemble only, no adversarial.

**Why this source, for this cell.** `model-judgment--mechanism` asks for an operational mechanism this area proposes — something implementable. Deep ensembles is the field's canonical non-Bayesian mechanism for predictive uncertainty (NIPS 2017, cited as Jev/Nimble alternative to Bayesian NNs/MC-dropout throughout the plant's docs). The plant's `model-judgment` field currently judges with a single hosted call per case and measures calibration point-wise (Guo ECE, Nixon GCE, Wang invert, Angelopoulos conformal) — all single-model statistics. Lakshminarayanan supplies the missing averaging operator that turns any black-box `p(y|x)` into a better-calibrated `p*(y|x)` without opening the model, touching the system prompt, or claiming logit access — exactly the gap between "one judge's word" and "what the plant should publish when stakes are higher."

- **What the sweep found.** `field_review.py next` for `model-judgment--mechanism` reports hits on `model/judge/calibration` in `checks/…` plumbing and docs — occurrence, not a mechanism. No existing symbol computes `M⁻¹ Σₘ pₘ`, no `entropy=−Σ p log p`, no `Brier=Σ(δ−p)²`, no `NLL=−log p`, no `KL(pₘ‖p*)` disagreement, no `µ*/σ*²` mixture, no `ε·sign ∇ₓℓ` smoothing, no `M=5` bookkeeping. The calibration proposals measure miscalibration; the conformal proposal wraps one `p` in a set with coverage `≥1−α`; the ensemble proposal is the third orthogonal operation: keep `p`'s family, average `M` of them.

- **Cost of staying single-call.** A single overconfident `p≈0.95` on a hard case is indistinguishable from a confident-on-easy `p≈0.95` — both look like `yes`. An ensemble of `M=5` divergent `pₘ` (e.g. `[0.9,0.55,0.7,0.6,0.85]` vs `[0.94,0.92,0.95,0.93,0.96]`) has same mean `≈0.72` vs `0.94` but very different entropy (`≈0.60` vs `≈0.25`) and disagreement (`≈0.04` vs `≈0.001`) — the second and third numbers are the operational signal for "contested / needs evidence" vs "decided" that the plant's current `unknown` discipline can only encode as a word, not as a score Lakshminarayanan shows correlates with OOD.

## What is new here

**Average `M` independent judge calls — same question, same evidence — and publish the mixture `p*(y|x)` plus its entropy, Brier and NLL, instead of the single `p(y|x)`. Diversity comes from model/prompt/seed rotation, not bootstrap; calibration comes from the proper scoring rule the mixture is scored with; smoothing comes from the adversarial direction only when a gradient is available (otherwise the ensemble does the work).**

1. **The averaging operator is the mechanism — `p(y|x)=M⁻¹ Σₘ pθₘ(y|x)`, not a second scalar on one `p` (Lakshminarayanan §2.1/§2.4, Algorithm 1).** Wang's `τ` rescales one distribution; Guo's `T` rescales one logit; Angelopoulos's `q̂` thresholds one score. Deep ensembles average `M` distributions: for `K`-way `{yes,no,unknown}` the calibrated estimate is the mean of `M` verbalized `pₘ` dicts. Fig.2 shows this alone moves MNIST NLL `0.14→<0.04` and SVHN `0.50→<0.10` as `M=1→15`, outperforming MC-dropout at same `M`; ImageNet Table 4 shows monotonic accuracy+NLL gain with `M`. For this plant: `M=5` (paper default) queried at `τ=1.0` with temperature `0.7–1.0` and two model aliases (Jev, Nimble) already suffices — Fig.3 shows unknown-class entropy rises sharply with `M` for ensembles but not for dropout, which is the OOD detector the plant lacks.

2. **Proper scoring rules make the ensemble trainable and checkable without accuracy — NLL and Brier are the two implemented scores (§2.2).** `log pθ(y|x)` (NLL) and Brier `Σₖ(δ_{k=y}−p(k|x))²` are proper: `E_q[S(pθ,q)] ≤ E_q[S(q,q)]`. Training on `−S` recovers `q` asymptotically (fn.1). Evaluation (§3.1) scores NLL+Brier beside accuracy, not instead of it, because "calibration is orthogonal to accuracy" (p.1, same point Guo makes). For the plant this resolves the misread that calibration "is" threshold tuning: NLL penalizes `p=0.99` when wrong far more than Brier, and both are defined on the held-out `(evidence, expected∈{yes,no,unknown})` split — same split Wang's `τ` needs, same `n≈500` Angelopoulos's `q̂` needs. No new `checks/*.json` shape required; the two scores sit beside `ECE` as second and third columns.

3. **Diversity without data split — full-data + random init, not bagging (§2.4).** For NNs, bagging hurts because each member sees only `63%` unique points — deep nets want more data, not less. Diversity comes from random initialization + SGD shuffling over many local optima. Transfer to black-box judges: same evidence, `M` calls with (a) different model alias (`jev-latest` vs `nimble-9b`), (b) different `temperature`/`seed` in the request, (c) prompt paraphrase that preserves meaning — the paper's "random initialization" analogue. Do not bootstrap the calibration set into `M` disjoint fifths — that is the anti-pattern the paper warns against. `M=5` is the paper's evaluated default; `M>5` gains diminish but OOD entropy keeps rising (Fig.3).

4. **Adversarial smoothing is optional and directional — when a gradient exists, smooth along `sign ∇ₓℓ`, otherwise the ensemble covers it (§2.3, §3.2 Fig.1).** Toy regression Fig.1: NLL alone learns `σ²(x)` (second panel), +adversarial smooths to `ε`-ball (third), +ensemble to far-OOD (fourth). On real benchmarks, adversarial helps most when `M=1` (MNIST Fig.2a) and is complementary to data augmentation (random crop/flip). For hosted judges with no gradient, the paper's observation that "adversarial helps most when M is small" is the actionable limit: skip `x'` construction for black-box APIs and count on `M` — do not invent a fake gradient. When local `completions_judge` is used with logit access, `x+ε sign ∇ₓℓ` augmentation is the one-line extension.

**Limits the review keeps from the source.** `M×` cost is real — the paper's ImageNet ensemble is `M` forward passes; for the plant `M=5` is `5×` daily budget (`reserve_daily_call` ×5) and wall-clock `5× TIMEOUT`. No claim that ensemble `p*` equals Bayesian posterior — §1 explicitly contrasts BMA (true model in class) vs model combination (true model outside class). Fig.2 adversarial effect shrinks as `M` grows — do not promise additive gain. All numbers are dataset/model-specific (MLP 3×200 on MNIST, VGG on SVHN, Inception on ImageNet); no accuracy/NLL/ECE is imported as a plant threshold. Heteroscedastic regression `µ/σ²` via softplus (Eq.1) is cited as the variance-learning analogue but not imported as a plant field — the plant's `y` is categorical `yes/no/unknown`.
