# The hidden prototype fallacy: naturalizing mind and agent as objects of external experience

## What we read

- **Tommi Vehkavaara — "Why and how to naturalize semiotic concepts for biosemiotics", *Sign Systems Studies* 30(1): 293–313 (2002), doi:10.12697/SSS.2002.30.1.19**, open-access journal article, Published 2002-12-31:

  - <https://doi.org/10.12697/SSS.2002.30.1.19> (DOI landing)
  - <https://ojs.utlib.ee/index.php/sss/article/view/SSS.2002.30.1.19/12497> (PDF 158_374 bytes fetched 200 2026-11-02 at /tmp/vehkavaara2002_full.pdf, 21 pp., sha256 `ecea52b0a033d035f95becbe02fa0ce14c855369d3c53efd83d47a16491c1ad3`, Creator Windows NT 4.0/Acrobat Distiller 4.05, Pages 21, CreationDate 2002-03-01T08:58:31Z, `pdftotext -layout` 922 lines retained; Abstract p.293, pp.293–304 read in full, Figure 1 Peirce classification p.298, Figure 2 object-agent/meta-agent p.300)

What the source gives, quoted from its own sentences with page anchors from the extracted layout:

- Three roles for biosemiotics in biology (p.293–295): (1) metaphor/analogy, (2) alternative philosophy/metaphysics of the living, (3) ground theory integrating vertical/horizontal biosemiosis (dual-code theory). Vehkavaara's concern is that role 1 alone "will rather die out than survive" (p.295) unless concepts are naturalized; he pursues roles 2–3.
- Naturalized vs mentalistic concepts (p.295–297): natural concepts refer "only to the objects of our external experience" (p.295); mentalistic concepts are "comprehensible only through reference to some objects of internal experience (or 'inner sense')" (p.296). Semiotic naturalism is re-definition of mentalistic concepts as natural ones, "extensive rather than restrictive" (p.296). Examples of mentalistic objects: pain, consciousness, and "such semiotic concepts as 'interpretation', 'sign' or 'representation', 'reference', and 'meaning' in their normal use" (p.296). Benefit is methodological/communicational, not metaphysical reduction to secondness (p.297).
- Hidden prototype fallacy (Abstract p.293 + p.297–301): "It is suggested that there really is an anthropomorphic error or 'hidden prototype fallacy' arising from Peirce's prototype for semiosis: the research process of an experimental scientist. The fallacy lies in the concept of the object of representation — it is questionable whether there are any objects of representation for bacteria and whether the DNA-signs have any objects" (Abstract). The abstract's conclusion: "Peircean semiotic concepts are naturalizable but only if they are based on some more primitive concept of representation. The causal origins of representations are not relevant, only their anticipative consequences (i.e. meaning)" (Abstract). The prototype's location in Peirce's classification: semeiotic as normative science of self-controlled thought (Figure 1, p.298–299); introspective "thought thinking of itself" vs the methodeutic/objective-logic perspective "thought thinking of other thought" (p.300–302).
- Phenomenology of the other one (p.299–301, Figure 2 p.300, after Campbell 1969): the study should concern "the way it is meaningful ... for the 'other one'" (p.299). The focus is the "object-agent" (the organism/bacterium/interpretive system under study) vs the "meta-agent" (readers, writers, observers, researchers — us as methodologists, Figure 2, p.300). Both the object-agent and its experienced objects "must be possible objects of our external experience" (p.300). If agent is kept, it must be object-agent, not meta-agent; the distinction is methodological like meta- vs object-language (p.300).
- Naturalization of the agent (p.300–303): "The naturalization (or alternatively, the elimination) of the concept of the semiotic agent is essential" (p.300 n.10 lists Bickhard, Collier & Hooker, Emmeche, Hoffmeyer, Salthe). Criteria must be "formal (i.e. non-substantial) and naturalized" (p.301), not "intuitive feelings that this or that is apparently an agent" — pre-model should be "organism rather than conscious human subject" (p.301). Peirce's own objective logic already generalized sign/mind as non-agential (p.302) but still kept scientist prototype (EP 2 Articles 24–28). Mind as object of external experience: "Mind has its universal mode of action, namely, by final causation. The microscopist looks to see whether the motions of a little creature show any purpose. If so, there is mind there" (CP 1.269, 1902 quoted p.302), with immediate caveat CP 5.473 (1907) that thermostat regulation is not semeiosy in ordinary parlance unless the system has control over its ends (p.302 n.12). Success requires studying "mind out there" via external criteria, not "mind in me" (p.303).
- What even naturalized interpretation means (Abstract last sentence + p.303 n.14): only anticipative consequences, not causal origins, count as meaning; purposiveness recast as anticipation, closure, autonomy, autopoiesis, self-maintenance of far-from-equilibrium (Bickhard, Emmeche, p.303).

Limits kept from the source: Vehkavaara 2002 is methodology, not empirical biosemiotics; it proposes a programme, reports no new experiment, and its abstract admits Peircean concepts are naturalizable *only if* rebuilt on a primitive representation. The small-n survey that the 2025 Habit glossary (Bernardi della Rosa & Zengiaro) used is not in this paper. The organism pre-model is deliberately tentative ("it may later appear that not all organisms are agents", p.301) and the agent concept itself "is not necessarily appropriate at all" (p.301 n.11). No operational threshold for "object of representation" is offered.

Why this source for this cell. `biosemiotics--misread` asks for a foundational idea we may have misread or applied too loosely. The predecessor for this cell (`biosemiotics--misread--2026-10-31`, applied, Peirce CP 2.228 via Favareau introduction, target observations.py) read the headline's ground/interprétant as Hoffmeyer context-fitting and proposed labelling `HEADLINE (mind only)`. The `biosemiotics--critique--2026-10-31` (Álvarez 2009) then showed the selector sweep itself misreads — counting English `sign` in regex/comments as semiosis. Vehkavaara 2002 is the *foundational critique* both of those cite (Álvarez cites Vehkavaara 1998/2003 on the triad; Bernardi della Rosa & Zengiaro 2025 habit cites the same lineage) and it names the mechanism: the hidden prototype. It is 2002, therefore foundational, and it is the primary text behind the "misconstrued triad / hidden prototype" claim the plant already invoked without reading. Fetched as OA PDF and read in full, it supplies the naturalization test the predecessor lacked.

## What is new here

**Vehkavaara's naturalization: treat mind/agent/interpretation as referring only to objects of external experience, split object-agent from meta-agent, and ground meaning in anticipative consequence, not causal origin — which dissolves the hidden prototype fallacy that the plant currently reproduces when it counts English "sign" as semiosis or any next action as interpretant.**

Three points transfer without inventing plant data, each bounded by what the source concedes:

1. **Hidden prototype — the scientist as default agent (Abstract p.293, p.301).** Peirce discovered the triad from "thought thinking of itself" (Critic, p.299–300); biosemiotics inherits that prototype and then asks whether DNA-signs have objects for bacteria (Abstract). The plant inherits the same prototype whenever it writes "Mind" as a chooser and "interpretation" as understanding. Consequence visible in the selector sweep: the English substring `sign` inside `ASSIGN_RE`/`signature` is read as evidence of biosemiotics. Vehkavaara's test is that a naturalized concept must be intelligible without appeal to inner sense (p.296). "Mind" that can only be paraphrased as "something that chooses" fails; "mind" as "externally observable liveness pattern (wall rewrite + DM + yield through lock, checkable as HEADLINE level and tape→wall linkage)" passes, because any observer with similar perceptual capacity could see the same event (p.296 flying-bird test).

2. **Object-agent vs meta-agent and the organism pre-model (Figure 2 p.300, p.300–301).** The paper replaces introspection with "phenomenology of the other one" (Campbell 1969, p.299 n.9): study what is meaningful *for the other one*, not for us. Applied without importing bacteria or thermostats as plant properties: in this plant the object-agent is the mind whose work is being read (its wall, its DM, its settlement attempt); the meta-agent is the reader/researcher/witness who checks it. The distinction is methodological, not ontological, like meta- vs object-language (p.300). The paper's tentative pre-model — organism rather than conscious subject (p.301) — maps to treating a mind as an organism-like locus of self-maintenance (anticipation/closure/autonomy) rather than a deliberating subject; Vehkavaara explicitly lists thermostats as non-agents despite using representations (p.303 n.13) and notes the agent concept may be eliminable (p.301 n.11). That caution transfers: do not require the plant to decide which minds "are agents"; require only that whatever is called a mind be specifiable externally.

3. **Meaning as anticipative consequence, not causal origin (Abstract last sentence, p.303).** "The causal origins of representations are not relevant, only their anticipative consequences (i.e. meaning)" (Abstract). Prediction/anticipation is the primitive that replaces the scientist's inquiry prototype (Bickhard/Emmeche closure, p.303). For the plant, this separates two things the predecessor conflated: any next action vs the consequence anticipated by the representation. A wall heading change anticipates the next mind repeating it (habit as disposition, Bernardi della Rosa & Zengiaro 2025 habit vs habit-taking) — that repetition is meaning. A causal chain (many connections, volume) without that anticipation is not interpretation, which is why mesh.md:19 warns "volume of connections is not the measure". The same split fixes the headline misreading: HEADLINE (mind only) GREEN anticipates bounded mind-liveness action (keep plate, yield), not plant-health verdict above UNKNOWN lanes.

What the predecessor discarded correctly still holds: no bulk import of Hoffmeyer/Emmeche closure machinery, no claim that walls are organisms, no threshold for agentiality. The critique cell already proposed narrowing the biosemiotics keyword from bare `sign` to `semiosis`/`interpretant`/`sign-relation`; this review does not re-propose that edit — it supplies the methodological ground for it.
