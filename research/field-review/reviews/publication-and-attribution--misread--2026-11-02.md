## What we read

International Committee of Medical Journal Editors — **"Recommendations for the Conduct, Reporting, Editing, and Publication of Scholarly Work in Medical Journals"**, updated January 2026 — read authoritative text at:

- <https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html> (HTML, fetched 200 2026-11-02, 30,391 bytes, section "II.A. Defining the Role of Authors and Contributors", updated January 2026 banner, identical to the PDF)
- <https://www.icmje.org/icmje-recommendations.pdf> (PDF, fetched 200 2026-11-02, 281 KiB, full Recommendations, same section II.A text)
- Supplementary within same Recommendations: §V. "Use of Artificial Intelligence in Publishing" at <https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html> (HTML, fetched 200 2026-11-02 via navigation; text mirrored in the defining-role page §II.A.4)

Six passages carry the misread this plant makes. Quotes are verbatim from the HTML section II.A (the PDF carries the same normative text); bracketed page is irrelevant — the URL is the authority.

1. **The four simultaneous criteria for authorship — substantial contribution AND drafting/review AND final approval AND accountability (II.A.1, boxed list).** "The ICMJE recommends that authorship be based on the following 4 criteria: Substantial contributions to the conception or design of the work; or the acquisition, analysis, or interpretation of data for the work; AND Drafting the work or reviewing it critically for important intellectual content; AND Final approval of the version to be published; AND Agreement to be accountable for all aspects of the work in ensuring that questions related to the accuracy or integrity of any part of the work are appropriately investigated and resolved." Limit kept: the first criterion's "or" is within substantial contributions only; the three ANDs between criteria are conjunctive — meeting 3 of 4 is not authorship.

2. **All-and-only plus opportunity, not disqualification by exclusion (II.A.1, paras. 2–3).** "All those designated as authors should meet all four criteria for authorship, and all who meet the four criteria should be identified as authors. Those who do not meet all four criteria should be acknowledged — see Section II.A.3 below. These authorship criteria are intended to reserve the status of authorship for those who deserve credit and can take responsibility ... The criteria are not intended for use as a means to disqualify colleagues ... by denying them the opportunity to meet criterion #s 2 or 3. Therefore, all individuals who meet the first criterion should have the opportunity to participate in the review, drafting, and final approval." Limit kept: authorship is collective determination by those who did the work, not editorial arbitration.

3. **Accountability as identifiability and confidence in co-authors (II.A.1, para. 2).** "In addition to being accountable for the parts of the work done, an author should be able to identify which co-authors are responsible for specific other parts of the work. In addition, authors should have confidence in the integrity of the contributions of their co-authors." Limit kept: accountability is not personal virtue but traceable parts plus answerability for the whole.

4. **Corresponding author as responsible correspondent and the large-group rule (II.A.1, paras. 4–5; II.A.2).** "The corresponding author is the one individual who takes primary responsibility for communication with the journal during the manuscript submission, peer-review, and publication process. ... should be available throughout the submission and peer-review process to respond to editorial queries ... and should be available after publication to respond to critiques ... When a large multi-author group has conducted the work, the group ideally should decide who will be an author before the work is started and confirm who is an author before submitting ... All members of the group named as authors should meet all four criteria ... and they will also be expected as individuals to complete disclosure forms. ... group name, with or without the names of individuals ... MEDLINE lists as authors whichever names appear on the byline." Limit kept: group name does not remove individual four-criteria obligation.

5. **Non-author contributors are acknowledged, not authored, with specified contribution and permission (II.A.3).** "Contributors who meet fewer than all 4 of the above criteria for authorship should not be listed as authors, but they should be acknowledged. Examples of activities that alone (without other contributions) do not qualify a contributor for authorship are acquisition of funding; general supervision of a research group or general administrative support; and writing assistance, technical editing, language editing, and proofreading. Those whose contributions do not justify authorship may be acknowledged individually or together as a group ... and their contributions should be specified (e.g., 'served as scientific advisors,' 'critically reviewed the study proposal,' ...). Because acknowledgment may imply endorsement by acknowledged individuals of a study’s data and conclusions, editors are advised to require that the corresponding author obtain written permission to be acknowledged from all acknowledged individuals." Limit kept: funding/supervision/writing assistance alone never qualifies.

6. **AI-assisted technology: disclosure, not authorship, and where (II.A.4 and §V.A).** "At submission, the journal should require authors to disclose whether they used artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted technologies (such as Large Language Models [LLMs], chatbots, or image creators) in the production of submitted work ... Authors who use such technology should describe, in both the cover letter and the submitted work in the appropriate section if applicable, how they used it. For example, if AI was used for writing assistance, describe this in the acknowledgment section (see Section II.A.3). If AI was used for data collection, analysis, or figure generation, authors should describe this use in the methods (see Section IV.A.3.d). Chatbots (such as ChatGPT) should not be listed as authors because they cannot be responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and originality of the work, and these responsibilities are required for authorship. ... Use of AI for writing assistance should be reported in the acknowledgment section (see also Section V)." Limit kept: ICMJE adds the AI sentence to the acknowledgement rule; non-human cannot meet criterion 4 and therefore cannot be author — disclosure replaces credit.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** Field `publication-and-attribution` is "the reader book, the DEV blog, commits, CI and pushes" and angle is *misread* — a foundational idea we may have applied too loosely. ICMJE authorship (Vancouver/Uniform Requirements since 1978, current January 2026) is the canonical operationalization of "honest attribution" that every journal claims to follow and routinely misreads as "who wrote it" or "who funded it." The plant invokes `honest agent voice, Mesh historical attribution` (docs/how-it-works.md:491-493) and attributes each publication by a `by <role>` string (`wall.py:262` `_HEADER`, `cli.py` wall/DM headers) — exactly the role-as-proxy misread where criteria 2–4 are assumed satisfied because a role name was supplied.

## What this plant already does

*The plant attributes every publication by role string alone, with no typed distinction between author and acknowledged contributor, no check of the four simultaneous criteria, no corresponding-author responsibility, and no typed AI-disclosure — so a role that supplied the byline and a role that was merely acknowledged read identically.*

- **Wall and feed authorship is a single `by <role>` header, not a four-criteria conjunction.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/wall.py:262-281` `outcome(home, role, kind, text, evidence)` formats `"Wall outcome <kind> by <role>"` and `_publish_outcome` enforces `validate_home_binding` plus the bound-evidence clause (`_bound_evidence_paths`) and digest placement, but never checks whether `<role>` satisfies any ICMJE criterion. `cli.py:79-88` `cmd_append` / `Feed.append` stores `source` as the author-string for chat threats, `feed.py:42` `_encode_entry` frames `"v1 {seq} {timestamp} {source} ::"` with the same single field; `wall.py:642-647` `message(home, source, recipient, text)` prefixes `"[dm] to={recipient}"` with `source` as the only attribution. `rg -n "author" src/mishe_tauftauf/wall.py` is empty until this review. The plant therefore treats "named on the byline" as authorship — precisely the misread ICMJE §II.A.1 forbids: 3 of 4 is not author.

- **Acknowledged-contributor activities are unnamed, and group authorship has no shared byline rule.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/post_check.py` (Merton CUDOS landing `MERTON_NORMS/SKEPTICISM_STATES`) and `src/mishe_tauftauf/outcome_publication.py` (C2PA `DISCLOSURE_LEVELS/MANIFEST_STATES`, Golaszewski critique `timestamp-agreement/revoked` etc.) type maturity of evidence, not who qualifies as author. The funding/supervision/writing-assistance-alone examples from ICMJE §II.A.3 have no vocabulary here, and the multi-author group rule ("decide who will be an author before the work is started ... MEDLINE lists as authors whichever names appear on the byline") is not embodied — a wall outcome authored by `group: steward+health` or a DEV article ghost-written by an agent under a human byline has the same single-role field as a sole author. `rg -n "acknowledg" src/mishe_tauftauf/wall.py` is empty.

- **Corresponding-author responsibility and permission are not typed.** ICMJE requires the corresponding author to handle administrative requirements, remain reachable through peer review and after publication, and obtain written permission from every acknowledged individual because acknowledgment implies endorsement. The plant's `wall settle/yield` and `pub` DEV publishing (`docs/how-it-works.md:488-495`, `.mishe-tauftauf/pub/commands.md:3` "honest agent voice") record no corresponding-role, no post-publication critique address, and no permission flag — an acknowledged `senses` sensor or a mesh historical author whose name appears in a claims section is not asked, because the word does not exist.

- **AI assistance has no disclosure word, and chatbots are indistinguishable from authors by header.** The January 2026 ICMJE added the explicit sentence "Chatbots (such as ChatGPT) should not be listed as authors because they cannot be responsible ..." with the positive rule to disclose LLM/chatbot/image-creator use in acknowledgments (writing assistance) or methods (data/analysis/figure generation). This lane runs entirely via LLM agents (`codex_review.py`, `laya_judge`, `wall_review.py` Codex-gated, `cli.py` operated by minds), yet a wall note `by discover` produced via a model and a note `by discover` produced without model assistance are identically labeled. `rg -n "AI|LLM|chatbot|artificial" src/mishe_tauftauf/wall.py` is empty before this review.

*Consequence the source predicts.* When authorship is reduced to "who is on the byline," the ICMJE warns, two failures follow: deserved authors are omitted because they were denied opportunity to meet criteria 2/3, and undeserved authors are added (guest/gift/honorary authorship) because funding or supervision alone was counted. In the plant, reading a wall outcome `"by health"` or a DEV article `"by pub"` gives no way to know whether `health` met all four (substantial contribution + drafting/review + final approval + accountability for the whole) or merely supervised, funded, or templated the prose — or whether an LLM drafted the body without disclosure. The reader cannot answer "who is accountable for correcting this after publication," because there is no corresponding-author fact.

## What is new here

**ICMJE reframes the plant's `by <role>` from "who is credited" to "which of the four simultaneous criteria that role satisfies, and therefore whether that role is an author, an acknowledged contributor, or an undisclosed assistant — with AI categorically in the last class."**

Five ideas transfer directly, each bounded by what ICMJE concedes:

1. **Four simultaneous criteria as a closed vocabulary, not prose advice (II.A.1).** Today the plant names Merton's `universalism/communism/disinterestedness/organized-skepticism` and SLSA `L0→L3`. The transfer adds `ICMJE_AUTHORSHIP_CRITERIA: Final = ("substantial-contribution","drafting-or-critical-review","final-approval","accountability")` where `substantial-contribution` itself covers the ICMJE disjunction `conception/design OR acquisition/analysis/interpretation-of-data`, and the four are conjunctive. An outcome publication or wall note can then be checked as `all four true → author`, otherwise `acknowledged` or `unknown` — funding/supervision/proofreading alone never qualifies. Limit kept: contribution is per-work, not per-career; criteria 2 is drafting *or* critical review, not both.

2. **Authorship vs acknowledged-contributor as distinct, typed contribution classes with a permission word (II.A.1 + II.A.3).** The transfer makes `CONTRIBUTORSHIP_STATUSES = ("author","acknowledged-contributor","unknown")` and `ACKNOWLEDGMENT_ONLY_ACTIVITIES = ("acquisition-of-funding","general-supervision-or-administrative-support","writing-assistance-or-technical-or-language-editing-or-proofreading")` as the closed list that alone does not qualify. An acknowledged name must carry a specified contribution and a `written-permission-obtained` fact (typed boolean, not prose) because acknowledgment implies endorsement. No wall write is blocked in this commit; the violation is named, not enforced.

3. **Identifiability and confidence as the accountability check (II.A.1, para. 2).** Accountability is not "means well" but "can say who did which part and answers for the whole, with confidence in co-authors' integrity." The transfer exposes `accountability_holds(*, can_identify_parts: bool, confidence_in_coauthors: bool) -> bool` pure predicate — both required for criterion 4. A single-role header never carries this; a multi-role byline must. Limit kept: the predicate is over caller-held booleans, not an investigation — it marks that the answer exists, not that the science is true.

4. **Corresponding author as the reachable, post-publication-responsible role (II.A.1, para. 4).** The plant's `seed yield`/`wall settle` produces many candidate authors at once. The transfer adds `CORRESPONDING_AUTHOR_DUTIES: Final = ("ensure-administrative-requirements","remain-reachable-in-review","remain-reachable-after-publication","handle-permission-for-acknowledgments")` and a pure helper `corresponding_author_is_responsible(corresponding: str, authors: list[str]) -> bool` that checks a corresponding name is present, is itself an `author` (all four), and covers the post-publication critique address. `rg -n "corresponding" src/` is zero until this landing — the word is the detector.

5. **AI disclosure, not AI authorship, with location-typed disclosure (II.A.4 / §V.A).** The transfer makes the January 2026 rule typed: `AI_USE_DISCLOSURE_LOCATIONS: Final = ("acknowledgment","methods","cover-letter")` with `AI_CANNOT_BE_AUTHOR = True` and helpers `ai_use_disclosure_is_valid(location, kind) -> bool` (writing assistance → acknowledgment; data collection/analysis/figure generation → methods; always also cover-letter-advised) and `is_ai_author_allowed() -> False`. Chatbot/model is never `author`; undisclosed assistance is a typed gap. Limit kept: ICMJE forbids listing chatbots as authors because they cannot be accountable (criterion 4); disclosure in the body, not a ban on use, is the remedy — this plant does not gate `Feed.append` on disclosure in this commit.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** ICMJE criteria were written for biomedical journal articles about humans, not for autonomous agents publishing wall outcomes and DEV posts; the transfer keeps the structural test (four ANDs, acknowledgment vs authorship, corresponding reachability, AI non-authorship) without claiming agents are human authors under Vancouver rules. No new wall lock, no `WALL_MAX_*` change, no chat-protocol wire-format change, no move of `SITE/artifacts/` into the public repo, no claim that a `CUDOS: suspended→cleared` plus an `ICMJE: author` publication is therefore true. The collective-determination rule ("it is the collective responsibility of the authors, not the journal, to determine who meets criteria") is kept as a typed fact: the submission site, not the dashboard renderer, decides attribution — renderer merely displays what the wall already typed.
