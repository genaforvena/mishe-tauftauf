# The plant optimizes the near and the seen — and forgets distant times, places, and failures (a myopia of learning critique)

## What we read

Four passages carry the critique this plant would reproduce if it adopted the self-development loop blindly. Page numbers are the printed SMJ pagination.

1. **Two mechanisms that make learning work and then make it myopic — simplification and specialization (pp.97–102, § TWO MECHANISMS OF LEARNING).** "Organizations use two major mechanisms to facilitate learning from experience. The first is simplification. Learning processes seek to simplify experience, to minimize interactions and restrict effects to the spatial and temporal neighborhood of actions. The second mechanism is specialization. Learning processes tend to focus attention and narrow competence." Simplification appears as buffers, departmentalization (p.97–98, Thompson 1967; Chandler 1962) and sequential attention to goals (Cyert & March 1992), and as coding outcomes into successes/failures with causal stories (p.98, Sagan 1993). Specialization appears as niche-focusing and competence narrowing (p.98–99). Neither is unique to learning; both become myopia when they hide distant consequences.

2. **Three forms of myopia — tendency to overlook distant times, distant places, and failures (p.95 abstract; pp.106–108 § problems of myopia; p.110 summary).** The abstract defines the paper as examining how simplification and specialization "contribute to three forms of learning myopia, the tendency to overlook distant times, distant places, and failures." Section analysis: *temporal myopia* — "Learning tends to sacrifice the long run for the short run" (p.110) because short-run feedback is clear and long-run survival is discounted (pp.106–107, survival in short run assures failure in long run). *Spatial myopia* — "Learning tends to favor effects that occur near the learner" (p.110), departmental and local search privilege nearby effects over distant organizational or ecological consequences (pp.107–108, nested adaptation). *Failure myopia* — "Organizational learning oversamples successes and undersamples failures" (p.110): successes are translated into knowledge and then into more successes, experience pools contain fewer and fewer failures as competence narrows (pp.108–109), confidence grows while the record becomes a poor predictor (p.108), attribution biases success→ability/failure→luck (p.108–109), selection retains successful people (p.109).

3. **Two dynamic traps that follow from the myopias — the failure trap and the success trap (pp.109–112, § The failure trap / § The success trap).** The *failure trap*: "Failure leads to search and change which leads to failure which leads to … an endless cycle of failure and unrewarding change" (p.109) — when exploration is driven by failure, each change restarts learning without accumulating competence. The *success trap* (competency trap): "the uncertain and distant returns to exploration …" are underweighted relative to the certain near returns to exploitation; rapid learners and successful organizations specialize until they are competent only at what they already do, and the adaptation that succeeded yesterday becomes the myopia that kills tomorrow (pp.110–111). Both traps are self-reinforcing consequences of the three myopias, not separate exogenous shocks.

4. **What the critique concedes — and what it keeps as remedy rather than abandonment (pp.95, 112, § Conclusion; p.97 § Confusions of experience, § Self-limiting properties).** The authors concede learning has "many virtues" (p.95 opening) and that recent strategy writing correctly highlighted learning as sustainable advantage (Senge 1990; Prahalad & Hamel 1990; Levinthal & March p.96). Experience is "often a poor teacher" because samples are small and ambiguous (Brehmer 1980; Fischhoff 1980), and because history is political (Sagan 1993) — but the remedy is not abandonment: "the imperfections of learning are not so great as to require abandoning attempts to improve the learning capabilities" (p.112). Organizations sustain exploration not by exhortation but by buffers, slack, and deliberate rules that preserve distant search in the face of exploitation pressure (§ exploration/exploitation balance, pp.110–112; March 1991). Limits retained: the paper is theoretical synthesis with illustrative cases, not a new empirical test; mechanisms are ideal types; simplification/specialization benefits are acknowledged before critiqued.

Distinct from prior `self-development-loop--critique--2026-10-29` (Lawrence 2005 politics-of-4I onto `follow_through.py` — power/influence/force/domination/discipline), and from the Bateson 1972 Learning I/II/III misread (2026-11-02) — this is Levinthal & March 1993 contemporaneous to March 1991 exploration/exploitation, addressing myopia rather than power levels.

- **Success and failure traps visible in follow-through.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/follow_through.py:10-28` composes `initiative_lines + substrate_lines + watch_lines` into `MIND WALL — shared follow-through evidence` (Crossan institutionalization step) with `LEARNING_PROCESSES / POWER_MODES / POWER_FORMS / ROUTING_POWER` already landed (Lawrence). Neither trap is typed.

- **Doctrine documents the buffers but does not type the myopia.** `docs/wall-coordination.md:328` describes living source and follow-through projection; `AGENTS.md` and `initiative.py` comments name buffers explicitly (512 MiB tape ceiling, 900 s snapshot staleness). The field sweep for `initiative/substrate/trial` surfaced these literals as occurrence counts, never as simplification/specialization moves — the plant therefore already practices exactly the two mechanisms (p.97) without naming the three myopias they produce.

## What is new here

**The myopia critique turns the self-development loop from "a window that measures recent follow-through" into "a learning system that simplifies to a 24-hour, role-local, success-sampled window — and then, by that very simplification, systematically overlooks distant times, distant places, and failures, falling into success and failure traps."**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with the line in this repository where it would land, and each bounded by what the source concedes.

1. **Name the three myopias as first-class detectors beside the 24-hour window (initiative.py).** Temporal myopia = condition judged only inside `WINDOW_SECONDS` while the governing assumption (`EVIDENCE_BINDING_CLAUSE_TIME`, clause 3c22087) ages beyond the window; spatial myopia = condition specialized by `kind/role/resolver` so distant but causally identical evidence (other roles' identical tape failures) is not co-counted; failure myopia = record oversamples `verified`/`accepted` outcomes and undersamples `UNKNOWN`/refused evidence. The plant already counts refusals (`_outcome_refusal_lines` in `follow_through.py:35`) but never attributes them to these three forms. Adding the vocabulary makes the 2026-11-02 `unknown:outcomes:field-review` repetition legible as myopia rather than as a fresh incident.

2. **Split the two mechanisms so a myopia can be diagnosed as simplification vs specialization.** Simplification = reducing interaction by buffers, ceilings, and sequential windows (`MAX_TAPE_BYTES`, `WINDOW_SECONDS`, `SNAPSHOT_MAX_AGE`); specialization = narrowing competence/attention to `kind/role` and to the archive subsets (`STAGED_SUBSETS`) that reward continued exploitation of successful lineages. The diagnosis matters because the remedies differ (Levinthal & March p.112): simplification myopias are eased by widening the window or restoring distant history (archive), specialization myopias by co-counting distant kinds/roles or preserving exploratory slack.

3. **Type the two traps so repetition is not mistaken for independent learning.** The *failure trap* = repeated `UNKNOWN` with search/change that re-learns the same failure (the `aged-wake:field-review` recurrence under same `kind`); the *success trap* = retained parent lineage that exploits past successes while long-run exploration decays. `initiative.py:is_stepping_stone()` already encodes the success logic (dip-then-jump stepping stone, DGM Fig.3); the missing piece is the myopia label that explains why a successful stepping stone can still be myopic (exploiting nearby successes at distant cost).

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** The three myopias and two traps are tendencies, not deterministic laws; simplification/specialization are useful and unavoidable (p.97) and the paper offers synthesis rather than a new regression test. This review therefore proposes a typed vocabulary/detector, not a causal claim that any particular `UNKNOWN` this week was caused by myopia. The success trap is not a prediction that this plant will fail; it is a standing risk whenever a 24-hour success sample drives the next institution (`pin`).
