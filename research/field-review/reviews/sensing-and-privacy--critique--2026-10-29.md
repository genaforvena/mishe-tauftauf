## What we read

Mihailis E. Diamantis — **"The Danger of Contextual Integrity"**, *Business Ethics Quarterly* (2025), pp. 1–21, DOI 10.1017/beq.2025.10085 — read full 21 pp. PDF at:

The paper's own prior construct is Nissenbaum **"Privacy as Contextual Integrity"**, *Washington Law Review* 79:119 (2004), already read for the misread cell (WLR pp. 101–158 via `https://nissenbaum.tech.cornell.edu/papers/H.%20Nissenbaum,%20_Privacy%20as%20Contextual%20Integrity.pdf`). Diamantis adopts Nissenbaum's 2004/2010 framework (§1: flow characterized by five parameters — sender, recipient, subject, information type, transmission principle; contexts as structured social settings with canonical activities/roles/norms/values; two-step procedure: 1) descriptive — find analogous entrenched context and test deviation from its transmission norms, 2) prescriptive — weigh deemed-suspicious flows against general moral/political values and context-specific ends, pp. 5–6) as the target of an *internal* legal-realist critique, not a comparison to an alternative theory (Introduction, p. 4: "The goal … is to advance an internal critique … not to advocate for any alternative …").

Four passages carry the critique this plant would reproduce if it adopted the 2004 vocabulary without its limits (each cited so the review cannot be read as claiming Diamantis proves CI must fail):

1. **Value indeterminacy — the open-ended second step ( §3.2, pp. 9–11).** The prescriptive step asks a judge to balance "general moral and political considerations" including an open list — "prevention of information-based harms, informational inequality, autonomy, freedom, preservation of important human relationships, democracy, freedom of speech, pursuit of wealth, efficiency, security, and 'other social values'" (Nissenbaum 2004 pp. 146–147, quoted p. 9). Diamantis: "one might worry that such an open-ended values inquiry confuses privacy with other important interests" (p. 9) and "As a purely mathematical matter, contextual integrity must be less determinate than more focused alternatives simply because it requires judges to balance a greater number of essentially contested values" (p. 10). Consequence for a neutral judge: "Few if any cases will be easy" (p. 11); open-ended multi-factor balancing is "paralyzingly imprecise" (p. 10, citing *Schechter Poultry* 295 U.S. 495). He notes most CS/law adopters stop at step 1 and repopulate step 2 with discipline-specific values (p. 11), which limits but does not eliminate the first-step indeterminacy.

2. **Context indeterminacy — there is no view from nowhere (§3.3, pp. 11–13).** Social contexts are co-constructed by all parties to an exchange; Diamantis's running example is Google search, which Nissenbaum casts as "researchers in a public library" (2004 p. 43). Google will say users are "consumers (of its services) or products (for its advertisers)" (p. 12) and content generators/advertisers add two more characterizations — "all four perspectives are, in a sense, accurate but also irreconcilable" (p. 12). The suggested objective anchor — "work of reputable philosophers, social scientists, and social theorists" (Nissenbaum p. 137) — would "compound[] indeterminacy" (Calo p. 34, cited p. 13) because those disciplines disagree internally; the paper cites Rule's observation that Nissenbaum's chosen social ontology is "far from the ascendant in western social science today" (p. 13).

3. **Indeterminacy and complexity favour the well-resourced litigant (§3.4, pp. 14–15).** "Ambiguous standards tend to favor sophisticated parties. Clearcut rules give less role for creative (i.e., expensive) advocacy" (p. 14); "only a handful of deep experts would be able to piece together a full account" of some flows (Nissenbaum 2006 p. 35, quoted p. 14) and "most of these experts will work for the highest bidder" (p. 14). Three further corporate asymmetries: coordinated uniform position across courtrooms vs. disparate individual interests, outsized power to set terms of new contexts pre-litigation as platform architects, and a privacy-policy text trail that neutral judges will consult even under CI (p. 15).

4. **Three dynamics that make the favour durable (§§3.5–3.6, pp. 15–17).** *Heads-Google-wins-tails-you-lose* (p. 15): the same library analogy that gives CI a pro-privacy result (patron search history is confidential) also requires that "before research information flows … transmission norms require identification information to flow from patrons to libraries" — a driver-licence gate at registration — so CI entails Google may demand verifiable ID before search, the opposite of the desired outcome. *Frog-in-hot-water* (p. 16): "If change happens gradually and norms shift against privacy, contextual integrity offers no recourse. Without a noticeable departure … no plaintiff will surface … And once the norms have shifted, contextual integrity cements them as the new benchmark" (p. 16); corporations can win by "incrementally shifting from presently acceptable data practices toward presently unacceptable practices … at every step" (pp. 16–17). *Conservatism* (implicit §3.6 & via Prégent/Gstrein/van de Poel citations pp. 16–17): CI's deference to entrenched norms entrenches existing power imbalances; "an uncritical adoption … might allow … powerful actors to promote one-size-fits-all solutions that lower existing protection standards" (Gstrein & Beaulieu 2022, cited p. 17).

What the paper concedes — and what the transfer must therefore keep (pp. 4, 8, 10): short-term CI in the hands of a well-meaning judge "could justify restrictions on some egregious forms of data collection" (p. 8; examples p. 7: Google Street View faces, limits on computerized public records, Facebook friend-correspondence ads); the exercise stacks the deck *in favour* of CI by assuming neutral, sincerely faithful judges (§3.1, p. 8); pro-corporate judicial selection would worsen every dynamic but is assumed away for the prediction; existing law's indeterminacy (Calo 2019) already hurts privacy and CI "would make matters worse" in degree, not kind.

Prior critiques surveyed but not separately read (each cited p. 3): Birnhack 2011 (ambiguous/necessarily normative judgment), Prégent 2025 (indeterminacy allows manipulation), Rule 2019 (different analysts will not converge), McDonald & Forte 2020 (norm-based analysis slips vulnerable populations), Richards 2021 ("theory of creepy"), Xia 2023 (impression of mathematical determinacy). The 2025-10-08 Prégent chapter and the Benthall/Gürses/Nissenbaum 2017 CS survey (§2, p. 2) are referenced for the "no dissenters" claim.

## What this plant already does

*The plant just landed the Nissenbaum vocabulary in additive form — closed-world tables, pure predicates, no verdict change — which already blunts two of the paper's indeterminacies but reproduces the frog/conservatism vector in its consent.*

- **A determinate, closed-world CI vocabulary beside the Redmi decoders.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/body_perception.py:29` adds, since the misread review, `PERSONAL_CONTEXTS` / `BODY_INFORMATION_TYPES` / `TRANSMISSION_PRINCIPLES` (frozensets), `_APPROPRIATENESS` and `_CI_FLOWS` tables, and three pure predicates `is_body_info_appropriate_for_context` / `contextual_integrity_allows` / `personal_body_flow_allows` (pp. 96–158 of that file). The module header cites Nissenbaum 2004 §§III–III.B and Barth et al. 2006 §2 and states "Either appropriateness *or* flow violation is a breach." Appropriateness is a frozenset lookup per context; flow is a `(context, principle) -> allowed-recipients` lookup; any pair not listed denies the flow. This is intentionally narrower than Nissenbaum's open-ended second step — there is no "democracy, freedom, wealth, efficiency, other social values" list, no balancing test, and no philosophy/social-theory anchor — so the §3.2 value indeterminacy and the §3.3 "view from nowhere" appeal are refused by construction.

- **A blanket personal-analysis consent with an unbounded future clause.** `.mishe-tauftauf/research/shared/personal-multibody-analysis-20261015.md:5` records the human's 2026-10-15 consent for analysis "about themselves from authorized sensed evidence" across "all authorized bodies and senses, including new not-yet-developed ones", with charter note "This consent concerns the human, not bystanders." The CI tables decode this blanket as the single principle `"human-consented-personal-analysis"` confined to non-public recipients (`senses, health, body-research, inference-research` for `personal-carrying`, `body_perception.py:71`). The principle is carried as a literal string, not derived from a policy document's text trail — so the §3.4 third asymmetry (judges consulting privacy policies as evidence of shared expectations) has no foothold today.

- **The plant has no host/body merging and no broadcast-by-default, but has a single broadcast channel.** `host_radio.py:1` decodes "this host's radio, wired-network and tailnet adapter state … never mutates a radio"; `body_perception.py:1` reads the linked Android Body consumer's retained Redmi observations and "never infer human movement." Publication is a single broadcast distribution: `outcome_publication.py:54` `EVIDENCE_REMEDY_SUFFIX` and `docs/how-it-works.md:239` show every wall outcome, DM and discovery event to every attached mind, and `docs/protocol-boundaries.md:224` treats a wall outcome as reserved-header + prose + single immutable record reference. The CI tables encode the consequence: `publication` carries `frozenset()` appropriateness (no body types appropriate by default) and only the principle `"public-by-explicit-disclosure"` allows `publication` recipients (`body_perception.py:86`); any body reading quoted in a wall outcome under `"human-consented-personal-analysis"` fails the predicate by design. The frog vector remains: adding a new info type (e.g., microphone, precise geolocation) under the same blanket and inserting it into `BODY_INFORMATION_TYPES` and `_APPROPRIATENESS` without revisiting the 2026-10-15 consent would be exactly the incremental shift pp. 16–17 warns about — each step respects the table as-then-written, then becomes the new norm the table cements.

- **No per-flow principle is published beside the reading.** The reading `sense.body.redmi10` publishes `(SENSE_ID, source, site, channel, freshness)` but no `(context, principle)` tuple; a later mind quoting `L=105lux |a|=9.84 incl-g` into a wall outcome cannot tell from bytes alone which principle authorized the quote. The tables exist to answer the question, but the link between the stored reading and its authorizing principle is prose, not typed data — the head-contains/Google-ID entailment (§3.5) is therefore checkable only by reading the code, not by checking a stored field.

## What is new here

**If the plant adopts CI vocabulary naïvely, it swaps one misread ("not sensitive / not private therefore OK") for the three failure modes Diamantis proves CI enables — and each has a literal line in this repository where it would happen.**

- **Value indeterminacy via an open-ended principle list.** Under the misread, `lux=105` was safe because it was "not sensitive." Under naïve CI, it would become safe because a reader could cite *some* value: "efficiency favours sharing `wifi_link_rssi_dbm` with `pub` for network debugging" or "research freedom favours sharing `ble_scan` with `inference-research` indefinitely." The 2004 list plus "other social values" makes any body flow justifiable to a well-meaning reviewer who picks a different value, which is exactly the p. 10 observation that CI must be less determinate than a focused rule because it balances more essentially contested concepts. The plant's current tables avoid this by enumerating four closed principles and denying any not listed — but nothing in the code says the list must stay closed.

- **Context indeterminacy via post-hoc relabelling.** Nissenbaum's Google→library analogy and Diamantis's inversion show that context characterisation is socially negotiable. In the plant, a `ble_scan` collected in `personal-carrying` could be relabelled post-hoc as `host-radio` ("it is a radio scan, not a body scan") or `health-diagnosis` ("it is needed for health"), invoking a different appropriateness row and a different recipient set. The tables as-shipped already encode distinct rows per context so the move is visible as a table edit, but only if the review records that context is fixed by the consent that authorised collection, not by the data type's shape.

- **Incremental erosion and conservatism — the blanket consent as the frog mechanism.** Diamantis pp. 16–17: without a noticeable breach there is no plaintiff, and once a shifted norm is entrenched "contextual integrity cements them as the new benchmark." Here the blanket clause "including new not-yet-developed ones" is the thermal gradient. Each new sensor (microphone, continuous GPS, CSI presence) added under the same sentence is a small, non-noticeable shift; after landing, its `BODY_INFORMATION_TYPES` entry and its `_APPROPRIATENESS` row become the entrenched norm the next reviewer measures against. The CI framework then defends the new scope rather than interrogating it, and a reader checking the evidence cannot tell from the reading's bytes that the human never revisited the 2026-10-15 grant.

- **Complexity favouring the producer (pp. 14–15) — not a courtroom here, but a review asymmetry.** The plant has one producer (`senses` publishing Redmi/host state) and many consumers (`health, genome, witness, inference-research, body-research, pub, docs`). The producer controls the platform, the terms (the consent sentence), and the dense observation log; consumers read. An open-ended CI check that requires "deep experts … to piece together a full account" of flows is one where the producer's proposal always carries more narrative than the reviewer's objection. The current additive check (`is_body_info_appropriate_for_context` is a single frozenset membership) is deliberately shallow for this reason — a property the critique says to keep.

None of this is a claim that CI is wrong — Diamantis explicitly offers an internal critique and allows that short-term CI in well-meaning hands can restrict egregious flows (p. 8). The new work is naming, in the same file that carries the vocabulary, the conditions under which that vocabulary would erode rather than protect privacy, so a reviewer does not have to rediscover them.

2. **Fix context to the authorising consent and forbid post-hoc relabelling.** Add to the `is_body_info_appropriate_for_context` / `contextual_integrity_allows` docstrings (and to the `_APPROPRIATENESS` comment) that the `context` argument is fixed by the collection context named in the 2026-10-15 personal-multibody mandate (`personal-carrying` for Redmi body channels, `host-radio`/`host-network` for adapter state), not by the information type's shape — so `ble_scan` collected as `personal-carrying` does not become `host-radio` because it mentions a radio, and `personal_body_flow_allows` remains the only shorthand for the Redmi consent. 3. **Make the blanket's future clause non-self-executing and name the frog detector.** Extend the `BODY_INFORMATION_TYPES` / `TRANSMISSION_PRINCIPLES` comments to state that the mandate's "including new not-yet-developed ones" does not auto-extend the typed vocabularies: adding a new information type (e.g., `microphone`, `geolocation`, `csi_presence`), a new context, or a new principle requires an explicit entry in `PERSONAL_CONTEXTS`/`BODY_INFORMATION_TYPES`/`TRANSMISSION_PRINCIPLES` *and* a revisited, per-type human consent record — not a table edit alone.
