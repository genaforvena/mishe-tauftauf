## What we read

Pierangela Samarati and Latanya Sweeney — **"Protecting Privacy when Disclosing Information: k-Anonymity and Its Enforcement through Generalization and Suppression"**, SRI-CSL Technical Report and IEEE S&P 1998, pp. 1–19 — read full PDF at:

- <https://dataprivacylab.org/dataprivacy/projects/kanonymity/paper3.pdf> (Data Privacy Lab, fetched 200 2026-10-29, 331,302 bytes, 19 pp., ESP Ghostscript 815.02, pdftotext 2,662 lines, Creator 2007/Mod 2011)
- Canonical DOI/venue: Sweeney, Samarati —IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy perspective; also Harvard Dataverse copy via Latanya Sweeney's site; the report is the presentation form the community cites as "Samarati & Sweeney k-anonymity (1998)" and is unchanged in the CMU reprint

Six passages carry the operational metric, each with a line number where it would land and each with its stated limits kept.

1. **Quasi-identifier and k-anonymity requirement (Def. 2.1–2.2 p.3–4).** `Quasi-identifier: set of attributes {A_i,...,A_j} ⊆ {A_1,...,A_n} whose release must be controlled` — attributes that in combination can be linked to external tables (Def. 2.1 p.3). `k-anonymity requirement: each release must be such that every combination of quasi-identifier values is indistinctly matched to at least k individuals` (Def. 2.2 p.4). Limit kept: Assumption 2.1 (p.4 fn.1) that all linkable external combinations are defined in a QI — if the QI is wrong the release is less anonymous than measured; Dalenius/Sweeney [18] risk remains and is handled by policy/contract, not by the metric itself.

2. **k-anonymity for a table as the measurable condition (Def. 2.3 p.4).** `T satisfies k-anonymity iff for each QI ∈ QI each sequence of values in T[QI] appears at least k times in T[QI]` — the test is on the released table's projection, not on external data, and is a sufficient condition for the requirement when Assumption 2.1 holds and each identity contributes at most one tuple. Limit kept: table-level k is only as good as the QI set; a subset of a QI refers to ≥k individuals but a mis-specified QI understates linkability.

3. **Minimal generalisation and distance vector (Def. 3.2–3.3 p.7–9).** Generalisation at attribute level with domain hierarchies `DGH_{D_i}` and value hierarchies `VGH_{D_i}` (Fig. 2 pp.5–6; Fig. 3–5 pp.6–8). `Distance vector DV_{T_j,T_i} = [d_1,...,d_n]` where `d_z` is path length from `dom(A_z,T_i)` to `dom(A_z,T_j)` (§3.2 p.7–8). `k-minimal generalisation: satisfies k-anonymity and no T_z with DV_{T_z,T_i} < DV_{T_j,T_i} also satisfies it` (Def. 3.3 p.8–9). Limit kept: minimality is lattice-relative; with disjoint QIs per-attribute generalisation is independent (§6 p.13 fn.4), otherwise serial.

4. **Suppression and minimal required suppression with MaxSup (Def. 4.1–4.3 p.10–12).** Suppression at tuple level, injective mapping `T_j ⊆ T_i` (Def. 4.1 p.10); `minimal required suppression: no smaller T_z at same DV satisfies k-anonymity` (Def. 4.2 p.10). For a fixed `k` and `MaxSup`, `k-minimal with suppression requires |T_i|-|T_j| ≤ MaxSup and no dominating DV also satisfiable` (Def. 4.3 p.11–12). Limit kept: completeness vs precision tradeoff is explicit — suppression moderates generalisation only within `MaxSup`; beyond it the table is unacceptable and generalisation alone would collapse all tuples to one value (the `hperson,02100i` extreme §3.2 p.7).

5. **Preference / cost of a release: absolute vs relative distance and distribution ( §5 p.12–13).** `AbsDist = Σ d_z`, `RelDist = Σ d_z / h_z` where `h_z` is hierarchy height for `dom(A_z,T_i)`; plus `maximum distribution = greatest number of distinct tuples` and `minimum suppression = largest |T_j|` (§5 p.12–13, Example 5.1). These are comparable costs across incomparable minimal generalisations. Limit kept: preference is recipient-subjective; the paper lists four and says the set is not exhaustive and choice is by requester at access time [18].

6. **Quality metric: precision and completeness on real data ( §7 p.15–17, Fig. 11 p.17).** `Loss due to generalisation = level / height` of the `DGH`; `precision = 1 - loss_{gen}`; `loss due to suppression = suppressed / |PT|`; `completeness = 1 - loss_{sup}` (§7 p.15–17). Measured on the 265-patient table with QI `{ZIP, birth date, gender, ethnicity}` (Fig. 10, Table 1 p.16) — all 265 `T[QI]` distinct before protection; 280 possible generalisations, 141 retained after distance-vector pruning, 44 minimal with `k=3..30` and `MaxSup=10% (27 tuples)`. Charts (C)–(D) p.17: larger k loses precision, completeness stays ≥0.90 at `MaxSup=10%`; the authors note `precise-but-generalised > suppressed` for availability. Limits kept: only one recipient's QI, no multiple-release or temporal-linkage analysis (open problems §8 p.18 [10,13,15]).

**Why this literature, for this cell.** Field `sensing-and-privacy` is "host radio, body perception, personal analysis" and angle is *metric* — a concrete, runnable measure of disclosure risk, not a governance stance. Sweeney's `k` plus `precision`/`completeness` is the canonical disclosure-control metric for person-specific microdata releases: it directly answers "how many bodies share this released quasi-identifier value, and how much data was lost to achieve that." The plant publishes exact per-body microdata (Redmi retained rows, dense-baseline histogram) — the exact object the metric is defined on.

## What this plant already does

*The plant publishes exact person-specific microdata and exact aggregates with no linkability metric, no suppression count, and no precision/completeness — precisely the "de-identifying by removing names is not anonymous" posture Sweeney is written to measure (§1 p.1–2 Figure 1).*

- **Exact microdata and exact aggregates with bounds checks only.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/body_perception.py:805` `DENSE_QUEUE_NAME="redmi10.observations.log"` / `DENSE_TAIL_BYTES=262144` (~2,500 lines) / `DENSE_WINDOW_ROWS=2000` (~2.8 h at 5 s cadence) and `read_redmi10_dense_baseline():849` publish a histogram over the retained queue tail — light-class counts (`_dense_light_class:839`), `lux` min/max/mean provenance, acceleration summary — plus `read_redmi10():436` light/battery/acceleration fields. `src/mishe_tauftauf/stats_boundary.py:91` `query()` publishes exact `(since,until,group_by)` sums/counts with `OUTPUT_LIMIT=10000` and symlink rejection but no linkability measure. Provenance is exact (`db_mtime`, `DENSE_FRESH_SECONDS=30.0`); the consumer can compute batch delay (`_worst_batch_delay:708`).

- **Privacy vocabulary exists but has no metric.** `body_perception.py:45` carries Nissenbaum CI (appropriateness + flow `PERSONAL_CONTEXTS / BODY_INFORMATION_TYPES / TRANSMISSION_PRINCIPLES / _APPROPRIATENESS / _CI_FLOWS / CONSENT_RECEIPT_VERSION / PERSONAL_CONSENT_RECEIPTS`) hardened per Diamantis BEQ §§3.2–3.6, and `stats_boundary.py` carries Dwork & Roth DP (`DP_EPSILON_DEFAULT=1.0` / `DP_DELTA_DEFAULT=0.0` / `DP_SENSITIVITY_COUNT=1` + `laplace_scale / dp_composition / is_valid_dp_params`). Both are pure, additive, verdict-free predicate/constant vocabularies — they answer "is this flow allowed?" and "what scale would noise need?" but not "how many bodies share this released QI value?" or "how much was suppressed/generalised to achieve that k?".

- **No `k`, no equivalence-class histogram, no suppression/completeness count.** There is no file that measures the size of the smallest QI-equivalence class over the last `DENSE_WINDOW_ROWS` rows, nor one that counts how many rows belong to classes `<k` (the rows that would have to be suppressed at level `DV=[0,...,0]`), nor a `precision = 1 - level/height` or `completeness = 1 - suppressed/total`. The dense baseline's `N` distinct-tuples-on-QI statistic and the `suppressed/N` vs `generalisation-level/height` costs (§5/§7) are therefore absent — the preceding mechanism commit explicitly deferred any noisy release and any budget ledger, so a linkability failure would not be typed until an analyst performed the Sweeney linking demo (Fig. 1 voter-list join) outside the plant.

- **Best nearby precedent for the transfer.** The four sensing-and-privacy predecessors landed pure, additive, verdict-free constants + predicates beside decoders: CI contexts/tables + predicates, BEQ critique hardening (closed-world invariants), 802.11bf negative capability, Kaye sticky receipts, and Dwork & Roth DP scales/composition. Each added `Final` constants + pure helpers with no new I/O, no `SENSE_ID` verdict, no daemon path, and kept `check_parsing` green. The k-metric reuses exactly that precedent: `Final` `QI`/threshold constants + pure histogram/k/suppression/completeness helpers beside `DENSE_WINDOW_ROWS` and `_dense_light_class`, with no file read beyond the caller's already-held rows.

*Consequence the source predicts.* §1 p.1: "there remains a common incorrect belief that if the data looks anonymous, it is anonymous … de-identifying data provides no guarantee … 12% had unique birth dates, 29% unique on (birth date, gender), 69% on (birth date, 5-digit ZIP), 97% on (birth date, full ZIP)" [18]. Without a measured `k` over the retained queue's QI projection, the plant's exact dense histogram and exact light/battery channels carry the same misreading the detector in `checks/field-review-contract.py` is built to prevent — promise of anonymity without the number that would refute it.

## What is new here

**A typed, pure, verdict-free linkability metric beside the dense queue — so the cost of a release is a measured k, a suppression count, and a completeness fraction, not just a row cap, and the generalisation-vs-suppression tradeoff (§5–§7) is comparable.**

Five ideas transfer directly, each with a landing line in the target file and each bounded by what Sweeney concedes.

1. **Quasi-identifier and k as typed facts, not prose (Def. 2.1–2.3 p.3–4).** Today the attributes an adversary could join are implicit. The transfer makes one concrete QI set explicit for the body's retained microdata and exposes `k` as the minimum equivalence-class size over `T[QI]` — the exact statistic §2.2–2.3 defines the disclosure risk at. Without this, `suppressed` and `completeness` have no denominator.

2. **Equivalence-class histogram as the primitive (Def. 2.3 p.4, §6 p.14–15).** The `T[QI]` projection with duplicate counts is the object the algorithm actually counts (§6: distinct tuples + occurrence counts, distance-vector pruning). The transfer exposes this as a pure helper `qi_histogram(rows, qi_keys)` returning `{qi_tuple: count}` so whitespace/ordering and missing fields are typed, and `k = min(hist.values())` is import-checkable without joining an external table.

3. **Suppressed-at-k as the rows the metric would remove (Def. 4.1–4.2 p.10, Def. 4.3 p.11).** At fixed `DV=[0,...,0]` (no generalisation), the rows in classes `<k` are exactly the outlier tuples Sweeney suppresses. The transfer exposes `suppressed_count(rows, qi_keys, k)` = `Σ_{c: |c|<k} |c|` and `completeness = 1 - suppressed/|rows|` (§7 p.15–17) as pure functions — the plant stays exact (nothing is actually suppressed or generalised), but the would-suppress count is now typed and the `MaxSup` threshold is a reviewer-visible budget.

4. **Absolute/relative distance and distinct-tuple count as comparable costs ( §5 p.12–13).** `AbsDist`/`RelDist` and `maximum distribution (distinct tuples)` are the preference policies the paper uses to choose among incomparable minimal generalisations. The transfer carries `distinct_tuple_count(hist)` and documents `AbsDist`/`RelDist` in the module header as the future generalisation cost when the plant implements `DGH` levels for a QI attribute — the reviewer sees that generalisation cost is per-attribute height-normalised, not raw step count.

5. **Precision/completeness as the quality metric ( §7 p.15–17, Fig. 11 p.17).** The paper measures loss `level/height` as precision and `suppressed/N` as completeness, with the empirical finding that at `MaxSup=10%` completeness ≥0.90 and larger `k` trades precision for privacy. The transfer exposes `completeness` (and documents `precision = 1 - level/height` for future `DGH` levels) so a release proposal can state both privacy (`k`) and utility (`completeness`) as numbers rather than as prose — with the explicit §8 open-problem note that multiple releases and temporal linkage (pp.18 [10,13]) are not measured here.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** No generalisation hierarchy `DGH`/`VGH` is implemented for any attribute (Fig. 2), no tuples are actually generalised or suppressed, no external join is performed (Assumption 2.1 kept as assumption, p.4 fn.1 risk acknowledged), no cryptographic or noise mechanism is called, no `sense.body.redmi10.k-anonymity` verdict row is published, and no utility claim is made beyond the two fractions §7 defines. Correct QI selection remains a modelling question (§8 p.18) handled by policy/contract ([18] §2.2 p.4), not by a perfect detector.
