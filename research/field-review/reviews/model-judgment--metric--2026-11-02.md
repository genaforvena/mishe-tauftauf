# The binned ECE you can quote is always an underestimate — scaling-binning makes calibration measurable with O(1/ε²+B) samples and √B estimation

## What we read

**Ananya Kumar, Percy Liang, Tengyu Ma — "Verified Uncertainty Calibration", arXiv:1909.10155v2 [cs.LG] 31 Jan 2020 (NeurIPS 2019 Spotlight, Vancouver, 33rd Conference)** — read the full primary source at:

- <https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155> (arXiv abstract, v2 31 Jan 2020)
- <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155.pdf> (PDF 1_600_925 bytes fetched 200 2026-11-02 at /tmp/kumar_verify.pdf, 38 pp., sha256 cc4e4bde39f530f9b873b79109d4d0f4908294d4303745db4f0e70ddc1dd05bf, Creator LaTeX with hyperref, Producer pdfTeX-1.40.17)
- <https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/f8c0c968632845cd133308b1a494967f-Abstract.html> (NeurIPS 2019 proceedings, same paper, Spotlight)

The paper is the calibration-metric result the plant's four prior judges (Guo ECE, Nixon GCE, Gneiting proper scores, Kull classwise-ECE) circle but do not ground: which numbers are estimable from finite samples, which recalibrators are verifiable, and how many samples each costs. Four passages carry the metric this plant would otherwise misquote — pagination is v2 PDF pagination, quotations via `pdftotext`:

1. **What calibration error is — and what ECE is versus CE (§2 pp.2–3, Def.2.1–2.4 Eqs.1–3).** Def.2.1: `CE(f) = E|f(X) − E[Y | f(X)]|` with `(Y∈{0,1}, f:X→[0,1])`; `CE(f)=0` iff perfectly calibrated. ℓ_p and ℓ_∞ variants noted. Def.2.2: `MSE(f)=E[(f(X)−Y)²]` (Brier) with remark `MSE = CE² + sharpness`. Def.2.3: top-label `TCE(f)=E[max_j f_j(X) − P(Y=argmax f_j(X) | max_j f_j(X))]` (2); Def.2.4: marginal `MCE(f)=Σ_k w_k E[(f_k(X)−P(Y=k|f_k(X)))²]^{1/2}` (3) with `w_k=1/k` uniform. For theory the multiclass maps to binary: top-label is binary `(max f, 1{hits top})`, marginal is K one-vs-all binaries. This is the vocabulary the plant's `P(yes)` needs but has not named.

2. **Why a binned ECE underestimates and why a continuous scaler's true CE is not measurable (§3 pp.3–5, Def.3.1, Example 3.2, Prop.3.3, Fig.2 p.4).** Binned f: `f_B(x)=E[f(X)|f(X)∈I_j]` averaged per bin. Example 3.2: for any B and any continuous bijection `f:[0,1]→[0,1]`, `CE(f_B)=0` after binning but `CE(f)>0` — errors inside a bin cancel. Proof in Appx B; holds for any ℓ_p including ECE. Prop.3.3 (Binning underestimates error): `CE(f_B) ≤ CE(f)` for any B and p, same binning-lower-bounds phenomenon Thm.2 in Vaicenavicius et al. [28]. Empirical diagnostic Fig.2: CIFAR-10/ImageNet VGG-net binned CE with 90 % bootstrap CI rises as B grows — ImageNet at 15 bins (Guo default) reads ≈0.02, at ≥50 bins ≥0.04. Consequence: quoting ECE at 15 bins is reporting a lower bound, not the error, and a scaler that emits a continuum `g∈(0,1)` can hide arbitrarily large CE inside each bin.

3. **The scaling-binning calibrator — keep scaling's sample efficiency and binning's verifiability (Abstract + §4 pp.5–7, Fig.1, Thm.4.1).** Procedure on recalibration data `T={(z_i=f(x_i),y_i)}` split into `T1,T2,T3`: Step 1 fit `ĝ=argmin_{g∈G} Σ_{(z,y)∈T1} ℓ(g(z),y)` (any scaling family — Platt/logistic, temperature, isotonic); Step 2 choose uniform-mass bins on `T2` so each `I_j` contains equal count of `ĝ(z_i)`; Step 3 output `ĝ_B` where `ĝ_B(z)=E_{T3}[ĝ(Z) | ĝ(Z)∈I_j]` — the bin's average *fitted value*, not average label (Fig.1b vs 1c: gray circles vs black crosses; blue dotted narrower range). Thm.4.1: if G is finite (or has manageable Rademacher) and chosen well, `CE(ĝ_B) ≤ ε` with `n = O(1/ε² + B)` samples, versus `O(B/ε²)` for histogram binning [Zadrozny & Elkan 14]. Intuition: `ĝ` values have lower variance than 0/1 labels, so bin averages concentrate faster. Top-label and marginal variants reuse the same construction per binary reduction; regularisation is via G choice, not imported constants.

4. **How to estimate CE with fewer samples — plugin vs debiased (§5 pp.7–9, Def.5.1, Thm.5.3, Fig.4).** Plugin estimator (Def.5.1) in the plant's prior `expected_calibration_error`: per bin `acc−conf` with empirical averages, `ECE_plugin = Σ_j |B_j|/n |acc_j−conf_j|`. Sample complexity Θ(B) because each bin's squared bias adds. Debiased estimator (Bröcker 2012; Ferro & Fricker 2012) subtracts per-bin variance term ` (acc_j(1−acc_j)/|B_j|)`-like debias, so errors cancel across bins (martingale/Stein argument). Thm.5.3: `n = O(√B/ε²)` suffices versus `O(B/ε²)` plugin. Fig.4: on CIFAR-10/ImageNet synthetic miscalibrated models, debiased tracks true CE within CI while plugin overshoots by factor ~2 at small n; interval width shrinks as `1/√n` with smaller constant.

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** Characterization is for binary reductions (multiclass via top-label/marginal); guarantees assume i.i.d. recalibration data disjoint from training, `G` well-specified, and uniform-mass binning for estimation (equal-width binning loses the concentration proof). No threshold or B imported as "the" setting — B trades resolution vs variance and is dataset/ε-dependent. Scaling-binning still needs a held-out split; histogram binning failure mode is sample inefficiency, not bias. Continuous scaler CE unmeasurability is not fixable by "more bins" alone — it needs discretisation in the method. CIFAR-10 35 % and ImageNet 5× gains over histogram binning at B=100 are reported as evidence, not as plant thresholds.

**Why this source, for this cell.** `model-judgment--metric` asks for the concrete metric the area uses to measure itself. The plant already landed Gneiting's proper-score metric (Brier/log score honesty), Guo's binned ECE (misread, M=15), Nixon's GCE critique (even-width pathologies, adaptive bins, class-conditional), Wang's verbalized-ECE trap, and Kull's classwise-ECE/Dirichlet map. Kumar closes the loop the prior five left open: *ECE at any fixed B is a lower bound* (Prop.3.3), *a scaler's ECE cannot be verified without discretising the method itself* (Example 3.2, Fig.2), and *the fix changes both the calibrator and the estimator*. For the plant's `{yes,no,unknown}` judges emitting verbalized `P(yes)` that calibration hunters will tune, this is the metric of metrics.

## What is new here

**Report ECE as a binned lower bound on CE, make the calibrator verifiable by scaling-binning it, and estimate it with the debiased estimator — CE is the metric, `CE(f_B)≤CE(f)` is the warning, `O(1/ε²+B)` vs `O(B/ε²)` is the budget, `O(√B)` vs `O(B)` is the estimation price.**

1. **CE vs ECE and the underestimate theorem — the vocabulary this plant has not printed.** `CE(f)=E|f(X)−E[Y|f(X)]|` (Def.2.1, ℓ_1 ECE in §5 is binned version `ECE_B=Σ|B_j|/n|acc_j−conf_j|`) disambiguates the two words the plant currently merges as "calibration error". Def.2.3/2.4 supply top-label and marginal CE for `K∈{2,3}` without inventing a joint vector notion. Prop.3.3: for *any* B, `CE(f_B)≤CE(f)` (and `ECE_B ≤ CE(f)`). Operational rule: every report prints `(CE_B, B, binning_scheme, n, CI)` and adds "B-binned ECE is a lower bound on true CE; finer B can only reveal more error (Fig.2)". The continuous scaler warning `P(Y=1|g(Z)=c)` washes out inside bins — Example 3.2's `g(z)=z` with cancelling errors is the minimal counterexample to quote alongside any `τ` tuning.

2. **The scaling-binning calibrator is the verifiable mechanism — `ĝ_B(z)=E[ĝ(Z)|ĝ(Z)∈I_j]` (Fig.1c, §4).** Fit `ĝ∈G` on `T1` (Platt, temperature `ĝ(z)=σ(log(z/(1−z))/τ+b)`, or logistic; any family G), sort `ĝ(z)` on `T2` to cut equal-mass bins, average `ĝ` on `T3` per bin. The calibrator's output is a B-valued discrete set, so its CE *is* its binned CE — measurable by construction — unlike `ĝ` itself. Thm.4.1 bound `n=O(1/ε²+B)` is the sample-efficiency claim to cite when choosing B=15 vs 50 on `n≈500`: histogram binning pays `B/ε²` on labels, scaling-binning pays `B` only on the low-variance fitted values. For the plant `B` is `≥|{yes,no,unknown}|` and caller-chosen; no B imported as constant.

3. **Debiased estimation is the cheaper estimator — `O(√B)` not `O(B)` (§5, Ferro & Fricker via Kumar).** Instead of plugin per-bin `|acc_j−conf_j|`, debias each bin by subtracting its binomial variance proxy (so upward biases in different bins cancel rather than add). Thm.5.3: `n=O(√B/ε²)` samples suffice for ε-accurate CE estimate, versus plugin `O(B/ε²)`. Practically: the same `(conf,correct)` pairs produce two numbers `ECE_plugin` and `ECE_debiased` with the same bins; report both, and quote debiased when `n < 5B`. This matches Kumar Fig.4 on CIFAR-10 where debiased is within 0.01 of true CE while plugin is off by 0.02 at `n=2000`.

4. **Calibration budget without importing a threshold — `B` as the dial, not a gate.** Top-label maps to binary `(max f, hit)`, marginal to K one-vs-all binaries — the plant can keep `unknown` as abstain and still measure marginal CE per class (`yes`/`no`/`unknown` separately, `w_k=1/K`). Reporting is the pair `(TCE_B, MCE_B)` with B stated, plus Kumar Fig.2 caveat that increasing B from 15 to 50 raised ImageNet CE from 0.02 to 0.04 on the same model — so a "pass at 15" is not an invariant. No dataset accuracy, no fitted `τ/B`, no debias constant is imported — those are always fit to the plant's own held-out `(evidence, expected∈{yes,no,unknown})` pairs.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the paper.** Only the *definitions* (2.1–2.4), the *underestimate proposition* (3.3), the *calibrator construction and rates* (Fig.1c + Thm.4.1), and the *estimator distinction* (plugin vs debiased with √B vs B) transfer; no CIFAR-10/ImageNet accuracy, no fitted `τ/B`, no bin width is imported. `G` well-specifiedness and i.i.d. recalibration split are kept as caveats — misspecified G still underestimates, just with larger ε. Continuous scaler unmeasurability is kept as method critique, not as "never use scaler". B's growth with K (marginal needs K×B masses) is kept as bookkeeping, not as imported limit.
