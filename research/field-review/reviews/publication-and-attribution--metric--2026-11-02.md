The manifesto was led by Diana Hicks (School of Public Policy, Georgia Tech) and Paul Wouters (CWTS Leiden) with three co-authors, published as a Nature Comment (not a research article), proposing 10 principles for measurement of research performance. The Nature HTML carries the canonical text; the leidenmanifesto.org site is the maintained translation/video hub.

Six passages carry the metric, each with its stated limits kept.

1. **Principle 1 — quantitative supports qualitative (Nature p.429 col.1, leidenmanifesto.org home, Wikipedia li 1).** "Quantitative evaluation should support qualitative, expert assessment." The text is explicit that metrics do not replace expert judgement; they inform it. Limit kept: the principle is for human research evaluation (hiring, funding, institutional review), not for autonomous wall agents; it does not forbid automation, it subordinates numbers to judgement.

2. **Principles 4 and 5 — open, transparent, simple collection plus right to verify (Wikipedia li 4–5; Nature p.430 "Keep data collection and analytical processes open, transparent and simple" and "Allow those evaluated to verify data and analysis").** Keep data collection and analytical processes open, transparent and simple; allow those evaluated to verify data and analysis. The demand is reproducibility of the indicator itself, not just of the science. Limit kept: simple is not simplistic — transparency of the calculation is required even when the underlying model is complex.

3. **Principles 6 and 2 — field variation and mission alignment (Wikipedia li 2,6; Nature p.430).** "Measure performance against the research missions of the institution, group, or researcher" and "Account for variation by field in publication and citation practices." The manifesto notes "top-ranked journals in mathematics have impact factors of around 3; top-ranked journals in cell biology have impact factors of about 30" — a factor-10 field effect that makes cross-field impact-factor ranking misplaced. Limit kept: field normalization does not fix inter-field comparison; it only makes within-field comparison honest.

4. **Principles 3 and 7 — locally relevant excellence and portfolio judgement for individuals (Wikipedia li 3,7; Nature p.430).** "Protect excellence in locally relevant research" (do not force Spanish local-labour-law research into English high-impact journals to score) and "Base assessment of individual researchers on a qualitative judgement of their portfolio." Limit kept: portfolio judgement assumes a bounded, curated set (a CV/portfolio) — the plant's tape is an unbounded journal, not a portfolio, so verbatim application would misread the principle.

5. **Principle 8 — avoid misplaced concreteness and false precision (Wikipedia li 8, verbatim extended; Nature p.431).** "Avoid misplaced concreteness and false precision. Use of scientific indicators may precede strong assumptions that are not necessarily correct. For example, when looking at a specific scientist, a low citation rate may lead the investigator to assume low research quality, which is implying causation from correlation. Providing clarification, as well as multiple, robust indicators, may reduce inappropriate concreteness. False precision is possible when indicator producers, such as Clarivate (which publishes the annual Journal Citation Reports) attempt to create an exact journal impact factor (i.e. three decimal places). Conceptual ambiguity and random variability of citation counts make it unnecessary to distinguish indices such as journal impact factors." Limit kept verbatim: three decimals is the canonical false-precision example; the cure is multiple robust indicators, not more decimals.

6. **Principles 9 and 10 — systemic effects and regular scrutiny (Wikipedia li 9–10; Nature p.431).** "Recognize the systemic effects of assessment and indicators" and "Scrutinize indicators regularly and update them." Indicators change behaviour — the manifesto treats reactivity and gaming as expected, not exceptional. Limit kept: the tenth principle makes every metric provisional — no indicator, including the manifesto's own adoption, is final.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** Field `publication-and-attribution` is "the reader book, the DEV blog, commits, CI and pushes" and angle is *metric* — a concrete metric or experiment this area uses to measure itself. TOP 2025 (the 2026-10-29 reading on this same cell, Grant et al. — seven research practices × three levels, TOP Factor retired) tells how *transparent* a publication is. The Leiden Manifesto (2015, 2,282 citations, Nature Comment, the predecessor DORA cites as companion) tells how *responsibly* any publication or citation metric — including TOP, including the plant's own `trial_metrics.collect` counts — should be used. Exactly the publishing-and-attribution question the plant trades on when it counts outcomes and reports `wakes=`, `settlements=`, `verified=` without yet carrying the manifesto's constraints.

## What this plant already does

*The plant counts publications and now grades their transparency, but has no typed word for whether a count is mission-aligned, field-normalized, open-to-verification, portfolio-bound, or false-precision-free — the five gaps Leiden predicts become gaming.*

- **Trial metrics counts but does not carry Leiden assessment context.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/trial_metrics.py:31-33` `PREFIXES = {"redeliveries":"seed redeliver ","observations":"seed observation ","addressed_messages":"[dm]","legacy_task_transitions":"[task-"}` plus `:36-37` `PLACEBO_WINDOW = timedelta(hours=10)` and `:45-57` `_instant/_window_counts` feed `collect(home,start,*,end)` (`:101-178`) which returns `{hours, counts, by_source/by_role, post-checks, health/services.json, outcomes/references/verified/incomplete/latency, placebo}` with a `limits` string noting services need deltas and outcomes are author reports. No principle name is typed — a `counts[settlements]=12` without `mission`, `field`, or `portfolio` is indistinguishable from gaming.

- **Transparency vocabulary exists without Leiden guardrails.** Prior `publication-and-attribution--metric` landing (2026-10-29) added `TOP_RESEARCH_PRACTICES / TOP_LEVELS ("not-disclosed","disclosed","shared-and-cited","certified") / TOP_VERIFICATION_PRACTICES / TOP_FACTOR_RETIRED` plus helpers `top_level_for / top_profile_for / top_factor_score` beside `PREFIXES/collect`. That vocabulary grades *disclosure* per practice, not whether the count should support qualitative judgement, whether local excellence is protected, or whether three decimals is false precision — `rg -n "LEIDEN|Hicks|DORA|misplaced|field.*variation" src/` is empty before this review.

- **Placebo and per-role windows gesture at field variation but type it as outages, not as evaluation fields.** `:37-43` `PLACEBO_WINDOW / PLACEBO_ENTRY_FLOOR=1000 / PLACEBO_COUNTS` marks low-coverage windows as gaps, and `:60-84` `placebo_scale` computes `mean_abs_swing` across placebo windows. That is a robustness check for the baseline, not a field-normalized rate within a field — a `docs` window and a `health` window are summed in the same denominator though Hicks #6 says citation/publication rates vary wildly by field.

- **Post-check and outcomes judge prose and binding, not metric use.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/post_check.py` (`MERTON_NORMS / SKEPTICISM_STATES`) and `src/mishe_tauftauf/outcome_publication.py` (`DISCLOSURE_LEVELS / MANIFEST_STATES` plus provenance helpers) judge whether a publication is readable and verifiable, not whether counting `addressed_messages` as productivity is itself legitimate per Leiden #1, #8, #9 — the plant already disclaims "Settlements and messages are not productivity or verified deliveries" in `trial_metrics.collect`'s limits prose, but without a typed principle the disclaimer is prose.

- **The plant has the inputs to satisfy Leiden 4/5 and does not name them.** Every `Feed.entries()` timestamp, every `checks/*.json` `created` row, every outcome `evidence.sha256` is already stored and could be verified by the evaluated — but no symbol marks "data collection open/transparent/simple" or "evaluated can verify" so a closed or complex tally is prose-invisible.

*Consequence the source predicts.* Leiden p.429: procedures designed to increase quality "are now threatening to damage the scientific system" when data drive replaces judgement. Applied here: reporting `trial_metrics.collect` weekly counts plus a retired-sum `top_factor_score` without the 10 principles makes `12 outcomes` read as quality regardless of mission, field, portfolio, or systemic gaming — exactly the misplaced concreteness and unscrutinized indicator the manifesto says to refuse.

## What is new here

**Leiden reframes the plant's `collect` counts and TOP transparency grades from "how many / how disclosed" to "how responsibly measured" — ten typed principles that make gaming, false precision, and unscrutinized reuse visible as `unknown` rather than as neutral numbers.**

Five ideas transfer directly, each bounded by what Hicks et al. concede.

1. **Ten principles as the closed metric-governance vocabulary (Nature 520:429-431, 10-item list).** Today the plant types `OUTCOME_KINDS`, `MERTON_NORMS`, `CREDIT_ROLES`, `TOP_LEVELS`. The transfer adds `LEIDEN_PRINCIPLES: Final = ("quantitative-supports-qualitative","measure-against-mission","protect-locally-relevant","open-transparent-simple","allow-verification","account-for-field-variation","portfolio-judgement-for-individuals","avoid-misplaced-concreteness-and-false-precision","recognize-systemic-effects","scrutinize-regularly")` as the typed assessment contract — the check is the detector, not a new gate on `collect`. Limit kept: principles are for human research evaluation; the plant maps them to role/window/message assessment without claiming personnel decisions are thereby human-compliant.

2. **Open-transparent-simple plus allow-verification as a verifiable-data contract (Principles 4–5) — a pure predicate over existing tape, not a new store.** The plant already holds `Feed.entries()` and `checks/` rows; the transfer makes `data_is_open_transparent_simple` a typed boolean (caller-held: can the indicator be recomputed from stored bytes with documented `PREFIXES / PLACEBO_WINDOW` simple rules?) and `evaluated_can_verify` a boolean (are raw counts and method visible to the evaluated role?). Limit kept: simple does not forbid the existing placebo swing calculation — it requires that the calculation be documented and recomputable, which it is not today.

3. **Field variation and mission alignment as the normalization the count needs (Principles 2,6) — a typed denominator, not a claim that fields are comparable.** `by_role` and placebo windows already exist; the transfer makes `field_variation_accounted` (is this count reported per field/role rather than as a single plant total?) and `mission_aligned` (is the mission of the measured window stated — e.g., trial vs baseline, placebo vs experimental?) typed booleans so a single `summed_all_roles=84` without per-field rates is a measurable field-variation gap. Limit kept: within-field honesty only; cross-field ranking stays misplaced — the manifesto does not bless normalization as comparability.

4. **Misplaced concreteness and false precision as a typed refusal of decimal exactness (Principle 8, three-decimal JIF example).** The transfer introduces `false_precision_holds(precision: int, variability: float) -> bool` pure logic and `misplaced_concreteness_holds(has_assumption_clarified: bool) -> bool` so that reporting `wakes=119.333±0.001` or `impact=2.847` with three decimals when underlying citation/settlement variability is high is a typed gap, not a neutral display. Multiple robust indicators (Hicks: reduce concreteness via clarification + multiple indicators) remain the cure. Limit kept: the threshold is not a law — it marks that three decimals with high variability is the canonical example, without fixing a universal decimal rule.

5. **Systemic effects and regular scrutiny as the metric's lifecycle (Principles 9–10) — reactivity and provisionality, not permanence.** Metrics change behaviour; the transfer makes `systemic_effects_acknowledged` (are gaming/reactivity risks stated with the count?) and `scrutiny_due(last_scrutinized_at: datetime, now: datetime, max_age_days: int = 90) -> bool` pure predicates so a count reported without a stated review date is `scrutiny_unknown` — the indicator is provisional until next scrutiny. Limit kept: no indicator is ever final, including Leiden compliance itself — the tenth principle applies to this vocabulary.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** No DORA or COARA pledge is added; no impact factor or citation count is introduced; no JIF is used as a proxy for quality; no individual portfolio is constructed from the unbounded tape — the principles are typed as role-reporting and window-reporting constraints on the counts the plant already emits, without claiming the plant thereby performs human research assessment. No `Feed.append`/`seed yield`/`wall` mutation, no new lock or `WALL_MAX_*` change, no chat-protocol wire-format change — only ten typed kinds as an import-safe, pure vocabulary beside the windowed counts, exactly the manifesto's scope that publication attributions and assessment metrics must be bounded before they are used.
