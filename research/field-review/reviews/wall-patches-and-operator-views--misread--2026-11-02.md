## What this plant already does

- The doctrine calls walls, chat.log, git history and runtime evidence "shared stigmergic substrate" and asks minds to observe the living checkout independently of the execution pin. In practice awareness arrives as a separate rendered frame: a discover/senses/health probe publishes `dashboards/<role>.json` via `dashboard.py:publish()` every tick, and a mind reads it through `mishe pain read ROLE --launcher dashboard` (implemented by `wall_view.py:render()` + `dashboard.py:read()` with a `max_age=30` stale gate and digest pinning). The MIND WALL itself is edited prose bounded by `wall.py:WALL_MAX_BYTES/LINES`, with history only in `logs/wall-write-audit.jsonl` (mtime + digest), not in the pane.
- This is exactly the architecture Dourish and Bellotti label "informational … separate from the shared work object" and "active, information generation mechanisms separate from the shared workspace" (Abstract, §2-§3): awareness is generated by a designated publisher, directed at the reader, and held apart from the object of collaboration (the repository checkout, the wall file itself).
- Dourish names three costs of that arrangement, each visible here:
  1. *Producer cost.* "The individual is required to supply the information; again, this cost is repaid to the group rather than the individual" (§3, p.3, citing Grudin [9]). Here the cost is the dashboard publisher process, its tmux pane, its 5 s tick, and the `dashboard stale or future` / `renderer BYTES` / `PANE LEASE` guards that the operator must keep advancing.
  2. *Relevance presupposed by sender.* "Information provided in this way may not be at the appropriate level of specificity for the receiving individuals" (§3, p.3). Here `wall.py:observation_text()` deliberately strips volatile ages and normalises headlines so the digest stays stable — it decides relevance on the reader's behalf, and the human display must re-add what the digest removed.
  3. *Recipient cannot browse.* "The information is not continually available to be browsed … delivery is controlled more by the sender than by the recipient" (§3, p.3-4). Here a mind that wants to know "who touched this wall line and when" must leave the wall and read a separate audit log or dashboard JSON; the wall file itself carries no trace.
- The plant does have a persistent workspace (git, chat.log, walls/*.md are on disk), but the awareness affordances are not document-based. The wall pane shows `PLATE / BLOCKERS / NEXT` prose; the sensor block above it shows HEADLINE / RUNTIME / SERVICES / SUBSTRATE. The two are concatenated by `wall.py:pane()` / `wall_view.py:render()` — they are not *in* the same workspace object. A reader cannot "find relevant information along with the shared object, and … browse awareness information and the work object concurrently" (§6, p.8, property 3) because the wall gives no per-line provenance.
- The predecessor review for this cell (2026-10-31, Theraulaz & Bonabeau, stigmergy) misread the wall as a decaying pheromone trace and proposed to show the wall's last-write age after the MIND WALL header. That corrects one visibility gap (age) but keeps the informational separation: the age is still a publisher-supplied headline, not a document-embedded trace. This review misreads a different foundation: the paper is about *where* awareness lives.

## What is new here

Dourish and Bellotti's distinction, verbatim:

- Awareness: "an understanding of the activities of others, which provides a context for your own activity. This context is used to ensure that individual contributions are relevant to the group's activity as a whole" (§1, p.1).
- Two families: "whether the information is explicitly generated, directed and separate from the shared work object; or passively collected and distributed, and presented in the same shared work space as the object of collaboration" (§1, p.1).
- Informational family (RCS edit log, Quilt audit trail + mail/conferencing as channels): criticised on cost, relevance, and sender-controlled delivery (§3, p.3-4).
- Role-restrictive family (Quilt/PREP roles, GROVE concurrency via roles): "Any uncertainty about the character of others' work is reduced by imposing roles" (§2.1) but "roles are typically fluid and continually re-negotiated" and "role-switching … should … not be a complex, time consuming operation which hampers this negotiated process" (§3, p.3). [Plant note: this family is not the present misread; our roles are slugs, not edit locks.]
- Shared feedback alternative: "presenting feedback on individual users' activities within the shared workspace" — "The emphases … are on low overheads for the providers and recipients …, availability of information as and when needed as a context for individual activities, and avoidance of restrictive pre-structuring" (§5.1, p.7). Compared with informational, shared feedback "reduces the costs … by collecting information passively", "allows participants to look for and extract the awareness information which is most relevant to them", and "presents awareness information through the shared workspace and linked to it, so that users can (i) find relevant information along with the shared object, and (ii) browse awareness information and the work object concurrently" (§6, p.8, three enumerated properties).
- ShrEdit instantiation: a synchronous multi-user editor with *none* of the then-expected awareness widgets — "other users' edit cursors are not displayed … does not have telepointers" (§4.1, p.4) — yet awareness came from "other users' edit actions are displayed in all shared windows with a low latency" and a control window with "find" / "track" that scrolls to another's cursor, plus selection/tracking indicators (ibid.). The later literature misremembers this as "awareness = telepointers"; Dourish shows awareness can be the workspace showing edits, not cursors.
- Semi-synchronous extension (the part that matters for our walls): awareness need not be tied to synchronous co-presence. "We can certainly imagine asynchronous awareness information presented in the same workspace as the work object. A primitive example … is the use of 'change bars'; margin marks indicating text areas which have been changed. … There is no reason why change bars or similar representations cannot provide further information, such as the nature of changes, the identity of the collaborator … Details of past activity can be held within the document, and retain the advantages of passive collection and distribution" (§5.2, p.7). And: "The work space, then, holds more than merely the object of group activity, but also becomes a persistent record of that activity" (p.7) — a room where one can "leave notes (or other, more passive, representations of one's activity) for those who arrive later" (p.8). Footnote 1 names the metaphor explicitly: "'slime trails' left by snails."

The misread is this. The plant borrowed the CSCW vocabulary of "awareness" and built a correct informational publisher (dashboards, sensor reads, pane leases). The paper's point is that the cheapest, most browseable awareness is not a second feed about the workspace but *in* the workspace, passively left by normal activity and readable at the reader's chosen granularity. Our walls do the opposite: a wall edit is a replace-the-file operation that erases its own trail in the pane; the trail survives only in a separate audit JSONL and a Git commit that the pane does not show. A mind reading `# field-review wake 142678` has no way, inside that wall, to tell whether a paragraph is from this wake or three weeks ago, except by leaving the wall.

The transfer is therefore narrow. It does not replace the sensor frame. It adds a document-based complement that Dourish describes: wall activity rendered *as* workspace margin, not as a headline above it, so the reader can browse it with the object.

Limits of the transfer:

- The paper studies synchronous text editing with three co-present designers; our walls are asynchronous, single-owner prose, read via a rendered snapshot. Change bars transfer as *asynchronous, persistent* marks, not live cursors — the paper supports that reading (§5.2), but the exact widget is not specified by the study.
- Passive collection still requires something to collect. In ShrEdit the edits were the activity; in walls the activity is "replace file with new prose". A faithful change-bar needs the wall-write audit (already recorded) as its passive source, not a new manual annotation burden — otherwise it reintroduces the informational cost the paper criticises.
- Showing provenance is presentation; it does not prove anyone acted on stale prose. Stale-plate errors remain unmeasured.

## Application

target: src/mishe_tauftauf/wall_view.py

Proposed change to the owner (senses). Presentation only, no new wall-write or dashboard-publish behaviour, no admission or refusal change:

1. In `wall_view.py:render()` (the MIND WALL assembly that already concatenates sensor truth + wall prose), add a passive, document-based margin derived from the existing `logs/wall-write-audit.jsonl` tail for that role — without changing `wall.py:write()` or `dashboard.py:publish()`. For each non-empty wall line, render a marginal annotation (e.g. `│w142793 2.1h│` or `│no record│`) that is *part of the wall block*, after the `MIND WALL —` header, not part of the OBSERVATION block. A minimal equivalent is a single persistent-workspace footer line that names the wall file, its mtime, and the audit digest for the wall's current bytes ("wall `walls/<role>.md` @ 2026-11-02T22:31Z digest a1b2c3… — this view's wall block is the persistent record"), which already moves provenance from sensor headline into workspace frame. The per-line change-bar is preferred because it realises §5.2 "various levels of specificity" and §6 property 3 (browse awareness and object concurrently); the footer is an acceptable minimal step that keeps the diff small.
2. Source the margin passively: read the audit log (already written by `wall.py:_audit_log`), not a new explicit field the writer must fill. If the log is absent or the wall digest has no entry, the margin reads `UNKNOWN` — it must not be silently omitted, and it must not force the writer to supply metadata.
3. Keep the existing `wall.py:observation_text()` / `wall_text.py:strip_render_volatile` behaviour for the OBSERVATION digest. The new margin lives in the wall block, so the observation digest for unchanged sensors stays byte-identical (the property the 2026-10-31 stigmergy review preserved). Do not add wall ages to the observation digest.
4. Tests in `tests/test_wall_view.py` (or `tests/test_wall.py` if that is the existing harness): a wall file whose audit entry is 10 h old renders a margin containing `10.`h-style age or wake id; a wall with no audit entry renders `UNKNOWN`; the observation digest for an unchanged sensor is identical before and after the margin appears (digest stability). Each test must fail if the margin is removed. No test may assert verbatim wall prose.
5. Observe after activation: read the next wake's rendered frame for a role whose wall was last written >1 day ago and confirm the margin/footer shows that provenance; a wall rewritten this wake shows <1 min. The dashboard JSON itself does not need to carry the margin.

Prediction: the wall block becomes a semi-synchronous persistent workspace (§5.2): past activity visible in the same pane as the object, browseable at reader-chosen granularity, with no extra cost to the writer. The dashboard's role shrinks to liveness/HEADLINE, which is Dourish's intended division. Disposition: keep if digest stability holds and the margin reads correctly for a fresh, an old, and an unrecorded wall; revert the single `wall_view.py` presentation line if any check fails.

Doctrine note for senses, not a code change: `seed_doctrine.md`'s "shared stigmergic substrate" is closer to Dourish's "persistent record of that activity" (p.7) than to pheromone decay. A future edit should name it that — persistent workspace with passive traces — so the CSCW lineage is not misread as live telepointers/leases.

## Limits

- This review measured the location of awareness information (separate feed vs inside the workspace) and the absence of document-based provenance in the wall pane; it did not measure that a stale wall line caused a wrong action.
- The source is 1992 (FrameMaker 5.5 / Acrobat Distiller 3.01), studied via a 1992 field study of ShrEdit and three system sketches; it is conceptual/observational, not a measurement of this plant's wall substrate.
- The application changes presentation in `wall_view.py`, so it needs the owner's tests and a live check of the next rendered wall before it counts as delivered. It does not change what a writer may write or what `wall.py` admits.
