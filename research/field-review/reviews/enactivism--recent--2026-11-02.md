  - Core enactive machinery restated (built on Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde 2009; Di Paolo 2009; Barandiaran 2017; Beer & Di Paolo 2023; Thompson 2007; Cummins 2014; Sanches de Oliveira et al. 2023): (a) Maturana & Varela autopoiesis as network that produces components that regenerate the network and constitute a distinguishable unity; minimal autonomy = organizational closure (behaviour fixed by internal states, Varela 1979) — then Varela's generalisation: autopoiesis is autonomy in the biochemical domain, autonomy can hold without biological aliveness (pp.55–57). (b) **Bare autopoiesis ≠ sensorimotor autonomy**: Di Paolo — autopoiesis is all-or-nothing, non-adaptive conservation possible; adaptivity is gradable and required for autonomous cognitive agency — regulation relative to viability conditions under precariousness (pp.57–58). (c) **Three operational conditions for genuine agency** (Barandiaran et al. 2009:370,376 summarised p.59): *individuality* (self-distinctive individual, not observer-imposed boundary), *interactional asymmetry* (system is source of its activity, modulates its coupling with environment — "alteration in the set of constraints that determine the coupling between S and E"), *normativity* (regulation according to norms generated by its own viability, not assigned externally; Parkinson's tremor vs adaptive action example p.60). Without conceptual language, intentions or self-awareness — an adaptive organisation of coupled processes (p.61). (d) **Observer co-construction**: autonomy is co-constructed by mechanism, observers and environment (Sanches de Oliveira et al. 2023, p.61–62); Cummins (2014) kitten/mother example p.62 — same physical coupling counted as non-agent (kitten moved by mother) or as agent (feline family as one system) depending on observer's system cut; limits are not fully observer-independent (pp.61–62). (e) **Precariousness** defined after Di Paolo & Thompson 2014:72 (fn24 p.59): without the enabling relations of the operationally closed network a constituent process stops or runs down; the system is restless and must "buy time" against imbalance, hence spontaneity. (f) **Extended and group agency** (pp.63–66): one-person extended human+artifact systems can satisfy the three conditions even though only the human is alive — coupling via continuous reciprocal causation (Clark & Chalmers 1998); the artifact-boundary is set by the human part but the agent is the whole extended system (pp.64–66). Limits kept: whether a purely artificial system can be autonomous requires separate treatment (p.54 n.4; §4 caveat p.62 "without him or her, all acting with reference to some intrinsic locus of value seems impossible — at least from today's level of technological development"); life not necessary but viability-rooted normativity is; functionalism vs enactive embodiment tension noted p.64 (reviewer footnote on Clark).

## What is new here

Tomczyk 2025 is the recent (2025) primary source this `recent` angle requires, and it does three things the plant has not embodied:

1. **It makes the closure vs autonomy gap testable.** Organizational closure (a loop that closes) is necessary but not sufficient; sensorimotor autonomy requires adaptivity — regulation relative to viability conditions under precariousness (Di Paolo 2009; Beer & Di Paolo 2023). The prior `enactivism--misread--2026-10-31` read only the abstract's sentence that "autopoiesis provides a systemic language for intrinsic teleology but needs adaptivity for sense-making" and could not operationalise it because the full text was unreachable — this paper supplies the operationalisation (three conditions + precariousness) and the explicit warning that a merely autopoietic system need not be adaptive or sense-making (pp.58, 62).

2. **It detaches biological aliveness from the three conditions.** Barandiaran et al.'s conditions do not invoke being alive (p.60–61); extended human+artifact systems satisfy them via the human's autopoietic normativity while the system as such is not alive (pp.65–66). For this plant that means a wall/tape/lock coupling can be assessed as an agent-like extended system (minds+records) without claiming the records are alive — the question is whether the coupling modulates its coupling adaptively, not whether the substrate metabolises.

3. **It names the observer's responsibility.** Autonomy attribution is co-constructed by mechanism, observer and environment (Sanches de Oliveira et al. 2023). The same mechanism is legitimately bounded as agent or non-agent depending on the observer's cut (Cummins kitten example, p.62; discussion p.61 "depending on the perspective set by one's specific research goals ... may overlap"). This directly applies to this plant's dashboard: the "autonomy loop health" fog label is itself an observer's cut that currently hides which boundary is being judged and whether the judgement is about closure or about viability-relative regulation. The source's compatibility note — extended systems require continuous reciprocal causation (Clark & Chalmers) — gives a check for when the whole vs the human-only boundary is warranted.

Limits retained: Tomczyk explicitly defers the purely artificial-system question (p.54 n.4), keeps Varela/Thompson's caution that autopoietic value "leaks in" with life (p.62), and flags the functionalism/embodiment tension in Clark's extended mind (p.64) — none of that is imported as a claim that this plant is alive or that any record coupling is automatically an agent.
