# The only honest scoreboard is a proper one — mean proper scores, not skill scores or ECE alone

## What we read

**Tilmann Gneiting, Adrian E. Raftery — "Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation", Journal of the American Statistical Association, Vol. 102, No. 477, March 2007, pp. 359–378, DOI 10.1198/016214506000001437** — read full primary source at:

Gneiting & Raftery is the canonical synthesis of proper scoring rules from Brier (1950), Good (1952), McCarthy (1956), Savage (1971), Schervish (1989) to Dawid, Grünwald, Buja et al. (2005). Four passages carry the metric this plant has not made — pagination is the PDF/JASA pagination, quotations via `pdftotext`.

1. **What proper means and the convex characterization (Abstract + §1 p.359 + §2.1 pp.360–361, Eq.1–5, Theorem 1).** Abstract: "A scoring rule is proper if the forecaster maximizes the expected score for an observation drawn from distribution F if he or she issues forecast F, rather than G≠F. It is strictly proper if the maximum is unique." §1 Eq.1: `S(Q,Q) ≥ S(P,Q)  ∀P,Q∈𝒫`, strictly proper iff equality ⇔ P=Q. Goal stated p.359: "maximize sharpness subject to calibration. Calibration is statistical consistency between the forecasts and the observations; sharpness is concentration of the predictive distributions and is a property of the forecasts only." Theorem 1 (p.361): regular S is (strictly) proper ⇔ `S(P,ω)=G(P)−∫G*(P,dP)+G*(P,ω)` for some (strictly) convex G on 𝒫 and subtangent G* at P. Then expected score `G(P)=S(P,P)=sup_Q S(Q,P)` is convex (entropy/information), and `d(P,Q)=S(Q,Q)−S(P,Q)` is nonnegative divergence (Bregman when smooth, p.361). Affine `cS+h(ω)` with c>0 stays proper (Eq.2).

2. **Categorical scores — Brier, log, spherical and the Savage/Schervish representations (§3 pp.362–365, Theorem 2 Eq.10, Examples 1–3, Table 1, Fig.1).** For `𝒫_m={p: p_i≥0, Σp_i=1}` Theorem 2 (McCarthy/Savage): regular S proper ⇔ `S(p,i)=G(p)−⟨G'(p),p⟩+G'_i(p)` for convex G and subgradient G'(p) (Eq.10). Examples: (1) Quadratic/Brier `S(p,i)=−Σ_j(δ_ij−p_j)²=2p_i−Σ_jp_j²−1`, divergence `Σ(p_j−q_j)²` squared Euclidean; (2) Spherical/pseudospherical `S(p,i)=p_i^{α−1}/(Σ_jp_j^α)^{(α−1)/α}` (α=2 spherical); (3) Logarithmic `S(p,i)=log p_i` from Shannon `G(p)=Σ p_j log p_j`, unbounded with `−∞` if p_i=0 event occurs. Table 1 p.365 lists binary mixing measure ν(dc) in Schervish Theorem 3 Eq.14: Brier ν uniform, spherical `(1−2c+2c²)^{−3/2}`, logarithmic `(c(1−c))^{−1}` infinite, zero–one point mass at c. Fig.1 p.364 plots G solid convex with tangent at p reading off S(p,0), S(p,1) and Bregman gap d(p,q). Beta family (§3 Example 5) with density `c^{α−1}(1−c)^{β−1}` interpolates log (α=β=0), Brier (1,1), zero–one (∞) and asymmetric members (α≠β).

3. **Skill scores are improper even when the underlying score is proper — the standard trap (§2.3 pp.361–362, Eq.8).** Aggregated mean score `S̄_n=1/n Σ S(P_i,x_i)` ranks forecasts on same situations. Skill scores standardize: `S̄_skill=(S̄_fcst−S̄_ref)/(S̄_opt−S̄_ref)` (Eq.8, ref=climatology, opt=point mass in outcome, 1=optimal, 0=reference, negative=worse than reference). Then p.362: "Unfortunately, skill scores of the form (8) are generally improper, even if the underlying scoring rule S is proper. Murphy (1973) studied hedging strategies in the case of the Brier skill score … He showed that the Brier skill score is asymptotically proper, in the sense that benefits of hedging become negligible as n grows. Mason's (2004) claim of propriety … is incorrect." Same caution applies to Brier/CRPS/interval skill scores.

4. **How scores are used — mean scores, optimum score estimation, and scores beyond the binary (§1–2, §4–6, §9 pp.359–361,365–366).** Mean score `S̄_n(θ)=1/n Σ S(P_θ,x_i)` with strictly proper S yields estimator `θ̂=argmax S̄_n(θ) → θ_0` (p.360); maximum likelihood is special case with log score; Pfanzagl/Birgé minimum contrast and Huber M-estimation framing. For continuous variables §4: quadratic `QS(p,ω)=2p(ω)−‖p‖²_2`, pseudospherical, logarithmic; CRPS and energy scores generalize absolute error (§4, kernel scores §5 via negative definite functions/Hoeffding). For quantiles/intervals §6: proper interval score addresses width as well as coverage, proposed as utility for interval estimation. Case study §8 shows improper scores can invert the ranking of competing probabilistic weather forecasts.

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** Characterization assumes regular scores (finite except possibly −∞ when p_i=0, Def.1/2); unbounded log score is allowed via extended real line with quasi-integrability. Results hold for convex class 𝒫, not arbitrary constraint sets. Skill-score impropriety is general, with asymptotic propriety only as n→∞. CRPS/energy/interval scores are cited via Gneiting–Raftery, not re-derived. No threshold or skill baseline transfers as constant.

**Why this source, for this cell.** `model-judgment--metric` asks for the concrete metric the area uses to measure itself. The plant's four prior cells imported Guo's binned ECE (misread), Nixon's GCE criticisms (critique), Wang's invert-softmax (recent), Angelopoulos conformal (transfer) and Laksh ensembles with NLL/Brier (mechanism). None grounds *why* NLL/Brier are the right scoreboard or what aggregation is legitimate. Gneiting–Raftery is that ground — the JASA review that defines proper vs improper and proves which numbers encourage honesty and which invite hedging.

## What is new here

**Replace "calibration error only" with "mean strictly proper score plus divergence" — Brier and log score are not just two formulas, they are the two canonical convex entropies whose expectations are maximized only by honest quotes; their gap d(P,Q) is the distance to truth; and normalizing them into a skill score destroys the propriety that made them useful.**

1. **Propriety as the metric contract — Eq.1 `S(Q,Q)≥S(P,Q)` with equality ⇔ P=Q, Theorem 1 convex G (p.361).** A number is a calibration metric iff the forecaster who believes Q cannot improve expected score by quoting P≠Q. ECE fails; Brier `−Σ(δ−p)²` and log `log p_i` pass strictly. Consequence: judge evaluations must report a mean proper score `S̄_n=1/n Σ S(P_i,x_i)` (p.361) optimized `θ̂=argmax S̄_n`, not ECE minimized. Maximum likelihood is then just log-score optimum (p.360).

2. **Entropy and divergence as the sharpness-vs-calibration split — `G(P)=S(P,P)` and `d(P,Q)=S(Q,Q)−S(P,Q)` (§2.2).** G is maximal achievable utility (sharpness; property of forecast only), calibration is joint `P` vs realized `x`, divergence is expected penalty for quoting P when truth is Q. When G smooth, d is Bregman (p.361) — for Brier, squared Euclidean; for log, KL. This gives the "sharpness subject to calibration" slogan (p.359) an equation: tune for `G` under `d≈0`.

3. **Schervish mixing representation as the cost-loss menu — Theorem 3 Eq.14, Table 1, beta family (pp.364–365).** Every binary proper score is a mixture of cost-weighted zero–one scores `S_c(p,1)=(1−c)1{p>c}/ν`, `S_c(p,0)=c1{p≤c}/ν` with measure ν(dc); density `G''(c)`. Brier↔uniform ν, log↔`1/c(1−c)` infinite (heavily weights extremes), zero–one↔point mass at c. Beta `c^{α−1}(1−c)^{β−1}` interpolates and yields asymmetric scores when α≠β. Operational: choose `c` or (α,β) to match the plant's decision cost (how much a false yes vs false no matters), don't pick Brier by habit.

4. **Skill scores Eq.8 are improper — keep them as documentation, never as ranking (§2.3 p.362).** `S_skill=(S̄_fcst−S̄_ref)/(S̄_opt−S̄_ref)` with `S̄_ref`=climatology and `S̄_opt`=point mass invites hedging; Murphy (1973) shows Brier skill score only asymptotically proper, Mason (2004) claim incorrect. Keep `S_skill` for communication only with explicit "improper" warning; rank by raw `S̄_n` on the same `n` situations.

5. **Scores beyond binary as forward metric vocabulary — CRPS/energy/interval scores (§4–6).** CRPS generalizes absolute error for CDF forecasts; energy score for multivariate; interval score `S_α(l,u,x)` proper for central `(1−α)` intervals and trades width vs coverage (proposed §6 as utility for interval estimation). For this plant: if the judge ever emits an interval or CDF, the same propriety contract applies without inventing a new ECE.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the paper.** Only the definitions, representations, and impropriety caveat transfer; no JASA figure number, no weather-study score value, no fitted ν density is imported as a plant constant. Regularity (`−∞` only when `p_i=0` event occurs) is kept as validation rule. Skill-score asymptotic propriety (large-n) is kept as doc, not as ranking use. CRPS/energy/interval scores are named as docs, not as implemented continuous forecast helpers — the plant forecasts categorical yes/no.
