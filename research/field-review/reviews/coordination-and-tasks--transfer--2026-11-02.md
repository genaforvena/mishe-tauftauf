## What we read

**Design Principles and Threats to Sustainable Organizations That Manage Commons** — Elinor Ostrom, Workshop in Political Theory and Policy Analysis, Indiana University, W99-6, 17 Feb 1999 (expanded Ch. 3 of *Governing the Commons*, Cambridge UP 1990) — read full PDF at:

- <https://dlc.dlib.indiana.edu/dlcrest/api/core/bitstreams/b741af75-4871-479e-8d68-e5ebba6d44ae/content> (Indiana DLIB, 99 kB, 8 pp., fetched 200 2026-11-02, pdftotext 614 lines)
- DOI/book: <https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511807763> (*Governing the Commons*, 1990)

Four passages carry the transferable mechanism, each with the plant-level analogue it replaces.

1. **A common-pool resource (CPR) and eight design principles (§1, pp.1–6; *Governing the Commons* Ch.3).** A CPR is jointly used where one person's use subtracts from what is available to others and exclusion is difficult. Sustainable self-governance is not explained by any single rule but by a configuration of design principles that appear together in long-surviving cases: (1) clearly defined boundaries of the resource and of authorized users; (2) congruence between appropriation/provision rules and local conditions; (3) collective-choice arrangements — most affected can participate in modifying operational rules; (4) monitoring accountable to users (often users themselves); (5) graduated sanctions depending on seriousness/context; (6) rapid low-cost conflict-resolution arenas; (7) minimal recognition by external authorities of users' right to organize; (8) nested enterprises — governance organized in multiple layers. Definition: a "design principle" is a conception used consciously or unconsciously by those constituting a continuing association (p.1). The list is configurational — principles work together (p.5) — and absence of several predicts failure (p.6).

2. **Boundaries and congruence (Principles 1–2, pp.1–2).** Without defined boundaries "no one knows what they are managing or for whom" — benefits leak to outsiders, destruction follows. Congruence means rules restricting time/place/technology/quantity are tailored to local physical/cultural/economic conditions; uniform national blueprints "can rarely take into account the specific attributes of a resource" and fail. Illustration: each long-surviving irrigation system uses subtly different water-fee/maintenance rules, but all satisfy proportionality — those receiving more water pay more (Tang 1992; Lam 1998, p.2). The move is to close the resource and then fit rules to the spot, not to generalize one rule.

3. **Quasi-voluntary compliance via mutual monitoring and graduated sanctions (Principles 4–5, pp.2–5, citing Levi 1988).** External enforcement is largely absent in robust CPRs; high compliance is explained as contingent self-commitment — "I follow the rules if others do and monitoring will catch free-riders" — sustained because monitoring is a by-product of use (the rotation irrigator waiting for his turn watches the predecessor, p.3) and yields private information about compliance rates. Costs/benefits of monitoring are not independent of rule design — users who craft their own rules learn to make them enforceable (p.3). Sanctions start low and escalate on repeated violation; apprehension has three effects: stops the infraction, informs the offender that detection is likely, and imposes reputation loss (p.4). Principles 1–5 are stated as an interlocking solution to the commitment-and-monitoring problem (p.5).

4. **Conflict resolution, recognition, nesting and the eight threats (§6–8, pp.5–8).** Even honest actors misinterpret rules ("is a child an 'individual' for canal cleaning?", p.5); without a local arena to discuss infractions, perceived unfairness collapses conditional cooperation. Principle 7: external authorities must at least not challenge users' right to devise institutions, else defectors appeal outward. Principle 8: larger systems nest enterprises by layer (a farmer-governed system with five layers, each with distinct rules — p.6). The companion threat list (pp.6–8) names failure modes even well-principled CPRs face: blueprint thinking, overreliance on simple voting, rapid exogenous change, transmission failure across generations, overuse of external help, aid ignoring indigenous institutions, corruption, lack of large-scale information aggregation.

**Limits the source states, which this review keeps.** Principles are empirical regularities across small-scale, community-governed cases (Japan, Switzerland, Philippines irrigation; Japanese fisheries; Cree hunting), not a covering law; the paper warns they do not guarantee survival and enumerates threats that overwhelm even principled institutions (p.6). Small-N cases characterized by few principles fail more often (Ostrom 1990; Morrow & Hull 1996, p.6) but the inference is configurational, not additive scoring. External enforcement absence is a scope condition — the argument does not generalize to CPRs where a central authority is present and effective.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** The field `coordination-and-tasks` names "addressed messages, walls and settlement" — the plant's coordination system. The *angle* is *transfer*: a cross-domain transfer onto one of this plant's parts. Ostrom's problem is coordination without a central enforcer — exactly the wall-coordination trial's premise (AGENTS.md: "Minds choose and organize useful work" without a central assigner). The CPR is the shared resource every mind withdraws from: wall bytes, feed sequence, host capacity, and `main` — rivalrous and hard to exclude.

## What this plant already does

*The shared checkout, walls and feed are governed as a CPR managed by one global lock and blanket refusal, without boundaries, congruence, graduated sanctions, or nested arenas — the pre-Ostrom baseline the paper predicts will not sustain quasi-voluntary compliance under stress.*

`src/mishe_tauftauf/wall.py:19` `WALL_MAX_BYTES=16384` / `:21` `WALL_MAX_LINES=200` is one uniform blueprint applied to every role regardless of local conditions (Principle 2 violation). `src/mishe_tauftauf/feed.py:42` `publication_lock` (`threading.RLock` + `fcntl.flock` on `.publication.lock`) is the single resource allocator for tape, wall and chat — no per-resource boundary (Principle 1) and no tailored appropriation rule (Principle 2). `src/mishe_tauftauf/wall.py:500` `_reject_peer_wall_copy` and `:559` `_reject_peer_history_copy` enforce binary refusal — any peer-copy digest is rejected outright, not a graduated sanction that starts low and escalates on repetition (Principle 5). Monitoring is externalized to `health`/`genome` (dashboard sensors, `mishe-self-development-current-*`, `observer-checks.service`), not by users accountable to users (Principle 4) — the dashboard's `SUBSTRATE RED controller-shed LIMITED` (wall 194486) shows what happens when the external monitor is shed. Conflict over "is this a violation?" has no local arena: refusal appends to `OUTCOME_REFUSAL_AUDIT` (`:24`) but there is no low-cost discussion step before refusal (Principle 6). Governance is flat: `wall.py:433` `message()`, `:463` `write()`, `:648` `settle()` and `task_state.py:549` `_append` all contend on the same single-layer enterprise, not nested layers (Principle 8). Collective-choice (Principle 3) exists only as `genome` patches and wall-coordination trial doctrine — walls themselves do not record rule change — matching the threat "blueprint thinking" (p.6) and "overreliance on simple voting" where the plant overrelies on arrival-order `sequence`.

*The plant's best nearby practice.* `wall.py:463` `write()` already keeps a per-role wall (`walls/<role>.md`) — a boundary primitive — and `wall_text.document_digest` normalizes prose before comparison, i.e. a congruence hook. `task_state.py:19` `DEPENDENCIES` (Malone transfer, 2026-10-29) types tasks by dependency, which is a step toward tailored rules. `feed.py:383` `_close_stale_sync_tasks` supersedes older `kernel-sync-*` tasks on replant — a nested-enterprise primitive (parent replant supersedes children). But none of these carry monitoring/sanction layering or graduated response.

## What is new here

**Govern the walls/feed as a CPR: boundaries before allocation, congruence before blueprint, graduated sanctions before blanket refusal, mutual monitoring before external health, and nesting before flat contention.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number where it lands and each bounded by what the source concedes.

1. **Boundaries and congruence as additive metadata — close the resource and fit the rule to the spot (Principles 1–2, p.1–2).** Today `Wall.write` treats every wall under one `WALL_MAX_BYTES/LINES`; the transfer records, per wall, *which* resource boundary it belongs to and whether its rule is congruent with its local condition. No new lock; just a typed annotation that makes blueprint violations visible before they become `LIMITED` shedding.

2. **Graduated sanctions and mutual monitoring instead of binary refusal (Principles 4–5, pp.2–5, Levi quasi-voluntary compliance).** Today a peer-copy is refused at the same cost whether it is a first honest duplicate of a template or the tenth deliberate wall-clobber. The transfer introduces a pure, read-only sanction-level estimator — first/copy/history-template infraction → low (audit + warning), repeated same-digest clobber → escalating — so `OUTCOME_REFUSAL_AUDIT` and `_reject_peer_*_copy` can distinguish deterrence from destruction. Monitoring becomes a by-product of use (the writer waiting for its turn watches the predecessor) rather than only `health` polling, matching the rotation-irrigation mechanism (p.3).

3. **Local conflict arena and nested enterprises (Principles 6 & 8, pp.5–6; threats p.6).** Today ambiguity ("is this wall text a copy or a legitimate restoration?") is resolved only by the writer's validator throwing `ValueError`. The transfer adds a pure conflict-classifier (honest-mistake vs slide-past) and a nesting check — per-role wall vs per-resource-board vs site-sync layer — so a `wall.py` vs `task_state.py` contention does not moves all layers at once. This does not grant external authorities new power (Principle 7) and does not replace voting/sequence — it replaces *overreliance* on them (threat 2, p.6).

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** No claim that eight principles guarantee coordination — the threat list is retained (p.6) and scored as residual risk. No new lock, no change to `WALL_MAX_*` enforcement, no wiring of external authority; the helpers are pure/read-only estimators usable for dashboard annotation and refusal-context, not hard gates. Small-N caveat stands: configuration is assessed qualitatively, not as an additive score.
