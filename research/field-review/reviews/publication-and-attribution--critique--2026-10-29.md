# Publication will mislead if it borrows C2PA's shape without its missing security goals — timestamp binding, revocation, strong integrity and validator agreement

## What we read

**Enis Golaszewski, Neal Krawetz, Alan T. Sherman, Edward Zieglar, Sai K. Matukumalli, Roberto Yus, Carson L. Kegley, Michael Barthel, William Bowman, Bharg Barot, Kaur Kullman — "Verifying Provenance of Digital Media: Why the C2PA Specifications Fall Short"**, 23 April 2026, 7 pp. executive paper + full study — read full text at:

- <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24890v1> (arXiv:2604.24890v1 [cs.CR], 27 Apr 2026, PDF, fetched 200 2026-10-29, 7 pages with Figs. 1–4)
- Full study with formal-methods analysis: <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/804> (IACR 2026/804, April 23 2026, linked from arXiv p. 1, ref [2])
- Companion critique for deployment reality: <https://afip.org/research/content-credentials-analysis> (AFIP Independent Analysis, updated 2026-04-20, fetched 200 2026-10-29, table of stripping by platform, privacy/equity analysis) and NIST overview <https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=959123> / <https://airc.nist.gov/docs/NIST.AI.100-4.SyntheticContent.ipd.pdf> (Feb 2024, same stripping/replay analysis, pp. 22–23)
- Specification under critique: C2PA Technical Specification 2.2–2.4 (spec cited by Golaszewski as failing; prior review read 2.2 at <https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html>)

Six passages carry the critique this plant would reproduce if it adopted the C2PA provenance vocabulary the previous two reviews just made typed — each with its stated limits kept.

1. **What C2PA claims vs. what any provenance system must claim (p. 2, "What C2PA Claims to Do" + "three additional essential goals").** C2PA makes only two security claims: (1) *claim integrity* — conforming validators can detect tampering of the Content Credentials, and (2) *weak file integrity* — validators can detect modification of bits *outside* the exclusion range, not inside it. The authors argue any trustworthy provenance system additionally requires (3) *timestamp agreement* — claim generator and validator agree on timestamp, (4) *validator consistency* — conforming validators sharing spec version and trust lists agree on validity of any claim, and (5) *strong file integrity* — validators detect modification of *any* bits including non-C2PA metadata. The five together are the yardstick; C2PA's self-description collapses them to two. Limit kept: the authors note cryptography itself is battle-tested; failure is in *integration*, not primitives.

2. **Timestamps can be replaced without detection (p. 3, Fig. 1, Finding 1).** "Nothing in the signed data references the timestamp, allowing removal and replacement without detection. We altered the timestamp in this image. C2PA validators display the date without noting that it may not be original." The layered protection — signature over media, optional trusted timestamp over signature — lacks a binding between the two, so a conforming validator accepts conflicting timestamps as valid. The authors propose "signed timestamped assertions" as a fix (provide evidence *when* content was created/signed bound to the claim). Limit kept: the flaw is protocol-level; a correctly signed manifest can still carry a forged time and read as `Trusted`.

3. **Revoked or compromised credentials are still accepted (p. 3–4, Fig. 2, Finding 2).** "Conforming validators are not required to check for revoked certificates, allowing adversaries to use compromised keys without detection, defeating all five security goals." Demo: Nikon Z6 III camera used to sign an AI image; certificate revoked Nov 2025; six months later Adobe Inspect reports valid while Verifieddit reports invalid; neither reports the revocation. Cause diagnosed: spec makes OCSP optional and *forbids* CRLs (privacy reason), so revocation checking is unavailable when needed. "To achieve the security goals, it is essential to check for revoked certificates. … CRLs offer much better privacy properties than OCSP." Limit kept: tradeoff is real — OCSP leaks which credential is being checked — but current design achieves neither privacy nor security.

4. **Different tools produce contradictory results (p. 4, Finding 3) and parts of files can be modified without detection (p. 4–5, Fig. 3, Finding 4).** Validator inconsistency: "same image can be labeled valid by one tool and invalid by another" + conflicting AI-generated verdicts, caused by missing version number, overly liberal policies, certification not checking source code. Exclusion-range mutability: "Google's conforming Pixel 10 Pro camera places GPS information in an exclusion range, enabling an attacker to insert a false GPS location. … Proofmode Verify does not detect our alteration." Exclusion exists for privacy (redact GPS) but currently allows undetected post-signing mutation of any metadata placed there. The authors suggest additional signatures over redacted ranges instead of exclusion. Limit kept: exclusion serves a legitimate privacy need; removal without replacement reintroduces it.

5. **Credentials expire and become unverifiable + certification does not ensure compliance (p. 5–6, Figs. 4, Findings 5–6).** Expired certificates make media unverifiable within months — Arizona Secretary of State pilot image validates Jan 2025, fails Jan 2026 though file unchanged — incompatible with 22-month election retention (52 U.S.C. §20701), 25-month financial, 2-year political-ad retention. Certification: "based largely on self-reported compliance with no examination of the product's functionality or source code" and specs "do not define security requirements for conforming validators," so "conforming" does not imply correct or secure. The 2.3 spec (Jan 2026, Pixel 10 Pro) incorporated some suggestions; 2.4 (Apr 2026) addressed none. Limit kept: authors call C2PA "promising" and list concrete remediations — this is not a rejection, but a "not yet for high-stakes" (financial, journalism, legal).

6. **AFIP/NIST deployment reality — stripping as the common case, history vs. truth, privacy/equity costs (AFIP Apr 2026; NIST 2024).** AFIP testing April 2026: Instagram/X/TikTok/LinkedIn/WhatsApp strip C2PA manifests (Facebook partial); "treating 'no credential' as 'not AI' is the single most common misreading" (Mixpeek guide, echoed by AFIP). RAND June 2025 quoted by AFIP: "C2PA suffers from a lack of rigorous security analysis … Threat modeling … is needed." Privacy: credentials embed name/org/geolocation/timestamps/device IDs, publicly readable, "cannot be retroactively removed from copies already in circulation" — "serious surveillance surface" (World Privacy Forum). Equity: trusted status requires ~$289/yr DigiCert certificate, no Let's-Encrypt equivalent — independent journalists appear "unknown source." Spec's `SHOULD NOT provide value judgments` box (C2PA Ch.1 p.2) means `Trusted` ≠ true; AFIP's phrase: credentials record *history* ("what a signing entity claims"), not *authenticity* ("whether scene actually occurred"). Limit kept: voluntary self-labeling is useful signal *when present and chain-maintained*; the critique is about relying on it as primary/sole mechanism.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** Field `publication-and-attribution` is "the reader book, the DEV blog, commits, CI and pushes" and angle is *critique* — a known failure mode we would reproduce if we adopted the area blindly. The plant just landed two provenance reviews onto `outcome_publication.py` (C2PA 2.2 ingredient/validation-state vocabulary) and `post_check.py` (Merton's communism/disinterestedness/organized-skepticism). Golaszewski's critique is the immediate, peer-reviewed check on that exact move: borrowing C2PA's typed facts without its missing bindings reproduces a system that *looks* like provenance while validators disagree, timestamps lie, revoked signers still validate, and excluded bytes silently change — the precise way a DEV article with ingredient hashes would mislead a reader.

## What this plant already does

*The plant has just made provenance typed — and has, by design, made the Golaszewski failures invisible in that typing.*

- **C2PA-shaped vocabulary now exists but encodes no critique.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/outcome_publication.py:80-91` adds `DISCLOSURE_LEVELS = ("minimal","standard","full")` (Ch.16.3), `MANIFEST_STATES = ("well-formed","valid","trusted")` (14.3/15.3), `PROVENANCE_ALG = "sha256"` (13.1/18.5), `TRUSTED_SIGNERS = ()` (14.4 placeholder), and helpers `provenance_data_hash` (hard binding §18.5), `provenance_ingredient` (§18.15), `provenance_manifest_summary` (Ch.11/10), `provenance_validation_state` (15.2/14.3 mapping `claimSignature.validated + assertion.dataHash.match → valid`, `+ signingCredential.trusted → trusted`), `provenance_disclosure_is_valid`. The header comment cites 7 C2PA sections and quotes `SHOULD NOT provide value judgments`. This is the correct vocabulary — and it is also the surface the critique attacks: a `valid`/`trusted` string that a reader trusts because it is typed.

- **The three gaps Golaszewski predicts are already gaps here, by construction:**
  1. *Timestamp is not bound to claim.* `OutcomePublication` (`:204-218`) carries `kind, role, reference, digest` and `decode_publication_body` (`:288-312`) verifies `kind/role/digest` — no `when`, no timestamp token, no binding of timestamp to `digest`. The module's `provenance_validation_state` maps status codes but has no predicate for "timestamp bound to claim" — Fig. 1's exact flaw reproduced as "timestamp is prose if present at all."
  2. *Revocation is unchecked and untypeable.* `TRUSTED_SIGNERS` is empty with comment "Valid is the ceiling" and `provenance_validation_state` promotes to `trusted` only on `signingCredential.trusted` — but no code distinguishes `revoked` from `unchecked` from `not-revoked`. Golaszewski's Fig. 2 contradiction (Adobe vs Verifieddit on same revoked credential) would be reproduced as two plant readers both computing `trusted` independently with different trust lists and both believing they checked revocation, because the vocabulary has no word for "revocation was not checked."
  3. *Exclusion-range mutability and validator inconsistency are not observable.* Wall outcomes are hard-bound (`sha256` over bytes) but the plant's evidence allowlist (`eligible_evidence_path` `:186-201`, `contained_evidence_path` `:172-183`) is about *which paths* can be referenced, not about *which bytes inside a referenced file* are excluded from the hash. A referenced artifact could carry an exclusion-range-style region (front matter, timestamp line) that changes without digest failure, and no helper distinguishes `weak-file-integrity` (excluded bytes ignored) from `strong-file-integrity` (every byte protected). Likewise two checkers (`checks/bound-evidence-clause-enforcement.py`, `checks/observer-view-agreement.py`, Laya/Codex `post_check.py: R01-R08/P01-P28`) can already disagree while an outcome still carries a single `valid` word — no `validator-consistency` predicate exists to type that disagreement as a finding.

- **The recent reviews kept the right limits, which are also the critique's limits.** `publication-and-attribution--recent` explicitly documented "No X.509 signer, no COSE claimSignature, no RFC 3161 timestamp, no JUMBF, no soft binding, no trust list or OCSP fetch … valid not trusted until a trust anchor exists" — the Golaszewski §14.4/14.5 caveat. `publication-and-attribution--misread` added `Merton 1942`'s `suspended` vs `cleared` and `communal vs private` gap. Both kept "Valid is ceiling" — which is honest but, per the critique, still misleading if a reader infers `Valid → timestamp correct` or `Valid → not revoked` when the spec makes those unchecked. AFIP's addition matters: stripping is the *common* deployment path (social platforms recompress), so "no credential" will be the reader's experience far more often than "invalid credential" — the plant's `unknown` (no checker / no manifest) currently conflates "not yet checked" with "stripped."

*Consequence the source predicts.* Golaszewski p. 2/6: "C2PA may mislead users, platforms, and policymakers if relied upon prematurely. … should not yet be relied upon for high-stakes uses such as financial disclosures, journalism, or legal evidence." The plant's high-stakes use is exactly its wall ledger (financial-ish: 205 standing-audit rows, 134 permanent losses) and its DEV articles (journalism-ish: Mesh historical attribution). Publishing an outcome with `validationState: valid` and ingredient digests — without a typed revocation word, without a timestamp bound to the digest, without a strong-vs-weak flag — gives a reader `Trusted`-shaped confidence with `Valid`-at-best bytes, and two validators will produce the Fig. 2 contradiction on the same bytes without the disagreement being a typed finding.

## What is new here

**The critique reframes the plant's newly-typed provenance from "hard binding + manifest states" to "five goals, of which two are claimed and three are missing — and the missing three must be typed before any `valid` or `trusted` is shown to a reader."**

Five ideas transfer directly, each bounded by what Golaszewski/AFIP concede:

1. **Five security goals as the closed vocabulary, not two (p. 2, Recommendations).** The paper's essential contribution is the goal list itself: *claim integrity, weak file integrity, timestamp agreement, validator consistency, strong file integrity*. Today the plant names only the first two (implicitly via `dataHash.match`). The transfer makes all five a `Final` tuple so that a review that claims `trusted` without naming which goals were checked is visibly incomplete. No signing is added; the vocabulary is the detector. Limit kept: `weak-file-integrity` remains the *claimed* goal; `strong-file-integrity` is the *essential* one — the tuple keeps both so `weak` passing does not masquerade as `strong`.

2. **Timestamp agreement as a bound predicate, not a displayed date (p. 3 Fig. 1 + §15.2).** C2PA's failure is that "nothing in the signed data references the timestamp." The transfer introduces a pure predicate `timestamp is bound to claim` — a manifest satisfies `timestamp-agreement` only when the status set contains a `timestamp.boundToClaim` / `claimSignature covers timestamp` code (or, for this plant, a `provenance_timestamp_binding_holds` check that the timestamp bytes were included in the `sha256` the claim signs). A date string alone is not a binding. Limit kept: authors propose signed timestamped assertions; this plant does not implement RFC 3161 — the predicate is over status codes/bytes already held, not over network time.

3. **Revocation as a three-valued state, not a missing check (p. 3–4 Fig. 2 + §15.6).** The paper's fix — "Require strict certificate revocation checking (including via privacy-preserving methods)" — requires that revocation be typed even when not fetched. The transfer introduces `revoked / not-revoked / unchecked` (unchecked = no revocation code in the status set, the spec-legal but unsafe default). `provenance_revocation_is_checked` maps `signingCredential.revoked`, `signingCredential.notRevoked`, `signingCredential.revocationUnknown/unchecked` codes to that trichotomy, so a manifest that is `valid` but `revocation: unchecked` cannot be promoted to `trusted` — the Fig. 2 forgery (revoked Nikon cert validating in Adobe Inspect) is typed as `revoked` rather than silently valid. Limit kept: privacy tradeoff preserved — CRL vs OCSP not implemented, only the *whether-checked* fact is required.

4. **Strong vs weak file integrity distinguished at the manifest, not at the hash (p. 4 Fig. 3 + §18.5/18.10).** "Protect the entire file (including non-C2PA metadata), not just selected portions." The transfer makes `strong` vs `weak` a manifest-level property: `provenance_strong_integrity_holds` is true only when no exclusion range is present *and* non-C2PA metadata is covered by the hash; any manifest with an exclusion that could hide a GPS/front-matter mutation is at most `weak`. For this plant's wall outcomes and DEV article sidecars, `strong` is the required level — the proposal documents that markdown front matter that is excluded from `provenance_data_hash` is a `weak` binding and must be labeled as such. Limit kept: exclusion exists for legitimate redaction (privacy); the predicate keeps `redactedAssertions` as `weak`-by-choice rather than `strong`-by-default.

5. **Validator consistency and certification theater as a typed disagreement, not a prose caveat (p. 4–6, Findings 3/6 + AFIP stripping table).** "Different tools produce contradictory results" and "certification does not ensure compliance" mean a single `valid`/`trusted` string from one checker is insufficient. The transfer adds a pure symmetric predicate over two validators' status-code sets: they *agree* when they map to the same `manifestState` *and* the same revocation/timestamp/strong-integrity trichotomies; otherwise the manifest is `validator-disagreement`. AFIP's stripping table adds the second disagreement: `stripped` (no manifest found) must be distinguished from `invalid` and from `unchecked` — "no credential" ≠ "not AI" / "not published" — so a wall outcome whose ingredient file was stripped has a typed `stripped` state rather than a missing manifest silently read as invalid. Limit kept: paper notes 2.3 incorporated some fixes (Pixel 10 Pro) while 2.4 addressed none — consistency is a property to measure across validators, not a claim any one validator can make.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** No X.509 signer, no COSE signature, no RFC 3161 timestamp, no JUMBF embedding, no soft-binding watermark, no trust-list or OCSP fetch, no CRL distribution, no change to `Feed.append` / `seed yield` / DEV publish paths, no redaction of assertions, and no claim that content is true — exactly the Golaszewski §17 and AFIP "history vs truth" boundary: `Trusted` still means "binding held under the goals that were actually checked," not "depicted facts are true." The repair path already exists (`EVIDENCE_REMEDY_SUFFIX` in the same file): it is not changed by this vocabulary.
