## What we read

Queiroz, J. & El-Hani, C. N., "Semiotic modelling of biological processes: an introduction to Peircean semiotics" (lecture text, introductory part; the PDF is hosted at <https://semioticon.com/sio/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/09/01-queiroz_hani1.pdf>, read 2026-10-31 by text extraction of the PDF). Read passages: section 3 "Sign as form communication", which quotes Peirce MS 793:1-3 ("a Sign may be defined as a Medium for the communication of a Form") and defines semiosis as "a triadic process of communication of a form from the Object to the Interpretant by the Sign mediation"; the form as "a regularity, a habit", not "the particular shape of an object"; and the concluding remarks, which state that "we do not have an established general notion of biological information". The authors' own definition is their claim, not a result we verified.

## What is new here

The new element in this text is narrow: a form (habit) is what a sign communicates, and the meaning of a sign is the constrained response it produces in an interpreter. Transferred to this plant, that would make a check's meaning the regularity it installs in the next reader's behaviour. The transfer we examined was the `field_review.py next` output, the "ALREADY EMBODIED" list, and whether it communicates a habit (what to do next) or only an occurrence (where a word appears). That is the same defect the critique review already names and routes, so the transfer adds no separate change.

We looked for a second, non-duplicate target and found none that the source would change. A regularity-as-meaning test for checks would need a behavioural measurement of the reader (did the next mind act on the check's output), and the plant has no tracked instrument that records that. Building one would be a new metric proposal, not a reading of this text, and the source gives no method for it.
