# The plant tunes parameters when it should meta-train for fast adaptation — MAML's gradient-through-gradient mechanism onto initiative

## What we read

**Chelsea Finn, Pieter Abbeel, Sergey Levine — "Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning for Fast Adaptation of Deep Networks," *Proceedings of the 34th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2017)*, PMLR 70, arXiv:1703.03400v3 [cs.LG] 18 Jul 2017** — read authoritative text at:

Four passages carry the operational mechanism this plant has not made (distinct from the `self-development-loop--mechanism--2026-10-30` Ashby 1960 ultrastability reading onto `substrate_watch.py`, and from the DGM 2025 empirical-archive and SRLM 2024 self-rewarding recent readings). Page numbers are the arXiv v3 PDF pagination.

1. **Problem set-up — tasks as training examples, K-shot adaptation ( §2.1, p.2–3, formal task tuple).** Each task `T = {L(x1,a1…xH,aH), q(x1), q(xt+1|xt,at), H}` with loss `L`, initial/transition distributions and horizon `H` (H=1 for i.i.d. supervised). Meta-learning samples tasks from `p(T)`; in K-shot the model is trained on K samples + loss from `Ti` and tested on new samples from same `Ti`; test error on sampled `Ti` *is* the meta-training error. This makes "a new wake / a new refusal class / a new coverage gap" a formal `Ti` — not an anecdote — with a train/test split. What the source keeps: the definition is agnostic to whether `L` is supervised loss or RL reward (eq. 2–4) and does not claim any particular `p(T)` covers this plant.

2. **MAML algorithm — one gradient step makes the adaptation, the meta-gradient goes through it ( §2.2, p.3–4, Algorithm 1, eq. 1, Fig. 1).** Inner step: `θ_i' = θ − α∇_θ L_Ti(f_θ)` (one or few steps on K examples). Meta-objective: `min_θ Σ_{Ti∼p(T)} L_Ti(f_{θ_i'}) = min_θ Σ L_Ti(f_{θ − α∇L_Ti(f_θ)})`. Meta-update: `θ ← θ − β∇_θ Σ_{Ti} L_Ti(f_{θ_i'})` (eq. 1). Computationally this is "a gradient through a gradient" requiring a Hessian-vector product (p.4: "additional backward pass through f"); a first-order approximation that drops the second derivative is explicitly offered (§5.2) with measured trade-off. Limit kept: the paper does not claim the inner step must be one; multiple steps are a straightforward extension, and step sizes α,β may be fixed or meta-learned.

3. **Model- and task-agnosticism — no extra parameters, no architecture constraint ( §1–2.2, p.1–3, vs. prior work p.5 §4).** Unlike Bengio/Schmidhuber/Andrychowicz/Ravi & Larochelle (learned update functions), Santoro/Duan (recurrent ingesters), Vinyals/Koch/Snell (metric/Siamese embeddings), MAML "does not expand the number of learned parameters nor place constraints on the model architecture (e.g. by requiring a recurrent model or a Siamese network)" and "can be readily combined with fully connected, convolutional, or recurrent networks" and with differentiable supervised losses *and* non-differentiable RL objectives. The generality is the mechanism's claim; the paper's evaluation keeps it honest by testing the same algorithm on three domains without changing its form: few-shot regression, few-shot classification (Omniglot/miniImageNet), and policy-gradient RL (Algorithms 2–3, p.5).

4. **Sensitivity view and empirical claim — train to be easy to fine-tune, not to solve the last task ( §1, p.1–2, Fig. 1 caption; §3–5 results).** "Our procedure optimizes for models that are easy and fast to fine-tune, allowing the adaptation to happen in the right space" and "from a dynamical systems standpoint … maximizing the sensitivity of the loss functions of new tasks with respect to the parameters: when the sensitivity is high, small local changes … can lead to large improvements in the task loss" (p.2). In effect the initialization θ is trained so its loss surface is steep in the directions spanned by `p(T)`. Results cited: state-of-the-art on two few-shot classification benchmarks with fewer parameters, good regression, and substantially faster RL fine-tuning than direct pretraining. Limit kept: "MAML vs. pretraining" (§5.1) and first-order ablation (§5.2) show the gain is from the meta-objective, not from more data; and §5 concedes the sensitivity-maximization can overfit if `p(T)` is narrow.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** Field is "the plant observing and changing itself" (substrate observer, follow-through, initiative watch), angle is *mechanism* — an operational mechanism, not a philosophy. The cell already has Ashby 1960 onto `substrate_watch.py` (veto/essential/step-function). MAML is the complementary mechanism for the `initiative` side: it turns "the plant observing and changing itself" from indefinite parameter tuning inside one window into a trainable initialization that is maximally sensitive to the *next* window's task distribution, with a stated gradient-through-gradient implementation and a model-agnostic contract. It connects directly to three predecessors — it formalises Argyris governing-variable explicitness as meta-parameters θ, Meadows level-12 parameter leverage as the inner step's α, and Simon near-decomposability as task-family decomposition `p(T)` — while adding what none provides: a computable sensitivity.

## What this plant already does

*The loop observes itself through one window with fixed thresholds, with no meta-objective that trains the observation to be easy to adapt.*

- **One window, not a task distribution.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative.py:34-43` fixes `EVIDENCE_BINDING_CLAUSE_TIME = 2026-10-06T16:22:11+00:00`, `WINDOW_SECONDS = 86400`, `SNAPSHOT_MAX_AGE = 900`, `MAX_PATCHES = 512`; `collect():385` classifies outcomes only inside the rolling 24 h window as `OBSERVED/PARTIAL/UNKNOWN`. The 2026-10-30 DGM addition (`INITIATIVE_ARCHIVE_VERSION / ARCHIVE_MAX_VERSIONS / STAGED_SUBSETS / STAGED_TRIGGER / stepping_stone_score / is_stepping_stone`, :48-151) adds a lineage archive that retains low-score but interesting children (MAML's §1 "sensitivity" analogue at the lineage level), and `initiative_watch.py:52-170` types governing variables (`GOVERNING_VARIABLES`, `is_single_loop_trap`, `MEADOWS_RANK`, `leverage_level`). None defines a `p(T)` over this plant's recurring tasks (e.g. `aged-wake:field-review`, `continuation-review`, `producer-stale`, `outcome-gap`, `refusal-class`) with train/test splits per task, so there is no meta-training error distinct from "did yesterday's wake clear."

- **Thresholds are tuned, not meta-trained for sensitivity.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative_watch.py:37-43` fixes `AGED_SECONDS=3600`, `CONTINUATION_SECONDS=1800`, `PRODUCER_MAX_AGE=180`, `ESCALATE_SECONDS=600`, `REMINDER_SECONDS=1800`; `evaluate():317` emits findings deterministically from those constants. In MAML terms the plant practices joint training on the current task (adjust θ to minimize today's loss) but not meta-training of θ for *next-task* loss after one gradient step. The 2026-11-02 SRLM reading (self-rewarding LM onto `initiative_watch.py`) adds a self-judge scorer but still scores one window, not a meta-gradient across windows. The sensitivity view (p.2: "small local changes can lead to large improvements") has no symbol in `initiative.py`.

- **Model-specific, not model-agnostic, adaptation vocabulary.** `initiative.py` helpers (`stepping_stone_score`, `is_stepping_stone`) are tied to the archive's score×children schema and to Python/benchmark pass rates. MAML's mechanism explicitly decouples the adaptation rule (gradient descent) from the model: "any model trained with gradient descent … fully connected, convolutional, or recurrent … differentiable supervised losses and nondifferentiable RL objectives" (p.1–3). This plant has the analogue: the same `evaluate()`/`collect()` thresholds govern shell, Python, wall, and CI tasks, but no helper states the agnostic contract — that the same inner step α and meta-step β apply regardless of whether the task is a wall-write refusal, a pin-drift, or a tape-overflow.

- **Gradient-through-gradient has no analogue; first-order shortcut untyped.** The plant's existing change path is `edit file → commit → push → pin → check PASS` (linear), not `θ → θ_i' → loss(θ_i') → ∇_θ loss(θ_i')`. The Hessian-vector product cost the paper names (p.4) has no counterpart typed in `initiative.py`, and the first-order approximation the paper ablates (§5.2) — which would be the plant's cheap analogue (use `∇L_Ti(f_θ)` without second derivative) — is likewise unnamed. Without the distinction a future optimizer cannot choose the cheap approximation knowingly.

- **Pretraining vs. MAML comparison has no metric here.** MAML's §5.1 shows direct pretraining (minimize Σ L_Ti(f_θ) on all tasks) underperforms MAML's meta-objective on new tasks despite similar training loss. The plant's closest analogue is "tune `WINDOW_SECONDS`/`AGED_SECONDS` to clear current findings" (joint training) versus "train them so one adjustment clears next week's new kind" (meta-training). No helper measures adaptation gain `L_Ti(f_θ) − L_Ti(f_{θ_i'})` or sensitivity `‖∇_θ L_Ti(f_θ)‖`.

## What is new here

**Train the plant's thresholds to be sensitive, not just correct — replace "tune parameters until this window is OBSERVED" with "meta-train initialization θ so one gradient step on K examples from the next task family gives maximal loss drop," measured as adaptation gain and sensitivity, with an explicit first-order shortcut.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number where it would land, each bounded by what the source concedes.

1. **Task distribution p(T) is the missing input ( §2.1 Fig. 1).** Define `META_TASK_FAMILIES: Final[tuple[str,…]]` in `initiative.py` beside `WINDOW_SECONDS` — the plant's recurring task kinds that `evaluate()` already emits: `("aged-wake", "continuation-review", "producer-stale", "patch-gap", "outcome-gap", "lifecycle-gap")` plus refusal-leading-text classes from `follow_through.py:35`. Each family carries a small K-sample split (K=5 in the paper's few-shot protocol; here K=3–5 findings of that kind from the archive). This preserves DGM's archive (`STAGED_SUBSETS/TRIGGER`) while adding the MAML distribution the archive samples from. Limit kept: the paper does not claim any fixed `p(T)` is correct; `META_TASK_FAMILIES` is documented as the current `p(T)` estimate, changeable without changing the mechanism.

2. **Sensitivity and adaptation gain type "easy to fine-tune" ( §1, p.2 dynamical view).** Add `MAML_SENSITIVITY_VERSION: Final[int]` and two pure deterministic helpers beside `stepping_stone_score/is_stepping_stone` (no Path/clock/net I/O, only caller-held dicts/lists): `adaptation_gain(before: float, after: float) -> float | None` — the loss drop `L(θ)−L(θ_i')` (returns None on incomplete/unknown inputs, the plant's existing idiom); `is_sensitive_initialization(gains: list[float], threshold: float = 0.0) -> bool | None` — True when median gain > threshold, else False, None on bad input. These operationalise the paper's sensitivity maxim ("high sensitivity → small change, large improvement") without importing gradient computation into the observer; the actual gradient step stays in the editor's hands (human mind), the observer only measures whether that edit was an easy adaptation.

3. **Model-agnostic contract and first-order shortcut ( §2.2, p.4; §5.2).** Add `MAML_FIRST_ORDER: Final[bool] = True` and helper `maml_requires_second_order(use_second_order: bool | None) -> bool | None` (None on unknown input) plus docstring citing p.4 Hessian-vector product cost and §5.2 ablation. The plant's cheap analogue — score the adapted state without recomputing the meta-gradient — is thereby named explicitly, so a future trade-off between exact meta-gradient and the running plant's 512 MiB/900 s budgets can be chosen and measured. Today the choice is implicit; after, it is a typed `MAML_FIRST_ORDER` flag in the same module that already types `MAX_TAPE_BYTES` ceilings.

**Limits the review keeps from the source.** MAML's 2017 text offers Algorithm 1 as a recipe and Fig. 1 as existence proof; it does not claim any particular initialization is optimal for this plant, nor that one gradient step suffices for every refusal class (the paper allows multiple steps as straightforward extension). Transfer is therefore a typed vocabulary/detector — task families, gains, sensitivity, first-order flag — not a claim that setting `AGED_SECONDS -= 60` via one step clears any particular `aged-wake` this week, nor a change in what the plant automatically adapts to. The earlier Ashby 1960 `ULTRASTABLE_ESSENTIALS / veto_holders` (proposed 2026-10-30, still not in `substrate_watch.py` at 2026-11-02 HEAD), Meadows 1999 `GOVERNING_LEVERAGE`, Argyris 1976 `GOVERNING_VARIABLES`, and Lawrence 2005 power typing are cited via the existing reviews; their originals are not re-argued.
