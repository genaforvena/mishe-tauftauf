# Calling the rhizome a metaphor closes it: Lawley's critique of the rhizome in organization studies, and a use-bound passage for "mesh"

## What we read

- Scott Lawley, "Deleuze's Rhizome and the Study of Organization: Conceptual Movement and an Open Future", *Tamara: Journal of Critical Postmodern Organization Science*, vol. 3 no. 4 (2004), pp. 36–44 read in full (the running headers carry the page numbers used here). Full text: <https://repozytorium.kozminski.edu.pl/bitstreams/cfe9ccb1-436a-40e6-ab4b-059e6df64ba7/download> (journal record <https://repozytorium.kozminski.edu.pl/pub/6611>). Fetched 2026-10-30; text extracted with `pdftotext` into the scratchpad, not committed.
- Quoted, with page numbers from the running headers:
  - p. 42: the rhizome "is criticised by Deleuze as an example of a root-tree type of ordering mechanism (Deleuze & Parnet, 2002). To describe what something is, or is like in terms of metaphor is to close off pathways towards other potential understandings."
  - p. 42: "presenting the rhizome as a metaphor might invite its reduction to a set of bullet points or 2x2 diagrams or similar mechanisms which foreclose its further conceptual potential."
  - p. 44: "Staying stratified – organized, signified, subjected – is not the worse that can happen: the worst that can happen is if you throw the strata into demented or suicidal collapse" (Deleuze & Guattari 1987, pp. 160–161, quoted by Lawley).
  - p. 44: "it is the systematic use to which things are put that determines good – nothing (e.g. the body-without organs, the virtual or the rhizome) is good in and of itself" (Deleuze 1995, p. 31, quoted by Lawley).
- Lawley's three uses of the rhizome in organization studies: (1) organizational structure and technology, with Cubitt (2001) describing transnational companies as "rhizomes" with "only the shifting, headless rhizome of connections"; (2) organizational activity, with Wood & Ferlie (2003) on communication and Boje (1995) on "Tamara"; (3) ontology, with Chia (1996, 1999) on "becoming-realism". His critique of each is what this review takes up.

## What this plant already does

- The plant's name is a metaphor in the sense Lawley criticises: `docs/mesh.md` says what mishe *is* in a sentence ("a local development culture in which agents observe their work, coordinate through shared records, and check the results of their actions") and then stops. The page does not say how the mesh is to be checked.
- The plant already measures use, which is the test Lawley asks for. Wall outcomes (`accepted`, `blocker-resolved`, `hypothesis-changed`), settled wakes (`changed`, `verified`, `blocked`) and the dashboard's "absent evidence UNKNOWN" rule are deterministic records of what the connections did. The dashboard states its own limit: "changed != fixed" and "Counts remain author reports". So the plant has the use-check Lawley wants, and it already declines to read a connection as an achievement.
- The `field-review` grid is a fixed, tree-like order (12 × 6, `next` takes the first uncovered cell). `research/field-review/README.md` says so openly, which is the arborescent knot the misread review already named.

## What is new here

Lawley's critique transfers to the plant in one specific way the misread review did not cover. The misread review says "mesh" is not a flat network: arborescent knots (wake arbitration, the `seed yield` lock, the genome push gate) are real and should be named. That is a claim about what the structure *is*. Lawley's point is about what the *name* does: a metaphor, once stated, invites a diagram, and the diagram closes the question. The plant's own docs are already at that step.

So the passage for `docs/mesh.md` should say how "mesh" is to be read, not what it contains:

1. "Mesh" is a hypothesis about coordination, to be checked against use (which records, wakes and locks in fact route work), not a description to be accepted.
2. The name must not be reduced to a diagram or a bullet list of properties; that reduction is the arborescent move the name warns against.
3. Connections are not valuable by number. Lawley's warning is that unguided movement can turn destructive. The plant's analogue (an inference, not a verified capability) is many messages, reviews or wakes that land nothing. The check is the outcome record, not the volume of connections.

Limits this review keeps. Lawley's critique is about how organization theorists used the rhizome in 2004, not about agent systems; the transfer is a hypothesis. Lawley's own paper uses "metaphor" and "structure" in the same sentence and is not free of the problem it names. Deleuze's "demented or suicidal collapse" is an argument for structure, not against it, so the passage should not read as an argument against the knots. The misread review's proposed passage (4–6 lines) and this one are two halves of one docs change; docs should land them together, not as two edits. Discover's pending passage on the same file (from `38fb1912`) is also still open.
