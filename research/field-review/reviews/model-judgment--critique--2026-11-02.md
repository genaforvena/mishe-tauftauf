# Verbalized confidences calibrate better than log-probs after RLHF — the critique our judges would replay with raw probabilities

## What we read

**Katherine Tian, Eric Mitchell, Allan Zhou, Archit Sharma, Rafael Rafailov, Huaxiu Yao, Chelsea Finn & Christopher D. Manning — "Just Ask for Calibration: Strategies for Eliciting Calibrated Confidence Scores from Language Models Fine-Tuned with Human Feedback", arXiv:2305.14975v2 [cs.CL] 24 Oct 2023** — read full primary source at:

- <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975> (arXiv abstract, v2 24 Oct 2023)
- <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14975.pdf> (PDF 964_830 bytes fetched 200 2026-11-02 at /tmp/xiong2305_real.pdf, 10 pp., sha256 dd4c8e43690ed8ce1a566d9cc1df01b558749379e4339156113ba93906562633, header `Just Ask for Calibration`)

Tian et al. is the direct critique of relying on conditional log-probabilities for RLHF-LMs that this plant's `model-judgment` judges would reproduce if they treated `P(yes)` from a sampled token as a calibrated score. Four passages carry the critique this plant would replay (quotations via `pdftotext`, page approximate for v2 pagination):

1. **RLHF systematically worsens log-prob calibration — framing and measurement (§1–2, Fig.2 p.2–3).** "Recent studies have shown that unsupervised pretraining produces LMs whose conditional probabilities are remarkably well-calibrated. However, the most widely-used LMs are fine-tuned with RLHF, and some studies have suggested that RLHF-LMs produce conditional probabilities that are very poorly calibrated" (Abstract). "The reinforcement learning objective encourages the model to allocate probability mass to the most preferred answer(s), rather than matching the relative frequency of possible answers" (p.1 col.2). Fig.2 quantifies it for Llama-2-70B pre vs post RLHF on TriviaQA/SciQ/TruthfulQA: ECE rises and AUC falls after RLHF for label probabilities. The paper studies gpt-3.5-turbo (ChatGPT, June 2023), gpt-4, claude-1, claude-2 and Llama-2-70b-chat — the deployed RLHF class this plant's completions adapter calls.

2. **Verbalized confidences beat conditional probabilities — the reversal (§1 Fig.1, §3 Tables 1–3 p.3–5).** "For RLHF-LMs such as ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Claude, we find that verbalized confidences emitted as output tokens are typically better-calibrated than the model's conditional probabilities … often reducing ECE by a relative 50%" (Abstract). Fig.1 (SciQ, gpt-3.5-turbo): raw log-prob reliability is over-confident (top-left); verbalized numerical probabilities (bottom-left) track accuracy across bins; verbalized expressions of likelihood (top-right) also improve. Table 1 (gpt-3.5-turbo): SciQ raw label-prob ECE 0.256 → Verb. 1S top-1 0.234 → top-2 0.132 → top-4 0.065; TriviaQA 0.140→0.068→0.050; ECE-t and BS-t show same direction. The gain is strongest for the hardest set: TruthfulQA (adversarial misconceptions) drops later with top-4 but needs more alternatives. Zero tuning of the verbalizer — "without any fine-tuning to learn verbalization" (p.2).

3. **Considering alternatives before scoring — the psychology transfer that fixes overconfidence (§1 p.2 + Fig.1 bottom-right, §3.3 Tables 2–3).** Inspired by Lord et al. (1985) and Mussweiler et al. (2000) "considering the opposite": the prompt lists k guesses (top-1, top-2, top-4) and asks for confidences on each. Fig.1 bottom-right (SciQ, gpt-3.5) shows top-4 verbalized further improves calibration beyond top-1. Table 2 (all five models, averaged): Verb. top-4 consistently beats top-1 across gpt-3.5/gpt-4/claude-1/claude-2/llama-2-70b-chat; with temperature scaling (Guo 2017, p̃∝ p^β fitted to minimize NLL) combined with top-4, the plant gets "generally better calibration than model probabilities … often reducing ECE by over 50%" (p.2). No model-specific threshold imported — k and β are prompt/fit choices.

4. **ECE alone is insufficient — discriminative power needs AUC and Brier (§2 Metrics p.3, fn.5 p.3).** "To measure ECE (Guo 2017), we bin … average (squared) error between average accuracy and confidence within each bin, weighted by fraction of samples" (p.3). "Although ECE is standard … it completely fails to capture discriminative power. For binary classification, a system that guesses randomly and outputs 50% confidence each time has perfect ECE" (fn.5). Therefore they also report **AUC** (discriminative ordering — higher better, distinguishes correct vs incorrect at threshold sweep) and **BS-t** (Brier score after temperature scaling). Verbalized top-k preserves or improves AUC where label-prob AUC is already high (TriviaQA 0.869→0.896 for gpt-3.5), so calibration gain does not sacrifice discrimination. TruthfulQA exposes the limit: raw AUC 0.418 label-prob → 0.236 entropy-only shows neither metric alone decides.

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** No threshold is imported — ECE/AUC/BS are reported per dataset, not as a plant PASS/FAIL; temperature β is fitted per held-out split, not a constant. Linguistic likelihood words ("highly likely") are evaluated and help but underperform numeric verbalization (Fig.1 vs text). TruthfulQA remains adversarial: verbalized top-1 still 0.389 ECE before top-4 0.203, so "ask" does not cure systematically wrong training (Discussion p.6: hallucination from misconceived data). The authors sample with sampling estimation where logprobs unavailable (widely used RLHF models hide them) — same incompleteness this plant faces. Results are for TriviaQA/SciQ/TruthfulQA short QA, not arbitrary agent traces; no claim about multi-turn tool-use calibration.

**Why this source, for this cell.** `model-judgment--critique` asks for the known failure mode we would reproduce. The plant just landed Nixon's GCE critique (even-bin ECE pathologies) and Farquhar's semantic-entropy comparator — both assume the score under test is already the right *kind* of score. Tian et al. supplies the upstream critique: for the RLHF models the plant actually calls (GPT-3.5/4/Claude lineage that `completions_judge.py` fronts), the kind is wrong: conditional probabilities are the poorly calibrated channel, and verbalized confidences are often the better-calibrated one. Without it the next `τ` or ECE comparison would be judged on the harmful channel.

## What this plant already does

*One score type, one metric family, RLHF treated as irrelevant.*

- **completions_judge.py — Chat Completions judge with categorical yes/no/unknown and no verbalized channel.** Module doc "Explicit Chat Completions judge adapter" (`:1`); `SYSTEM_PROMPT` JSON verdict `{"verdict":"yes"}` etc. (`:31`); `Result.verdict ∈ {yes,no,unknown}`, `Result.probability` ∈ {1.0,0.0,None} (`:66`), `_transport` DNS+body bounded (`:112`), `evaluate()` returns one Result. `model-judgment--recent` (Farquhar) added semantic-entropy helpers elsewhere, but no prompt enumerates k alternatives, no helper parses a verbalized "Confidence: 72%" from token space, and no doc distinguishes verbalized vs logprob calibration. Controls check 12 categorical answers — not ECE/AUC/Brier.

- **jev_judge.py / judges.py — single-sample hosted and local adapters with the same assumption.** `jev_judge.py` "Optional System One judge" (`:2`), `evaluate(document)->(float|None,str)` asks Jev one yes/no question and maps out-of-range to unknown; `MAX_STATE_CHARS=100_000`, `TIMEOUT_SECONDS=60`, `MAX_DAILY_CALL_LIMIT=1000`. `judges.py` `QUESTIONS`, `classify(probability)->yes/no/uncertain`, `run_external` one generation (30 s, `RESULT_RE`/`UNKNOWN_RE`). All three are single-shot, max-prob-flavoured, and would — per Tian Fig.2 — degrade under RLHF if read as logprobs.

- **Measurement vocabulary — ECE named via Nixon/Guo, but ECE-vs-AUC blind.** `model-judgment--critique 2026-10-30` (Nixon) landed `NIXON_CALIBRATION_CRITIQUE_VERSION`/`GCE_AXES_DOC`/`ECE_PATHOLOGIES_DOC` and `expected_calibration_error/adaptive_calibration_error` in `jev_judge.py` so the plant can state *which* ECE it computed. What is still missing is the Tian distinction: that a perfect ECE (50% everywhere) can hide zero discrimination (fn.5), so calibration error without AUC/Brier is incompletely described, and that RLHF shifts which channel (verbal vs conditional) the numbers should even be computed on. Docs (`decision-models.md:41` "benchmark calibration does not transfer") read calibration as vendor trait, not as "which elicitation prompt produced this number."

- **What the misread costs if ignored.** Optimizing any temperature `β` on label-prob confidences for a post-RLHF model will select a `β` that minimizes NLL on the wrong channel while verbalized top-4 at the same operating point is 2–4× better calibrated (SciQ 0.256→0.065). A future `τ=0.72` claim calibrated on logprobs would read as "calibrated" while verbalized under the same prompt remains miscalibrated in the opposite direction, and ECE alone would not reveal that ordering is preserved (needs AUC). The plant would then ship a threshold tuned on the channel Fig.2 proves RLHF degraded.

## What is new here

**For RLHF-LMs, ask the model to say its confidence and to list alternatives before scoring — then report calibration on that verbalized channel with ECE *and* AUC/Brier, not on conditional probabilities alone. The transfer is the prompt family and the three-metric reporting, not a tuned threshold.**

1. **Verbalized vs conditional — the channel switch is the critique (Tian Abstract + §1 p.2, §3 Tables 1–3).** Verbalized confidences are tokens the model emits ("I am 72% confident") — available even when per-token logprobs are hidden (true for ChatGPT/GPT-4/Claude), and empirically often halve ECE vs label-prob/Is-True-prob/entropy on TriviaQA/SciQ/TruthfulQA across all five RLHF models. The mechanism is immediate: the prompt asks for `{"verdict":…, "confidence": 0.0–1.0}` beside the answer, and the evaluator uses the emitted number, not `P(token)`. The plant's `completions_judge.py` SYSTEM_PROMPT already requests a verdict word — extending it to request a co-emitted confidence is one field, and keeping logprob as a second reported channel (not the evaluated one) matches the paper's paired reporting.

2. **Top-k alternatives prompt ("considering the opposite") — the debias that survives without fine-tuning (Tian §1 p.2 citing Lord/Mussweiler, Fig.1 bottom-right, §3.3).** Before giving the confidence, the model enumerates k guesses (k=1,2,4 in paper) and scores each. Table 1: gpt-3.5 SciQ 0.234→0.132→0.065 as k 1→2→4; TriviaQA 0.068→0.050→0.054 (most gain by k=2, cf. ensembles note); combined with temperature scaling (fitted β) gives the strongest ECE-t/BS-t (p.2). Practically: add a `k` parameter to the verbalized prompt — `List 4 plausible answers, then give P(each) that sum meaningfully` — without changing the transport/budget/timeout contract. The cost is `k` times the token budget for that one call, not a new endpoint.

3. **Calibration error ≠ discrimination — report the triple, keep the caveat (Tian §2 Metrics fn.5 + AUC/BS-t).** ECE by Guo (weighted squared |acc−conf| per non-empty bin, paper's definition) alone declares random 50% "perfect." The paper's triple ECE/ECE-t/BS-t and AUC (pairwise ordering, comparable to AUROC in Farquhar) makes the Fig.2 regression visible: RLHF can lower AUC while ECE rises. Brier (mean (conf−correct)²) captures sharpness under temperature scaling. The plant already documents Nixon's five GCE axes; Tian adds the *metric family* axis: every future ECE publication states ECE, ECE-t, Brier-t and AUC together, with the dataset tag (TriviaQA vs TruthfulQA) because no number transfers across them (TruthfulQA ECE 0.451 baseline vs SciQ 0.256).

**Limits the review keeps from Tian.** Only the *elicitation family and metric family* transfer, not any dataset-specific ECE or β value (no gpt-3.5 SciQ 0.065 or β imported as constant); thresholds and bin counts stay caller-stated. Linguistic "likely" scale is noted as helping but not as replacement for numeric verbalized. TruthfulQA's adversarial floor (0.203 even with top-4) is kept as evidence that verbalized does not cure systematic misconception. The paper's June 2023 snapshot of chat models is treated as evidence, not as law about every future RLHF checkpoint. The plant's `unknown` for incomplete evidence stays the handler for missing confidence text, same None idiom as Nixon/Farquhar helpers.
