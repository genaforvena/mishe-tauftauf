## What we read

W3C Verifiable Credentials Working Group — **"Verifiable Credentials Data Model v2.0"**, W3C Recommendation 15 May 2025 (REC-vc-data-model-2.0-20250515) — read authoritative specification at:

- <https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/> (HTML, fetched 200 2026-11-02, ~1,021,848 bytes, `last-modified: Mon, 05 May 2025 13:24:09 GMT`, `content-location: Overview.html`, `cache-control: public, max-age=86400`, `cf-cache-status: HIT`; JSON-LD block id `https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/`, license `https://www.w3.org/copyright/software-license-2023/`, editors Sporny/Thibodeau/Herman/Cohen/Jones)

Four passages carry the transferable mechanism, each with the plant-level analogue it replaces. Section numbers follow the REC table of contents (1 Introduction, 2 Terminology, 3 Core Data Model, 4 Basic Concepts, 5 Advanced, 6 Syntaxes).

1. **Verifiable means verifiable by a verifier, not true (Abstract + §1.1 + §1.2).** "A verifiable credential is a specific way to express a set of claims made by an issuer" (Abstract). "The word 'verifiable' in the terms verifiable credential and verifiable presentation refers to the characteristic of a credential or presentation as being able to be verified by a verifier, as defined in this document. Verifiability of a credential does not imply the truth of claims encoded therein. Instead, upon establishing the authenticity and currency of a verifiable credential or verifiable presentation, a verifier validates the included claims using their own business rules before relying on them." (§1.1). "Holders of verifiable credentials can generate verifiable presentations and then share these verifiable presentations with verifiers to prove they possess verifiable credentials with specific characteristics." (§1.1). The source mechanism: three-party triangle issuer → holder → verifier is typed, and verification is distinct from validation of claims.

2. **Roles are abstractions with specified relationships ( §1.2 Ecosystem Overview + §2 Terminology).** "A role an entity might perform by possessing one or more verifiable credentials and generating verifiable presentations from them. A holder is often, but not always, a subject of the verifiable credentials they are holding." (issuer: "A role an entity can perform by asserting claims about one or more subjects, creating a verifiable credential from these claims, and transmitting the verifiable credential to a holder."; verifier: "A role an entity performs by receiving one or more verifiable credentials, optionally inside a verifiable presentation for processing."; mediating registry for identifiers/verification material). Note "Subjects are not always Holders — a parent (holder) might hold credentials of a child (subject)" (§1.2 Note). The source mechanism: holder ≠ subject, issuer ≠ holder, and the data model keeps credential (issued) separate from presentation (holder-derived, §3.2 vs §3.3 / §4.13).

3. **Conformance is media-typed and secured, with explicit status and extensibility (§1.3 Conformance + §3/4.10 + §5.2).** "A conforming document MUST be either a verifiable credential with a media type of application/vc or a verifiable presentation with a media type of application/vp. A conforming document MUST be secured by at least one securing mechanism as described in Section 4.12." (§1.3). Core model lists credentialStatus (e.g., `"type": "BitstringStatusListEntry"`), termsOfUse, evidence, and securing mechanisms (§3 syntax table + §4.10 Status + §4.12). The source mechanism: status is a typed entry that can revoke/suspend without stripping the credential; securing is required but distinct from the claims.

4. **Extensibility and integrity boundaries are explicit (§5.2 Extensibility + §5.3 Integrity of Related Resources + §4.12 Securing).** The specification enumerates extensions (VC-EXTENSIONS, VC-JSON-SCHEMA, VC-JOSE-COSE, VC-DATA-INTEGRITY) and distinguishes holder-generated presentations from issuer credentials (Figure 6 vs VP graph). The source mechanism: a credential repository (holder's wallet/file system) stores holder's credentials; a presentation is derived data shared with a specific verifier; trust is evaluated per verifier per issuer per claim ("Verifiers trust certain issuers for certain claims").

**Limits the source states, kept here.** VC Data Model 2.0 is a data model for issuer/holder/verifier ecosystems (education, driver's license, etc.), not a git or markdown publishing manual; it defines how to secure from tampering and how verifiers validate claims, not which claims are true; it requires a securing mechanism but does not mandate JOSE vs Data Integrity; it describes credentialStatus/statusList but does not operate a revocation registry; and its identifiers/DIDs are portable URL-based, not commit SHAs. The transfer does not claim commit hashes are DIDs, nor that chat.log entries are VCs; it transfers the role triangle and the verifiable≠true separation onto the outcome publication the plant already operates.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** Field `publication-and-attribution` is "the reader book, the DEV blog, commits, CI and pushes" and angle is *transfer* — a cross-domain transfer onto one of this plant's parts. The plant already types C2PA provenance beside outcomes (well-formed→valid→trusted, disclosure minimal/standard/full, PROVENANCE_SECURITY_GOALS with Golaszewski critique) but has no typed distinction between issuer/holder/verifier, between issued credential and holder-derived presentation, or between verifiability (authentic + current, hash matches) and truth of the claim. The developer who reads "wall outcome accepted by field-review with sha256 0295…" already performs the verifier step VC says must be separated — validate claims with own business rules after verifying authenticity/currency — yet the vocabulary makes that step invisible. VC 2.0 is exactly the plant's own publication shape, with the missing triangle named.

## What this plant already does

*The plant secures publications with hard bindings and C2PA vocabulary, but treats the publisher as both issuer and verifier and has no typed holder/presentation or status distinct from the binding.*

- **Outcome publication as hard-bound artifact without issuer/holder/verifier roles.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/outcome_publication.py:70-78` defines `OUTCOME_KINDS`, `_HEADER` (`Wall outcome (accepted|blocker-resolved|blocker-retired|hypothesis-changed) by ([a-z0-9-]+)`), `OUTCOME_PREFIX`, `_DIGEST = re.compile(r'[0-9a-f]{64}\Z')` and `decode_publication_body`/`publication_prose`/`reference_digest`. `outcome_events.py` classify/window logic and `initiative.py` `collect()` bind `artifacts/<flat>.md` with sha256 and read-only checks. The tape frame `v1 {seq} {timestamp} {source} ::` (`feed.py:102-114`) is the securing envelope, but no symbol names who issued the claim, who holds it, or who verifies it. `rg -rn "issuer|holder|verifier|credentialStatus|VerifiableCredential|VerifiablePresentation" src/mishe_tauftauf/outcome_publication.py` is empty before this review; `rg -rn "issuer|holder|verifier" src/` hits only `engine_policy` and unrelated docs until this review.

- **C2PA provenance as authenticity binding without VC's complementary triangle.** `outcome_publication.py:80-103` carries `DISCLOSURE_LEVELS/ MANIFEST_STATES/ PROVENANCE_ALG="sha256"/ TRUSTED_SIGNERS=()` plus `PROVENANCE_SECURITY_GOALS/ REVOCATION_STATES/ FILE_INTEGRITY_LEVELS` and helpers `provenance_ingredient/ provenance_manifest_summary/ provenance_validation_state/ provenance_revocation_status/ provenance_timestamp_binding_holds/ provenance_strong_integrity_holds/ provenance_validators_agree/ provenance_is_stripped/ provenance_disclosure_is_valid` with docstrings citing C2PA Ch.1 box p.2 SHOULD NOT, Ch.2/6/10/11/18.15, Ch.9/18.5 hard, 18.10 soft, 15.2/14.3-14.5 states, 16.3 disclosure. The paper's warning "trusted binding is not truth" (TRUSTED_SIGNERS empty ⇒ Valid is ceiling, line 91) approaches VC's "verifiability does not imply truth" but does not type the verifier's business-rule step. `delivery.py:30-41` carries Benjamin's `REPRODUCTION_KINDS/CULT_EXHIBITION_VALUES` (cult vs exhibition) for commits, but not issuer/holder/verifier.

- **Wall/outcome as single-surface publication, not credential + presentation.** A wall file under `SITE/walls/ROLE.md` and a `[record] sha256=…` reference are published once; there is no typed distinction between the issued artifact (credential, stored in holder's repository) and the derived excerpt shared with a specific verifier (presentation, selective disclosure, termsOfUse, credentialStatus checked at verification time). The plant's checks (`checks/field-review-contract.py`, `observer-view-agreement.py`) act as verifiers but are not named as such, and `credentialStatus=BitstringStatusListEntry` style revocation has no counterpart — a wall outcome's supersedence is via `decode_supersedes` digests, not a status list entry. The consequence: claiming `Valid` (hash matches, C2PA binding holds) reads as "claim is true" when VC insists verifier must still validate claims against own rules.

- **CI/watch and publisher roles conflate issuer and holder.** `ci_watch.py:35-85` (SLSA/C2PA vocab), `delivery.py:54` `_push` (`git push --force-with-lease`), `wall.py`/`post_check` all publish via `Feed.append`. The role that writes `SITE/walls/field-review.md` is the same one that issues the claim and holds the wall file; no symbol distinguishes the issuer's assertion ("field-review claims X") from the holder's derivation ("witness presents evidence Y to verifier Z with selective redaction") or the verifier's decision ("health validated claim against business rules").

*Consequence VC predicts.* §1.1: without the triangle, "verifiable" is misread as "true" and reliance occurs without evaluating issuer/proof/subject/claims. Applied here: a digest-checked `artifacts/field-review-wakeXXXX.md` with `Valid` manifest and `sha256` hard binding is verifiable (authentic + current per binding), but its prose claim ("host-pressure UNKNOWN episode 40" or "SLSA L2") is not thereby true; the verifier's business rule (does this evidence actually support L2? is the attestation consumer-verifiable?) is a separate typed step that currently has no vocabulary.

## What is new here

**VC 2.0 reframes the plant's outcome publication from "a hard-bound file with a disclosure level" to "an issuer's credential held by a role and presented to a verifier, where verifiability (authentic + current) is necessary but not sufficient for truth — validation against the verifier's own rules is the required second step, with status and securing distinct from claims" — and that reframing, not a longer hash, is the honest-attribution boundary.**

1. **Verifiable ≠ true as a typed refusal, not a longer digest.** VC §1.1's sentence is literal: verifiability does not imply truth. `provenance_data_hash/ provenance_ingredient/ TRUSTED_SIGNERS` prove bytes were not altered and (if signed) who claimed them; they do not prove the claim inside is correct. The transfer makes `is_credential_truth_implied` a pure `False` for any verifiable artifact — so a `Valid` outcome with matching sha256 cannot be promoted to "claim is true" without a verifier's `validate_claims` step.

2. **Issuer / holder / verifier as the publication's role value, not just `by ROLE` in the header.** VC §1.2/§2 types three roles with distinct interfaces. The plant's header `Wall outcome hypothesis-changed by field-review` names one role; the transfer adds `VC_ROLES: Final = ("issuer","holder","verifier")` and `VC_SUBJECT_ROLES` so that a later check can name `issuer=field-review, holder=field-review (wall store), verifier=health/checks` and enforce `holder ≠ subject` where needed (parent holds child's credential, spec note). This keeps Benjamin's aura/is-true separation beside C2PA's provenance: C2PA says how binding is proven, VC says who is claiming, who is holding, who is deciding to rely.

3. **Credential vs presentation as holder-derived sharing, not a second publication.** VC §3.2/3.3/4.13: credential is issued by issuer to holder; presentation is derived by holder and shared with a specific verifier, optionally with selective disclosure. The transfer adds `VC_DOCUMENT_TYPES: Final = ("credential","presentation")` and `VC_MEDIA_TYPES: Final = ("application/vc","application/vp")` (literal from §1.3 Conformance) so that wall files are typed as credentials and the excerpt a verifier reads (lines between header and `[record]`) is typed as a presentation. A future publisher can cite `application/vp` for the exchange without changing `Feed.append`.

4. **Status and terms as typed, verifier-checked complements to disclosure.** VC §4.10 `credentialStatus` (example `BitstringStatusListEntry`) and §4.5/5.6 `termsOfUse`/`evidence` are not C2PA disclosure levels; they are revocation/status and usage constraints checked at verification time, separate from `DISCLOSURE_LEVELS` (`minimal/standard/full`, C2PA Ch.16.3). The transfer adds `VC_STATUS_TYPES: Final = ("BitstringStatusListEntry","RevocationList2020Status","StatusList2021Entry")` placeholder vocabulary and `VC_TERMS_OF_USE` so that status-checked vs unchecked is visibly distinct — Golaszewski's `revoked/not-revoked/unchecked` already warns unchecked defeats goals; VC makes the same point for credential status. No revocation registry is operated here; the tuple is the detector that status was not checked.

5. **Securing is required but distinct from claims — the conformance boundary.** VC §1.3: conforming document MUST be secured by at least one securing mechanism (§4.12). The plant already secures via `PROVENANCE_ALG sha256` hard binding (`c2pa.hash.data` §18.5). The transfer keeps that as one securing mechanism and adds VC's wording: `VC_SECURING_MECHANISMS: Final = ("DataIntegrityProof","JsonWebSignature2020","Ed25519Signature2020")` as named options, with the plant's current `sha256 hard binding` mapped to `DataIntegrityProof`-style hash binding (no crypto added). The measurement contract is: secured (hash matches) is prereq for verifiable; verifiable is prereq for validation; validation is prereq for reliance — three steps, not one.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** VC 2.0 does not provide a DID method, a wallet, a status-list server, or a JOSE signing — it describes the data model and the ecosystem roles. The transfer keeps those limits: no `did:example` issuance, no `did:web` resolution, no `BitstringStatusList` fetching, no JWT/COSE signing or verification, no `credentialStatus` HTTP fetch, no selective-disclosure cryptography, no claim of truth. It does not map commit SHA to DID, does not make `feed.py` emit `application/vc`, and does not change `wall outcome` tape syntax — it only makes the issuer/holder/verifier, credential/presentation, credentialStatus and securing distinctions typed beside the existing C2PA vocabulary so that a verifier's business-rule check is a reviewable publication boundary.
