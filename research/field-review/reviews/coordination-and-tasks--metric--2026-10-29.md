## What we read

Marcelo Cataldo, Patrick Wagstrom, James Herbsleb, Kathleen Carley — **"Identification of Coordination Requirements: Implications for the Design of Collaboration and Awareness Tools"**, *CSCW 2006*, ACM 1-59593-249-6/06/0011 — and Marcelo Cataldo, James Herbsleb, Kathleen Carley — **"Socio-Technical Congruence: A Framework for Assessing the Impact of Technical and Work Dependencies on Software Development Productivity"**, *ESEM 2008*, ACM 978-1-59593-971-5/08/10 — read full PDFs at:

Four passages carry the metric this plant lacks.

1. **Coordination Requirements as a people-by-people matrix derived from work (§3.2, CSCW 2006 p. 354–355; ESEM 2008 §3.2 p. 4–5, Eq. 1 / Fig. 1).** A work item provides a Task Assignment matrix A (person i × task j = person i is assigned task j, here a "developer i modified file j" row from the modification-request / version-control record) and a Task Dependency matrix D (task i × task j = file i depends on file j). Coordination Requirements CR = (A · D) · Aᵀ, dichotomized to {0,1}: cell (i,j) = 1 when person i works on tasks that share dependencies with tasks worked on by j. The paper multiplies assignments through dependencies to get who *should* be aware of whom. This is Malone & Crowston's §2.2 dependency vocabulary made computable from archival data: "who must coordinate with whom to get the work done" (§1 abstract).

2. **Congruence = satisfied / required, an intensive 0–1 fit (§3.4 CSCW 2006 p. 355; §3.2 ESEM 2008 p. 5 Eq. 2).** Given CR and an Actual Coordination matrix CA (person i × person j = 1 when i and j actually interacted through a measured channel — same team, same site, co-commented on the MR report, or exchanged IRC messages mentioning the MR id), congruence is `Congruence(CR,CA) = |{ (i,j): crij>0 & caij>0 }| / |{ (i,j): crij>0 }| = Diff(CR,CA)/|CR|` when |CR|>0, else undefined (the paper excludes MRs with |CR|=0 as not representative of coordination). Four Actual Coordination matrices are built per MR: Structural (same formal team), Geographical (same site), MR-comment (both commented on the focal plus duplicate MR reports, notified via CC), IRC (both mentioned the bug/question in the IRC window for the MR, coded by three blind raters, 97.5% agreement on 10% sample).

3. **The experiment congruence performs — volatile requirements, not static structure (§3.2 Fig. 1; §5 CSCW 2006; §4–5 ESEM 2008).** Weekly CR slices show 30–70% out-group coordination required and the *set* of required partners churns week to week ("quite dynamic and volatile" p. 355) — standard operating procedures and static team/site boundaries cannot pre-contain where coordination must happen. The regressions (N=809 multi-team MRs sampled from 1,983 in three releases, CSCW 2006; N=2,375 multi-team MRs across four releases / 39 months / 114 developers / 8 teams / 3 sites, ESEM 2008) test `log(resolution time)` against four (CSCW) / eight (ESEM: 2 D × 4 CA) congruence scores plus controls (priority, reassignments, dependencies on other MRs, change size, programming/domain/component experience, team load, release). Result: MR and IRC congruence significantly shorten resolution time (CSCW Table 3–4, ESEM §5 Tables 2–3); structural/geographic fit do not. 2008 adds: logical FCT dependencies ("files changed together" per MR, 19-month moving window) predict congruence far better than syntactic call/data graphs (CGRAPH via C-REX, quarterly), call/data dependencies capture a narrow, language-construct view, FCT captures the semantic bundle that actually drives work.

4. **What the metric is not (§6 CSCW 2006; §6.1–6.2 ESEM 2008).** Resolution time is highly left-skewed (log-transformed for OLS); <3 min and >3 day tails excluded; binary mitigated/impacted and "assigned" definitions are proxies (MR assigned to team id vs person id). The evidence is archival production data plus coded IRC, not an RCT — coordination may follow rather than cause faster resolution, and "communication ≠ coordination" (a chat about football is communication, not task coordination). The four CA channels are approximate; email/CC recipients overstate who actually read. No claim that higher congruence always helps — only that, conditional on the chosen D and the volatile CR it implies, the proportion satisfied covaries with speed, and the right D matters more than more communication.

**Limits the source states, kept here.** Both papers call congruence *fit* in contingency-theory terms — match between design and ability to carry out the task — and warn it extends prior temporal-dependency/structural-fit measures, not replaces them. The 19-month FCT window, the team/site dichotomies, and the log(RT) model are scoped to the studied product (a large distributed system) and to multi-team MRs only; the authors invite replication on other dependency sources and work types. No universal threshold for "good" congruence is given; it is a distribution to be compared parametrically, not a verdict word.

## What this plant already does

*The plant detects coordination failure as binary findings on one tape; it has no denominator-normalized fit, so it cannot tell whether 3 findings on 10 required pairs is healthier than 3 on 100.*

- **Entry and ownership path names tasks and producers, but not CR/CA matrices.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/task_state.py:22` `TaskState` carries `retry_task`/`retry_event`/`retry_at`/`parent`/`managed` and `registry()` :140 projects lifecycle from `FeedEntry` bodies; `task_state.py:294` `candidates()` and `eligible()` :255 check liveness and reservation against `pending_tasks()` — a shared-resource allocator. `src/mishe_tauftauf/feed.py:42` `publication_lock` and `wall.py:433` `[dm]` give the *channels* over which coordination could happen, `wall.py:648` `settle` and `records.payload` publish decisions, `task_state.py:637` `signal()` is a typed lifecycle event. None is counted as a denominator.

- **Coordination checks are categorical, not intensive.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/coordination_checks.py:43` `project()` + `:98` `anomalies()` emit findings: `stale-kernel-sync` (site refreshed while `kernel-sync-*` task stayed open), `missing-producer`/`dropped-producer`/`completed-producer` (prerequisite chain), `dependency-cycle`, `conditional-ready`, `double-claim` (two wakes claim same identity), `repeated-prerequisite` (three receipts with same structured prerequisite):98–180. `eligible()` and `retry_at` measure liveness. Each is an existence claim — "there is a cycle" — not "what fraction of required pairs coordinated." A burst that creates 50 prerequisite edges and satisfies 45 looks the same as one that creates 5 and satisfies 2, except for raw count of findings. The intensive question the 2006/2008 paper answers — "of the pairs that *should* coordinate given dependencies, how many did?" — has no reification.

- **Time and team/site structure approximate CA elsewhere, but are not compared to CR.** `task_state.py:281` `active_wakes()` and `pending_tasks()` expose who is active; `wall.py` enforces `WALL_MAX_BYTES/LINES` and draws `board()` :393 from `states()`/`candidates()`; `coordination/site_sync.py:326` `_close_stale_sync_tasks` supersedes stale `kernel-sync` tasks. The dashboard and substrate observer report staleness and pane leases, `checks/coverage-*` report coverage gaps, but no pure function compares `CR = (A·D)·Aᵀ` to `CA = co-comment ∨ co-signal ∨ same-owner-hold`. The closest compound is `repeated-prerequisite` at :158–163, which at least inspects the *producer* edge per MR — a prerequisite dependency's CR of size equal to its chain length — yet it is a latch on three identical receipts, not a ratio.

- **Volatility and dependency-type choice are not measured.** Fig. 1's weekly CR churn has a direct analogue here: each wake's `Feed.entries()` yields a different MR/tick dependency graph as tasks open/close, but `anomalies()` has no windowed stability statistic, and neither `task_state.py` nor `coordination_checks.py` distinguishes syntactic (file-call) from logical (files changed together, or `retry_task`/`depends_on` co-occurrence) dependencies — the 2008 paper's central finding is precisely that the latter predicts productivity better, and this plant has no way to test the same split.

- **Best nearby precedent for the transfer.** `research/field-review/reviews/coordination-and-tasks--transfer--2026-10-29.md` (this field, Malone taxonomy) already adds typed `dependency`/`depends_on` to `TaskState` so the dependency *kind* is named; `research/field-review/reviews/coordination-and-tasks--mechanism--2026-10-29.md` (same source §2.3.2) adds Smith & Davis `announce/bid/award` and Malone's production/coordination/vulnerability triple as typed tape entries and a telemetry helper. What remains is the §3.4/Eq. 2 ratio that turns those named dependencies and those bid/award channels into a single intensive number whose denominator is not "all pairs" but "required pairs."

## What is new here

**Add the intensive congruence metric `|CR ∩ CA| / |CR|` beside the categorical `anomalies()` — the proportion of coordination requirements that were satisfied, with both matrices computed purely from the feed and from the structures the feed already carries.**

1. **CR from archival tape, two dependency views, same as 2006/2008 §3.2–3.3.** For one snapshot `entries: list[FeedEntry]` at wall-settlement or check time: (a) assignment matrix A is `assignee i —[TaskState.owner / helpers / attempt_observation]→ task file j` aggregated per (owner, task identity) over a bounded window (e.g., last K entries or a time window, mirroring the paper's 19-month FCT window and quarterly CGRAPH slices); (b) dependency matrix D has two constructors — logical `depends_on` edges (`retry_task`, `parent`, and the `dependency=prerequisite/transfer` fields landed by the transfer review, plus synthetic FCT: tasks that appear together in the same `lines()` or share an `evidence` path) and syntactic co-occurrence (file call/reference edges when available, analogous to C-REX). CR is the dichotomized product `(A·D)·Aᵀ`, yielding the set `required_pairs ⊆ persons × persons` that *should* coordinate during that snapshot. Retaining both D views reproduces the 2008 test that logical dependencies dominate syntactic ones — the plant can compare `congruence(CR_FCT, CA)` vs `congruence(CR_CGRAPH, CA)`.

2. **CA from the same tape, four channels, read-only (§3.5–3.5.1).** Actual coordination per snapshot is the union of: structural (same owner sharing a `candidates()` eligibility window), geographic/site-like (same site prefix in `kernel-sync-<sha>-<site>` decomposition), MR/report (both roles wrote a `set_step`/`record_attempt`/`signal` entry for the same `identity` within the window — the paper's "both commented on the same MR" including duplicates), and direct message (both touched the same `[dm]` conversation within the window). Each is a pure `set[tuple[owner,owner]]` built from `entries` and `registry()`, with no new I/O or probe — the exact four CA columns of Table 1 in the 2006 paper, transposed onto `FeedEntry`/`TaskState`/`wall message`.

3. **One intensive statistic with a volatility companion (§5, Fig. 1).** `congruence(entries, *, d="logical", ca="report") -> float | None` returns `Diff/|CR|` in [0,1] when `|CR|>0`, else `None` (the paper's exclusion of zero-denominator MRs, preserving UNKNOWN rather than 0 or 1, as with AoI warmup and window-expiry Undecidable). The companion `congruence_stability(entries_history) -> dict` reports week/wake-to-week churn of CR — |CR_t Δ CR_{t-1}|/|CR_t ∪ CR_{t-1}| and the out-group share, exactly Fig. 1's out-group vs change curves — so "high churn" is not an anecdote but a time series the board can render alongside the raw `anomalies()` count. Because the metric is intensive, it is comparable across wakes of different scale, unlike absolute finding counts.

4. **Limits kept from the sources, stated as non-threshold measurement.** The helpers publish no verdict word (`GREEN`/`RED`/`UNKNOWN`) and set no bound: as with the source, congruence is a regression covariate (with controls for size, priority, load, experience) not a threshold. `log(resolution_time)` is not part of the helper; the plant's analogue is time from `task-add` to `task-close` per identity, left to caller analysis. The paper's skew, window choice, proxy limitations, and "communication ≠ coordination" caveat become docstring limits. This matters because `coordination_checks.checks` are findings; congruence is their denominator.

A caller (board, dashboard, or a future `checks/congruence-report.py` read-only reporter) can log the scalar and the denominators, reproducing CSCW Fig. 1 without changing threshold enforcement. 3. **Keep the intensive, not-extensive, contract visible in docstrings.** Each helper documents: interval is the entries slice/window (mirrors the paper's per-MR unit of analysis); logical vs syntactic D is explicit; zero-denominator returns `None` not 0; actual channels are approximate (comment/signal ≠ coordination proof, CC/BCC over-counts); skew and window sensitivity remain caller concerns. This preserves the paper's §6 cautions and prevents the metric from being misread as a verdict.
