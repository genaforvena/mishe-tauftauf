  - Mechanism read — layered operational definitions (Fig.1 p.4): each inner layer's necessary and sufficient conditions are the operational requirements at the top of that layer *including all previous outer layers* (e.g., agency requires autonomy + adaptivity). **Autonomy** as organizational closure (Varela 1979:55): network of processes recursively dependent and constituting a unity (§2.1 p.6). **Adaptivity** (Di Paolo 2005, §2.2 pp.8–9): bounded finite-measure viability set, capacity to monitor trajectory within it and regulate to compensate — distinct from `adaptedness` (observer-relative, not operational, fn.4 p.31). Without adaptivity, autopoiesis gives only all-or-nothing norm (alive/not) with no gradation. **Agency** (Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde 2009, §2.3 p.10): autonomous system that *adaptively regulates its sensorimotor coupling* and thereby contributes to self-maintenance under precariousness; distinguishes internal metabolic regulation from interactive sensorimotor regulation (Fig.2). **Multi-agent system** (§3.1 pp.12–13, after De Jaegher & Di Paolo 2007:493, tightened to adaptive agency): `regulated coupling between at least two adaptive agents, where the regulation is aimed at aspects of the coupling itself so that it constitutes an emergent autonomous organization in the domain of relational dynamics, without destroying the adaptive agency of at least two individuals`. **Cognition** (§4.1 pp.17–19, after Barandiaran & Moreno 2006/2008): hierarchically decoupled nervous-system dynamics that sustain autonomous sensorimotor structures (e.g., habits, p.18) — normativity underdetermined by metabolism. **Socio-cognitive interaction** (§4.3 pp.22–24): `co-regulated sensorimotor coupling between at least two cognitive agents, where the regulation of each is aimed at aspects of the mutual coupling itself such that (1) a new autonomous organization emerges spanning internal + shared relational domains, (2) cognitive agency of at least two is not destroyed, (3) regulation of one is complemented by coordinated regulation of another` — illustrated by infant giving (Fogel 1993, Di Paolo 2009c:59–60) where `opening may only be completed by the other` and latent intentions can `become crystallised through joint activity` (p.24).

  - Worked contrast: Auvray, Lenay & Stewart 2009 perceptual-crossing experiment (§§3.2, 4.2 pp.14–15, 20–21): participants collectively locate each other because mutual interaction is the most stable dynamics, yet individually they cannot discriminate avatar from shadow above chance and do not report social phenomenology — multi-agent success without co-regulation (necessary but not sufficient). Corridor mirroring example (p.14): interaction process self-sustains `despite` intentions to stop — autonomous organization constraining individuals. Slime-mold Dictyostelium example (pp.13–14) used to show stage-dependence of the multi-agent attribution.

  - Limits retained: substrate neutrality is systemic not mystical (§1.2 pp.3–4: `autonomous system` not reified as entity, fn.2 p.31; Buddhist `middle way` noted). The cultural layer is `still in much need of further clarification` (Fig.1 caption p.4). Language, writing, technology deferred to Stewart 2011 (§5). The `problem of other minds` is not claimed solved (§4.3 p.22). No claim that any record coupling is alive; the definitions permit non-metabolic autonomy only where the layered conditions are met.

## What is new here

This 2011 source is the mechanism angle's primary source, read here in full for the first time on this lane, and it supplies an operational mechanism the plant has not embodied:

1. **A layering rule that is a check, not a metaphor.** Fig.1 p.4 is explicit: inner phenomena depend *necessarily* (not just developmentally) on all outer layers, and their sufficiency conditions include those outer operational requirements. The plant currently says `autonomy loop health: fog/landing/gate drift` as a single closure check (`seed_culture_views.py:1218`). The paper's layering says a closure diagnostic alone cannot satisfy the adaptivity or agency checks that sit above it — exactly the gap closed in `enactivism--recent/misread--2026-11-02` for seed culture, but not yet for the sensing side. For multibody, the analogous mistake would be to read `supported` as `co-regulated`.

2. **An implementable distinction between aggregation and co-regulation without drifting any bound.** Multi-agent autonomous organization (criterion: regulation *aimed at coupling itself*) versus socio-cognitive co-regulation (criterion 3: one agent's regulation creates an opening `that can only be completed by the other's coordinated regulation` — giving example p.23). Both are observable without changing a threshold: ask where the regulation is aimed and whether completion depends on complement. The Auvray result (collective success without individual discrimination, p.21) operationalises the difference: stability of the interaction process can drive success with no social phenomenology, so `most clicks are correct because mutual interaction is more likely to persist` does not license a social-recognition claim.

3. **A non-metabolic reading of normativity that still needs a viability condition.** Adaptivity is `capacity to measure type and severity of tendency according to changes in regulative resources required` (p.9), bounded by the viability set — but fn.4 p.31 distinguishes it from evolutionary `adaptedness`. The sensorimotor-coupling test (Fig.2) separates metabolic internal regulation from `regulated sensorimotor cycles` as action. This matches the plant's `no threshold auto-change` discipline: the check does not propose to let a gap probe move its own bound; it proposes to check whether a regulation that *did* happen was metabolic, sensorimotor, and co-regulated.

Limits carried forward: the paper explicitly defers the cultural/language layer, the `child's relations with others` (Merleau-Ponty 1964) individuation story, and a full solution to other-minds (p.22) — none is imported as a claim that multibody overlaps are social. The substrate-neutral extension (Varela 1991 immune/nervous/social systems p.6) is retained only as permission to ask the question of a given boundary, not as an answer.
