# The flat-ontology trap: Brenner, Madden and Wachsmuth on assemblage as ontology, and what a mesh that forgets its contexts reproduces

## What we read

- **Neil Brenner, David J. Madden and David Wachsmuth — "Assemblage urbanism and the challenges of critical urban theory", *City* 15:2, 2011, pp. 225–240**, DOI 10.1080/13604813.2011.568717, read full primary source as 17-page PDF at:

Brenner et al. is the critique-angle text for this cell; its object is the import of Deleuze & Guattari's *agencement* (via Massumi 1987 *A Thousand Plateaus* translation, Phillips 2006 p.108 convention) into urban studies as an ontology. Page refs below are the PDF pagination of the downloaded offprint (225–240).

1. **Three articulations, one critique target (pp. 231–233, Table 1).** "there is no single 'assemblage urbanism', and therefore no coherence to arguing for or against the concept in general" (p.229 Abstract). Level 1: assemblage as empirical research object (Sassen 2006, Graham & Marvin 2001). Level 2: assemblage as methodology that builds upon urban political economy (urban political ecology, Swyngedouw 2006). Level 3: assemblage as "alternative ontology for the city" (Farías 2010 p.13 quoted p.231) that "displaces the investigation of capitalist urban development" and treats the urban as "a huge collection of human and nonhuman actants within a flat ontology devoid of scalar or territorial differentiations" (p.232). The paper's critique addresses only level 3: "the merits of levels 1 and 2 … have already been theoretically and substantively demonstrated … we are much more skeptical regarding … analyses conducted on level 3" (p.233).

2. **Naïve objectivism and the missing context of contexts (pp. 233–235).** Ontological assemblage "deprives itself of a key explanatory tool for understanding the sociospatial 'context of contexts' in which urban spaces and locally embedded social forces are positioned" (p.229 Abstract; elaborated pp.233–234). Without structure, "there is no immanent principle for distinguishing relevant and irrelevant actants" (p.233); Bender (2010 p.305 quoted p.233) warns of "indiscriminate absorption of elements into the actor-network" levelling significance. Sayer (1992 p.45 quoted p.233): a "'naïve objectivism' … presupposes that the 'facts' … speak for themselves rather than requiring mediation … through theoretical assumptions and interpretive schemata" (p.233). McFarlane's Mumbai housing vignette shows building materials mediate inequality, but "it is precisely the 'contexts of deeply unequal resources and precarious lives' (p.216) that are bracketed in his analysis" (p.234); without "historical geographies of land ownership, dispossession, … global divisions of labor … legacies of colonial statecraft … circuits of capital investment/disinvestment" (p.234), the thick description "remains radically incomplete" (p.234).

3. **Decontextualized normativity and passive-voice politics (pp. 235–236).** Critical theory holds "the possible as an immanent, constitutive moment of the real — as contradiction and negation" (p.235); McFarlane instead treats potentiality as exteriority — "any assemblage may, in principle, be decomposed and a new one formed by incorporating new sociomaterialities" (p.235) — an "ontologically presupposed rather than … historically specific or immanent" possibility (p.235). Normative categories (right to the city, commons, cosmopolitanism) are introduced "as abstract possibilities that might be pursued" (p.235) with "no clear basis on which to understand how, when and why particular critical alternatives may be pursued under specific historical–geographical conditions" (p.235). Finally, "a passive-voice politics prevails in which assemblages are anonymously, almost mysteriously destabilized or dismantled" (p.236); "if … 'urban assemblages are structured through various forms of power relation' (McFarlane 2011a p.210) … it is essential to explore who (or what, as the case may be) is doing the structuring to whom" (p.236).

4. **What the critique concedes.** The paper is not anti-assemblage: it welcomes "the innovative, intellectually adventurous impulse" (p.227), praises assemblage's "careful attention to the multiple materialities of socionatural relations" and "how and when nonhuman actants … may generate significant forms of 'reactive power' or agency" (p.233), and states levels 1–2 "have productively amended and continue to transform the research focus … of urban political economy" and "certainly warrant further elaboration" (p.233). Its closing question keeps assemblage useful when "linked systematically to the intellectual tools and political orientations of critical geopolitical economy" (pp.236–238).

**Why this source for this cell.** `deleuze-guattari--critique` asks for a known critique or failure mode the plant would reproduce if it adopted the lens blindly, including what the critique concedes. ## What is new here

**Brenner et al. 2011: keep assemblage as empirical and methodological lens, refuse it as flat ontology; the failure to refuse it is naïve objectivism, loss of context-of-contexts, and passive-voice normativity — and each has a direct plant analogue that the current mesh declaration does not name.**

1. **Three levels as reading discipline for the mesh declaration (pp. 231–233).** Level 1 — describe a specific coupling as an assemblage (a concrete wall write with its tape entry, audit line, and branch push). Level 2 — use assemblage methods to extend political economy: ask how that coupling's materialities (audit log, lock file, CI SHA) build upon and reformulate the gate logic (level 2 "builds upon urban political economy while extending … some of its core elements" p.232). Level 3 — the trap to refuse: treating the whole plant as a flat plenitude where every DM, wall, and wake is coeval and scale/territory are only data, "an alternative mapping of the urban social universe" (p.232). The plant already travels levels 1–2 when a steward asks "which lock holder on which release settled which wake on which SHA"; it drifts to level 3 when the dashboard narrates "many messages, many wakes" without positional context. The review adds the three-level distinction as a reading test, not a new gate.

2. **Naïve objectivism as indiscriminate absorption (p.233).** "*no immanent principle for distinguishing relevant and irrelevant actants … indiscriminate absorption … with the 'effect of levelling the significance of all actors' (Bender 2010 p.305) … a 'naïve objectivism' (Sayer 1992 p.45)*" (p.233) — applied: a wall scrape that counts walls, DMs, and commits as raw tallies and treats the counts as self-evident. The existing Ovezik note warns against pre-processing choices moving a concentration number, and the dashboard warns "Counts remain author reports", but neither names the leveling mechanism. The discipline is: a wall or tape fact speaks only when mediated by a stated interpretive schema (which window, which clustering rule, which resolver's context). Without it, a thick description of "what happened in Mumbai" — or in a given wake — leaves "the contexts of deeply unequal resources and precarious lives" (p.216 quoted p.234) bracketed.

3. **Context of contexts and immanent versus exterior possibility (pp. 233–236).** The "broader (global, national and regional) structural contexts … including formations of capital accumulation … historically entrenched … uneven spatial development … multiscalar frameworks of state power … and the politico-institutional legacies of … contestation" (p.233) — plant analogue: the pin-retention release (`054d4c64` vs `401807ac`), the supervisor's wake arbitration, the `seed yield` lock, and CI/live-consumer verdicts are contexts that position any given wall/DM as enabled or null, however many "materials" (glossy docs, banner DM, PowerPoint policy) co-function. Possibility likewise is not exterior ("any assemblage may … be decomposed and a new one formed by incorporating new sociomaterialities" p.235) but immanent: which reassemblages are actually enabled by the current configuration's contradictions and which are abstractly imaginable but suppressed. The passive-voice drift — "wakes were shed", "the branch was pushed", "assemblages are destabilized" (p.236) — hides the resolver: who is doing the structuring to whom (p.236). For the plant the answer is always a named resolver with a disposition time (health, genome, witness), not anonymous reassemblage.

**Limits the review keeps from Brenner et al.** Only the three-level distinction and its three correlated failure modes move; no urban fact, housing datum, Mumbai ethnography, or planetary-urbanization thesis is imported as a plant property. Brenner et al.'s own political economy (capital accumulation, postcolonial statecraft, Harvey's structured coherence, Lefebvre's complete urbanization) is cited as the structure they think assemblage displaces, not as a claim about agent infrastructure. Their concession that levels 1–2 are productive is preserved — this review does not argue against describing couplings as assemblages or using their materialities methodologically. No score, gate, or CI check is proposed; the critique is declared as a reading discipline for future docs and dashboard edits. Lawley's diagram-closure warning remains distinct and untouched; this critique adds the flattening warning the Lawley review did not cover.
