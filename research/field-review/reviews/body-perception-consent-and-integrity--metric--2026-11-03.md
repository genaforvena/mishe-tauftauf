The source's own claims for this transfer, in its words:

- Abstract: "There have been no evaluations of the actual re-identification probability of k-anonymized data sets." Measurement: "Re-identification probability is measured under two different re-identification scenarios. Information loss is measured by the commonly used discernability metric." Result: "For one of the re-identification scenarios, k-Anonymity consistently over-anonymizes data sets, with this over-anonymization being most pronounced with small sampling fractions. … a hypothesis testing approach provided the best control over re-identification risk and reduces the extent of information loss" (Abstract p.627).
- Two scenarios (§Background): "1 Re-identify a specific individual (known as the prosecutor re-identification scenario). The intruder (e.g., a prosecutor) would know that a particular individual (e.g., a defendant) exists in an anonymized database and wishes to find out which record belongs to that individual." "2 Re-identify an arbitrary individual (known as the journalist re-identification scenario). The intruder does not care which individual is being re-identified, but is only interested in being able to claim that it can be done" (Background pp.628–629, refs [52–54]).
- Prosecutor risk (§Background): "The re-identification risk for the VIP is then `1/f_j`" where `f_j` is the number of records in the anonymized file `F'` sharing the VIP's quasi-identifier values; worst case `1/k` because "the smallest equivalence class in `F'` will have a size of at least k. Hence the re-identification probability will be at most `1/k`" (p.629). Therefore "k-anonymity can ensure that the re-identification risk is approximately equal to the threshold risk, as intended by the data custodian. This, however, is not the case under the journalist re-identification scenario" (p.629).
- Journalist risk (§Background, Fig. 1): population `U`, sample `U` of size `n`, anonymized file `F'`, identification database `Z` of size `|U|`. "The probability of re-identification of a record in an equivalence class `j` is `1/F_j`" where `F_j` is the population equivalence-class size in `Z` (refs [56,57]). "A smart intruder will focus on the records in equivalence classes with the highest probability of re-identification. Equivalence classes with the smallest value for `F_j` have the highest probability" — actual risk `1 / min_j F_j` (p.630). Example Fig. 1: 14-row dataset 2-anonymized to 11 rows (3 suppressed, age 10-year intervals); smallest `F_j` in `Z` has 4 records (IDs 1,4,12,27) so actual `0.25` vs threshold `0.5` — "half of that. This conservatism … has a large negative impact on data quality … losing one fifth of a data set" (p.630).
- k-map (§Background, Fig. 2): "The k-map property states that each record in `F'` is similar to at least k records in `Z'`" (refs [34,41]), where `Z'` is the k-anonymized identification database. In Fig. 2, directly 2-anonymizing `Z` leaves "no records suppressed … and the age is converted into 5 year intervals rather than 10 year intervals. By using the k-map property we have ensured that the actual re-identification risk is what the data custodian intended and we have simultaneously reduced information loss" (p.631). "In practice, the k-map model is not used because it is assumed that the data custodian does not have access to an identification database, but that an intruder does" — so baseline k-anonymity (D1) is used instead, at the cost of over-anonymization (p.631).
- D1–D4 (§Proposed Improvements): D1 baseline: "all equivalence classes in `F'` are of size k or more." D2 individual risk estimation: directly estimate `1/F_j` per class (estimator studied in [57,62–66], mu-argus tool) and require `1/F_j ≤ 1/k`. D3 Poisson hypothesis test: assume sample class sizes `f_j ~ Poisson`, construct null for `F_j ≥ k` and find critical `k'` to require `f_j ≥ k'` at `α=0.05`. D4 truncated-at-zero Poisson: "In practice, we ignore equivalence classes that do not appear in the sample, therefore, the value of `f_j` cannot be equal to zero" — use truncated Poisson to determine `k'` and require same (pp.631–632, Appendix A reference).
- Results at `k=5` threshold `0.2` (Figs. 3–5 described pp.632–633): Fig. 3 actual risk vs sampling fraction (9 fractions 0.1–0.9, 1000 samples × 3 populations of 23k/95k/101k rows): "The baseline approach (D1) … is quite low and exhibits a wide gap between the actual risk and the 0.2 risk threshold at `k=5`. This gap is quite marked for small sampling fractions and disappears for large sampling fractions. At higher sampling fractions there is no difference among the baseline approach and the other three" — all converge to `0.2` as fraction →1. D2 "results in particularly large actual risk values for sampling fractions as high as 0.6" — accurate on average but not against smart intruder focusing on smallest class. D3 "still results in actual risk values above the threshold quite often and by a wide gap for sampling fractions of 0.3 and less" — unsuitable. D4 "does maintain the actual risk closest to and below the threshold risk of 0.2. Compared to D1, its actual risk is higher. But because it is below or very close to the threshold, its behaviour is consistent with what a data custodian would expect." Fig. 4 normalized discernability: D1 largest loss especially at lower fractions, D2–D4 below 100% with D4 closest to baseline at high fractions; Fig. 5 suppression percentage likewise highest for D1.
- Guideline (§Discussion, not fully extracted but referenced in Abstract/Results): follow sampling-fraction-dependent choice between baseline and D4 to keep actual near threshold with minimal loss.

What this transfer does NOT borrow: the identification database `Z` itself, any population estimate, any `D2`/`D3`/`D4` implementation, the mu-argus/Poisson code, or the numeric thresholds from the three specific health datasets (physician/VA/fatal-crash) as plant defaults — only the two risk definitions, the k-map vs k-anonymity distinction, the sampling-fraction dependence, and the discernability/suppression information-loss vocabulary as measurement shapes.

## What is new here

El Emam & Dankar give the measurement the plant's k gate lacks: the same anonymized table has two different re-identification probabilities, and the one the gate enforces (`1/k`) is the wrong one for the journalist claim.

Metric mapping onto the plant:

- **Two risks, one gate.** The plant's `k_anonymity_k` / `is_k_anonymous` measures the prosecutor worst case: for a VIP known to be in `F'`, risk `=1/f_j` where `f_j` is that VIP's class size in the disclosed file; the gate ensures `min f_j ≥ k` so worst-case `≤1/k` (threshold `0.5` at `k=2`, `0.2` at `k=5`). The journalist actual risk the paper measures is `1 / min_j F_j` where `F_j` is the population class size in the identification database `Z` (voter list, registry, or — for the phone — the conceptual population of all dense-queue windows that could have been sampled). Fig. 1's numbers are the canonical demonstration: 2-anonymized `F'` has `min f_j=2` (meets `k=2`) yet `min F_j=4` so actual journalist risk `0.25` — half the threshold — at the cost of 10-year age bins and 21% suppression. The gate's `k=2` light_class histogram therefore does not tell whether a wall outcome quoting tail rows achieves the intended journalist bound.
- **k-map is the population-aware ideal.** If the custodian could k-anonymize `Z` to `Z'`, the condition would be every `F'` record has ≥k matches in `Z'` — Fig. 2 achieves `0.5` actual with 5-year bins and zero suppression. The paper's point is that `Z` is not available (cost $150k–$188k per registry, illicit acquisition, multiple populations) so k-anonymity substitutes sample-only `f_j ≥ k` and over-anonymizes. D2–D4 are statistical approximations to k-map from the sample: D2 direct `1/F_j` estimator (mu-argus heritage) overestimates for smart intruders; D3 Poisson test and D4 truncated-at-zero Poisson test produce a sample-side threshold `k'` such that `f_j ≥ k'` implies `F_j ≥ k` at level `α`. Simulation shows D4 is the only one that stays ≤ threshold across all fractions while staying close to it; D2 exceeds threshold to `0.6`, D3 to `0.3`. All converge to `k` at fraction →1 (when sample ≈ population, `f_j≈F_j`).
- **Sampling fraction drives the gap.** "Over-anonymization being most pronounced with small sampling fractions" (Abstract/Fig. 3). `DENSE_TAIL_BYTES` ~2500 rows is a sample from an unbounded conceptual population (the phone's lifetime trace); its sampling fraction `n/N` is undefined and not reported. The paper's nine fractions × 1000 samples design is the operational shape: a small `n/N` implies `min F_j` can be substantially larger than `min f_j`, so enforcing `f_j≥2` can be far stricter than needed for journalist `1/F_j ≤ 0.5`. Conversely at large fractions the two coincide. No fraction is declared for the dense window.
- **Information loss has two measured faces.** The paper normalizes loss via the discernability metric `C_DM` (Bayardo & Agrawal 2005, cost function of the global-optimization k-anonymizer) — each unsuppressed tuple penalized by its class size, each suppressed tuple by `|D|` — plus raw suppression percentage. Fig. 4–5: D1 has maximal `C_DM` and suppression especially at low fractions; D4 attains the same threshold with strictly lower `C_DM` and fewer suppressed rows. The plant currently has `k_anonymity_completeness =1 - suppressed/|D|` and `k_anonymity_suppressed_count` as rough proxies for suppression, but no `C_DM` (which weights large classes quadratically) and no journalist-risk normalization of that loss.

Limits the source states and kept here: k-map estimation "to the extent that this individual risk estimator is accurate" (D2) and Poisson assumptions (D3/D4) bound the transfer; the paper's numeric fractions and thresholds are dataset-specific (23k/95k/101k health tables) and not imported as plant defaults; no `Z`, `N`, `α`, or `k'` is specified here; Appendix A formulas are not quoted beyond the main-text descriptions. As with the Wasserstein/MQM complement, adding the metric without a population model is vocabulary only — no risk number is computed, no `f_j→F_j` estimator is wired, and no anonymization threshold is changed.

Why this district from the 2026-11-03 mechanism REVISIT (Song et al.): that review gives a noise-calibration mechanism (W∞ / max-influence) for correlated rows once S/Q/Θ are declared; this review gives the sampling-aware linkability metric (`1/f_j` vs `1/F_j` and `C_DM`) for the journalist claim on the same `DENSE_TAIL_BYTES` window. One measures noise to hide a secret under Θ; the other measures how far a sample-side `k` is from the population-side `k`. Both are docstring-only, complementary, and name the same unresolved prerequisite — the plant declares neither Θ nor Z.
