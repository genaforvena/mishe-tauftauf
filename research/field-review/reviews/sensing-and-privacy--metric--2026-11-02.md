Companion citations opened for context but not separately read as primary for this cell: Samarati & Sweeney 1998 k-anonymity [21][24] (already landed in this plant's metric), Li et al. 2007 t-closeness [Li et al., ICDE 2007] (cited §2 p.2), Aggarwal 2005 high-dimensional failure [2] (cited §2 p.2). The review's claims are grounded in Machanavajjhala et al.'s own text.

Six passages carry the metric this plant lacks.

1. **Two attacks on k-anonymity (§1.1 pp.1–2, Figures 1–2).** *Homogeneity attack* (p.2): the 4-anonymous table in Figure 2 (generalised from Figure 1) has q*-block `130** / 3* / *` (records 9–12) where every tuple carries sensitive value `Condition = Cancer`; Alice, knowing Bob is `31, American, 13053`, isolates Bob to that block and learns `Cancer` even though the table is 4-anonymous. *Background knowledge attack* (p.2): Alice knows Umeko is `21, Japanese, 13068` (block 1–4: two `Heart Disease`, two `Viral Infection`) and additionally knows "Japanese have an extremely low incidence of heart disease" (demographic background knowledge §2–§3), so she eliminates `Heart Disease` and concludes `Viral Infection` with near certainty. Both rely only on diversity / prior distribution of the sensitive attribute, not on QI linkage.

2. **Bayes-optimal framing and its limits (§3 pp.3–6, Theorem 3.1 p.5, Equation 1, Definitions 3.1–3.2 p.4).** Prior belief `α(q,s)=P_f[t[S]=s | t[Q]=q]` versus posterior `β(q,s,T*) = n(q*,s)f(s|q*)/ Σ_{s'} n(q*,s') f(s'|q*)/f(s'|q)` (Theorem 3.1, Eq.1). Positive disclosure `β>1-δ` and negative disclosure `β<ε` (Definitions 3.1–3.2). The uninformative principle (Principle 1 p.5) would bound `|β-α|`, but the paper lists four drawbacks (§3.3 pp.5–6): publisher does not know `f`, does not know how much adversary knows, cannot model instance-level knowledge ("Bob does not have diabetes"), and must guard many adversaries simultaneously. This motivates a practical definition that needs no `f`.

3. **The ℓ-diversity principle (Principle 2 p.7).** A q*-block is *ℓ-diverse* if it "contains at least ℓ 'well-represented' values for the sensitive attribute S" (p.7). A table is ℓ-diverse if every q*-block is ℓ-diverse. Derived from two causes of Equation 2 (`∃s ∀s'≠s n(q*,s') f(s'|q)/f(s|q*) ≪ n(q*,s) f(s|q)/f(s|q*)`): lack of diversity (`∀s'≠s n(q*,s') ≪ n(q*,s)` — Equation 3 p.6) and strong background knowledge (`∃s' f(s'|q)/f(s'|q*) ≈ 0` — Equation 4 p.6). With ℓ well-represented values, "Alice needs ℓ-1 damaging pieces of background knowledge to eliminate ℓ-1 possible sensitive values and infer a positive disclosure" (p.7).

4. **Entropy ℓ-diversity (Definition 4.1 p.7, Figure 3 p.7).** Let `p(q*,s)=n(q*,s)/Σ_{s'}n(q*,s')` be the fraction of tuples in the q*-block with sensitive value `s`. Then `entropy(q*) = - Σ_{s} p(q*,s) log p(q*,s) ≥ log ℓ`. Every q*-block has at least ℓ distinct sensitive values as a consequence (p.7). Concave property `entropy(q*) ≥ min(entropy(qa*), entropy(qb*))` when splitting a block implies the whole table's entropy must be ≥ log ℓ (p.7). Figure 3 shows a 3-diverse version of Figure 1 (two blocks of 4, one block of 4) that prevents both attacks from §1.1. Limit kept (p.7): too restrictive when one sensitive value is very common (e.g., 90% "heart problems") — then no ℓ>1 is achievable without extreme generalisation.

5. **Recursive (c,ℓ)-diversity (Definition 4.2 p.8).** Let `r1 ≥ r2 ≥ … ≥ rm` be sensitive-value counts in a q*-block sorted descending. Given constant `c`, the block satisfies recursive (c,ℓ)-diversity iff `r1 < c·(rℓ + r_{ℓ+1} + … + rm)` (p.8). 1-diversity always holds. Intuition (pp.7–8): in a 2-diverse table no value should appear "too frequently" (`r1 < c(r2+…+rm)`); for ℓ>2 we can eliminate one value and still have (c,ℓ-1)-diversity. This is less conservative than entropy: it does not require roughly uniform frequencies, only that the most frequent value not dominate the tail. Variants: pd-recursive (c,ℓ)-diversity (Definition 4.3 p.8) with allowed disclosure set `Y` for frequent non-sensitive values (e.g., "Healthy").

6. **Multiple sensitive attributes and what ℓ-diversity does not do (§4.3–4.4 pp.8–9).** Example block `{(q*,s1,v1),(q*,s1,v2),(q*,s2,v3),(q*,s3,v3)}` is 3-diverse in `S` and in `V` separately yet knowing `S≠s1` reveals `V=v3` (p.8). Multi-attribute ℓ-diversity (Definition 4.5 p.8) requires diversity when every other sensitive attribute is treated as part of the quasi-identifier — cost grows with number of sensitive attributes. Discussion (p.9): ℓ-diversity needs no `f`, guards against the two attack types above, but does not prevent skewness or similarity attacks — later t-closeness addresses distribution distance (EMD), and semantic similarity ("gastric ulcer" vs "gastritis") remains outside the syntactic count.

**Limits the source states, kept here.** No threshold for `ℓ` or `c` is mandated; choice trades utility vs attack model (§5 experiments). Entropy variant needs table entropy ≥ log ℓ and is conservative when `domain(S)` is skewed. Recursive variant is syntactic — equal counts of distinct but semantically close values still pass (similarity attack, §3.2–4.2 lead-in). Multiple sensitive attributes require larger q*-blocks or suppression over sensitive attributes (§4.3). The paper does not claim to model arbitrary instance-level background knowledge (§3.3: "Bob does not have diabetes").

**Why this literature, for this cell.** Field `sensing-and-privacy` is "host radio (host_radio.py), body perception, personal analysis" and angle is *metric* — a concrete, runnable measure of disclosure risk, not a governance stance. The cell's 2026-10-29 landing supplied the linking metric `k = min |QI class|` (Samarati & Sweeney 1998 Def. 2.3) beside `DENSE_WINDOW_ROWS` — necessary but, by §1.1 pp.1–2 here, not sufficient: the paper is written precisely to show that a 4-anonymous table still discloses. The metric for this revisit is therefore the diversity measure the same family uses to make homogeneity and background-knowledge failures typed: `entropy ≥ log ℓ` or `r1 < c·tail` over the sensitive distribution inside each `T[QI]` class.

## What is new here

**A typed, pure, verdict-free sensitive-diversity metric beside the QI histogram — so the disclosure cost of a `T[QI]` class is not just its size `k` but also the entropy and recursive (c,ℓ) of its sensitive distribution, and the two §1.1 attacks become numbers a reviewer can run.**

Five ideas transfer directly, each with a landing line in the target file and each bounded by what Machanavajjhala et al. concede.

1. **Sensitive attribute as a typed fact distinct from QI (Definitions 2.1–2.2 p.3, §2–§4).** Today the attributes an adversary could join are collapsed into `QI_DEFAULT_KEYS`. §1 pp.1–2 partitions attributes into *quasi-identifier* (nonsensitive, linkable: gender, date of birth, zip) and *sensitive* (must be kept secret: medical condition). The transfer makes the split explicit: a module-level `L_DIVERSITY_SENSITIVE_KEY: Final[str | None] = None` placeholder (docstring cites §1–§2 partition and notes that choosing the sensitive attribute is a policy/content decision reviewed under `docs/protocol-boundaries.md`, not inferred) and per-call `sensitive_key` parameter on helpers so whitespace/ordering and missing fields are typed — the same way `qi_histogram` already treats missing QI fields as a distinct `None` bucket.

2. **Per-class sensitive distribution as the primitive (Def. 4.1–4.2 pp.7–8, Figure 3 p.7).** The `T[QI]` histogram counts QI equivalence classes; ℓ-diversity counts sensitive values *inside* each class. The transfer exposes this as a pure helper `qi_sensitive_histogram(rows, qi_keys, sensitive_key) -> dict[tuple, dict[value,int]]` (projects each row onto `QI` for the key and onto `sensitive_key` for the value; missing sensitive field is a distinct `None` bucket so counting is strict; returns empty dict on empty input — the `UNKNOWN`-style denier is `None` in the ℓ wrappers, not an invented verdict). This is the object §3.1's `n(q*,s)` and §4.1's `p(q*,s)=n(q*,s)/Σ n(q*,s')` count over.

3. **Entropy ℓ-diversity as runnable metric (Definition 4.1 p.7).** The paper's first instantiation: `entropy(q*) = -Σ_s p(q*,s) log p(q*,s) ≥ log ℓ` (natural log, same as Figure 3's "actually 2.8-diverse" note p.7). Transfer is two pure helpers: `entropy_of_q_star(counts: dict[value,int]) -> float` (computes Shannon entropy over the sensitive fractions for one q*-block) and `is_entropy_l_diverse(rows, qi_keys, sensitive_key, ell: int) -> bool | None` (returns `True` iff every q*-block's entropy ≥ log ℓ; `None` on invalid `ell<1`, empty input, or missing `sensitive_key` when rows nonempty — typed deny analogous to `k_anonymity_k`'s `None` on empty). Header notes whole-table entropy ≥ log ℓ is then necessary (concavity note p.7), and that a skewed `domain(S)` (90% one value) makes entropy ℓ-diversity infeasible at ℓ>1 — the §4.2 limit kept.

4. **Recursive (c,ℓ)-diversity as the practical syntactic check (Definition 4.2 p.8).** Less conservative than entropy: sort counts descending `r1≥r2≥…≥rm`, then `r1 < c·(rℓ+…+rm)` for each q*-block (p.8). Transfer is pure `is_recursive_c_l_diverse(rows, qi_keys, sensitive_key, ell: int, c: float) -> bool | None` (returns `True` iff every block satisfies the inequality; `None` on invalid `ell<1`, `c` not finite, empty input etc.; 1-diversity always `True` by definition p.8). Docstring reproduces the definition verbatim and notes the constant `c` controls how much more frequent the top value may be than the tail — the tuning knob the paper's §5 experiments vary. This mirrors `k_anonymity_is_k_anonymous` but over the sensitive distribution per QI class, not over class size alone.

5. **What ℓ does not guarantee, typed as limits (§3.3–§4.4 pp.5–9, §4.3 multi-attribute p.8).** The paper states ℓ-diversity "does not provide a complete solution" for skewness/similarity — many distinct but semantically close values (e.g., three cancer sub-types) still satisfy ℓ yet add little real privacy (similarity attack, §3.2 lead-in and §4.4 discussion) — and multi-attribute ℓ-diversity (Definition 4.5 p.8) needs `Q ∪ {all other Si}` as quasi-identifier, so larger q*-blocks or suppression over sensitive attributes (§4.3). Transfer carries these as module docstring limits: the two helpers are syntactic diversity counts, not semantic/t-closeness distances (Li et al. EMD, §2 p.2 citation), and a column that looks diverse under `COUNT DISTINCT` may still be semantically narrow.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** No generalisation hierarchy `DGH`/`VGH` is implemented, no tuples are actually generalised or suppressed, no `f` or `α/β` distribution is estimated (the point of §3.3—§4 is to avoid needing `f`), no instance-level background knowledge ("Bob does not have diabetes" p.6) is modelled, no multi-attribute correlation check is added in this commit beyond documenting Definition 4.5 — each sensitive attribute is checked separately and the docstring states that a block ℓ-diverse in `S` and in `V` separately may still fail when `S` is known (§4.3 example, p.8).
