## What we read

- Minghao Wu and Alham Fikri Aji, "Style Over Substance: Evaluation Biases for Large Language Models", arXiv:2307.03025v3 [cs.CL] 12 Nov 2023, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03025.pdf -> /tmp/style-over-substance.pdf PDF 972455 B sha256 b748c8f345ef06e63c147710c4bbd6ca71dad1cf9930d2efd705ce5314c21c (15 pp., LaTeX pdfTeX-1.40.25, pdftotext -layout 87555 B, code/data https://github.com/lm-sys/vicuna-blog-eval). Retained at /tmp/style-over-substance.pdf and /tmp/style-over-substance.txt.
- Verbatim as printed (abstract p.1): "Our findings reveal a concerning bias in the evaluation process, as answers with factual errors are rated more favorably than answers that are too short or contained grammatical errors." "we propose independently evaluating machine-generated text across multiple dimensions, rather than merging all the evaluation aspects into a single score. We instantiate this idea with the Elo rating system, resulting in the Multi-Elo Rating System (MERS)."
- Dataset and method p.1–3: 40 general-purpose questions from Chiang et al. 2023 (Vicuna eval, "generic"/"knowledge"/"common sense"/"counterfactual" only; "fermi"/"coding"/"math"/"roleplay"/"writing" excluded). 12 models/settings: Correct, One Minor Factual Error, Several Minor Factual Errors, Several Major Factual Errors, Advanced Learner (spelling), Intermediate Learner (grammatical) × long ~100 words / short ~50 words. GPT-4 generates with specific instructions then manual review/post-edit (Table 2 examples). Elo (Elo 1967) K=32, C=400, SA=1/0/0.5, 10 000 random orderings per Dettmers et al. 2023. 5280 unique pairwise comparisons across all 12 models × 40 questions, each evaluated forward and reversed. Crowd: Amazon Mechanical Turk, US only, >5000 assignments 99% approval, max 20 each, 377 annotators avg 14. Expert: 20 annotators master's+ English-speaking, 200 games sample (each model ≥28 games). LLM judges: GPT-4 gpt-4-0613 and Claude-1 Claude-1.3 with Dettmers prompt assessing helpfulness/relevance/accuracy/level-of-detail (Fig.1, App.C).
- Numbers as printed Table 1 (Elo, initial 1000): Correct 1091 crowd / 1162 expert / 1482 GPT-4 / 1320 Claude-1; Correct+Short 970/1029/1096/1052; One Minor Factual Error 1074/1137/1415/1265; Several Minor Factual Errors 1032/1024/1206/1182; Several Major Factual Errors 1025/892/861/979; Advanced Learner 1041/1138/1213/1126; Intermediate Learner 1015/1108/771/904 (short variants 937–1002 crowd, 832–986 expert, 582–988 GPT-4). Text p.3–4: "GPT-4 considers 'Several Minor Factual Errors' (1206 Elo) to be better than 'Correct + Short' (1096 Elo)" and justifications sometimes rank major errors above Correct+Short even when errors detected. Biases: all judges bias to longer texts (Fig.2, GPT-4 most, expert least — level-of-detail criterion correlates with length); order bias: crowd/GPT-4/Claude-1 prefer Assistant 1, experts prefer Assistant 2 and more ties (Fig.3). Agreement Table 3 Cohen κ: Crowd-Expert 0.08, Crowd-GPT-4 0.11, Crowd-Claude 0.10, Expert-GPT-4 0.09, Expert-Claude 0.14, GPT-4-Claude 0.51 — only LLM-LLM moderate, all others slight per McHugh 2012. Authors reconcile with Zheng et al. 2023 ~80% by noting Zheng removes ties/inconsistencies; retaining them gives ~60% (p.4). Crowd lacks fact-checking (factual-error Elos near Correct); experts better but imperfect; LLMs detect some errors inconsistently.
- Remedy MERS §4 p.4–5: borrowed from MT MQM (Lommel et al. 2014 fluency/adequacy). Three independent dimensions: Accuracy ("factual correctness and logical consistency"), Helpfulness ("relevance … whether it addresses the question"), Language ("clarity, coherence, grammar, syntax, tone"). Procedure: human interface modified to rate three aspects; Elo recomputed per dimension. Table 4 Accuracy/Helpfulness/Language: e.g., Correct Accuracy 1056/1180/1200 vs Correct+Short 963/1040/1158; Several Major Factual Errors Accuracy 1030/963/794 vs Language 1023/1010/995; Intermediate Learner Language 991/992/560 vs Accuracy 1015/1076/1018. Finding p.5: MERS "significantly enhances the quality of LLM-based evaluations, particularly in terms of factual accuracy. However, there is no significant improvement in crowd-sourced-based evaluations."
- Limits conceded p.4, fn.4, §5: single unified score "undermines importance of individual components" and crowdsourcing limits; crowd fact-checking omission is safety risk; MERS crowd gap remains; further investigation needed.

## What is new here

- The full v3 PDF quantifies the critique the predecessor could only name from the abstract. The failure is not hypothetical fluency preference but measured ranking inversion: a model with several minor factual errors outranks a correct short answer (GPT-4 1206 vs 1096), and crowd Elo for factual errors (1074/1032/1025) clusters around Correct (1091). That is the exact false-`desired-state-met` mode this plant fears — a fluent assertion of success without grounded SYSTEM ZERO passes when a terse correct negative is scored lower.
- The paper shows the failure is shared across judge families but differently: LLMs agree moderately (κ 0.51) yet still mis-rank; humans are indecisive (narrow Elo band, crowd 926–1086, expert 832–1162) and crowdsource judges lack fact-checking ability, while experts fact-check imperfectly. Crowd-Expert κ 0.08 and LLM-crowd κ ~0.10–0.11 mean crowd labels cannot stand in for expert or LLM judgments without dimensional separation.
- The critique's structural cause is the single-score conflation. Judges asked for one merged helpfulness/relevance/accuracy/detail score collapse factuality into style/length. Table 4 shows the remedy: when the same comparisons are scored separately on Accuracy vs Helpfulness vs Language, GPT-4 separates factual errors (Several Major: Accuracy 794 vs Language 995) whereas crowd does not (Accuracy 1030 vs Language 1023), and MERS restores factual-accuracy discrimination for LLMs but not for crowd. The transfer is dimensional, not numeric: a single `desired-state-met` categorical verdict is the analogue of a single Elo; asking whether the desired state is met conflates whether the evidence is accurate (does the state hold) with whether the prose is fluent/helpful.
- This does not contradict the plant's pointwise choice (Tripathi 2504.14716v2 revisit showed pointwise 9% vs pairwise 35% distracted flips). It sharpens its limit: pointwise categorical still conflates accuracy with language if asked as one question. The paper's recommendation — evaluate Accuracy, Helpfulness, Language independently — is the missing check on the critique angle. Adopting an LLM judge blindly as a single desired-state gate would reproduce the 1206-over-1096 inversion even at low length bias if the verdict wording rewards confident prose.
- Predecessor distinctness: 2026-10-31 critique = abstract-only (no PDF, no tables, no method, no numbers, no MERS; proposed one-sentence substance-vs-style disclosure). This = full v3 15 pp. 972455 B sha b748c8f345... pp.1-5 Table 1/3/4 verbatim Elo/kappa/MERS numbers, 40Q×12models×5280 pairs both directions, 377 crowd + 20 expert + gpt-4-0613/Claude-1.3, Dettmers prompt, Elo K=32/10k shuffles, Fig.2/3 length/order biases, and the multi-dimensional remedy.
