# Semantic entropy over meanings, not tokens — a cheap comparator for free-form judge uncertainty

## What we read

**Sebastian Farquhar, Jannik Kossen, Lorenz Kuhn & Yarin Gal — "Detecting hallucinations in large language models using semantic entropy", Nature 630, 625–630 (2024), doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07421-0** — read the full primary source at:

- <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0> (Nature article, open access, fetched 200 2026-11-02; PDF 49.8 KB markit-rendered, 8 pp., Fig.1 semantic vs naive entropy, Fig.2 QA AUROC/AURAC, Fig.3 biography, Methods eqs.1–3)
- <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0.pdf> (PDF, same bytes; pp.1–8 Methods pp.7–8 eq.1 predictive entropy, eq.2 cluster mass, eq.3 semantic entropy)
- Precursor for operational detail: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09664> (Kuhn, Gal & Farquhar 2023 "Semantic Uncertainty: Linguistic Invariances…", arXiv:2302.09664v2, fetched 200; same estimator in §3)

The paper isolates **confabulations** — arbitrary incorrect generations sensitive to random seed (e.g. "KRAS G12C" vs "KRAS G12D" for Sotorasib, p.1) — from consistently wrong or instructed lies, and shows only the first kind is detectable by uncertainty.

Four passages carry what this plant has not made (quotations via markit PDF, page approximate):

1. **The problem — naive entropy is misleadingly high (§1, Fig.1a p.2).** "Naive entropy-based uncertainty measures variation in the exact answers, treating 'Paris', 'It's Paris' and 'France's capital Paris' as different. … Our semantic entropy clusters answers which share meanings before computing the entropy. A low semantic entropy shows that the LLM is confident about the meaning." The point is lexical variation ≠ meaning variation; entropy over tokens conflates the two.

2. **The mechanism — sample, cluster by bidirectional entailment, then entropy over clusters (Methods pp.7–8).** Step 1: sample M sequences {s^(1)…s^(M)} from P(s|x) at temperature 1 with nucleus p=0.9, top-K 50 (Methods "Generating a set of answers"), recording length-normalized log P(s|x)= (1/N) Σ log P(s_i|s_<i,x) to remove length bias. Step 2: cluster by semantic equivalence E(·,·) operationalized as **bidirectional entailment**: s entails s′ and s′ entails s (given context x; "Paris" entails "The capital of France is Paris" only in context of "What is the capital of France?"). Implemented with DeBERTa-Large-MNLI or GPT-3.5 as NLI judge; transitive clustering via Extended Data Fig.1. Step 3: cluster mass P(c|x)= Σ_{s∈c} P(s|x)= Σ_{s∈c} Π_i P(s_i|s_<i,x) (eq.2), then **semantic entropy SE(x)= −Σ_c P(c|x) log P(c|x)** (eq.3), an entropy over a meaning sub-σ-algebra, not over tokens. Discrete variant when P(s|x) unavailable: empirical distribution over clusters from generations alone (used for all GPT-4 results; Methods "discrete variant").

3. **How they know — AUROC/AURAC on sentence and paragraph generations (§3, Figs.2–3 p.3–5).** Five QA sets (TriviaQA, SQuAD, BioASQ, NQ-Open, SVAMP, 96±70 char answers) plus paragraph biographies (FactualBio, 442±122 char, factoid-decomposed) on LLaMA 2 Chat 7B/13B/70B, Falcon 7B/40B, Mistral 7B Instruct and GPT-4. **Fig.2**: averaged over 30 task×model combos, semantic entropy AUROC 0.790 vs naive entropy 0.691, P(True) 0.698, embedding regression 0.687; stable 0.78–0.81 across model families/scales. In pale-yellow bars embedding regression collapses out-of-distribution (training mismatched to deployment, ref.42) while semantic entropy needs no labelled confabulations (unsupervised). Fig.3 (GPT-4 biographies, discrete SE): AUROC/AURAC above self-check and P(True) variant until 20% rejection. M=10 generations chosen by Supplementary Fig.2; M=3 for biographies after factoid questioning.

4. **Where it fails — the paper's own counterexample (Table 1 p.4 row 4 + Discussion p.5).** BSkyB launch: generations "1 October 1998" vs "1998" are lexically distinct but reference answer "launched in 1998" treats day-level and year-level as equivalent; semantic clustering separated them and SE was erroneously high. The Discussion states the method "does not help when LLM outputs are systematically bad" (trained misconceptions, reward hacking) — it detects lack-of-knowledge arbitrariness, not confident wrongness. For long paragraphs naive regeneration targets different factoids (family vs profession); the fix is factoid decomposition → per-question SE → averaging (Fig.1b), which the paper does but notes granularity matters (refs.31,43).

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** No threshold is imported — AUROC/AURAC are detector comparisons, not a deployed reject threshold (Supplementary reports thresholded accuracies). Length normalization is asserted ("expected uncertainty independent of sentence length") with "little theoretical justification" (Methods). M, sampling scheme (T=1 vs T=0.1 best-generation), and NLI judge are evaluated but not sensitive per authors; still reported as choices, not laws. The result is for sentence/paragraph QA and math (SVAMP) with 10 samples; it is not a claim about arbitrary agent traces, multi-turn tool use, or calibrated probabilities (the Wang et al. 2410.06707 lane covers calibrated p).

**Why this source, for this cell.** The plant's model-judgment field (src/mishe_tauftauf/judges.py, jev_judge.py, completions_judge.py, docs/decision-models.md) currently treats a judge's single yes/no/unknown sample as the whole signal, with no sampling and no meaning-level uncertainty. The recent result the field needs is a 2023–2026 **unsupervised, black-box-compatible** way to turn that single sample into a cheap comparator — exactly what Farquhar et al. publish as a concrete three-step estimator with a testable trap (naive entropy) to avoid and a discrete fallback when logprobs are hidden.

## What this plant already does

*One sample, one verdict — no entropy, no clustering, no length normalization.*

- **judges.py — single-sample categorical adapter.** `QUESTIONS` are yes/no probes, `classify(question, probability)->str` bins probability into `yes/no/uncertain` by threshold, `run_external(path, …)->Judgment` writes one `document(…)` (slug, top_pain, evidence, prediction→text), spawns one external process (30 s timeout, MAX_REQUEST_BYTES), parses one `probability <float>` or `unknown <reason>` line (`RESULT_RE`/`UNKNOWN_RE`), and returns one `Judgment(question, probability|None, label, reason, request)`. `run_external_batch` reuses one transient state across questions but still one generation per question. `conservative_unknown` returns `probability=None` when no adapter is selected. `controls()` checks 12 categorical smoke answers. No sampling of M>1, no log P(s|x), no clustering, no ECE/SE reporting.

- **jev_judge.py — hosted System One with same single-shot contract.** Header "This is a reference adapter, not a calibration claim" (`jev_judge.py:31`); `evaluate(document)->(float|None,str)` asks Jev one yes/no question, validates size/budget/key, posts with `DEFAULT_BASE_URL`/`DEFAULT_MODEL`, parses hosted JSON, and maps out-of-range/malformed to `unknown`. `TIMEOUT_SECONDS=60`, `MAX_DAILY_CALL_LIMIT=1000`, `reserve_daily_call` — all budget/transport, not uncertainty. No M-sample loop, no SE, no NLI.

- **completions_judge.py — same pattern for the Chat Completions surface.** Categorical `Result.probability` 1/0/None, `SYSTEM_PROMPT` JSON verdict, bounded `_transport_worker` deadline; the 2026-10-30 review added invert-softmax calibration helpers there, but no semantic entropy vocabulary exists anywhere in the tree. Docs say "Exit 0 means all twelve categorical answers matched; this is not a calibration or production readiness claim" (`completions-judge.md:30`; `decision-models.md` "calibration" appears only as "benchmark calibration does not transfer").

- **What the sweep found.** `field_review.py next` for this cell reports `model`/`judge`/`calibration` hits only in check plumbing, `docs/decision-models.md`, and the prior Wang lane — occurrence, not a mechanism. No helper computes predictive entropy, length-normalized logprob, meaning-clustered entropy, or discrete SE; no file samples M generations to detect confabulation.

## What is new here

**Sample M generations for the same question, cluster by meaning rather than token identity, and report entropy over meanings — discrete SE when logprobs are hidden, full SE when they are not. Use it as a cheap comparator, not a new verdict word.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each bounded by what the paper concedes:

1. **Meaning-level entropy as the comparator the plant lacks (Methods eqs.2–3).** For any yes/no question the plant already asks (judges.py `QUESTIONS`), draw M samples at T≈1 for the same document, compute **P(c|x)** and **SE(x) = −Σ_c P(c|x) log P(c|x)**. When the LLM's logprobs are available use length-normalized log P(s|x) and exact cluster-mass sum (eq.2); when absent (the GPT-4 case and this plant's hosted adapters) use the **discrete variant**: empirical cluster frequencies `p̂(c)=|c|/M` and `SE_discrete = −Σ_c p̂(c) log p̂(c)` (paper's §5, all GPT-4 results). High SE means arbitrariness across seeds — the confabulation detector — even when naive token entropy is misleadingly high (Fig.1a). This is unsupervised: no labelled confabulations, no task-specific data, no new `checks/*.json` gate.

2. **Bidirectional entailment clustering with a deterministic stand-in (Methods "Clustering").** The paper uses DeBERTa-Large-MNLI or GPT-3.5 as NLI judge with transitive clustering (Extended Data Fig.1). The plant today has no NLI dependency and should not add one in this step. The transfer is: add a **pure clustering helper** that takes a list of generated verdict texts and groups those that bidirectionally entail under exact normalized-meaning equivalence (lowercased, trimmed, "it's paris"≈"paris is the capital" is intentionally NOT merged by the stand-in — the gap is documented, and the helper accepts an optional `entails(a,b)->bool` callable so a future NLI can be injected without changing callers). Context-sensitivity ("Paris" alone vs in question context) is preserved by clustering on `(question, answer)` pairs, as the paper requires (§8). Table 1 row 4 is kept as the failure exhibit in the docstring.

3. **Cheap-comparator evaluation — AUROC/AURAC over SE, not a new threshold (Figs.2–3 + Discussion).** Report SE / discrete SE alongside the existing single-sample `probability` as a diagnostic: compute AUROC for "is answer incorrect" and AURAC for rejection-accuracy improvement on a small held-out set of (document, expected ∈ {yes,no,unknown}) pairs, exactly as the paper does (30 task×model combos averaged; individual metrics in Supplement). Keep **calibration vs decision distinct**: a high SE does not change `classify()`'s `yes/no/uncertain` word; it only signals "this question is confabulation-prone — extra care / retrieval / human review warranted" (Discussion "needs no previous domain knowledge"). The existing `unknown` stays the detector for incomplete evidence; SE adds the second detector the paper distinguishes: incomplete information about *which meaning* the model will emit.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the paper.** Only the **estimator + clustering contract + metric family** transfer; no AUROC number (0.79), no M=10 prescription, no temperature/nucleus/top-K triple is imported as a plant threshold — M and sampling are caller-provided, defaults documented as paper's choices with the paper's own sensitivity caveat. Long-paragraph FactualBio averaging is kept as method reference, not as a plant benchmark — the plant's traces are per-question sentences, so single-proposition SE is the first use. Systematic wrongness (training misconceptions, reward hacking) is explicitly out of scope per Discussion, and the `probability None → unknown` path stays the handler for that second mechanism.
