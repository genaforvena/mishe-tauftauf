## What we read

Di Paolo, E. A. (2005), "Autopoiesis, adaptivity, teleology, agency", Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences 4(4), pp. 429–452, <https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11097-005-9002-y>. Read 2026-10-31 through its abstract only, as published on the openevo literature base <https://openevo.eva.mpg.de/literaturebase/di-paolo-2005-autopoiesis-adaptivity-teleology-agency/3>. The abstract says autopoiesis "provides a systemic language for speaking about intrinsic teleology" but "needs to be elaborated further in order to explain sense-making", and defines adaptivity as "a many-layered property that allows organisms to regulate themselves with respect to their conditions of viability". The full text is not reachable from here: the Springer page redirects to a login, and the Sussex repository lists the file as not publicly available.

## What is new here

The abstract's distinction is that organisational closure (autopoiesis) names a systemic vocabulary for teleology, while sense-making needs adaptivity: regulation relative to viability conditions. Applied to the plant, that would mean the autonomy-loop check should test whether a mind's regulation is tied to a viability condition, not only whether its loop closes. That is a sharper reading, but I cannot check it here: the abstract does not say how adaptivity is operationalised, and any concrete check would have to guess at the full argument.
