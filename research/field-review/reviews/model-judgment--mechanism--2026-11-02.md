# Dirichlet calibration — a natively multiclass linear map on log-probabilities that contains temperature scaling and fixes classwise calibration

## What we read

**Meelis Kull, Miquel Perello-Nieto, Markus Kängsepp, Telmo Silva Filho, Hao Song, Peter Flach — "Beyond temperature scaling: Obtaining well-calibrated multiclass probabilities with Dirichlet calibration", arXiv:1910.12656v1 [cs.LG] 28 Oct 2019 (NeurIPS 2019, Vancouver, 33rd Conference)** — read the full primary source at:

- <https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656> (arXiv abstract, v1 28 Oct 2019)
- <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656> (PDF 2_223_574 bytes fetched 200 2026-11-02 at /tmp/dirichlet.pdf, 27 pp., sha256 4a891404fb5bd7a929a8eb8ed325323f799e126148ec10e8a29896506cb66a55, Creator pdfTeX-1.40.17, LaTeX with hyperref)

The paper is the general-purpose, natively multiclass post-hoc calibration method that generalises beta calibration to `k>2`, proposes a `k×k+ k`-parameter family, and shows on CIFAR-10 wide ResNet that a confidence-calibrated model can remain classwise-miscalibrated — the exact gap temperature scaling cannot close. Four passages carry the mechanism this plant can borrow without lifting its theorems — pagination is v1 PDF pagination, quotations via `pdftotext`:

1. **Three definitions of calibrated, not one — multiclass, classwise, confidence (§2 pp.2–3, Def.1–3, Eqs.1–4, Fig.1 p.3).** Def.1 multiclass-calibrated: `P(Y=i | p̂(X)=q)=q_i ∀q∈∆_k ∀i` (1) — the full vector must match. Def.2 classwise-calibrated: `P(Y=i | p̂_i(X)=q_i)=q_i ∀i ∀q_i` (2) — each one-vs-rest marginal must match. Def.3 confidence-calibrated: `P(Y=argmax p̂(X) | max p̂(X)=c)=c ∀c` (3) — only the top-class probability must match. The weaker notions are necessary but not sufficient for (1). Confidence-ECE is the binned version of (3); classwise-ECE is new, defined as `classwise-ECE = 1/k Σ_j 1/m Σ_i |B_{i,j}|/n |ȳ_j(B_{i,j})−p̂_j(B_{i,j})|` (4) averaged over `k` classwise-reliability diagrams. Fig.1a→1b: the same c10_resnet_wide32 is overconfident before and near confidence-calibrated after temperature scaling (`conf-ECE 0.0451→0.0078`, p=0.017 on Vaicenavicius [25] test, α=0.01 fails to reject) — yet Fig.1c shows it still systematically over-estimates class 2 (`class-2-ECE 0.0098`, all bins `p̂_j` below diagonal) and under-estimates class 4 (Supp Fig.12a), with `cwECE=0.1857` after temperature scaling vs `0.1795` after Dirichlet (p<0.0001 rejects classwise-calibrated for temperature, p=0.016 for Dirichlet). The point: confidence-ECE alone is the wrong scoreboard for multiclass.

2. **The Dirichlet family — one linear layer on log-probabilities, three parametrisations, contains identity and temperature (§3 pp.4–5, Eqs.5–8, Thm.1).** Assume `p̂(X)|Y=j ∼ Dir(α^{(j)})` (5) with `α^{(j)}∈(0,∞)^k` per class. Bayes gives generative `μ̂^{DirGen}(q;α,π)=(π_1 f_1(q),…,π_k f_k(q))/z` (6) with Dirichlet densities `f_j` and normaliser `z`. Two alternative parametrisations of the same family: linear `μ̂^{DirLin}(q;W,b)=σ(W ln q + b)` (7) where `W∈R^{k×k}`, `b∈R^k`, `ln` elementwise, `σ` softmax; canonical `μ̂^{Dir}(q;A,c)=σ(A ln(q/(1/k))+ln c)` (8) with `A∈[0,∞)^{k×k}` (each column has a zero), `c∈∆_k`. Thm.1: the three families are equal. Benefit (§3 bottom p.5): linear form is "a logarithmic transformation followed by a fully connected layer with softmax activation" — one `k×k` matrix plus bias, implementable in Keras as `log → Dense → softmax`. For `k=2` it coincides with beta calibration [15]; temperature scaling is the special case `μ̂^{TempS}(q;t)=μ̂^{DirLin}(q;1/t·I,0)` (Prop.1 Supp). Matrix scaling `σ(Wz+b)` on logits `z` is related but has an informational advantage because `ln σ(z)` loses information (end §3). The family contains the identity (`W=I,b=0`, equivalently `A` zero matrix with `c=u=(1/k,…,1/k)`).

3. **Interpretability at k+1 points and why off-diagonals matter (§3 p.5, Fig.2).** Canonical `(A,c)` is unique: `a_{ij}=w_{ij}−min_i w_{ij}`, `c=σ(W ln u + b)` with `u=(1/k,…,1/k)`. `a_{ij}` "increases the calibrated probability of class i (and decreases others), with effect size depending on uncalibrated probability of class j" (p.5). `c` is the image of the simplex centre; the `k` columns of `A` govern behaviour near the facet centres `(ε,…,1−ε,…,ε)` where one coordinate is near zero. Fig.2a (MLP on abalone, k=3) visualises the four interpretation points and their arrows; Fig.2b (SVHN_convnet, k=10) shows canonical `A` with e.g. `a_{3,9}=0.63` that adds class-3 mass when class-9 is high, and Fig.2c shows the resulting confusion-matrix delta (−26 confusions of class 2 for 8). The off-diagonal coefficients are the mechanism temperature scaling cannot express — exactly the classwise bias Fig.1c exhibits.

4. **Fitting with a proper loss and ODIR regularisation — when L2 is not enough (§3 p.6, §4 pp.6–10, Table 1).** Fitting minimises log-loss on a hold-out validation set for `μ̂^{DirLin}`: `L=1/n Σ logloss(μ̂^{DirLin}(p̂(x_i);W,b),y_i)+ λ/(k(k−1)) Σ_{i≠j} w_{ij}^2 + μ/k Σ_j b_j^2` — off-diagonal and intercept regularised separately, diagonal free ("allowed to freely follow the biases of classes"). The authors note Guo's claim that "any calibration model with tens of thousands of parameters will overfit to a small validation set, even when applying regularization" and show ODIR makes Dirichlet (and matrix scaling) outperform temperature scaling even with `k=100` (10100 parameters) — `λ,μ` tuned by internal CV on validation. On 21 UCI datasets × 11 classifiers (231 settings, 5×5 external CV, 3-fold internal CV, ensemble of 3 maps averaged [24]), Dirichlet_L2 average rank 2.34 for p-cw-ECE (Friedman p=7.54e−85) and 2.25 for log-loss (p=4.39e−77) vs temperature 4.37/4.61 and uncalibrated 5.02/5.54 (Tables 1–2 p.6). Evaluation always pairs calibration (ECE) with proper losses (log-loss, Brier) via the decomposition `loss = calibration + refinement` [14,25]; predicting the marginal class distribution gives ECE≈0 but maximal proper loss, so the check is not ignorable.

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** Requires exchangeable hold-out validation disjoint from training, clipping `p̂_j=0` to `2.2e-308` before `ln`; guarantee is statistical ("better calibrated") not per-input; regularisation hyper-parameters are data-dependent, no `λ/µ` imported; matrix scaling retains a logit advantage over Dirichlet on deep nets because `ln σ(z)` discards logit magnitude; results are for UCI/CIFAR with log-loss/Brier/cwECE/conf-ECE, not for language-model verbalized `P(yes)`; the Dirichlet assumption (5) is a modelling choice whose canonical map (6)–(8) is the usable family — the assumption itself is not claimed as truth.

**Why this source, for this cell.** `model-judgment--mechanism` asks for an operational mechanism this area proposes — implementable, not philosophy. Dirichlet calibration is the field's canonical natively-multiclass post-hoc mechanism that generalises beta calibration and contains temperature scaling as a one-parameter special case. The plant's `model-judgment` field currently judges with single thresholds (`judges.py` 0.20/0.80), binned ECE helpers (Guo M=15, Nixon five axes), Wang invert-softmax, Gneiting proper scores, Lakshminarayanan ensembles (`M⁻¹Σ p_m`), Farquhar semantic entropy (`−Σ p(c) log p(c)`), Angelopoulos conformal (`q̂`), and Mozannar deferral (`r^B=1{max η ≤ P(Y=M|X)}`) — all still either confidence-only or single-class. Kull supplies the missing multiclass mechanism: one `log → W·+b → softmax` layer with a defined classwise objective (4) and regulariser that the plant can add without touching transport or verdict words.

## What is new here

**Add a natively multiclass calibration map that lives on log-probabilities — `μ(q)=σ(W ln q + b)` — with the trichotomy that makes it usable: it contains identity, it contains temperature scaling, and its canonical `A/c` form is interpretable at k+1 points. Measure it with classwise-ECE (4), fit it with log-loss + ODIR.**

1. **The Dirichlet linear map is the mechanism — `μ̂^{DirLin}(q;W,b)=σ(W ln q + b)` (Kull §3 Eq.7).** For this plant `k∈{2 (yes/no), 3 (yes/no/unknown)}`, the map is a `k×k` matrix plus bias on `ln q`. Special cases: `W=I,b=0` is identity (already-calibrated case, desideratum 2); `W=1/t·I,b=0` is temperature scaling (Prop.1 Supp, §3 "Temperature scaling maps all belong to the Dirichlet family"). Fitting is multinomial logistic regression on features `ln p̂`, convex in `(W,b)` with log-loss, clipped at `2.2e-308` before `ln`. Implementation cost: one `log → Dense(k×k) → softmax` layer; no logit access required, applicable to any `p̂∈∆_k` from hosted judges, unlike matrix scaling which needs logits. The generative view (5)–(6) is the justification, the linear view (7) is the implementation, the canonical view (8) is the interpretation — Thm.1 says they are the same family.

2. **Classwise-ECE is the metric temperature scaling hides — Eq.4 `cwECE=1/k Σ_j Σ_i |B_{i,j}|/n |ȳ_j−p̂_j|` (§2 p.3).** Confidence-ECE bins by `max p̂`; classwise-ECE bins per class by `p̂_j` and averages. The operational use is the pair `(conf-ECE, cwECE)` plus per-class `class-j-ECE` — same `m` bins (Kull uses `m=15` like Guo), same `|B|/n` weighting. A report that only states `conf-ECE=0.0078` (Fig.1b) is insufficient when `class-2-ECE=0.0098` (Fig.1c) remains; Dirichlet moves the second number to `0.0033` (Fig.1d). For the plant's `{yes,no,unknown}` judge, `class-conditional` ECE (Nixon) and `classwise-ECE` (Kull) coincide at `k=3` but the latter's definition (4) is the one to publish.

3. **Canonical `A/c` and the k+1 interpretation points make the matrix readable (§3 pp.5–6, Fig.2).** `A` has non-negative entries with a zero per column; `c=σ(W ln u + b)` at centre `u=(1/k,…,1/k)`; each column of `A` governs a facet centre `(ε,…,1−ε,…,ε)`. The `k+1` arrows in Fig.2a (simplex with `k=3`) summarise the map without enumerating `k²` numbers: at centre mass moves to `c`, at each facet it moves proportionally to one column. Operational reading: large off-diagonal `a_{ij}` means "when uncalibrated class j is high, calibrated class i should be higher" — inspect `A` to diagnose systematic biases (e.g. `a_{3,9}=0.63` fixing confusion of class 2 for 8 in Fig.2b–c). For the plant this is the inspectable artifact that a scalar `t` never provides.

4. **ODIR regularisation makes the `k²+k` map fittable on `n≈500` validation pairs (§3 p.6, §4.1 Tables 1–2).** `L = 1/n Σ logloss(μ(q_i),y_i) + λ/(k(k−1)) Σ_{i≠j} w_{ij}² + μ/k Σ_j b_j²` — diagonal free, off-diagonals and intercept penalised separately (different scales: multiplicative vs additive). Tuned by internal CV on validation. Empirically Dirichlet_L2 ranks first on 21 UCI × 11 classifiers for both cwECE and log-loss (avg rank 2.34 and 2.25 vs next best beta 3.15/2.92, temperature 4.37/4.61). Limit kept: regularisation does not make `k=100` free — validation size must still dominate parameters, and logit-based matrix scaling may still edge Dirichlet on deep nets due to information loss `ln σ(z)`.

**Limits the review keeps from the source.** Only the *family* (7)–(8), the *definitions* (1)–(4), the *containments* (identity, temperature), the *interpretation construction* (k+1 points, `2.2e-308` clipping) and the *regulariser form* transfer; no UCI accuracy, no fitted `W/b/A/c`, no `λ/µ` or `t` value is imported — those are always fit to the plant's own held-out `(evidence, expected∈{yes,no,unknown})` pairs. Matrix-scaling logit advantage, non-neural vs neural comparisons, and Vaicenavicius significance test are kept as method reference, not as plant thresholds. The plant's existing `unknown` stays the detector for incomplete evidence; the map only corrects the `p̂∈∆_k` that was emitted.
