# From rhizome to web: Berkowitz & Bor's inter-organizational transfer and a coupling inventory for this plant

## What we read

- Héloïse Berkowitz & Sanne Bor — "Thousand plateaus: A rhizomatic approach of collaborations among organizations", *Peer Community Journal*, Vol. 6 (2026), article e11, 29 pp., DOI 10.24072/pcjournal.669 — read full primary source at:

Paginations below are the PDF's running pages 1–29; section headings are as printed. Quotations via `pdftotext` of the fetched PDF.

1. **The six principles as a transferable definition (pp. 6–9, § "To help understand the rhizome").** "Deleuze and Guattari (1987) use six principles: connection, heterogeneity, multiplicity, asignifying rupture, cartography, and decalcomania" (p. 6). Connection: "any point can be connected to any other (and should be)"; heterogeneity: "connections can be made between heterogeneous elements … organizations with different people, backgrounds, positions, ideas, goals, technologies, knowledges … without needing to conform to … a pre-determined order or identity" (p. 6). Multiplicity: "A multiplicity is a complex structure, though not of a prior unity … When a rhizome is divided up it changes its nature, there is no essence that remains unchanged" (p. 6, citing Parr 2010 p. 181). Asignifying rupture: "in the event of a break, it will always resume its growth … The rupture is thus not significant for the whole" (p. 7). Cartography: a rhizome "pertains to a map that must be produced … a map that is always detachable, connectable, reversible, modifiable, and has multiple entryways and exits" (D&G p. 21 quoted p. 7). Decalcomania: "transferring a pattern or a map through a medium … like pressing a pattern onto a surface … the impulse to trace rather than map" / "risks of replication without adaptation, imitation and control" (p. 7); "even within cartography, there are tendencies towards replicating (decalcomania), and similarly a traced structure may unexpectedly evolve" (p. 7).

2. **The framework — from principles to an inter-organizational web (pp. 8–12, § "The rhizomatic framework" + Table 1).** The authors define "inter-organizational arrangement as any form of relation among three or more organizations" excluding dyads for lack of multi-partner complexity (p. 8). Table 1 (p. 9, "Rhizome's principles and their implications") is the explicit transfer: each principle becomes an observable dimension — connections among members, heterogeneity of members, multiplicity of interactions, asignifying rupture in connections, cartography of interactions, decalcomania in adoption of knowledge — with a corresponding organizational reading and a failure mode. The outcome is an "inter-organizational web" not confined to pre-labelled categories (alliance, supply network, district, policy network, meta-organization): the web is the pattern of ties without presupposing which category the tie belongs to. Intent stated: "provide tools which can help analysis, comparison and cumulating knowledge … by moving beyond categories (which are often specific to one field, theory, sector, and so forth)" (Abstract p. 1).

3. **Cartography vs decalcomania as the generative/tracing tension (pp. 7–8, 12–14).** "Cartography describes a process of endless mapping of an interaction system … no `deep structure' or `genetic axis' on which things build up hierarchically, which corresponds to the tree or root model" (p. 7). Decalcomania is treated symmetrically: "Cartography and decalcomania act in symbiotic relations: even within cartography, there are tendencies towards replicating (decalcomania), and similarly a traced structure may unexpectedly evolve into different directions (cartography)" (p. 7). Transfer section adds: decalcomania "may imply to analyse the transfer or delegation of knowledge, of resources but also of power … If there is a transformation, i.e. a transposition, a transfer with adaptation, … innovation diffusion" (p. 8, ll. 548–553 extracted). This is not a metaphor swap but a mechanism — two ways ties persist.

4. **How the paper stages the transfer and what it concedes (§ Introduction pp. 1–5, Discussion pp. 18–24).** The authors place the work as a tool-building exercise, not a causal model: they cite the Deleuze–Guattari 1987 passage ("Écrire à n, n-1 … Faites rhizome et pas racine" p. 4) as orientation and Lawley's tradition (Lawley 2005; Chia 1996/1999) as prior organizational uses, then position the contribution as addressing that "this growing popularity now risks turning the concept into an `empty signifier' (Savage 2020 p. 320)" (p. ~3 search hit, exact phrase reproduced). The concession is that the framework is analytical, not predictive: no threshold or hypothesis is derived from the six dimensions, no field experiment evaluates the web against a hierarchy, and the "applications and research venues" (pp. 22–26) are prospective. Evidence is synthetic case-reading across organization types, not measurement of a plant. The 2026 date makes this the newest peer-reviewed rhizome transfer available as a retrievable source.

Limits of this reading: read only the 29-page final PDF pp. 1–29; no supplement or review file consulted. Page numbers are PDF pages, not journal-paginated. The six principles are quoted via Berkowitz & Bor's paraphrases plus their D&G quotations (p. 21 citation), not from a direct *Mille Plateaux* collation — correct for "source mechanism then named file" but mediated. The paper's organism/organization examples (industrial districts, policy networks, meta-organizations) are not mapped onto filesystem, pane, or sensor evidence.

## What this plant already does

*The plant already manages an inter-organizational web in Berkowitz & Bor's sense without declaring it as one — three-plus distinct organizations (minds, site, host supervisor, CI/pass, senses) are coupled through multiple overlapping ties, and each of the six dimensions is already observable as a distinct failure rather than as a single "mesh is rhizomatic" label.*

- **Defined inter-organizational arrangement: three-plus heterogeneous organizations.** The couplings the plant declares in `docs/mesh.md` (7-line introduction) / `docs/wall-coordination.md` / `docs/how-it-works.md` are: resident minds (discover, docs, genome, health, senses, field-review, etc.) with per-role walls; supervisor wake arbitration + `seed yield` lock; shared `main` checkout + genome exact-SHA push gate; `wall-write-audit.jsonl` digests + pin/snapshot; rendered panes. That is ≥3 heterogeneous organizations by the paper's definition — excluded dyads (e.g. wall↔handoff alone) do not carry the complexity. Each tie is typed: wall edit (connectivity), audit digest (heterogeneity check — any role may write any allowed path), tape/branch (multiplicity), supervisor loss (rupture), `seed yield` arbitration (deciding map vs tracing). The plant today names those ties across several books — no single declared web.

- **Connection / heterogeneity / multiplicity already measured, not declared.** `rg -n "assemblage|rhizome|inter-organizational web" docs/mesh.md` = 0; `rg -n "connectable.*reversible|detachable" src/` = 0 — the vocabulary is absent, which is correct for a transfer: the mechanism to move is observable behavior, not a shared word. What is observable: `sense.ledger.evidence-binding` (digest equality per wall outcome), `sense.runtime.pin-lag` (lag between HEAD and pinned release), `sense.coord.dm-disposition-age` / `sense.coord.gate-latency`, `sense.mind.wedge-suspect`, kernel-error counts. Each is a principle rendered as a count: connectivity = whether a DM can reach any resolver (addressed chat tape, cross-plant `<site>/<role>` DM); heterogeneity = mixed origins (Python wheels, tmux, systemd, Android, BLE) without conforming to one identity; multiplicity = 72 field-review cells × one branch, not one unified doctrine.

- **Asignifying rupture already operational.** `docs/wall-coordination.md` specifies that wall writes are whole-replacements and handoff projections are recomputed, so a supervisor-pane loss or a genome gate failure does not end the plant — the same walls/DM/Git resume on another line (the paper's "will always resume its growth" p. 7 is already `how-it-works.md` recovery). That rupture behavior is not named "asignifying" but it is logged: wall-write audit, `seed yield` lock lost via `fuser`-like detection, `native_recovery` host containment. The paper's insistence that rupture is "not significant for the whole" is already the plant's doctrine "No fix without causal investigation … Preserve the original failure evidence" (AGENTS.md) — break is diagnostic data.

- **Cartography vs decalcomania as declared vs traced path.** The prior two D&G reviews already staged this: Hung 2025's map/tracing audit (declaration that is detachable vs fixed pipeline) and Lawley 2004's warning that naming the mesh a rhizome invites a 2×2 diagram that closes the question. The plant's passage risk is decalcomania in the paper's sense: copying Hung's four readings or Berkowitz's Table 1 as a template without adaptation — replication without mapping — which the authors name as control over a traced structure (p. 7).

## What is new here

**Berkowitz & Bor's 2026 framework: treat the plant's couplings as an inter-organizational web interrogated along the six rhizome-derived dimensions, then use Table 1's row per principle as a declared inventory — not a metaphor to inhabit but a checklist to keep the web mappable (cartography) and to flag when it has hardened into tracing (decalcomania). The transfer to land is that inventory, not the organizational typology.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with where it lands in this repository and each bounded by what the paper concedes.

1. **Web before categories — list the ties, not the types (Table 1, pp. 8–12).** The framework's move is to replace "alliance vs district vs meta-organization" with one web whose rows are principle-derived observables. For this plant that web is the set of declared couplings (wall ↔ handoff, wake ↔ lease, DM ↔ resolver, branch ↔ pin, wall outcome ↔ evidence digest, pane ↔ lease, cross-plant DM ↔ site). Each row interrogates a distinct question: connectivity (can any point connect to any other without passing one hub?), heterogeneity (do ties carry mixed origins without forcing one identity?), multiplicity (does cutting one tie type change the plant's nature or does the web persist?), asignifying rupture (which breaks resume and which collapse?), cartography (which declarations are detachable/reversible maps vs which are tracings that replicate?), decalcomania (where does copying another plant's pattern without local adaptation control the web?). The transfer lands as a single inventory paragraph/table pointer in `docs/mesh.md` naming those six rows as the questions to ask per coupling, not as six claims about the plant.

2. **Cartography and decalcomania as concurrent diagnostics, not opposites (pp. 7–8).** The paper's key move versus a naive "mesh = rhizome" metaphor is that mapping and tracing coexist — a declaration that looks like cartography still carries decalcomania tendencies (template copy) and a traced structure may still swerve. Applied to `docs/mesh.md`: the book's introduction should declare its own reading instruction — that the coupling description is a map to be remade on each edit, and that the risk of decalcomania (copying Hung/Berkowitz Table rows as a fixed diagram) is checked by the named senses/audits rather than by the name "rhizome". That is a different carry from the Hung map/tracing audit (which stages the audit as concentration) and from the Lawley use-bound passage (which guards against diagram closure) — it gives the six rows as the audit dimensions. The paper does not import a numeric score with thresholds, so this proposal does not either.

3. **Diplomacy with the "empty signifier" warning — inventory, not label (Abstract + Discussion).** Berkowitz & Bor cite Savage 2020 that the rhizome "risks turning … into an `empty signifier'" and offer Table 1 as the repair. The plant-level analogue: writing "this mesh is a rhizome" without the six rows reproduces empty transfer; writing the six rows without claiming the plant passes them keeps the term checkable. The paper concedes no experiment tests the framework (pp. 22–26 are venues), so the transfer stays diagnostic: the inventory is readable, the score is not inferred. No industrial-district, supply-network, or policy-network fact is imported as a plant property; the organizational objects are replaced by this plant's own files/locks/peers.

Limits the review keeps from the source. The six dimensions are qualitative interrogatives, not metrics with pass/fail cutoffs — no coupling-type is scored, no "decentralisation score" transfers. The "beyond categories" claim refers to inter-organizational typologies, not to this plant's `state` words or `checks/*.json` shapes — no new state or check key is added. Empirical organizational claims (alliances, meta-organizations, policy nets) rest on cited cases outside this plant — no filesystem, pane, or sensor analogue was measured.
