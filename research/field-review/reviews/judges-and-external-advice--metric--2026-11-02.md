## What is new here

Wataoka et al. give this plant a quantitative language it lacks for asking "does our judge systematically side with text that looks like its own output, beyond what a human would?" The transferable pieces are operational, not philosophical:

1. A **metric with a human anchor**. Self-preference is not "how often the judge says the self text wins" but how much its recall diverges conditional on human preference: Bias = TPR_self − TPR_other (Eq.2). A high unconditional rate `P(Y'=1|S=1)−P(Y'=1|S=0)` (Eq.4) can simply reflect quality; a high conditional gap does not. The paper's Table 2 vs Fig.1b divergence for Vicuna-13b (0.382 unconditional vs lower conditional) is the concrete warning.
2. A **scoring protocol for pairwise judges**. Even though the plant's production judge is pointwise, the metric only makes sense pairwise: present two documents for the same question (positive vs negative control, or provider-A vs provider-B evidence for same correctness), score each via a normalized token probability for a forced choice `[[A]]`/`[[B]]` (Eq.3), average over swapped order to remove position bias (Zheng's mitigation reused). The paper's exclusion rule — drop non-compliant `[[ ]]` outputs rather than imputing — maps directly onto the plant's fail-closed `unknown`.
3. The **perplexity stratification diagnostic**. Figs.3–4 replace the binary self/other split with a continuous distractor f = perplexity difference conditioned on prompt, binned and compared to the human win rate per bin. The finding — LLMs' win curves steepen faster than humans' as perplexity gap grows, and self vs other curves coincide — tells the plant that if it measures bias, it should bin by a familiarity proxy, not just count wins. For closed providers the paper itself excludes them from that figure because perplexity is unavailable; the same limitation applies here, so the binned diagnostic is only actionable for open-weight judges where the evaluator's own perplexity of each document can be computed.
4. A **decision use without moral interpretation**. §6's ensemble advice — "when a model exhibits low perplexity on a sample, decreasing the weight assigned to that model's evaluation" — and the caveat that Demographic Parity "cannot demonstrate unfairness" frame the metric as a routing signal, not a moral verdict. The point is to detect when weight should move to another judge, not to declare a judge biased in absolute terms.

Tian's calibration metric (ECE/BS/AUC with temperature β) and Dubois' length-controlled win rate each measure a different distortion; Wataoka measures a third, and the three are not interchangeable. A judge can be well-calibrated per Tian yet show a large Equal-Opportunity gap per Wataoka, and vice versa.

Proposed owner change (documentation only, no default-judge or verdict-logic change): add a short subsection under "Comparing two judges on one question set" or "Explicit external-judge selection" titled "Self-preference score (operator experiment)" that does five things additively, using Wataoka et al. §3–§6 as the metric specification:

1. Names the experiment: a pairwise replay of the same question set evaluated by two providers, scored via Definition 4.1 and Eq.3–4. For each question, present two documents (positive-control vs negative-control, or equivalently evidence-A vs evidence-B where one is self-generated by the evaluator and one is not) to evaluator f with the Zheng et al. 2024 `[[A]]`/`[[B]]` prompt, compute `score_A` from the token distribution after "[[" per Eq.3, swap positions and average, declare `Y'` as the argmax, and jointly record human preference Y (the known control label) and origin S.
2. Defines the two numbers to compute from the resulting triples (Y,Y',S): the conditional bias `P(Y'=1|S=1,Y=1)−P(Y'=1|S=0,Y=1)` (Eq.2, range −1..1, 0 unbiased, GPT-4 0.520 in the paper) and, for reference only, the unconditional gap `P(Y'=1|S=1)−P(Y'=1|S=0)` (Eq.4, GPT-4 0.749 Table 2). Report both with per-cell counts (confusion matrix as in Fig.2) and note the caveat that Eq.4 without conditioning cannot demonstrate unfairness.
3. States the stratification step: for open-weight evaluators where perplexity can be computed, bin the paired documents by perplexity difference conditioned on prompt (as in Figs.3–4) and plot evaluator vs human win rate per bin; the diagnostic is that a gap which tracks perplexity rather than S suggests familiarity, not identity, is the driver. For closed providers, skip the perplexity bins — the paper itself excluded GPT-4/3.5 from Figs.3–4 for that reason.
4. Gives a minimal operator recipe without code: reuse the existing `judges.controls()` 12 documents to build pairs, run the same `--cases` through two `--model` values with the forced-choice wrapper (validate `[[A]]`/`[[B]]` presence else treat row as `unknown` for that metric and exclude it), keep `document_sha256` grouping by `identity` so comparisons are per question, and append a `self_preference.jsonl` per-question artifact carrying (Y,Y',S, score_A, score_B) — not a gate, diagnostic only. Do not reuse a cached single-document verdict across providers.
5. States limits and decision rule verbatim from the paper: pairwise open-ended dialogue only, Chatbot Arena is the reference distribution (33k dialogues, tie excluded per Y=1 conditioning), non-compliance excluded, perplexity unavailable for closed models, and human labels assumed ground truth. The verdict for production stays categorical pointwise; if conditional bias exceeds a pre-stated threshold (e.g., >0.3 as exemplified by Vicuna-13b-class bias), route that question's evaluation to an ensemble or a different provider rather than changing the judge's yes/no mapping.
