The transfer fragment is pp.05–06: von Foerster defines the vocabulary the field uses — "The term 'machine' in this context refers to well-defined functional properties of an abstract entity rather than to an assembly of cogwheels, buttons and levers, although such assemblies may represent embodiments of these abstract functional entities" (p.05); "A trivial machine is characterized by a one-to-one relationship between its 'input' (stimulus, cause) and its 'output' (response, effect). This invariant relationship is 'the machine'. Since this relationship is determined once and for all, this is a deterministic system; and since an output once observed for a given input will be the same for the same input given later, this is also a predictable system" (p.05); "Non-trivial machines, however, are quite different creatures. Their input-output relationship is not invariant, but is determined by the machine's previous output. In other words, its previous steps determine its present reactions. While these machines are again deterministic systems, for all practical reasons they are unpredictable: an output once observed for a given input will most likely be not the same for the same input given later" (p.06); "While in the trivial machine only one internal state participates always in its internal operation, in the non-trivial machine it is the shift from one internal state to another that makes it so elusive" (p.06); "All machines we construct and buy are, hopefully, trivial machines. A toaster should toast, a washing machine wash, a motorcar should predictably respond" (p.06); and the design directive plus warning: "In fact, all our efforts go into one direction, to create trivial machines or, if we encounter non-trivial machines, to convert them into trivial machines. The discovery of agriculture is the discovery that some aspects of Nature can be trivialized: If I till today, I shall have bread tomorrow" (p.06) versus "While our pre-occupation with the trivialization of our environment may be in one domain useful and constructive, in another domain it is useless and destructive. Trivialization is a dangerous panacea when man applies it to himself" (p.06); plus the diagnostic that makes the transfer testable: "Tests are devices to establish a measure of trivialization. A perfect score in a test is indicative of perfect trivialization: the student is completely predictable and thus can be admitted into society" (p.06); "I shall call a question to which the answer is known an 'illegitimate question'" (p.06, attrib. ## What is new here

The new piece from this source is the name for a design choice the plant already makes and the limit it already approaches, in field-native terms.

1. **A check is a trivial machine by intention.** Von Foerster's test — "same input → same output, always" (p.05) — is exactly what `CHILD`'s import-path rebuild achieves: it makes the check's output a function of the tree's bytes, not of the host's prior installs. The header can say so directly, citing the definition, so a reader can see that `PYTHONPATH_RE` is not incidental hygiene but the trivialization step that keeps the machine trivial.

2. **History makes the machine non-trivial, even when deterministic.** The same source says a non-trivial machine's output "is determined by the machine's previous output" (p.06) and that the shift of internal state makes it unpredictable. In the plant, reading the observer twice is trivial, but reading the *published* snapshot (`published_runtime`, `published_section`, `snapshot_age` / `checked_at`) depends on when the plant last published and whether the evidence block was refreshed after a new projection was added — precisely the 49bb564 case where the verdict agreed while the published block was missing. The published reading is therefore history-dependent: correct for the moment it was written, wrong after. Naming it as the intentional non-trivial remainder prevents reading "AGREE today" as "AGREE forever".

3. **Trivialization is a device, not a panacea.** The paper warns that perfect trivialization (perfect test score, p.06) makes the student predictable and that applying it to humans is destructive. For the plant, the transfer is the inverse caution: do not trivialize what must stay history-sensitive. The plant's `AGREE/DISAGREE` is trivial (ten fields), but the follow-through evidence it is meant to serve is non-trivial (availability over time of the block). Collapsing the two is the same category error von Foerster warns about, in engineering form.

Complements, not repetitions: the Ashby 2022 critique (model defines observer — what to observe) and this transfer (trivial vs non-trivial — what to expect from the observation) answer different questions about the same checks. Nizami 2020 supplies the Glanville bridge — that whitening via input/output can turn a Black Box into a white box only while both sides stay trivial; once either side is non-trivial, whitening is never complete — which is the same limit the `checks/observer-view-agreement.py` frame analysis already describes (a wall quote of a projection does not make the block carry it).
