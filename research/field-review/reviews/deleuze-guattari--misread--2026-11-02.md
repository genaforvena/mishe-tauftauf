# *Agencement* is not *assemblage*: the translation misreading that turns a capacity to act into a parts list, and what it costs the mesh declaration

## What we read

- **Anna Bogic — "Theory in Perpetual Motion and Translation: Assemblage and Intersectionality in Feminist Studies", *Atlantis* 38.1, 2017, pp. 138–149** — read full primary source at:
  - <https://atlantisjournal.ca/atlantis/article/view/4774> (HTML landing, abstract and metadata)
  - <https://atlantisjournal.ca/atlantis/article/download/4774/4551> (PDF 171_507 bytes fetched 2026-11-02 at /tmp/agencement_final.pdf, 12 pp., sha256 `f03925282feaedaf706853b0458e9bf0f81ba6204c808380a89e4d630feb9ebf`, Creator Adobe InDesign CC 2017, Pages 12)

Bogic is the misread-angle primary text; its object is the rendering of Deleuze & Guattari's *agencement* as English *assemblage*. It quotes and synthesises the philological sources the cell asks to check side-by-side with the plant's own text: John Phillips (2006) "Agencement/Assemblage", *Theory, Culture & Society* 23(2–3), pp. 108–109, DOI 10.1177/026327640602300219; Massumi/Polan/Tomlinson & Habberjam translation history; Callon (2006) on *agence*; Bennett, Braun, Dewsbury, Venn. Page refs below are the Bogic PDF pagination (138–149).

1. **The mistranslation (p. 140, § A Translation History: From Agencement to Assemblage).** "*Agencement* was first translated as *assemblage* by Paul Foss and Paul Patton in their translation of Deleuze and Guattari's article 'Rhizome' which appeared in the journal *I & C* in 1981 (Phillips 2006). This translation solution was subsequently maintained by other translators, Brian Massumi, Dana Polan, Hugh Tomlinson and Barbara Habberjam" (p. 140). "There are two main philosophical principles that underpin the concept of *agencement*" (p. 140): it includes both the arranging act and the resultant arrangement, and it includes both human and non-human elements. In French "*it implies both a process and a state of being: both the act of fitting/arranging and the arrangement itself. As in French, the English word assemblage tends to imply only the state of being, therefore eliminating the active aspect, the process*" (p. 140). "*Both in English and French, the term assemblage emphasizes collection, collage, and content as opposed to relations*" (p. 140).

2. **What English erases — agency and relations (pp. 140–141).** Via Callon (2006, p. 13 quoted p. 140): "*Agencement has the same root as agency: agencements are arrangements endowed with the capacity of acting in different ways depending on their configuration*" (p. 140). The parts have "*agentic capacities, that is, agencements in their different configurations are a form of distributive agency*" (Bennett in Kennedy et al. 2013, quoted p. 140). "*The word-play evident in the French term agencement – highlighting the role of agency and the active aspects in the concept – is erased in English, exposing only the state of affairs at the detriment of relations and connections*" (p. 141). "*What is lost in translation is a sense of the relationship 'between the capacity to act [and] the coming together of things' as 'a necessary and prior condition for any action to occur', be that human or non-human, organic or inorganic (Braun 2008 quoted in Dewsbury 2011, 149)*" (p. 141). Recent re-translation as *arrangement* (Guattari in Puar 2012, quoted p. 141) still fails: "*it fails to account for the actual process of arranging and the connectivity between heterogeneous elements*" (p. 141).

3. **The two axes that the noun hides (p. 140).** Via *A Thousand Plateaus* (2004/1987, pp. 97–98 quoted p. 140): "*a horizontal axis comprises two segments, content (bodies, actions, passions, an intermingling of bodies reacting to one another) and expression (acts and statements)*" and "*a vertical axis … refers to the processes of reterritorialization which stabilizes the agencement and deterritorialization which carries it away; or, in other words, agencements are continuously being made and becoming unmade*" (p. 140). The "feudal agencement" example (body of the earth, social body, knight/horse/stirrup, weapons/tools – "a whole machinic assemblage", Deleuze & Guattari 2004/1987 p. 98 quoted p. 140) shows the intermingling is not a catalogue.

4. **Bogic's own thesis and its limit for this cell.** Bogic defends two claims: (i) Puar's assemblage-as-corrective to intersectionality repeats arguments once made for intersectionality itself (Abstract p. 138, p. 139), and (ii) "*a historical and linguistic contextualizing can help us understand what is at stake*" (p. 139) because translation "*always produces an imperfect rendering … the instability of translation leaves space for new meanings*" (p. 141). She concludes "*we can ask whether this mistranslation can have productive effects (Puar 2011, para. 12)*" (p. 140). The feminist reception history (§ From Assemblage to Intersectionality, pp. 141–145), Crenshaw traffic metaphor revisit, and Puar/intersectionality debate are not transferred to the plant; only the philological diagnosis of *agencement* moves. Phillips (2006) itself is 2 pp. and was not fetched separately — Bogic quotes it verbatim and the Crossref DOI 10.1177/026327640602300219 resolves to Sage.

- **What the misreading costs if ignored.** Read as English *assemblage* (collection/collage), the declaration "*walls, tape, branch, wake, lock, push gate*" is heard as a parts list — "*the plant has these six things*" — and a later failure is read as one part failing. Read as French *agencement*, the same line is heard as "*this configuration of walls + tape + lock + branch, with these humans and non-humans intermingled, is endowed with the capacity to act in this way only so long as this configuration holds; a different configuration enables a different act*" (Callon's gloss). The controller-shed LIMITED episode and the 411h pin-lag on the current dashboard are live examples: the same walls and tape existed, but the agencement's capacity to settle—via that specific lock holder on that specific release — did not. The English noun hides that the arranging never stopped; the French doublet keeps both axes visible (horizontal content/expression, vertical territorialization/deterritorialization per p. 140).

## What is new here

**Bogic 2017 via Phillips 2006/Callon 2006: restore the active sense of *agencement* that English *assemblage* erases — both the arranging and the arrangement, with agency as a property of the configuration, not of a single mind — and apply it as a reading discipline for the plant's coupling declaration, not as a new mechanism.**

1. **Process + state as the declaration test (p. 140).** The question is not "which parts does the plant couple?" but "for *this* configuration, in *this* wake, what capacity to act does the togetherness enable, and what does it still have to arrange?" English *assemblage* invites the first; French *agencement* requires the second. Phillips's point is textual, not speculative: French *agencement* is literally the act of fitting/arranging *and* the fitting/arrangement (p. 140). Applied: a wall write is not just the wall file (state); it is also the anchoring, audit-append, peer-copy refusal, and digest that *arrange* it as writable now. The horizontal axis (bodies/actions/passions vs acts/statements, p. 140) maps directly onto `docs/wall-coordination.md:33–72` without renaming any command: a wall without a fresh staging anchor is not writable even though the file exists — content exists, expression is refused. That is the active sense the English noun drops.

2. **Agency as configuration, not possession (p. 140, Callon).** "*Agencements are arrangements endowed with the capacity of acting in different ways depending on their configuration*" (Callon 2006 p. 13 quoted p. 140). "*Agencements in their different configurations are a form of distributive agency*" (Bennett quoted p. 140). For the plant this reframes `seed yield`'s lock: the capacity to settle a wake is not genome's or the supervisor's alone; it is distributed across lock holder PID on release `054d4c64`, the tape sequence, and the wall audit — a different release or a stale holder and the same lock enables nothing, even though the "parts" are present. The tape already evidences this (wake 203714 dashboard: `DEPLOYED 054d4c64` drift 411h while HEAD `fc5b07c9` carries new reviews), but the docs currently narrate it as a parts list rather than as a configuration test. Bogic's framing also links to Puar's move to ask "*what assemblages do as opposed to what they are*" (p. 142) — which is exactly the `FIELD_REVIEW` check's substance, but the book does not yet say it.

3. **Relations over collection (p. 140–141).** "*Both in English and French, the term assemblage emphasizes collection, collage, and content as opposed to relations. Agencement … encompasses both the subject (the agent) and the object (the result)*" (p. 140). "*The word-play … is erased in English, exposing only the state of affairs at the detriment of relations and connections*" (p. 141). Applied without importing feminist categories: the wall is not separated from the mind that arranges it; the arrangement is already an agentic relation. The "feudal agencement" catalogue (earth body, social body, knight/horse/stirrup, weapons/tools, p. 140) models this for the plant: operator pane, supervisor process, wall file, audit log, branch and CI are not a parts inventory but a "symbiosis of bodies" whose togetherness must be checked per wake. The vertical axis (reterritorialization stabilises, deterritorialization carries away, p. 140) gives back the diagnostic docs already half-names via "Knots are real": knots are territorializations; a deterritorializing line is a re-route along an old line.

**Limits the review keeps from Bogic.** Only the translation diagnosis and the two axes move; no intersectional, race/class/gender, Puar, Crenshaw, or queer-terrorist category, fact, or thesis is imported as a plant property. Bogic's feminist re-reading of intersectionality vs assemblage (Abstract p. 138, §§ pp. 141–149) is cited as framing, not as demonstrated plant trait. No capacity number or gate is transferred; the active sense is declared, not scored. Phillips (2006) is cited via Bogic; the two-page original was not separately extracted and no claim is made beyond Bogic's quotes. The rhizome misreading diagnosed in `deleuze-guattari--misread--2026-10-30` (flat-network vs arborescent knots) remains distinct and untouched; this review adds the orthogonal translation misreading.
