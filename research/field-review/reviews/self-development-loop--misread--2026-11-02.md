# The plant treats every recurrence as the same level of error — it has no context marker for Learning I vs II vs III (a Bateson misread)

## What we read

**Gregory Bateson, "The Logical Categories of Learning and Communication," in *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (originally 1972; University of Chicago Press edition 2000, pp. 279–308)** — read authoritative text at:

- Canonical citation: Bateson 1972, *Steps to an Ecology of Mind*, Chandler Publishing 1972; Jason Aronson reprint 1987; Chicago Press 1972/2000. Not a summary of a summary.

Four passages carry the misread this plant reproduces. Page numbers are the Chicago Press pagination 279–308 reproduced in the biolinguagem PDF; logical definitions are Bateson's own recapitulation p. 293.

1. **Logical typing as the framing error — name ≠ thing, class ≠ member (pp. 281–283, Theory of Logical Types).** "The theory asserts that no class can, in formal logical or mathematical discourse, be a member of itself; that a class of classes cannot be one of the classes which are its members; that a name is not the thing named; that 'John Bateson' is the class of which that boy is the unique member" (p. 282). "It is not at all unusual for the theorists of behavioral science to commit errors which are precisely analogous to the error of classifying the name with the thing named — or eating the menu card instead of the meal" (p. 283). The barrier Bateson targets is not missing data but mis-typed data: treating a message about a context as if it were a message inside that context.

2. **Hierarchy by error corrected — Zero / I / II / III (pp. 283–293, especially recapitulation p. 293).** From the stochastic (trial-and-error) hierarchy: "Zero learning will then be the label for the immediate base of all those acts (simple and complex) which are not subject to correction by trial and error. Learning I will be an appropriate label for the revision of choice within an unchanged set of alternatives; Learning II will be the label for the revision of the set from which the choice is to be made; and so on" (p. 284). Recapitulated p. 293: "Zero learning is characterized by specificity of response, which — right or wrong — is not subject to correction. Learning I is change in specificity of response by correction of errors of choice within a set of alternatives. Learning II is change in the process of Learning I, e.g., a corrective change in the set of alternatives from which choice is made, or it is a change in how the sequence of experience is punctuated. Learning III is change in the process of Learning II, e.g., a corrective change in the system of sets of alternatives from which choice is made. (We shall see later that to demand this level of performance of some men and some mammals is sometimes pathogenic.) Learning IV would be change in Learning III, but probably does not occur in any adult living organism on this earth."

3. **Context and the context marker — the signal that types the set (pp. 288–292).** "We may regard 'context' as a collective term for all those events which tell the organism among what set of alternatives he must make his next choice" (p. 289). "At this point it is convenient to introduce the term 'context marker.' An organism responds to the 'same' stimulus differently in differing contexts, and we must therefore ask about the source of the organism's information. From what percept does he know that Context A is different from Context B? In many instances, there may be no specific signal or label which will classify and differentiate the two contexts" (p. 289). Context markers are the typed percepts — smell of the laboratory, harness, tone — that classify the context; when absent or misleading, context cannot be learned and the hierarchy collapses.

4. **The double bind — demanding Learning II/III where only Learning I is available, made pathogenic (pp. 299–303).** The reversal-learning experiment: when discrimination becomes impossible, "The context markers (e.g., the smell of the laboratory and the experimental harness) now become misleading because the animal is in a situation which demands guesswork or gambling, not discrimination. The entire experimental sequence is, in fact, a procedure for putting the animal in the wrong at the level of Learning II. In my phrase, the animal is placed in a typical 'double bind,' which is expectably schizophrenogenic" (p. 301). Bateson adds limits: "Learning II is adaptive only if the organism happens to be right in its punctuating of events" (p. 297); "Learning III is likely to be difficult and rare" and "to demand this level of performance ... is sometimes pathogenic" (pp. 293, 297–298); deuterolearners who replace premises acquired by Learning II pay a high cost and often retain contradictory premises. The essay is theory-building from Russell/Whitehead via Pavlovian, Gestalt and field observations, not a new experiment.

**Why this source for this cell.** Field is "the plant observing and changing itself" (substrate observer, follow-through, initiative watch), angle is *misread* (foundational idea we applied too loosely). The prior `self-development-loop--misread` (2026-10-29) used Argyris 1976 ASQ (single- vs double-loop and governing variables, itself an application of Bateson via Schön). This review reads the foundation Argyris cites: Bateson's logical types. Where Argyris made the governing variable explicit, Bateson makes the *level* of the error and the *marker* that types it explicit — the distinction the plant still collapses.

## What this plant already does

*The plant observes itself at one logical level and has no way to say whether a recurrence is a Learning I error, a Learning II punctuation error, or a pathogenic Learning III demand.*

- **Follow-through already types power and process, but not learning level.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/follow_through.py:30-35` fixes `LEARNING_PROCESSES = ("intuiting","interpreting","integrating","institutionalizing")` (Crossan 1999 via Lawrence 2005 p. 181), `POWER_MODES`/`POWER_FORMS`/`PROCESS_POWER_MAP`/`ROUTING_POWER` (p. 182–183) and `follow_through_power_form()`/`follow_through_mode_for_form()` as pure type helpers. - **Initiative watch knows thresholds, not types.** `src/mishe_tauftauf/initiative_watch.py:76-82` fixes `AGED_SECONDS=3600`, `CONTINUATION_SECONDS=1800`, `PRODUCER_MAX_AGE=180`, `ESCALATE_SECONDS=600`, `REMINDER_SECONDS=1800`; `GOVERNING_VARIABLES` (Argyris, p. 367) and `MEADOWS_RANK` (Meadows 1999) make the variable scrutable, and `is_single_loop_trap()` names repetition under the same variable. But `evaluate()` classifies every finding the same way — `id/kind/role/ref/summary/primary/escalation/action` with `UNKNOWN/ATTENTION/OBSERVED` — and re-emits `aged-wake:field-review` next cycle as a fresh `initial → escalation → reminder` instance. Whether the wake aged because the role chose late inside the right window (Learning I), because the window/punctuation is the wrong set (Learning II), or because the plant demands a system change while the context marker still says "old routine" (pathogenic III) is untracked.

- **The double-bind shape is already in the tape and the health guard.** When `chat.log` exceeds 75% of `MAX_TAPE_BYTES` (512 MiB) the observer goes `UNKNOWN ("evidence exceeds")` and the `checks/initiative.json` snapshot stalls; `follow_through.health()` then emits `follow-through observation UNKNOWN` and routes to health/genome/witness while still projecting `OUTCOME WRITER REFUSALS` counts. A finding that demands "settle faster" while the context marker ("tape too large for the reader that proves settlement") still says "evidence unavailable" is exactly Bateson's double bind — the organism is put in the wrong at Learning II by a misleading context marker, and Bateson calls the demand for Learning III under those conditions pathogenic (p. 301).

- **Documentation repeats the levels without the marker.** `docs/wall-coordination.md:328` "Living source and follow-through" and `docs/mesh.md:53` describe the plant observing itself, but neither names a percept that would let a mind know whether this wake is a new choice, a new punctuation, or a demand to change the system of sets. The plant thus does what Bateson warned against: it treats all learning as if it were Learning I (revision within the set) and then is surprised when the set itself is the error.

## What is new here

**Bateson's level is not Argyris's loop count.** Argyris 1976 (prior review) says "question the governing variable"; Bateson 1972 says "name the level of error being corrected and the percept that marks which set you are choosing from — without that marker, questioning the variable is just another choice inside the wrong set, and demanding Learning III is pathogenic."

Five ideas transfer directly, each bounded by what Bateson concedes and each additive to the existing `LEARNING_PROCESSES`/`POWER_MODES`/`GOVERNING_VARIABLES` vocabulary.

1. **Four bounded learning levels as a typed set, not a count.** The plant already counts wakes, patches and refusals; it does not type them. Bateson levels 0–III are a type: Zero = no correction, I = correct choice within set, II = correct the set / punctuation of sequence, III = correct the system of sets (p. 293). Limit kept: Learning IV "probably does not occur" in adult organisms — the hierarchy stops, it is not unbounded escalation.

2. **Error-by-level hierarchy, not error-by-count.** Bateson's ordering follows "the types of error which are to be corrected in the various learning processes" (p. 284) — an error of choice vs an error of set vs an error of system of sets are logically different, even when the observable behaviour (a late settlement) looks the same. Limit kept: Learning II is adaptive only when the organism's punctuation of events is right (p. 297); mistyped punctuation makes Learning II systematically wrong.

3. **Context and context marker as the typing percept.** "Context" is the collective events that tell the organism *which* set the next choice is drawn from; the "context marker" is the percept that classifies the context (p. 289). Without a marker — or with a misleading one — two contexts are indistinguishable and the organism cannot know whether it is in Learning I or gambling in Learning II. Limit kept: "In many instances, there may be no specific signal" (p. 289) — markers are often absent, and their absence is itself a finding.

4. **Double bind as a typed Level-II pathology with a detector.** A double bind is not "hard"; it is the specific condition where context markers become misleading *and* the organism is simultaneously demanded to perform correctly at Learning II (p. 301) — "expectably schizophrenogenic." Transfer: a bounded double-bind predicate can be evaluated from already-persisted rows — same `kind+role` recurrence + unchanged governing variable + misleading stale/missing marker (e.g., `tape_exceeds_reader`). Limit kept: Bateson's evidence is Pavlovian and anthropological illustration, not a controlled trial of the pathology rate — the transfer is the *type*, not a prevalence claim.

5. **Pathogenic Learning III demand.** "To demand this level of performance of some men and some mammals is sometimes pathogenic" (p. 293) and Learning III is "difficult and rare" (p. 297). Transfer: a guard that distinguishes "escalate harder" (Learning I) from "re-punctuate the sequence" (Learning II) from "demand systemic change" (Learning III) can refuse the third as `UNKNOWN` with repair guidance, rather than routing it as a harder Learning I reminder. This is the correct analogue of `follow_through.health()` already returning `UNKNOWN` when producer snapshots are stale.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** Bateson's scope is mammalian learning via Russell's logical types, not software plants; the analogy is structural (level of error + marker) not causal. The paper is pre-replication-crisis theory synthesis (Cannon, Maxwell, Russell/Whitehead) offered as illumination, not elimination, of misunderstanding (p. 281). The hierarchy is logical, not chronological — a wake can need Level II on its first occurrence.
