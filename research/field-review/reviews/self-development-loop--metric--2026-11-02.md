Distinct from the prior `self-development-loop--metric` (2026-10-30) DORA four-keys/five-metric reading (`dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics-four-keys`, Forsgren et al. *Accelerate* Ch.2–3) which measured only throughput vs stability. Four passages carry the metric this plant has not made (CACM pagination pp.46–51; quotations from the fetched PDF):

1. **The five dimensions — S/P/A/C/E — and the anti-single-metric thesis (p.46 Abstract, p.47 §Myths, p.48 §SPACE Framework, p.50 Figure 1).** Abstract: "[O]nly by examining a constellation of metrics in tension can we understand and influence developer productivity. This framework, called SPACE, captures the most important dimensions of developer productivity: satisfaction and well-being; performance; activity; communication and collaboration; and efficiency and flow." §Myths names six myths (activity-only, individual-only, one-metric-that-matters, only-managers, only-systems, invisible work) and the takeaway "productivity cannot be reduced to a single dimension (or metric!)." Figure 1 (p.50) is the 3×5 metric table: levels Individual / Team or Group / System × the five dimensions, with explicit cells, e.g. Satisfaction: developer satisfaction, retention†, satisfaction with code reviews assigned; Performance: code review velocity, story points†, reliability (uptime); Activity: commits, PR merge times, coding time; Communication: knowledge sharing/discoverability, quality of meetings†; Efficiency: code review timing, flow through the system, lack of interruptions†.

2. **Satisfaction and well-being as a leading indicator — correlated, not coincidental (p.48–49 §Satisfaction and well-being).** "Satisfaction is how fulfilled developers feel with their work, team, tools, or culture; well-being is how healthy and happy they are, and how their work impacts it." "Measuring satisfaction and well-being can be beneficial for understanding productivity and perhaps even for predicting it. For example, productivity and satisfaction are correlated, and it is possible that satisfaction could serve as a leading indicator for productivity; a decline in satisfaction and engagement could signal upcoming burnout and reduced productivity." WFH natural-experiment evidence (2020–2021, citations 8/3): commits and PR merge speed rose while qualitative well-being fell — activity alone painted the wrong picture, and only the S dimension corrected it. Example S metrics: employee satisfaction, developer efficacy ("Whether developers have the tools and resources they need"), burnout (excessive prolonged stress).

3. **Performance as outcome, Activity as count, Communication as coordination — and why isolation misleads (pp.48–49 §§ Performance / Activity / Communication and collaboration).** Performance: "Performance is the outcome of a system or process. The performance of software developers is hard to quantify… High-quality code may not deliver customer value." Outcome metrics: quality (reliability, absence of bugs), impact (customer satisfaction, adoption/retention, feature usage). Activity: "Activity is a count of actions or outputs… Developer activity, if measured correctly, can provide valuable but limited insights… [metrics] should never be used in isolation." Intractable activities (meetings, brainstorming, unblocking, architectural guidance) cannot be comprehensively quantified; trackable proxies (design docs, PRs, commits, code reviews, builds/tests/deploys, incidents) are waypoints only. Communication: captures "how people and teams communicate and work together… high transparency and awareness, information flow, diverse/inclusive teams." Proxies: discoverability of docs/expertise, speed of integration, quality of reviews, network metrics, onboarding time. Limits kept: invisible and articulation work (citations 18/21) are hard to measure yet load-bearing; optimizing one dimension alone shifts the others (e.g. collaboration at expense of individual flow, p.49).

4. **Efficiency and flow plus the code-review worked example that covers all five dimensions (p.49–50 §§ Efficiency and flow / Framework in Action, p.51 sidebar).** Efficiency: "the ability to complete work or make progress on it with minimal interruptions or delays, whether individually or through a system," including value-stream mapping (steps idea→customer), minimizing delays/handoffs, and the DORA subcase — "The DORA framework introduced several metrics to monitor flow within teams — for example, deployment frequency… and lead time." Example E metrics: handoffs (within/across teams), perceived ability to stay in flow, interruptions (quantity/timing/spacing/impact), time through system (total/value-added/wait). Tradeoff stated: "Higher efficiency… may also negatively affect other factors… maximizing flow and speed may decrease quality… Optimizing for individual efficiency by reducing interruptions may decrease ability to collaborate." Code-review illustration (p.50): same activity (code review) maps to all five dimensions depending on proxy — S: perceptual measures/learnings/mentorship, P: code-review velocity, A: number completed, C: quality/thoughtfulness score, E: timing and interruption characteristics — showing one activity yields five distinct measurements. Figure 1 marks † "Use these metrics with (even more) caution — they can proxy more things." Sidebar p.51 applies SPACE to SRE incident management (satisfaction with IM/on-call, performance as MTTR by severity, activity as incidents created/resolved, communication as cross-team participants, efficiency as handoffs/hops).

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** SPACE is a multidimensional framework for *choosing* and *interpreting* metrics, not a scoring function; no aggregate number is offered. All five dimensions are necessary, none sufficient; figure cells are templates to customize, not a checklist to sum. Metrics map to levels (individual/team/system); level mismatch is a known misuse. Several cells carry † "with (even more) caution" (retention, story points, LOC, quality of meetings) because they proxy many things. The source cites survey/case evidence and experience, not a randomized experiment; causation is not claimed. CACM and Queue versions are the same article (Queue id 3454122 maps to CACM doi 3453928 via the Queue footer "Article development led by queue.acm.org"). The history guide's "DORA is one row inside Efficiency" containment is explicit: DORA is not replaced, it is situated.

**Why this literature, for this cell.** Field is "the plant observing and changing itself" (substrate observer, follow-through, initiative watch), angle is *metric* (a concrete metric or experiment the area uses to measure itself). The plant already has a mechanism (`Ashby 1960` ultrastability onto `substrate_watch.py`), a recent result (`DGM 2025` archive onto `initiative.py`), a critique (`Levinthal & March 1993` myopia onto `initiative.py`), a misread (`Argyris 1976` single/double-loop onto `initiative_watch.py`), a transfer (`Simon 1962` / `Meadows 1999` / `Lawrence 2005`), and one prior metric (`DORA 2018–2026` onto `initiative.py`, 2026-10-30). ## What is new here

**Borrow SPACE's constellation — keep DORA as Efficiency's sub-row — and publish the five dimensions as a pure, typed, level-aware vocabulary beside `follow_through.lines()` so the plant can distinguish "many safe deploys" from "many handoffs, poor reviews, low satisfaction" without changing a threshold, a verdict word, or a `checks/*.json` shape.**

Three ideas transfer directly, each with a line number where it would land, each bounded by what Forsgren et al. concede.

1. **Five dimensions are the cut, not five scores — and the †-caution is load-bearing (pp.46-47 Abstract+Myths, p.50 Figure 1).** The five names are `Satisfaction and well-being (S)`, `Performance (P)`, `Activity (A)`, `Communication and collaboration (C)`, `Efficiency and flow (E)`. Transferring the *names* and the *anti-single-metric* constraint lets `follow_through.py` expose `SPACE_DIMENSIONS: Final[tuple[str,...]] = ("satisfaction","performance","activity","communication","efficiency")` and `SPACE_CAUTION_METRICS: Final[frozenset[str]] = frozenset({"retention","story_points","lines_of_code","meeting_quality"})` (Figure 1 †) without changing `collect()`'s `OBSERVED/UNKNOWN` vocabulary. The invariant is SPACE's: Activity counts, Efficiency flow, Performance outcomes, Satisfaction perceptions, Communication quality — four of them can move while the fifth does not, so they must be reported together.

2. **Levels × dimensions is the table, not a flat list — Individual/Team/System matters for attribution (p.50 Figure 1, p.51 sidebar).** Each SPACE metric is located at a level: Individual (one mind), Team or Group (minds that coordinate via tape/DM), System (end-to-end pipeline: tape→snapshot→wall→CI→pin). Transferring the *level* keeps the plant from misattributing a System-level flow (`Velocity/flow through the system`, `Frequency of deployments`) to an Individual-level activity (`Number of code reviews completed`, `Coding time`). The patch therefore adds `SPACE_LEVELS: Final[tuple[str,...]] = ("individual","team","system")` and a pure mapper `space_level(metric: str | None) -> str | None` mirroring `follow_through_power_form()`'s shape — pure, deterministic, no I/O — that types each Figure 1 row by its level, returning `None` for incomplete/unknown inputs (the plant's existing `None`-for-unknown idiom). The code-review worked example (p.50) then becomes a runnable check: the same underlying raw event (a `wall outcome` or `patch`) yields five distinct SPACE measurements depending on which dimension's proxy is evaluated.

3. **Efficiency contains DORA; the other four explain why DORA-alone is gamable — system flow without satisfaction, quality, or communication is the trap *Accelerate* and SPACE jointly name (p.50 §Efficiency and flow + p.47 Myths).** SPACE's Efficiency row explicitly lists `Frequency of deployments`, `Velocity/flow through the system`, `Code review timing` — the DORA subcase — but bounds it with the other rows' counter-metrics: `Code review velocity` under Performance, `Number of code reviews` under Activity, `Knowledge sharing/discoverability` under Communication, `Perception / Lack of interruptions` under Satisfaction. Making the containment explicit as `SPACE_CONTAINS_DORA: Final[bool] = True` and `DORA_IN_SPACE_DIMENSION: Final[str] = "efficiency"` lets `follow_through.py` publish a windowed SPACE summary alongside `counts` without contradicting the already-proposed `DORA_METRICS` in `initiative.py`: DORA stays the Efficiency measurement, SPACE is the constellation that gives it meaning. The sidebar's SRE mapping (MTTR under Performance, incidents under Activity, handoffs under Efficiency, cross-team participants under Communication, escalation satisfaction under S) is the precedent for locating each plant event in the right cell rather than inventing a new sixth dimension.

**Limits the proposal keeps from the source.** No aggregate SPACE score is offered — the framework is constellation, not index; the helpers return per-dimension slots, never a rolled-up number. † metrics remain caution-typed and are not aggregated as performance. Satisfaction is perceptual/survey-typed by construction; the helpers therefore type its inputs as `perception: str | None` (survey analogue) and never infer burnout from counts. The existing `UNKNOWN` vocabulary stays the detector for incomplete evidence; the new helpers add only the second clause SPACE distinguishes: incomplete information about *which dimension* the window moved. Causation is not claimed: a High-Efficiency + Low-Satisfaction window does not prove speed caused dissatisfaction.
