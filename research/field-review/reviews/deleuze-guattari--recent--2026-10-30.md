# DAOs as rhizomes: a recent diagnostic for declaring couplings without pretending they are trees

## What we read

**Alvin Hoi-Chun Hung — "DAO as Rhizome: Reimagining Rules, Governance and Organisations", Law and Critique, Vol. 37 No. 1, pp. 203–224, published online 26 September 2025, DOI 10.1007/s10978-025-09432-w** — read full primary source at:

Hung is the recent re-reading of Deleuze & Guattari for this cell; the 1980/1989 ontology is read only through Hung's synthesis (pp. 204–207) and his citations of *A Thousand Plateaus* (Deleuze & Guattari 1989). Pagination is the PDF/printed pagination (203–224); quotations via `pdftotext` of the fetched PDF.

1. **Rhizome as ontological alternative to arborescence — the six attributes (pp. 204–207, § "Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari's Rhizome").** Assemblages group multiplicities that are "not regarded as unified or stable entities" but "constantly shaping and being shaped" (p. 205, citing Hipfl 2018; Barlott & Turpin 2022). Hung distinguishes three assemblages: arborescent (tree with roots/above-ground, "hierarchical systems with centres of significance and subjectification, controlled by central automata like organised memories … An element only receives information from a higher unit, and only receives a subjective affection along pre-established paths" — D&G 1989 p. 16 quoted p. 205), radicle (fragmented root where "the root's unity subsists, as past or yet to come, as possible" — D&G 1989 p. 5 quoted p. 205) and rhizome: "acentered, non-hierarchical, non-signifying system without a General and without an organising memory or central automaton, defined solely by a circulation of states" (D&G 1989 p. 21 quoted p. 205). Rhizome attributes enumerated pp. 205–207: (i) connectivity and heterogeneity — "any point to link with any other … multiple entry and exit points … boundless expansion" (p. 206); (ii) multiplicity — not units but "dimensions or dynamic trajectories in constant motion" (D&G 1989 p. 9 quoted p. 205); (iii) asignifying rupture — "A rhizome may be broken, shattered at a given spot, but it will start up again on one of its old lines, or on new lines" (D&G 1989 p. 9 quoted p. 216); (iv) map vs tracing — "The map is open and connectable in all of its dimensions; it is detachable, reversible, susceptible to constant modification" (D&G 1989 p. 2 quoted p. 206; elaborated p. 217 "map is open and connectable," "not a static image"); plus deterritorialisation/reterritorialisation and the anti-genealogy character ("profoundly rethink fundamental ideas" p. 205, "dismantle the strata … fell trees in favour of veritable rhizomes" D&G 1989 p. 190 quoted p. 206).

2. **DAOs as attempted rhizomes — four rhizomatic readings applied (pp. 208–218, §§ Connectivity/Heterogeneity §213, Multiplicity §214, Asignifying Rupture §216, Mapping/Tracing §217).** Hung traces DAOs from cooperative/hierarchical norm (Ahrne et al. 2007, Butler 1995) through rhizome lens. Connectivity/heterogeneity: DAOs via token-based membership and global interconnectivity embody "non-hierarchical configurations devoid of a central organising hub but adorned with multiple entry and exit points" (p. 206 framing, applied p. 212–213); DAOs "deploy smart contracts to automate core functions and displace traditional forms of governance" (Abstract p. 203). Multiplicity: DAOs as "constant process of flow, or change, rather than being a thing unto which change is done" (Lawley 2005 quoted p. 214), founders phase out and "relinquish control … project's future firmly in the hands of the community" (p. 214), Compound DAO transition cited as exemplar (Axelsen et al. 2022) with COMP distribution and multisig oversight (p. 214). Asignifying rupture: exit by selling tokens, hard fork as rupture that regenerates — "In the presence of significant issues or disagreements, members retain the choice to exit by selling their tokens. As other individuals acquire the tokens … the DAO rebounds after the rupture" (p. 216), hard fork as "breaking off ongoing development and initiating new development in another direction" (Kurniawan 2022 cited p. 216), contrasted with conventional failure framing ("formation of breakaway organisations is often framed as a sign of leadership failure" Dyck & Starke 1999 p. 216). Mapping/tracing: DAOs as maps — "A map always has multiple entryways, one can start where one wants, and no single entryway is privileged … DAOs have no single correct point of entry" (p. 217), community members map "uncharted territory" (DuPont 2017 p. 217), with coexistence warning: "we should avoid the simple dualism of 'contrasting maps to tracings, as good and bad'" (D&G 1989 quoted p. 217) and "knots of arborescence in rhizomes, and rhizomatic offshoots in roots" (D&G 1989 p. cited p. 218) exemplified by MolochDAO → MetaCartel hard fork that "replicated certain patterns" (Peter 'pet3rpan' 2019 p. 218).

3. **The critique that matters to this plant — rhizomatic rhetoric can mask reconsolidated power (Abstract p. 203, §§ "Connectivity" through Conclusion pp. 212–220).** Abstract's thesis: DAOs "gesture towards radical democratic possibilities, they are equally saturated with ideological investments that reproduce rather than rupture dominant paradigms. The analysis emphasises the tension between the rhetoric of decentralisation and the often-consolidated realities of power embedded in code and protocol" (p. 203). MakerDAO case: governance "structured as one vote per MKR token, reveals a profound asymmetry, as voting power concentrates disproportionately in the hands of a few large token holders who dominate decision-making" (Sun et al. 2024 cited p. 213), low participation of small stakeholders, proxy voting "further exacerbate … consolidating authority into even fewer hands" (Fritsch et al. 2024 p. 213), "reproduce a form of decentralised governance that mirrors neoliberal hierarchies, redistributing power upward to a concentrated elite" (p. 213). Corporate ethos: "majority of DAOs are created to manage financial protocols … privileges corporate efficiency in capital accumulation … legal frameworks like Wyoming's recognition of DAOs as entities, which codify corporate hallmarks" (p. 213). Anonymity paradox: flattening enables "expression … devoid of conventional markers of identity — gender, race, or socio-economic status" (p. 215 citing Leitner 2015) but undermines "relational obligations" and "diverges sharply from the ideal of collective engagement that prioritises shared benefit" (p. 215) vs Habermas ideal speech situation. Rupture critique via Laclau & Mouffe/Schmitt: unlike democracy that must "confront and negotiate antagonism … within a shared political space," DAOs let dissenters "exit and establish parallel organisations … recasting governance … into frameworks of adaptation rather than radical transformation" (p. 217; Chandler 2015 posthuman warning p. 214; Gandorfer 2022 hyper-individualism p. 214; Land 1993 accelerationism p. 218).

4. **How Hung stages the method — what is claimed, what remains limited (§§ Introduction p. 204, References pp. 221–224).** The paper positions as intervention in a gap: "there remains a conspicuous gap in interrogating their transformative implications … through the ontopolitical prism of Deleuze and Guattari" (p. 204), drawing on organisation-theory precursors (Lawley 2005; Pick 2017; Carter & Jackson 2004; Linstead & Thanem 2007). No new formal model or measurement is proposed; evidence is synthetic case reading (MakerDAO, Compound DAO, Democracy Earth, VitaDAO, MolochDAO, Kromatika DAO manifesto pp. 218–219) plus citation to empirical voting-power studies (Fritsch et al. 2024) and decentralisation metrics (Axelsen et al. 2022). Hung closes: DAOs are "significant and innovative … [but] not yet functioned as genuine tools for political self-organisation" (p. 219) and transformative potential is "ultimately contingent upon the introduction of structured deliberative processes and the cultivation of a progressive, collective ethos" (p. 220).

**Limits the source states and this review keeps.** Hung's rhizome is an interpretive lens, not a metric with thresholds — no coupling-type is scored, no "decentralisation score" transfers as a number. The six attributes are presented as diagnostic dimensions, not laws that predict governance quality; the tracing/mapping coexistence ("knots of arborescence in rhizomes") means any classification is provisional and site-specific. Empirical DAO claims rest on cited case studies (2020–2024 Ethereum DeFi context) — no filesystem, pane or sensor analogue was measured. The normative cautions (Habermas, Chandler, Laclau & Mouffe, Land) are cited as framing, not as demonstrated effects of this plant's couplings. No year-specific protocol or smart-contract detail transfers as a plant file edit.

**Why this source, for this cell.** `deleuze-guattari--recent` asks for a recent (2023–2026) result in the assemblage/rhizome/machinic area, read as primary source, applied to "the plant's own couplings, and what it declares about them" (`fields.json` plant_part). - **What the gap costs if the critique is ignored.** A coupling declaration that only traces a central tree (genome → wake → wall → push) invites a visitor to assume hierarchy where the plant actually permits peer re-entry (any mind may write any allowed path on `main`, any wall may ask any peer, sense `UNKNOWN` defers without centre). Conversely, a declaration that only claims rhizome (minds choose, peer DMs, one shared checkout) masks the actual central automata (supervisor leases, `seed yield` arbitration, genome's exact-SHA CI) that do concentrate authority — the MakerDAO analogue: declared decentralisation with concentrated token/schedule power. Without Hung's diagnostic, the next docs edit risks either over-tracing (repeating the pipeline as the only entry) or over-mapping (claiming flat heterogeneity that hides the supervisor/genome bottleneck).

## What is new here

**Hung's 2025 DAO-as-rhizome diagnostic: treat a coupling declaration as a map that must remain detachable/reversible, probe it with four rhizomatic readings, and then audit the rhetoric-vs-reality gap where code and protocol reconsolidate the power the narrative分散 claims to dissolve — that audit, not the rhizomatic analogy itself, is the transferable work.**

1. **Map vs tracing as the declaration test — "detachable, reversible, susceptible to constant modification" (D&G 1989 p.2 via Hung p.206) and "we should avoid the simple dualism … knots of arborescence in rhizomes" (Hung p.217).** A coupling declaration (mesh.md's plant/site/channel table, wall-coordination's wall/DM rules) can be written as a tracing — a fixed pipeline that a reader must follow — or as a map — an open, connectable arrangement where any communicated observation can be re-linked to a different resolver in a later wake. Hung's reading (pp. 217–218) makes this actionable: keep both tendencies visible in the canonical doc itself. The actionable carry is to state the map/tracing coexistence explicitly and to mark entry multiplicity — a declaration that lists multiple entry points without privileging one (Hung p.217 "no single correct point of entry") while naming the persistent arborescent knots (genome's push gate, supervisor wake arbitration).

2. **Four diagnostic readings for the plant's declared couplings (Hung §§ pp.212–218 applied):** (a) **Connectivity/heterogeneity** — does the declaration let "any point link with any other" (p.206) or does it funnel through a centre? Test: can a mind's observation reach any resolver without passing a single hub? (b) **Multiplicity / constant becoming** — is the plant staged as "a constant process of flow … rather than a thing unto which change is done" (Lawley 2005 p.214) with founders/agents phasing out vs a static org chart? (c) **Asignifying rupture** — can a coupling break and "start up again on one of its old lines, or on new lines" (D&G 1989 p.9 p.216) without the plant's demise? Test: wall/chat/Git do after supervisor or pane loss (the wall-coordination recovery claims). (d) **Mapping** — is the declaration an experimental map of uncharted territory (DuPont 2017 p.217) read as provisional, with multiple entryways? These four are the transfer, not DAO-specific token details.

3. **Rhetoric-reality audit as the required second step — Hung's central critique (Abstract p.203, MakerDAO pp.212–213, Conclusion p.220).** The transfer is not "declare more rhizome" but "declare then audit concentration." Hung demonstrates the audit on DAOs: smart-contract automation re-embeds power in maintainers and token weights — "power is entrenched in those who maintain and control the underlying algorithms" (Maker Team 2020 p.213), "voting power concentrates disproportionately" (Sun et al. 2024 p.213), proxy delegation consolidates further (Fritsch et al. 2024 p.213). For this plant the analogue is explicit: the couplings that most read as rhizomatic (peer DMs, minds choose work) are mediated by concentrated automata (wakes issued by supervisor, `seed yield` arbitration, `wall-write-audit.jsonl` central log) and by the single `main` push gate Genome owns. Hung's prescription — decentralisation alone does not make an assemblage democratic; "structured deliberative processes and … progressive, collective ethos" (p.220) must accompany it — becomes a docs principle: publish the audit of where concentration persists alongside the map, with the same concision as `docs/how-it-works.md`'s loop.

4. **Rupture as generative, not as failure — hard fork as the model for wall/Git continuity (pp.216–217).** Hung reframes breakaway as "not construed as anomalous disruptions … enables DAOs to promote experimentation and competition … intrinsic characteristic of decentralised organisational structures" (Sims 2019 p.216). Applied to the plant: `docs/wall-coordination.md` already treats wall writes as replaceable and `how-it-works.md` treats recovery/revert paths as normal — Hung supplies the named concept (asignifying rupture) for that stance and the governance consequence: dispersal without negotiation privileges exit over antagonism management (Laclau & Mouffe vs Schmitt p.217). That distinction belongs in the doc that lists the couplings: note when rupture creates a new lineage (fork/hard-fork analogue for a mind's branch experiment) and when negotiation within the shared checkpoint is required.

**Limits the review keeps from Hung.** Only the six-attribute framing and the rhetoric-reality audit move; no DAO token, voting, anonymity, or Ethereum-specific claim is imported as a plant property. Hung's empirical DAO base (DeFi 2020–2024) and normative anchors (Habermas ideal speech, Chandler Big Data posthumanism, Land acceleration — pp.214–218) are cited as cautionary frames, not as demonstrated traits of this plant. No metric threshold or "decentralisation score" is transferred — the diagnostic is qualitative and must be re-applied to each coupling's evidence (the four readings above).
