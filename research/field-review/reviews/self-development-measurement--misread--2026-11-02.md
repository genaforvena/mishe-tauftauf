# Self-development measurement — misread REVISIT: Design 2 (O1 X O2) is a bad example — history, maturation and instrumentation are not controlled by one baseline

## What we read

- Primary source: Donald T. Campbell and Julian C. Stanley, "Experimental and Quasi-Experimental Designs for Research" (1963, reprinted 1966, Houghton Mifflin; chapter in N. L. Gage Handbook of Research on Teaching). - Claims relied on (verbatim or close paraphrase, with page refs from the PDF pagination):
  - Foundational distinction (p.5): "internal validity is the basic minimum without which any experiment is uninterpretable: Did in fact the experimental treatments make a difference in this specific experimental instance? External validity asks the question of generalizability" — the two are "frequently at odds" and both matter, but internal is the sine qua non.
  - Eight threats to internal validity (p.5): Campbell & Stanley list history, maturation, testing, instrumentation, statistical regression, selection, mortality, and selection-maturation interaction as "classes of extraneous variables ... [that] might produce effects confounded with the effect of the experimental stimulus" if not controlled.
  - Design 2 definition and verdict (pp.7-8): Design 2 is the One-Group Pretest-Posttest Design, diagrammed as `O1 X O2`. The authors state it "is introduced here as a 'bad example' to illustrate several of the confounded extraneous variables that can jeopardize internal validity. These variables offer plausible hypotheses explaining an O1-O2 difference, rival to the hypothesis that X caused the difference" (p.7).
  - History (p.7): "The first of these uncontrolled rival hypotheses is history. Between O1 and O2 many other change-producing events may have occurred in addition to the experimenter's X." "A minus has been entered for Design 2 in Table 1 under History." They add "History becomes a more plausible rival explanation of change the longer the O1-O2 time lapse" and note "experimental isolation ... can almost never be assumed in research on teaching methods."
  - Maturation (pp.7-8): "A second rival variable ... is designated maturation ... to cover all of those biological or psychological processes which systematically vary with the passage of time ... Thus between O1 and O2 the students may have grown older, hungrier, more tired, more bored, etc., and the obtained difference may reflect this process rather than X."
  - Table 1 convention (p.8 note): "a minus indicates a definite weakness, a plus indicates that the factor is controlled, a question mark indicates a possible source of concern, and a blank indicates that the factor is not relevant ... No + or - indicator should be respected unless the reader comprehends why it is placed there."
  - Remedy by design (pp.37-42): The chapter's remedy is not a statistic but a different design — Design 7, the Time-Series Experiment (`O1 O2 O3 O4 X O5 O6 O7 O8`), whose repeated pre-X and post-X observations make a history or maturation trend visible (Figure 3, p.38). Design 12 (Separate-Sample Pretest-Posttest) and Design 13-14 are presented as patched-up controls that still leave history uncontrolled unless time is multiplied.

The transfer here is about measurement threats, not about ANOVA or Latin squares.

## What is new here

- Campbell & Stanley's transferable lesson is that a single pre/post difference without a control group or a time series cannot separate X from the other things that change with time. For this plant that means: the trial window and the baseline window differ in at least four rival ways that travel with the O1-O2 interval, each already named in the 1963 chapter:
  1. **History** — specific events between O1 and O2 besides X: host-pressure `LIMITED` episodes, `controller-shed` (coordination/initiative/observer-checks), feed outages, or peer load changes. The chapter's note that history plausibility grows with O1-O2 lapse maps directly onto 10-hour windows that span real incidents.
  2. **Maturation** — processes that vary with time passage itself: drift in wake rate, daily/weekly cycles in the feed, or gradual volume inflation (the 82-189% growth already measured). A monotone trend across placebo windows mimics an X effect.
  3. **Instrumentation** — changes in the measuring instrument: a different `PREFIXES`/`decode_lifecycle` path or wall-text format for O1 vs O2. The Huang REVISIT already names decoder parity; Campbell names it as a separate threat that applies even at equal cost.
  4. **Selection / regression** — the placebo exclusion rule (`n < 1000` excluded as feed-coverage gap) and choice of window bounds is a selection step; a baseline that is extreme relative to its neighbours invites regression toward the placebo mean.
- The common misread the chapter warns against is treating Design 2 as if it were a time-series (Design 7) because a few placebos were added after the fact. Placebos help in the way Abadie et al. 2010 in-space placebos help — they show ordinary variation — but as the chapter stresses, adjacent windows share history and maturation regimes and are autocorrelated, so they are not exchangeable controls. Calling the scaled difference `q` without that caveat misreads Design 2 as Design 7. The chapter's own wording — Design 2 is a "bad example" — is the corrective label this plant lacks.
- Why this matters beside the Huang audit: Huang asks whether the delta survives equal information, cost, and prompt. Campbell asks the prior question: even at equal information/cost/prompt, is the design one that can speak causally at all? The two audits are orthogonal and both belong as annotations, not gates.

- Add pure helper `campbell_design2_audit(sample, baseline, placebo, *, history_events=None) -> dict` beside `placebo_scale` and the proposed `huang_checks` (no Path/clock/net I/O beyond the dicts already held):
  - Inputs are the dicts `report()` already has: `sample`, `baseline`, `placebo` (from `placebo_scale`), and an optional `history_events` list of strings (e.g., feed gaps, `LIMITED` episodes) supplied from the samples' `services`/`recovery` evidence when available, otherwise None.
  - Output is a dict with one entry per Table-1 internal threat relevant to Design 2, e.g. `{"design": "O1 X O2 (Design 2)", "history": "UNCONTROLLED" | "UNKNOWN", "maturation": ..., "instrumentation": "ok" | "mismatch" | "UNKNOWN", "selection": ..., "regression": ...}` — values are `"ok"` only when controlled, otherwise `"UNCONTROLLED"` or `"UNKNOWN"` where no evidence is present. A single-O1/O2 never returns `"controlled"` for history or maturation; those are `"UNCONTROLLED"` by design, per Table 1's minuses for Design 2. `instrumentation` is `"ok"` when `PREFIXES`/`decode_lifecycle` are the same for trial and placebo windows (reuse the Huang decoder-parity check); `selection` is derived from `placebo["excluded"]` and window-bound choices; `regression` is flagged when the baseline is the placebo min or max.
  - Helper also computes `maturation_trend`: Spearman or simple monotonic count of placebo-window series to surface a drift that rivals X, returned as `trend="flat|rising|falling|UNKNOWN"` with `"UNKNOWN"` when kept windows < 3.

- Extend `_placebo_lines` / `report()` to print, below the existing scaled-differences table, one audit block (additive, no table column removed, strict UNKNOWN on missing data):
  ```
  Campbell Design 2 audit (O1 X O2): history=UNCONTROLLED — single O1 vs single O2 shares history between measures; maturation=UNCONTROLLED — time-series not collected (see Design 7); instrumentation=ok/mismatch; selection=excluded k=...; regression=baseline is/is-not extreme; maturation_trend=flat/rising/falling/UNKNOWN.
  Limit: this audit labels threats per Table 1; it does not estimate a causal effect and placebos remain autocorrelated shared-regime windows, not exchangeable controls.
  ```

- Limits, in the chapter's own terms and ours: (a) Transfer from classroom experiments to feed counts is analogy; Campbell & Stanley's history/maturation are about pupils, ours about host load and volume growth — the mechanism (confounded time) transfers, the substance does not. (c) Design 7 time-series is the proper remedy the chapter offers; the audit does not make Design 2 into Design 7, it labels Design 2 as such. (d) The audit and the Huang audit overlap on `instrumentation`/`decoder_parity`; the two should share that check rather than duplicate it, and the Campbell audit should cite Huang where they coincide.
