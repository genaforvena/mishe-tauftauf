Quoted from the paper (section and page of the 14-page PDF):

- Abstract + §1: Spanner exposes "a novel time API that exposes clock uncertainty. This API and its implementation are critical to supporting external consistency" — timestamps reflect serialization order and satisfy external consistency (linearizability [20]): "if a transaction T1 commits before another transaction T2 starts, then T1's commit timestamp is smaller than T2's."
- Table 1 (p.5) TrueTime API:
  - `TT.now() -> TTinterval: [earliest, latest]`
  - `TT.after(t) -> true if t has definitely passed`
  - `TT.before(t) -> true if t has definitely not arrived`
  The argument `t` is of type `TTstamp`; the TTinterval endpoints are TTstamps; time epoch is analogous to UNIX time with leap-second smearing.
- §3 text: "TrueTime explicitly represents time as a TTinterval, which is an interval with bounded time uncertainty (unlike standard time interfaces that give clients no notion of uncertainty)." "The TT.now() method returns a TTinterval that is guaranteed to contain the absolute time during which TT.now() was invoked." "Define the instantaneous error bound as epsilon, which is half of the interval's width, and the average error bound as epsilon-bar."
- §3 implementation: implementation "keeps uncertainty small (generally less than 10ms) by using multiple modern clock references (GPS and atomic clocks)." Armageddon masters equipped with atomic clocks; GPS masters from nearby and distant datacenters plus Armageddon masters; daemons poll a variety and "apply a variant of Marzullo's algorithm [27] to detect and reject liars, and synchronize the local machine clocks to the non-liars." "Between synchronizations, a daemon advertises a slowly increasing time uncertainty that is derived from conservatively applied worst-case local clock drift." GPS masters "advertise uncertainty that is typically close to zero" while Armageddon masters advertise "a slowly increasing time uncertainty that is derived from conservatively applied worst-case clock drift (200 microseconds/second)." "In our production environment, epsilon is typically a sawtooth function of time, varying from about 1 to 7 ms over each poll interval. epsilon-bar is therefore 4 ms most of the time. The daemon's poll interval is currently 30 seconds, and the current applied drift rate is set at 200 microseconds/second, which together account for the sawtooth bounds from 0 to 6 ms. The remaining 1 ms comes from the communication delay to the time masters. Excursions from this sawtooth are possible in the presence of failures" — "occasional time-master unavailability can cause datacenter-wide increases in epsilon. Similarly, overloaded machines and network links can result in occasional localized epsilon spikes" and "machines that exhibit frequency excursions larger than the worst-case bound … are evicted."
- §4.1.2 commit wait: coordinator assigns commit timestamp `s >= TT.now().latest` after `e_server`, then "ensures that clients cannot see any data committed by Ti until TT.after(s) is true" — "Commit wait ensures that s is less than the absolute commit time of Ti, or s < t_abs(e_commit_i)." Proof chain `s1 < t_abs(e_commit_1) < t_abs(e_start_2) <= t_abs(e_server_2) <= s2 => s1 < s2`. "Because the coordinator leader chose s based on TT.now().latest, and now waits until that timestamp is guaranteed to be in the past, the expected wait is at least 2 * epsilon-bar. This wait is typically overlapped with Paxos communication."
- §4.1.1/§4.1.3 safe time: Paxos leader leases 10 s default with disjointness invariant; `tsafe = min(tPaxos_safe, tTM_safe)` where `tPaxos_safe` is highest-applied Paxos write timestamp, `tTM_safe` is ∞ if zero prepared transactions else `min_i(s_prepare_i,g) - 1`. A replica can satisfy a read at `t` only if `t <= tsafe`. Augmentations via fine-grained lock-table mappings (§4.2.4) remove false conflicts but do not change the invariant.

The mechanism is therefore threefold and stated with its limits: (1) represent wall time as a bounded interval `[earliest, latest]` with explicit `epsilon`, not a point; decide comparisons only via `TT.after`/`TT.before` (interval definitely past / definitely not arrived); (2) assign commit timestamps at `latest` and wait out the interval (`commit wait >= 2*epsilon-bar`) so timestamp order implies external order; (3) gate reads on `tsafe` — offer `t` only when the replica is known up-to-date. The paper's concession is explicit (p.1, §1, §3): "If the uncertainty is large, Spanner slows down to wait out that uncertainty." External consistency is bought by waiting, not by assuming clocks agree.

## What is new here

Spanner's contribution beyond the already-typed Akidau watermark is not another completeness estimate on event time — it is a clock-uncertainty interval on wall time and a wait that makes point comparisons honest. The Dataflow watermark asks "have we seen all events up to time W?" TrueTime asks "do we know what time it is right now to within epsilon?" The two mechanisms are complementary and non-substitutable: high-water epoch moves the freshness anchor from last row to `max(epochs)` (still a point); TrueTime replaces the point anchor with an interval `[now.earliest, now.latest]` whose half-width `epsilon = (latest-earliest)/2` is an explicitly advertised bound.

What the source gives that this plant lacks:

1. **Point vs interval.** §3 Table 1: `TT.after(t)` is true only when `t has definitely passed` (`t < earliest`), not when `now_point > t`. The plant's `now - last_epoch` and `last_epoch - first_epoch` are of the latter form — a point difference taken as fact. With Spanner's numbers, `epsilon` sawtooths 1–7 ms and `epsilon-bar = 4 ms`; expected commit wait is `>= 2*epsilon-bar` (~8 ms) precisely because `TT.now().latest` overstates. The plant's 30 s `FRESH_SECONDS` / `DENSE_FRESH_SECONDS` gate is two orders larger than Spanner's `epsilon`, but the Lamport divergence measured on the frozen tail is `100 s` lag and `25.6%` behind high-water — the wall-clock + phone-clock divergence dominates Spanner's `epsilon` by `>10,000×`. Declaring a verdict inside that divergence without an `epsilon` repeats Lamport's C1/C2 conflation at larger scale.

2. **Waiting out uncertainty vs assuming freshness.** §4.1.2 commit wait: assign at `latest`, then block until `TT.after(s)` (s in the past). Freshness in the plant is decided instantly from one subtraction with no wait and no `TT.after` check; `read_redmi10_dense_baseline` publishes `break=4.2s verified` as if the wall and phone clocks agreed to sub-second. Spanner's rule would require `now.earliest - last_epoch.latest > 30` to declare fresh, or at least to publish the interval `[now.earliest - last_epoch.latest, now.latest - last_epoch.earliest]` and stay UNKNOWN while the threshold lies inside it.

3. **Safe time vs last-row time.** §4.1.3 `tsafe` (and §4.2.4 `MinNextTS` advancement every 8 s, default `8 s` idle worst case) gates reads on replica up-to-dateness, not on the newest row's wall time. The plant's closest analogue `read_redmi10_delivery_gaps` (`GAP_WINDOW_SECONDS = 86400`, `class-c-delivery-gap-detectors.jsonl` 128 lines, `excursion-log-redmi10.txt` `_worst_batch_delay`) observes halts exogenously after the fact and `_gap_records` returns UNKNOWN on any malformed line (gap-stays-gap consistent with Tail at Scale's concession "Eliminating all sources of latency variability … is impractical"). Spanner's `tsafe` is a per-replica invariant before serving `t`; the plant has no per-replica `tsafe` and no `MinNextTS` heartbeat — a read at `t` that coincides with a halt is served with whatever `last_epoch` happens to be.

Limits that travel with the mechanism (the paper states them):

- The small `epsilon` (1–7 ms, `epsilon-bar 4 ms`) is achieved only by continuous GPS + atomic references, cross-checking rate against local clock, Marzullo liar rejection over diverse masters, and eviction on frequency excursion. Datacenter-wide and localized `epsilon` spikes still occur on master unavailability or overload. The plant has none of these: no time masters, no reference interval to bound `epsilon`, no 30 s poll, no 200 µs/s drift rate, no eviction. Any `epsilon` declared here is an assumption, not a measurement, and must be labelled as such (cf. Akidau footnote 6 that most watermarks are heuristically established).
- The `2*epsilon-bar` wait is "typically overlapped with Paxos communication" (p.7–8) — waiting is affordable inside a distributed commit. In a single-node `5 s` cadence file tail, waiting `8 ms` is negligible but waiting `100 s` to clear the Lamport divergence would flip the `30 s` freshness gate by construction. The correct transfer is therefore not to wait `100 s` per read, but to publish the interval and let the verdict stay UNKNOWN while the threshold is inside it, exactly as `TT.before`/`TT.after` do.
- `tsafe` and `MinNextTS` depend on Paxos leadership (10 s leases, `smax` advancement, §4.1.1 disjointness) that the plant does not have — single-writer file with sequence numbers but no leases, no participant `prepare` timestamps, no coordinator wait. The transfer is at the level of the discipline (don't serve `t` you are not safe for), not the protocol.

Distinction from already-landed REVISITs on this cell: Akidau (mechanism 2026-11-01) supplies the watermark/high-water mechanism on event time; RFC 9002 (transfer 2026-11-03) supplies dual packet/time loss thresholds and PTO probe; RFC 3550 (metric/transfer 2026-11-01) supplies `fraction lost`/`J` measurement. None supplies the wall-clock interval/watch-then-serve mechanism; Spanner's Table 1 / `epsilon` / `commit wait` / `tsafe` is the missing complement. Schafer & Graham, Rubin/MAR, Narayanan & Shmatikov, Pufferfish, and Lange et al. remain the gap/re-identification stack; TrueTime does not replace them.
