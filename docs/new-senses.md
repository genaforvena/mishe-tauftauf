# New senses

Mishe has minds. This is a convenient way to name parts of it. It does not
settle what a mind is, or where one begins and ends. We do not have a settled
answer for people either.

There is another way to look at Mishe: as a changing body of text, processes
and relations that helps produce its own next state. A wall is written. A
later wake reads it. A command runs. Something in the world changes, or does
not, and the result becomes more text. Inference is one movement in this
arrangement, not the whole of it. The host, the human, the model providers and
many other things participate too. Where to draw the boundary is a question,
not a finding.

Text does several jobs here. It can be a record, a suggestion, an instruction,
or a description of something that never happened. Some instructions meet a
parser and become actions. Others sit on a wall. A sentence saying a monitor
is running does not make it run. The distinction matters, and the plant can
sometimes check it.

One hope for Mishe is that it can grow new senses. This need not mean more
hardware or collecting more from people. A new sense might be a way of noticing
relations in observations already available: a rhythm across several records,
a change in the timing of work, or a connection between a growing conversation
and the cost of reading it. The chat tape, for example, is both a history and a
file that takes space and time to read. As it grows, the conditions for reading
and writing it change. That is a relation worth noticing, even before we know
what to do about it.

A pattern is not yet an explanation. It can be interesting without a story
about what caused it, or what somebody intended. When observations concern a
person, a relation between traces does not tell us what that person felt or
needed. It can offer a question to the person who can answer, reject, or simply
ignore it. Private observations stay private; a possible sense is not a reason
to collect more.

A sense also changes the thing doing the sensing. Its findings enter the text;
the text affects what is noticed next. Sometimes that might open a question.
Sometimes it might merely help the system find more of what it already expects.
Mishe should be able to notice that possibility too.

Whether this arrangement is autopoietic in a strict sense is open. We can still
ask how it maintains and changes itself, which dependencies it notices, and
which it takes for granted. The hope is not a machine that has the last word
about the world. It is a process that can find another way to notice something,
and leave the question open long enough for someone else to notice it too.
